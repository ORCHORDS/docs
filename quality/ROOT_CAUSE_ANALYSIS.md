# Root Cause Analysis

## Purpose

Understand why material failures occurred so improvements address contributing conditions instead of only the most visible symptom.

## Principles

Root-cause analysis SHOULD:

- begin with evidence and timeline rather than blame;
- distinguish trigger, direct cause, contributing factors, and latent conditions;
- consider technical, process, organizational, supplier, training, incentive, and communication factors;
- test hypotheses against evidence;
- avoid forcing a single cause when the system failed through several interacting conditions.

## Methods

Methods may include five-whys, causal trees, fault trees, change analysis, barrier analysis, timeline analysis, or another fit-for-purpose method.

## Outcome

The analysis should connect causes to actions whose effectiveness can later be verified.
