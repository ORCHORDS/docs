# AI Governance Policy

## Purpose

Define accountable, risk-based governance for acquiring, building, deploying,
and using artificial-intelligence capabilities.

## Principles

- AI use has a defined purpose and accountable owner.
- Human responsibility is not delegated to a model.
- Risk review is proportionate to impact, autonomy, data sensitivity, scale,
  user reliance, and reversibility.
- Training, evaluation, and production data are handled under privacy, security,
  data, and contractual requirements.
- Material limitations and uncertainty are considered before relying on output.
- High-impact uses require stronger testing, oversight, monitoring, and
  fallback paths.
- Generated output is not treated as authoritative solely because it is
  confident or fluent.
- Security testing includes misuse, prompt/instruction manipulation, data
  leakage, unsafe tool use, and dependency risk where relevant.

## Lifecycle

AI governance covers intake, risk assessment, design/acquisition, evaluation,
approval, deployment, monitoring, incident handling, material change, and
retirement.

## Evidence

Material AI decisions record purpose, owner, risk tier, data considerations,
evaluation evidence, human-oversight model, limitations, approval, and review
date.

## Standards

NIST AI RMF 1.0 and NIST AI 600-1 provide current reference guidance. AI RMF
1.0 is under revision; this policy does not treat a future revision as final.
