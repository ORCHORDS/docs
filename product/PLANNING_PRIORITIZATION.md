# Planning and Prioritization Policy

## Purpose

Make prioritization explicit, evidence-based, revisable, and connected to
business outcomes.

## Inputs

Prioritization considers:

- user or customer impact;
- business value;
- security, legal, or operational risk;
- urgency and time sensitivity;
- confidence in evidence;
- effort and opportunity cost;
- dependencies;
- reversibility;
- learning value.

## Decision discipline

- Separate facts from assumptions.
- State what evidence would change the decision.
- Do not present roadmap intent as a commitment unless explicitly approved.
- Reassess work when risk, evidence, constraints, or strategy materially
  changes.
- Security and critical operational work can override normal feature scoring.

## Scoring

A scoring framework such as RICE may inform decisions, but scores do not make
the decision. Qualitative risk, strategic fit, uncertainty, and mandatory work
remain explicit.

## Review cadence

Active priorities are reviewed regularly. Longer-range plans use wider
confidence ranges and fewer false-precision dates.

## Outcomes

Every material initiative should have:

- accountable owner;
- intended outcome;
- success measure;
- key assumptions;
- important dependencies;
- review point.
