# modern-slavery-act-compliance

**Issue:** Modern Slavery Act 2015 (UK) and equivalent legislation transparency statements
**Date:** 2026-08-11
**Status:** documented

## Symptom / Context
UK Modern Slavery Act Section 54 requires commercial organizations with global annual turnover of GBP 36M+ doing business in the UK to publish an annual transparency statement covering steps taken to prevent slavery and human trafficking in supply chains.

## Pattern / Solution
Mandatory transparency statement content (at minimum):
1. Organization structure, business, and supply chains
2. Policies in relation to slavery and human trafficking
3. Due diligence processes for slavery and human trafficking in business and supply chains
4. Risk assessment and management: parts of business and supply chains at risk
5. Key performance indicators: how effectiveness of steps is measured
6. Training: about slavery and human trafficking for staff

Publication requirements:
- Published on company website within 6 months of financial year end
- Signed by director (or equivalent for LLP/partnership)
- Link from homepage (or accessible from homepage)
- Approved by board

Best practice (beyond minimum):
- Supplier code of conduct covering labor standards
- Supplier self-assessment questionnaires on labor practices
- Third-party audits of high-risk suppliers
- Worker grievance mechanism accessible to supply chain workers
- Disclosure of specific high-risk geographies or sectors

Similar laws globally:
- Australia Modern Slavery Act 2018: entities with AUD 100M+ revenue; joint venture statements allowed
- California Transparency in Supply Chains Act (SB 657): retail sellers and manufacturers >$100M global receipts
- Germany Supply Chain Due Diligence Act (LkSG): mandatory due diligence for human rights and environment

## Gotchas
- UK government can name and shame non-compliant companies publicly — enforcement escalating
- "Doing business in the UK" is broad — includes UK sales without UK incorporation
- Statement cannot be generic — specificity about actual due diligence steps required
- Subsidiaries may need separate statements; check if consolidated statement covers all entities
