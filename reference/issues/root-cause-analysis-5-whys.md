# Root Cause Analysis: 5 Whys

## What is 5 Whys?

The 5 Whys is a simple yet powerful iterative questioning technique used to identify the root cause of problems. Despite its name, the technique doesn't require exactly five questions—typically 3-7 iterations suffice to reach true causation.

## How It Works

Start with a problem statement and ask "Why?" repeatedly. Each answer forms the foundation for the next question. The process continues until you reach a fundamental cause that can be addressed directly.

Example:
- Problem: Machine breaks down
- Why? Because belt snapped
- Why? Because it was over-tightened
- Why? Because operator didn't follow procedure
- Why? Because training wasn't completed
- Why? Because budget was cut for training

## Causal Chain Methodology

The 5 Whys creates a causal chain where each "why" builds upon the previous answer. This systematic approach prevents jumping to surface-level symptoms and ensures comprehensive analysis.

Each iteration should move from effect to cause, from symptoms to underlying factors. The technique works best when team members with relevant knowledge participate in the questioning process.

## Stopping Condition

The analysis stops when you reach a root cause that can be directly addressed through action. This typically occurs when:
- The answer is actionable and controllable
- No further "why" questions reveal deeper causal relationships
- The solution would prevent recurrence of the problem

Avoid stopping too early (at symptoms) or too late (at unrelated factors).

## Common Pitfalls

**Premature closure**: Stopping after only 2-3 iterations without thorough exploration.

**Blaming individuals**: Focusing on people rather than processes or systems.

**Assumption-based answers**: Providing answers without evidence or verification.

**Groupthink**: Team members deferring to authority figures instead of questioning assumptions.

**Overcomplicating**: Adding unnecessary complexity to simple problems.

## Alternative: Fishbone Diagram

While 5 Whys focuses on sequential questioning, the Fishbone (Ishikawa) diagram provides a visual approach. It categorizes causes into major groups (People, Methods, Machines, Materials, Environment, Measurements) and maps contributing factors.

The fishbone method complements 5 Whys by providing structure to organize multiple potential root causes before applying iterative questioning to specific areas.
