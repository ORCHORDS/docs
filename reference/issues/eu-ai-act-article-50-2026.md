# eu-ai-act-article-50-2026

**Issue:** A team deploys an AI system that generates synthetic media (text, images, video). The team doesn't know EU AI Act Article 50 requires the content to be machine-readable marked as AI-generated. The team also doesn't know about the deepfake disclosure obligation.

**Date:** 2026-08-10
**Repo:** example-org/example-repo
**Author:** ORCHORDS
**Status:** published

## Symptom

EU AI Act Article 50 covers transparency obligations for AI systems that interact with people, generate content, or recognize emotions. The 2026 default: assume Article 50 applies; mark AI-generated content; disclose AI involvement; document the exceptions.

## Root cause

Article 50 has 6 transparency obligations. Non-compliance is a Tier 4 violation (up to €7.5M or 1% of global turnover). The obligations apply from August 2, 2025 (Article 5 + 50 effective). The 2026 production pattern: implement all 6; document exceptions.

## The 6 Article 50 obligations

Article 50 covers 6 scenarios.

1. **AI systems interacting with people** — disclose AI nature (Article 50(1))
2. **Emotion recognition systems** — inform the person (Article 50(3))
3. **Biometric categorization systems** — inform the person (Article 50(3))
4. **Deepfake systems** — disclose that content is artificially generated or manipulated (Article 50(4))
5. **AI-generated synthetic content** — machine-readable marking as artificially generated (Article 50(4))
6. **AI systems generating text published to inform the public** — disclose AI generation (Article 50(4))

The 6 obligations cover the user-facing AI surface.

## The 5 obligated actors

| Actor | Obligation |
|---|---|
| Provider (deploys the model) | ensure the system is designed for compliance |
| Deployer (uses the system) | apply the disclosures in the user-facing context |
| Distributor | provide information to downstream |
| Importer | verify compliance before placing on market |
| Manufacturer | ensure technical documentation |

The 5 actors map to the 5 supply chain roles in EU product safety law.

## The 4 technical marking methods

For "machine-readable marking" (Article 50(4)):

1. **Watermarks** — visible or invisible signals in content (SynthID, C2PA)
2. **Metadata** — embedded in file format (C2PA Content Credentials, XMP)
3. **Cryptographic signatures** — digital signatures verifiable offline
4. **Standardized identifiers** — recognized by EU standards

The 4 methods are the 2026 production baseline; C2PA is the de facto standard.

## The 3 exceptions

Article 50 has 3 exceptions where the obligations may not apply.

1. **Law enforcement** — if disclosure would interfere with the purpose
2. **National security** — Member State may restrict for national security reasons
3. **Artistic, creative, satirical, fictional works** — disclosure not required if the AI generation is part of obvious artistic/creative work

The 3 exceptions are narrow; document the basis for any exception.

## The 5-step implementation pattern

1. **Map your systems** to the 6 obligations (interaction, emotion, biometric, deepfake, synthetic, public-information text)
2. **For each applicable obligation**, design the disclosure (UI label, metadata, watermark)
3. **For synthetic content**, use C2PA or similar standard
4. **For deepfakes**, add visible disclosure ("This content has been AI-generated")
5. **Document exceptions** with a written policy

The 5 steps are the 2026 production baseline.

## The 4 deepfake disclosure patterns

| Pattern | Example | When |
|---|---|---|
| Visible label | "AI-generated" overlay | image, video |
| Text description | "This message was generated by AI" | chat, email |
| Audio cue | "This audio was AI-generated" beep | voice |
| Watermark | C2PA Content Credentials | all media |

The 4 patterns cover the 2026 deepfake disclosure surface.

## The 5 best practices

1. **Default to disclosure.** Disclose even when the exception might apply; review per use.
2. **Use C2PA for synthetic content.** C2PA is the de facto 2026 standard for content credentials.
3. **Make disclosures user-visible.** Hidden disclosures (metadata only) are insufficient for deepfakes.
4. **Document exceptions in writing.** Article 50 exceptions require a legal basis.
5. **Test the user experience.** The disclosure should be clear without overwhelming the content.

## The 5 anti-patterns

1. **No disclosure because "the user knows."** Article 50 doesn't have a "user knows" exception.
2. **Tiny text in the corner.** A 6pt "AI-generated" tag is not user-visible.
3. **Metadata only, no visible label.** C2PA metadata is required; deepfakes also need a visible label.
4. **No C2PA, just a custom metadata.** C2PA is the de facto standard; custom schemes don't satisfy "machine-readable."
5. **Exemption claim without documentation.** Article 50 exceptions require a legal basis.

## The 4 C2PA components

C2PA (Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity) has 4 components.

1. **C2PA Manifest** — the metadata document with claims
2. **C2PA Assertion** — a specific claim (e.g., "AI-generated," "captured by camera X")
3. **C2PA Signature** — cryptographic signature validating the assertion
4. **C2PA Store** — where the manifest is stored (often embedded in file metadata)

The 4 components together make content verifiable.

## The 5 chat interaction patterns

For AI systems that interact with people:

1. **Welcome message** — "You're chatting with [AI assistant name], an AI."
2. **Footer label** — persistent "AI assistant" label in the UI
3. **First message disclaimer** — "I'm an AI; my responses may be incorrect."
4. **Voice disclosure** — "This is an AI assistant speaking" (for voice/IVR)
5. **Avatar / persona** — clearly non-human (robot icon, "AI" badge)

The 5 patterns cover the 2026 chat UI surface.

## The 3 emotion recognition obligations

For AI systems that infer emotions:

1. **Inform the person** that the system is being used (Article 50(3))
2. **Process only the data necessary** for the stated purpose
3. **Do not infer emotions in protected categories** — race, religion, etc.

The 3 obligations are specific to emotion recognition systems; broader than face recognition alone.

## The 4 biometric categorization obligations

For AI systems that categorize people by biometric data:

1. **Inform the person** that categorization is happening (Article 50(3))
2. **Document the categorization categories** — race, age, gender, etc.
3. **Do not infer sensitive attributes** that aren't part of the stated purpose
4. **Audit for bias** in the categorization

The 4 obligations are specific to biometric categorization.

## The 5 standards for AI content marking

| Standard | Use | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| C2PA | image, video, audio | most complete 2026 standard |
| SynthID (Google) | text, image, audio, video | proprietary but available |
| Watermark (generic) | any content | simple, fragile |
| Metadata (XMP, EXIF) | image | standard but easy to strip |
| Digital signature | any | verifiable, requires key management |

The 5 standards cover the 2026 content marking surface; C2PA is the default.

## The 3 verification patterns

For verifying AI-generated content:

1. **C2PA verifier** — checks the C2PA manifest and signature
2. **SynthID detector** — detects Google's watermark
3. **Custom detector** — for proprietary schemes

The 3 patterns are the 2026 verification surface; C2PA verifier is the de facto standard.

## Verification

The tell that Article 50 compliance is real:

- Every AI interaction with a person is disclosed
- Every emotion recognition system informs the user
- Every biometric categorization system informs the user
- Every deepfake has a visible disclosure
- Every AI-generated synthetic content has a C2PA manifest
- Article 50 exceptions are documented with legal basis

The tell it isn't:

- "The user knows"
- "It's a chatbot; everyone knows"
- No C2PA; no watermarks
- Deepfakes without visible labels
- "We're exempt" without legal basis

## Gotchas

- **Article 50 applies to all AI systems meeting the criteria, not just high-risk.** The scope is broader than Annex III.
- **C2PA is the de facto standard.** Custom schemes don't satisfy "machine-readable" cleanly.
- **Visible labels are required for deepfakes.** Metadata alone is insufficient.
- **Exceptions require documentation.** "We're exempt" is not enough; legal basis is required.
- **The 1% penalty is for misleading information.** Article 50 violations fall into the Tier 4 category; up to €7.5M or 1%.
