# IRS Business Recordkeeping

## Purpose

Provide public governance guidance for maintaining business records that support U.S. federal tax reporting and substantiation.

## Baseline

The IRS states that good records help businesses monitor progress, prepare financial statements, identify income, track deductible expenses and basis, prepare tax returns, and support items reported on returns.

A business may generally choose a recordkeeping system suited to its operations so long as it clearly shows income and expenses and retains supporting documentation appropriate to the transactions recorded.

## Governance guidance

- Maintain records sufficient to support gross receipts, expenses, credits, payroll, assets, and other reportable transactions.
- Retain supporting documents such as invoices, receipts, deposit records, paid bills, and other transaction evidence where applicable.
- Keep tax records for the period required by the applicable tax rule, transaction, or event rather than applying one universal retention period.
- Preserve records in a form that remains readable, complete, and retrievable throughout the retention period.
- Separate business and non-business transactions where necessary for accurate reporting.
- Restrict access to tax records based on confidentiality and role need.

## Verification

Confirm that material tax-return positions can be traced to underlying books and supporting documents and that retention schedules are based on applicable requirements rather than convenience alone.
