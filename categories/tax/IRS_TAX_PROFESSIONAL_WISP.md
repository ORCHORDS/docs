# IRS Tax Professional Written Information Security Plan

## Purpose

Provide public governance guidance for Written Information Security Plans (WISPs) used by tax and accounting practices handling taxpayer or client information.

## Baseline

The IRS and Security Summit state that tax professionals are required to create and maintain a written information security plan tailored to the size, scope, complexity, and sensitivity of the customer information they handle.

## Governance guidance

A WISP should address, as applicable:

- designation of one or more people responsible for coordinating the information security program;
- identification and assessment of risks to customer information;
- evaluation of safeguards already in place;
- design and implementation of safeguards proportionate to identified risks;
- employee training and management;
- information-system protection;
- detection and management of system failures or security events;
- oversight of service providers handling customer information;
- periodic monitoring, testing, and adjustment of the program.

## Evidence examples

Evidence may include the approved WISP, risk assessments, safeguard testing, training records, service-provider requirements, incident records, and periodic review or revision history.

## Verification

Confirm that the WISP reflects the current operating environment, actual customer-data handling, current service providers, and results of recent monitoring or testing. A static template alone is not evidence of an operating security program.
