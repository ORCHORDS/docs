# Antitrust Merger Review Governance

## Sources

- U.S. Federal Trade Commission — 2023 Merger Guidelines: https://www.ftc.gov/reports/merger-guidelines-2023
- U.S. Federal Trade Commission — Mergers and antitrust guidance: https://www.ftc.gov/advice-guidance/competition-guidance/guide-antitrust-laws/mergers

## Purpose

Corporate transactions can raise competition-law issues before closing. The FTC explains that Section 7 of the Clayton Act addresses mergers and acquisitions whose effect may substantially lessen competition or tend to create a monopoly. The FTC and U.S. Department of Justice use the 2023 Merger Guidelines as an analytical framework for merger review; the Guidelines are not themselves legally binding.

## Governance expectations

For transactions that may create competition-law exposure:

- identify relevant jurisdictions early;
- obtain qualified legal review before making external claims about antitrust clearance;
- assess whether premerger notification or waiting-period rules may apply;
- preserve the evidence and assumptions used in the competition analysis;
- avoid presenting agency guidelines as a substitute for applicable law or case-specific legal advice; and
- keep transaction-sensitive information within the approved confidentiality boundary.

## Decision record

The transaction file should record, where applicable:

- jurisdictions reviewed;
- whether notification thresholds were assessed;
- outside-counsel or specialist review status;
- material competition risks identified;
- required approvals or waiting periods; and
- conditions that must be satisfied before closing.

## Public claims

Do not state that a transaction is "approved," "cleared," or free of antitrust risk without current evidence supporting that statement. Do not expose confidential merger-investigation information in public documentation.

This document describes governance only and does not assert that any ORCHORDS transaction is pending, reviewed, reportable, or cleared.
