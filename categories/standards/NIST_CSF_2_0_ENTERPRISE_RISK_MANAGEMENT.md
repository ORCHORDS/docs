# NIST CSF 2.0 and Enterprise Risk Management

## Purpose

Provide public guidance for integrating NIST CSF 2.0 cybersecurity outcomes with broader enterprise risk-management processes.

## Baseline

NIST provides a CSF 2.0 Quick Start Guide for Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) practitioners. The guide explains how CSF outcomes can support consistent cybersecurity-risk communication and integration with organization-wide risk processes.

## Integration guidance

- Use common risk terminology and scales where practical so cybersecurity risks can be compared with other enterprise risks.
- Connect CSF outcomes to business objectives, risk owners, impact scenarios, and treatment decisions.
- Escalate material cybersecurity risks through the same governance channels used for other significant enterprise risks.
- Record dependencies between cyber risk, operational resilience, suppliers, legal obligations, finance, and strategic objectives.
- Distinguish inherent risk, current controls, residual risk, and target treatment where those concepts are used.
- Reassess cyber risks when material business or technology conditions change.

## Evidence examples

Evidence may include enterprise risk registers, cybersecurity risk assessments, risk-acceptance decisions, committee records, treatment plans, and cross-functional reporting.

## Verification

Confirm that cybersecurity risks are represented in enterprise risk processes at a level proportionate to their potential impact and that material decisions have accountable owners and documented rationale.
