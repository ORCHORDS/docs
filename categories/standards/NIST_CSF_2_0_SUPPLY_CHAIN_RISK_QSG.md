# NIST CSF 2.0 — Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk

## Purpose

Provide public guidance for using NIST CSF 2.0 to improve cybersecurity supply-chain risk management for technology products and services.

## Baseline

NIST’s CSF 2.0 Quick Start Guide for Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management is intended to help organizations become more informed acquirers and suppliers of technology products and services.

## Governance guidance

- Identify critical suppliers, products, services, and dependencies.
- Define cybersecurity expectations before acquisition and include them in evaluation and contracting where appropriate.
- Assign ownership for supplier-risk decisions, exceptions, and escalation.
- Monitor material supplier changes, incidents, vulnerabilities, and concentration risk.
- Include supplier dependencies in incident response, continuity, and recovery planning.
- Reassess supplier risk when scope, architecture, data access, ownership, or threat conditions change.

## Evidence examples

Evidence may include supplier inventories, criticality assessments, due-diligence records, contract requirements, risk exceptions, monitoring records, incident notifications, and exit or contingency plans.

## Verification

Confirm that critical suppliers are identified, risk requirements are proportionate to dependency and exposure, and supplier assurances are supported by current evidence rather than marketing claims alone.
