# NIST CSF 2.0 — Informative References

## Purpose

Provide public guidance for using NIST CSF 2.0 Informative References to connect CSF outcomes with other standards, frameworks, regulations, and guidance.

## Baseline

NIST describes Informative References as mappings that help users understand relationships between CSF Core outcomes and other cybersecurity documents or requirements.

## Governance guidance

- Treat mappings as aids to analysis, not automatic equivalence or proof of compliance.
- Record the exact source, version, scope, and date used for a mapping.
- Review mappings when either the CSF or the referenced source changes.
- Distinguish direct relationships from partial, contextual, or interpretive mappings.
- Validate high-impact mappings with subject-matter expertise before using them for assurance or regulatory decisions.
- Avoid implying that satisfying one mapped control necessarily satisfies the corresponding CSF outcome or vice versa.

## Evidence examples

Evidence may include version-controlled crosswalks, mapping rationale, reviewer notes, source citations, change records, and identified gaps or ambiguities.

## Verification

Confirm that mappings are current, traceable, scoped, and represented with appropriate confidence. Do not use stale or undocumented mappings as conformance evidence.
