# AI-Assisted Research Analysis

## Purpose

Govern use of generative AI and other AI tools when summarizing, coding, classifying, or interpreting research material.

Primary sources:
- https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework
- https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence

## Control principles

AI output is an analytical aid, not evidence by itself. Research conclusions must remain traceable to the underlying observations, records, or datasets.

When AI assists analysis:

- define the task and permitted data before use;
- avoid submitting confidential, personal, or participant data to an AI service unless the approved data-handling arrangement permits it;
- preserve source material independently of generated summaries;
- record the model/tool and material configuration details needed to understand the analysis context;
- review generated classifications, themes, summaries, and quotations against the source material;
- test for material omissions, unsupported claims, and systematic bias;
- require human review before findings are published or used for significant decisions.

## Reproducibility

Where AI assistance materially affects a finding, retain enough evidence to explain how the finding was produced without depending on the model to reproduce the same wording later. Model outputs can vary over time and across configurations.

## Claims boundary

Do not present AI-generated interpretation as participant testimony, measured fact, or independently validated evidence. Distinguish source observations from generated synthesis.

## Escalation

Escalate when AI use could materially affect participant rights, sensitive-topic interpretation, legal/compliance conclusions, or high-impact business decisions.
