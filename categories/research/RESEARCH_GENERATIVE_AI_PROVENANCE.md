# Generative AI Provenance Governance

## Source

NIST AI 600-1 — Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile: https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1

NIST describes content provenance as tracking the origin and history of digital content and notes that provenance metadata can include creator/developer information, date or time, modifications, sources, and other history relevant to authenticity and integrity.

## Governance expectations

When generative AI materially contributes to research artifacts, analysis drafts, summaries, synthetic data, images, audio, or other evidence-bearing outputs:

- record that generative AI was used when that fact is material to interpretation or assurance;
- retain the source material needed to independently verify factual conclusions;
- distinguish human-authored findings from model-generated suggestions or transformations;
- record significant modifications to synthetic content when provenance affects trust or reuse;
- do not treat provenance metadata alone as proof that content is correct; and
- preserve privacy, confidentiality, intellectual-property, and data-minimization boundaries when recording provenance.

## Research record

Where proportionate to risk, the research record should capture:

- tool/model or service used;
- date of use;
- source inputs or a reference to the controlled source set;
- material prompts or task description when necessary for reproducibility;
- human review performed;
- substantive edits or transformations; and
- limitations that affect downstream interpretation.

## Claims boundary

Provenance improves traceability; it does not establish truth, scientific validity, legal ownership, or absence of manipulation by itself. Generated material used as evidence must still pass the normal research verification and approval process.

This public document defines governance expectations only and contains no private prompts, credentials, internal datasets, customer information, or unreleased research results.
