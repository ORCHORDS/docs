# Security Reporting Culture

## Purpose

Encourage early reporting of mistakes, suspicious activity, control weaknesses, and near misses so risk can be reduced quickly.

## Principles

Personnel should be able to report honest mistakes and uncertainty without incentives to conceal them. Intentional misconduct and repeated reckless disregard are handled separately from good-faith reporting.

## Reportable examples

Examples include accidental sharing, suspicious authentication prompts, lost devices, unexpected access, secret exposure, unsafe configuration, social-engineering attempts, or a security control that appears ineffective.

## Response quality

Reports should receive acknowledgement appropriate to urgency, and reporters should be given enough feedback to understand that the issue was handled when practical.

Metrics must not punish teams merely for reporting more events; higher reporting can indicate healthier detection culture.
