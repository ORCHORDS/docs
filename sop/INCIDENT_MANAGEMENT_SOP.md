# SOP: Incident Management

## Trigger

Start when an event causes or credibly threatens material security,
availability, integrity, privacy, legal, or business impact.

## Procedure

1. **Declare.** Create an incident record, set initial severity, and appoint an
   Incident Commander.
2. **Stabilize.** Protect people, users, data, and critical business capability.
3. **Preserve.** Retain relevant logs, timestamps, artifacts, and decisions.
4. **Scope.** Separate confirmed facts from hypotheses and identify affected
   capabilities.
5. **Contain.** Reduce blast radius without destroying needed evidence.
6. **Communicate.** Set update cadence and provide factual, audience-appropriate
   updates.
7. **Remediate.** Remove the cause or active threat.
8. **Recover.** Restore service carefully and monitor for recurrence.
9. **Close.** Confirm recovery, assign follow-up actions, and capture the final
   timeline.
10. **Learn.** For material incidents, perform a blameless review focused on
    system and process improvement.

## Severity changes

Severity may increase or decrease as evidence changes. Record the reason.

## Communications

Do not speculate. State what is known, unknown, being done, and when the next
update will occur. Legal, privacy, contractual, or regulatory notification
requirements are escalated to the accountable role immediately.

## Post-incident review

A material incident review should identify contributing conditions,
well-functioning controls, failed assumptions, corrective actions, owners, and
due dates. Avoid reducing the analysis to a single "root cause" when multiple
conditions interacted.
