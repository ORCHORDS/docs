# Crisis Communications

## Purpose

Define communication principles for severe operational, security, privacy, or
business disruptions.

## Principles

Crisis communication MUST be accurate, timely, audience-appropriate, and
coordinated with the incident command structure.

Communications should:

- distinguish confirmed facts from investigation hypotheses;
- avoid exposing sensitive defensive or investigative details;
- identify an accountable message owner;
- state user action only when it is genuinely necessary;
- use timestamps and clear update cadence for evolving events;
- preserve records of material external statements;
- coordinate legal, privacy, security, and operational review when relevant.

## Audiences

Potential audiences include affected users, personnel, partners, suppliers,
regulators or authorities, insurers, and the public. Not every incident
requires every audience.

## Corrections

If a material statement is later found inaccurate, correct it clearly rather
than silently editing history where that would mislead recipients.

## Evidence

Major incidents should record the timeline of approved communications and the
basis for material claims.
