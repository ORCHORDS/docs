# Feedback Loop Into Product

## Purpose

Establish an accountable, evidence-based approach to routing customer-success signals into the product organisation so that customer outcomes inform product investment. The objective is to ensure that feedback arrives in a form product teams can act on, that prioritisation is visible to customer success, and that closure is communicated back so that the customer-success function can speak honestly to customers about what was heard and what was done.

## Scope

This policy applies to any signal originating from the customer-success function — observed adoption friction, outcome-regression patterns, customer-asked features, integration gaps, recurring support themes, expansion blockers, and comparable inputs — that is intended to inform the product roadmap, the defect backlog, the design pipeline, or the documentation pipeline. It does not apply to feedback that the customer addresses directly to product teams through standard product-feedback channels; that feedback follows the product organisation's own intake.

## Requirements

- The feedback loop MUST have a documented intake that the customer-success function can address without ambiguity. The intake MUST distinguish requests, observations, regressions, and incidents; the categories MUST be recorded and used for prioritisation.
- Each feedback item MUST identify the originating customer engagement, the date the item was raised, the supporting evidence, and the customer-success owner. Items submitted without an owner or evidence MUST be returned to the submitter for completion before they enter the queue.
- Items MUST be classified by severity, impact, and frequency. Classification MUST be applied consistently across submissions and reviewed by the receiving team.
- Customer-identifying information MUST be minimised in the feedback item. Where identifying information is required, it MUST be retained only for the documented purpose and accessed only by the documented role; aggregate patterns are preferred over individual disclosures.
- Each item MUST have a documented lifecycle: submitted, triaged, accepted, scheduled, delivered, closed, or declined. The lifecycle stages MUST be visible to the customer-success owner.
- Items accepted into the product backlog MUST be traceable to their product ticket. The customer-success owner MUST be able to retrieve the current status without depending on ad-hoc communication.
- Items declined MUST be declined with a documented reason. The reason MUST be specific enough that the customer-success owner can communicate it to the customer without inventing detail.
- Items that are accepted and scheduled MUST have an estimated delivery window. The window MUST be communicated back to the customer-success owner so the customer can be informed.
- Items delivered MUST close the loop. Closure MUST include a description of what was delivered, the version or release, and a confirmation that the customer-success owner can reference.
- Patterns observed across multiple customers MUST be surfaced as themes, not as individual items. Theme reports MUST be evidence-based, include the customer-success sources, and identify the proposed product response.
- The feedback loop MUST be evaluated at the documented cadence. Evaluation MUST consider the proportion of items triaged within the documented time bound, the proportion declined without a reason, the proportion delivered within the estimated window, and the proportion for which closure feedback was returned.
- Items originating from a customer who has explicitly opted out of being referenced MUST be submitted in aggregate form. The opt-out MUST be honoured regardless of how strongly the customer-success function considers the item important.
- The feedback loop MUST NOT be used to circumvent product governance, privacy review, security review, or legal review. Where an item implies any of those reviews, the relevant function MUST be engaged.
- The feedback loop MUST be auditable. An auditor MUST be able to reconstruct, from the system of record, what was submitted, when, by whom, with what evidence, how it was classified, and how it was resolved.
- The feedback loop MUST be reviewed for bias. Items originating from high-revenue customers MUST NOT be silently prioritised over items originating from smaller customers with comparable evidence.

## Workflow

1. The customer-success owner captures the signal in the intake form, with evidence and a unique identifier.
2. The receiving product team triages the item, classifies it, and either accepts, schedules, declines, or returns it for clarification.
3. The status is recorded in the lifecycle tracker; the customer-success owner is notified.
4. The customer-success owner communicates the status to the customer using language that does not over-commit.
5. On delivery, the closure is recorded and the customer-success owner is notified; the customer is informed.
6. Patterns and themes are surfaced at the documented cadence; the product response is documented.
