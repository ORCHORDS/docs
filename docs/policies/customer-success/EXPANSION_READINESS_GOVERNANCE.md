# Expansion Readiness Governance

## Purpose

Establish an accountable, evidence-based approach to determining whether a customer is ready for expansion — additional scope, additional seats, additional modules, additional services, or upgraded commercial terms. The objective is to ensure that expansion is pursued only when the customer is positioned to realise the expanded value, that the evidence for readiness is defensible, and that the customer is not pressured into commitments whose delivery the company cannot honour.

## Scope

This policy applies to any expansion proposal initiated, sponsored, or co-sponsored by the customer-success function, including expansion of seats, modules, geographies, integrations, support tiers, and managed-service scope. It does not apply to renewal of existing scope on substantively the same terms, which is governed by renewal policy.

## Requirements

- An expansion proposal MUST document the customer outcome the expansion supports, the evidence that the outcome is realistic, the dependencies, the risks, and the customer-impact statement.
- Expansion MUST NOT be pursued when the customer's current outcomes are below baseline in any material respect, unless the proposal explicitly addresses the regression and documents the path back to baseline before or alongside expansion.
- Expansion MUST NOT be pursued when the customer's success-plan adoption milestones are materially behind, unless the proposal identifies the catch-up path, the additional capacity required, and the acceptance criteria.
- Expansion MUST NOT be pursued when the customer's support, security, privacy, or compliance posture is open in a way that the expansion would compound. Open issues MUST be resolved or formally accepted before the proposal advances.
- Expansion proposals MUST include a documented capacity assessment. The assessment MUST consider delivery capacity, support capacity, integration capacity, and the customer-side capacity required to absorb the expansion.
- Expansion proposals MUST include a documented dependency map. The map MUST identify customer-side, company-side, and third-party dependencies, the owners, and the readiness of each.
- Expansion proposals MUST include a documented pricing assessment. The assessment MUST distinguish the change in scope from the change in commercial terms and MUST NOT conflate scope expansion with discount commitments that obscure either.
- Expansion MUST be approved through a documented approval path. Approval MUST involve an independent reviewer who is not the customer-success owner of the deal, and the reviewer MUST consider whether the evidence supports the proposed commitment.
- Expansion proposals MUST be supported by the customer sponsor (or their documented delegate). Approval that proceeds without sponsor endorsement MUST be escalated to senior leadership before it advances.
- Expansion that materially changes the customer's risk profile (for example, by adding a new integration, a new data class, or a new regulatory exposure) MUST be reviewed by security, privacy, and legal before commitment.
- Expansion MUST NOT be used to disguise churn avoidance. Where a customer is at risk of churn, expansion proposals MUST be evaluated separately from retention actions, and the underlying retention issue MUST be addressed on its own merits.
- Expansion that has been proposed and declined by the customer MUST NOT be re-pitched within the documented cooling-off period unless the customer opens the conversation or new material evidence changes the picture.
- Expansion contracts and orders MUST contain the same commitments the proposal documented. Discrepancies between the proposal and the order MUST be resolved before the order is signed, not after.
- Expansion MUST be evidenced in the success plan after commitment. The plan MUST identify the new outcomes, the new milestones, the new owners, and the new review cadence.
- Expansion outcomes MUST be evaluated at the documented cadence. Expansion whose realised value diverges materially from the proposal MUST be reviewed, and the lessons captured in the expansion-readiness register.

## Workflow

1. The customer-success lead drafts an expansion proposal using the documented template, citing the outcome, evidence, dependencies, risks, capacity, and approval path.
2. A peer reviewer verifies the evidence base and the assumptions; an independent approver signs off on the readiness judgement.
3. Security, privacy, and legal review the proposal where its scope requires their input.
4. The proposal is presented to the customer through the customer's documented governance path; the customer sponsor (or delegate) confirms the readiness view.
5. The order is drafted from the proposal; discrepancies are resolved before signature.
6. The success plan is updated post-commitment with the new scope; outcomes are evaluated at the documented cadence.
