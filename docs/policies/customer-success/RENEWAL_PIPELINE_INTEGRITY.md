# Renewal Pipeline Integrity

## Purpose

Establish an accountable, evidence-based approach to preserving the integrity of the renewal pipeline — the system through which upcoming renewals are tracked, classified, and acted upon. The objective is to ensure that the pipeline reflects reality, that probability calibration is grounded in evidence rather than optimism, that stage definitions are consistently applied, and that the renewal forecast is honest about the cases most likely to slip.

## Scope

This policy applies to the renewal pipeline at every stage, including early visibility, qualification, commercial alignment, risk assessment, forecasting, and post-renewal recertification. It covers any data, model, workflow, or report that influences renewal decisions, including stage definitions, probability assignments, age-based weighting, exclusion rules, and the recertification cycle. It does not replace commercial forecasting policy; it focuses on the customer-success contribution to that forecasting.

## Requirements

- The renewal pipeline MUST have documented stage definitions. Each stage MUST describe the criteria for entry, the criteria for exit, the actions required at that stage, and the typical duration.
- Stage entry and exit criteria MUST be applied consistently across the pipeline. Manual override of stage criteria MUST be approved by an independent reviewer and recorded with the reason.
- Probability assignments MUST be calibrated against ground truth at the documented cadence. Calibration results MUST be retained and reviewed; uncalibrated probabilities MUST NOT be used as a basis for forecast commitments.
- Probabilities MUST NOT be adjusted to make the forecast look better. Adjustments made for any reason MUST be documented, justified, and reviewed; "presentation-friendly" adjustments are prohibited.
- The pipeline MUST distinguish cases at material risk of churn from cases that are merely behind on adoption. The two categories MUST be tracked separately so that intervention design does not conflate them.
- The pipeline MUST distinguish cases whose renewal is on substantively the same terms from cases where scope, pricing, or contract structure is changing. Renewal forecasting MUST NOT conflate the two populations.
- Age-based weighting (for example, the longer a deal sits in a stage without progressing, the higher the risk) MUST be applied only where the weighting is documented, calibrated, and re-evaluated at the cadence. Inherited or convenience weights are prohibited.
- Exclusion rules (cases that are removed from the pipeline before completion) MUST be documented. Exclusions MUST be reviewed to ensure that the customer has not been quietly removed from active monitoring.
- The pipeline MUST be auditable. From any number in the forecast, an auditor MUST be able to retrieve the underlying cases, their stage at each point, the probability at each point, the actions taken, and the rationale for any overrides.
- The pipeline MUST be recertified at the documented cadence. Recertification MUST compare forecast versus actual, identify drift, and either close the gap or update the policy.
- A renewal that closes MUST be retained in the pipeline with its final state and the recertification outcome. Closing a renewal MUST NOT delete the history; the history MUST remain auditable.
- The pipeline MUST reflect the customer-side governance. Where the customer's own procurement, legal, or approval cycle affects the renewal timing, that cycle MUST be visible in the stage notes.
- The pipeline MUST surface open service, security, privacy, legal, or financial issues that bear on renewal. The pipeline MUST NOT present an "all-clear" forecast when material issues are open.
- The pipeline MUST be evaluated for bias across customer sub-populations. If the pipeline's accuracy varies materially by sub-population, the variation MUST be investigated and either explained or mitigated.
- Renewal-stage actions MUST have documented time bounds. Actions that exceed their bound MUST be escalated through the customer-success action-aging path.
- The pipeline MUST NOT be used to coerce a customer into renewing before their evaluation is complete. Pipeline pressure that conflicts with customer decision-making is prohibited.

## Workflow

1. New renewal opportunities are entered into the pipeline with a documented creation source and an initial stage.
2. Cases progress through stages per the documented criteria; each transition is recorded with the date, the reason, and the operator.
3. Probability is calibrated and reviewed at the documented cadence; the calibration record is retained.
4. Age-based weighting is applied only where the weighting is documented and calibrated.
5. Exclusions are documented and reviewed; recertification is run at the cadence.
6. Closed renewals are retained in the pipeline with the full history; lessons are captured.
