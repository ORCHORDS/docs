# Success Plan Baseline Drift

## Purpose

Establish an accountable, evidence-based approach to detecting and correcting drift between the documented success-plan baseline — the recorded outcomes, milestones, owners, dependencies, and assumptions — and the reality the customer and the customer-success team now face. The objective is to ensure that the plan remains a useful instrument for joint decision-making rather than a stale artefact that misleads either party.

## Scope

This policy applies to any active success plan whose documented baseline is older than the documented recertification cadence. It covers drift in outcome targets, milestone dates, owner assignments, dependency maps, commercial assumptions, scope statements, and success criteria. It does not apply to plans that have been retired or superseded, which are governed by retention and archival practice.

## Requirements

- Every success-plan baseline MUST carry an as-of date, a version identifier, and a documented recertification cadence. The baseline MUST be re-evaluated at or before that cadence.
- Drift detection MUST consider all baseline dimensions: outcomes, milestones, owners, dependencies, scope, commercial assumptions, and success criteria. Drift in any one dimension MUST be reviewed; silent acceptance of drift is prohibited.
- Drift MUST be classified by severity. Material drift (a change that affects the customer's outcomes, the company's commitments, the timing of milestones, or the dependencies on which the plan rests) MUST trigger a documented review; minor drift (a change that does not affect outcomes, timing, or dependencies) MAY be carried forward with a note.
- Drift review MUST distinguish between drift caused by customer-side change, drift caused by company-side change, and drift caused by both. Each category MUST be addressed with the appropriate governance path.
- Drift that invalidates the plan's premise — for example, an outcome the customer no longer prioritises, or a feature the company can no longer deliver — MUST trigger a plan rebuild, not a quiet amendment. Plan rebuilds MUST be documented with both the old and new versions retained.
- Drift that affects a customer commitment MUST be communicated to the customer before it is acted on. Silence in the face of commitment-affecting drift is prohibited.
- Drift that affects a company commitment MUST be escalated internally before the commitment is reset. Resets made without escalation are prohibited.
- Drift that is identified by automated detection MUST be reviewed by a human reviewer before it triggers a customer-facing action. Automation that acts on drift without human review is permitted only for narrow, well-calibrated cases.
- Drift records MUST be retained for the audit-retention window. The records MUST include the as-of date of the prior baseline, the as-of date of the new baseline, the change, the reason, the reviewer, and any resulting actions.
- Drift that recurs across recertifications MUST trigger a structural review. The review MUST consider whether the baseline as designed is fit for purpose, or whether the plan itself needs redesign.
- Drift reviews MUST NOT be skipped because the relationship is "going well." Stable engagements are the engagements where drift is least likely to be noticed and most consequential when it occurs.
- Drift reviews MUST NOT be skipped because of forecast pressure. Drift that affects a renewal MUST be addressed on its merits, regardless of the commercial timing.
- Drift that affects security, privacy, or compliance commitments MUST be reported through the appropriate incident or risk process in parallel with the customer-success review.
- Drift MUST NOT be used to retroactively justify commitments that the company never made, or to remove obligations the company already accepted. Drift is a record of change, not a tool for revising history.
- Drift that materially changes the success plan's outcomes or scope MUST be confirmed in writing by the customer sponsor or their documented delegate.

## Procedure

1. The recertification cadence triggers a drift review at or before the documented date.
2. The reviewer compares the current state against the documented baseline across every dimension.
3. Drift is classified by severity and category (customer-side, company-side, both).
4. Material drift is escalated to the customer-success lead; minor drift is recorded for the next review.
5. Customer communication is sent where the drift affects a customer commitment; internal escalation is sent where the drift affects a company commitment.
6. The plan is either amended (with both versions retained), rebuilt, or retired; the action and the rationale are recorded.
7. The drift record is stored in the system of record for the audit-retention window.
