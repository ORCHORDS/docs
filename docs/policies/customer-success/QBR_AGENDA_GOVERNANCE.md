# QBR Agenda Governance

## Purpose

Establish an accountable, evidence-based approach to the agenda, evidence sources, decisions captured, and classification of Quarterly Business Reviews (QBRs). The objective is to ensure that QBRs operate as a consistent, decision-relevant forum — neither a marketing showcase nor a one-way status report — and that the conclusions are traceable, acted upon, and revisable when evidence changes.

## Scope

This policy applies to any QBR convened by or on behalf of the customer-success function, including executive QBRs with customer sponsors, internal QBRs across teams, and joint QBRs between the company and the customer. It covers the agenda, the supporting evidence pack, the decisions captured, the actions recorded, and the post-QBR follow-through. It does not apply to ad-hoc executive briefings that are not QBRs.

## Requirements

- A QBR MUST be convened at a documented cadence appropriate to the engagement. Cadences MUST be honoured unless the customer has explicitly agreed to a change; ad-hoc cancellation or postponement without documented customer agreement is prohibited.
- A QBR MUST have a written agenda distributed in advance. The agenda MUST identify the topics, the owners of each topic, the time allocated, the decision expected from each topic (information, discussion, or decision), and the supporting evidence available.
- The agenda MUST cover the documented baseline topics: outcome attainment against the success plan, value-realisation evidence, adoption and adoption barriers, support and reliability themes, open escalations, risk and renewal outlook, expansion readiness where appropriate, and forward priorities.
- A QBR MUST be supported by an evidence pack. The evidence pack MUST cite the sources for each claim, the as-of date, the freshness bound, and any caveats. Quantitative claims MUST reference the underlying calculation; qualitative claims MUST reference the customer or internal source.
- The agenda MUST include a recurring topic for outstanding actions from prior QBRs. The status of each action MUST be reviewed at every QBR until it is closed.
- A QBR MUST capture decisions in writing. Each decision MUST record the topic, the decision taken, the rationale, the dissents (if any), the owner of follow-up, and the deadline.
- QBR minutes MUST be classified for sensitivity. Material that includes confidential customer information, security posture, pricing, or competitive strategy MUST be handled with the controls that classification requires.
- QBR minutes MUST be reviewed by an internal moderator before they are circulated. The moderator MUST verify that the minutes are accurate, that the decisions are recorded, and that no claim exceeds what the evidence supports.
- QBR minutes MUST be shared with the customer within the documented time bound. The customer MUST have an opportunity to correct factual errors before the minutes are finalised.
- Decisions captured in a QBR MUST be traceable to a downstream action. Each decision MUST have an owner, a deadline, and a place in the work-management system where its completion is tracked.
- QBRs MUST be honest about regressions and open issues. The agenda MUST NOT omit material risk to preserve a positive tone; concealment of material risk for relationship management is prohibited.
- QBRs MUST distinguish fact from interpretation. The minutes MUST record claims, the source, and the inference drawn, where they are different.
- The cadence and format of the QBR MUST be reviewed at the documented cadence. Reviews MUST consider whether the QBR continues to deliver value, whether decisions are being acted upon, and whether the format suits the participants.
- QBRs MUST consider diversity of input. Where multiple stakeholders on the customer side have different perspectives, those perspectives MUST be sought before decisions are taken.
- The QBR format MUST be adaptable to customer governance. Where the customer's own governance prefers a different cadence or a different structure, the customer's preference MUST be honoured where it does not conflict with this policy.

## Procedure

1. The QBR owner drafts the agenda, supported by the evidence pack, and circulates it to the customer at the documented lead time.
2. The customer reviews the agenda and the evidence pack and confirms the participants and the decisions expected.
3. The QBR is convened, decisions are captured in real time, and minutes are drafted within the documented time bound.
4. The internal moderator reviews the minutes; the customer reviews the minutes for factual accuracy.
5. Decisions are entered into the work-management system with owners and deadlines.
6. The QBR record is stored in the system of record for the audit-retention window.
7. The cadence and format are reviewed at the documented cadence.
