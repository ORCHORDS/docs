# Customer Community of Practice

## Purpose

Establish an accountable, evidence-based approach to operating a customer community — the forum, working group, advisory board, or peer network through which customers share practice. The objective is to create durable value for participants while protecting confidentiality, ensuring fair treatment, and preventing the community from being misused as a channel for marketing pressure, undisclosed commercial influence, or unsafe information exchange.

## Scope

This policy applies to any community of practice, advisory panel, user group, peer network, or comparable forum that the customer-success function operates, sponsors, or co-sponsors, including online forums, in-person events, private working groups, and customer-to-customer introductions. It does not apply to commercial customer marketing events that are not forums for shared practice, nor to social-media accounts that the company does not control.

## Requirements

- Every community MUST have a written charter that identifies the purpose, the membership criteria, the moderation approach, the escalation path, the confidentiality and data-handling rules, the conflict-of-interest expectations, and the conditions under which the community may be wound down.
- Community governance MUST distinguish operational decisions (which may be made by the moderation team) from policy decisions (which require documented approval) and from membership decisions (which require a documented and reviewable process).
- Membership criteria MUST be transparent and consistently applied. Membership MUST NOT be used as a tool to favour or disadvantage a customer for commercial reasons. Where membership confers tangible benefits (such as early access or direct support routing), the criteria for those benefits MUST be documented.
- Moderation MUST be timely, fair, and consistent. Moderation actions MUST be logged with the reason, the moderator's identity, the date, and the appeal path. Appeals MUST be reviewed by a moderator who was not involved in the original decision.
- Confidentiality rules MUST be stated at the point of entry to the community and re-stated when a member joins a sub-group with a higher confidentiality expectation. Members MUST affirm their understanding before participating in any confidential exchange.
- Information shared by a member MUST NOT be forwarded, summarised, or attributed outside the community without the member's consent. Aggregate insights derived from community discussion MUST be anonymised and reviewed before they leave the community boundary.
- Conflicts of interest MUST be disclosed by members where they bear on a topic under discussion. Conflicts MUST also be disclosed by company participants whose commercial role could influence their contribution.
- Events and synchronous meetings MUST have an agenda, a note-taker, a confidentiality classification for the notes, and a follow-up action register. Notes MUST be reviewed by an independent moderator before they are distributed.
- The community MUST NOT be used as a substitute for support, security, privacy, or legal channels. Where a participant raises an issue that belongs in another channel, the moderator MUST redirect them and document the redirection.
- Participants MUST have a documented channel to raise safety, ethical, or compliance concerns about the community itself. Concerns MUST be reviewed by an independent reviewer who is not the community owner.
- Member data MUST be handled under the customer-success data-minimisation and tenant-isolation policies. The community MUST NOT retain personal data beyond the documented retention period, and members MUST be able to request correction or deletion of their data within the applicable rights framework.
- The community MUST be evaluated at the documented cadence against the charter's purpose. Evaluations MUST consider whether the community continues to deliver value to its members and whether the governance is operating as designed.
- Wind-down of the community MUST follow a documented plan that addresses member notice, data handling, ongoing commitments, and the closure of sub-groups. Members MUST be given a reasonable period to export their own contributions where the community platform permits.

## Controls

- A published code of conduct sets the tone for participation, identifies prohibited behaviours (harassment, undisclosed promotion, off-topic commercial pitching), and states the consequences.
- A moderation log is retained for the audit-retention window and is reviewed by an independent reviewer at each governance cycle.
- A membership register records the criteria applied to each admission or removal, the date, and the decision-maker.
- A content-classification scheme distinguishes public, member-only, and confidential material, with access controls applied accordingly.
- An escalation route is published inside the community so that members can raise a concern with someone other than the moderation team.
- An annual governance review tests the charter against actual practice and identifies drift between the two.
