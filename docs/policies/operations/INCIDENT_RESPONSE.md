# Incident Response Policy

## Purpose

Establish consistent preparation, detection, response, recovery, and learning
for security and reliability incidents.

## Model

Incident management follows the risk-management approach in NIST SP 800-61
Rev. 3 and NIST CSF 2.0. Preparation, response, and recovery are continuous
organizational activities rather than a standalone emergency phase.

## Severity

Severity is based on actual or credible impact, including:

- safety or security;
- confidentiality, integrity, or availability;
- number and type of affected users;
- regulatory or contractual exposure;
- financial impact;
- duration and recoverability;
- active exploitation;
- reputational impact.

## Roles

- **Incident Commander:** owns coordination and decisions.
- **Technical Lead:** drives diagnosis, containment, remediation, and recovery.
- **Communications Lead:** manages accurate stakeholder communication.
- **Scribe:** maintains timeline, decisions, evidence, and actions.
- **Subject-matter responders:** support investigation and restoration.

One person may fill multiple roles for smaller incidents, but command ownership
must remain explicit.

## Expectations

- Stabilize user and business impact first.
- Preserve relevant evidence.
- Record major decisions and timestamps.
- Separate facts from hypotheses.
- Use secure channels for sensitive information.
- Communicate uncertainty rather than speculate.
- End the incident only after recovery is verified and ownership of follow-up
  work is assigned.
