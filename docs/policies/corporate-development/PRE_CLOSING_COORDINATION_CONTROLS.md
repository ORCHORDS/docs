# Pre-Closing Coordination Controls

## Sources

- U.S. Federal Trade Commission — Avoiding antitrust pitfalls during pre-merger negotiations and due diligence: https://www.ftc.gov/enforcement/competition-matters/2018/03/avoiding-antitrust-pitfalls-during-pre-merger-negotiations-due-diligence
- U.S. Federal Trade Commission — Premerger Notification Program: https://www.ftc.gov/enforcement/premerger-notification-program
- U.S. Federal Trade Commission — HSR Rules: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/hsr-rules

## Purpose

Signing a transaction agreement does not automatically permit the parties to operate as one business before closing. The FTC warns that pre-merger information sharing and operational coordination can create antitrust risk and, in reportable transactions, may contribute to unlawful "gun jumping" if a buyer effectively obtains beneficial ownership or operational control before the applicable waiting period has ended.

## Independent-operation principle

Until closing, transaction parties should continue to make their own ordinary-course commercial and operational decisions except where qualified legal review confirms that a specific contractual protection or coordination mechanism is permissible.

The transaction team should not use diligence or integration planning as a reason to direct the counterparty's:

- pricing or customer terms;
- bids, sales strategy, or negotiations;
- production or service levels;
- supplier choices;
- employee decisions;
- product launches or withdrawals; or
- other competitively significant day-to-day activity.

## Information-sharing controls

Where competitively sensitive information is required for legitimate diligence or integration planning, use controls proportionate to the risk. These may include:

- limiting access to identified personnel;
- using clean-team or outside-adviser arrangements where appropriate;
- minimizing the precision, age, or granularity of competitively sensitive data;
- documenting why the information is necessary;
- prohibiting onward distribution outside the approved group; and
- obtaining legal review before expanding access or permitted uses.

## Escalation triggers

Escalate before acting when a proposed pre-close step would allow one party to approve, veto, direct, or materially influence the other's ordinary-course commercial decisions, or when diligence requests seek current competitively sensitive information beyond what is reasonably necessary.

## Evidence

The transaction record should identify the applicable information-sharing rules, approved recipients, material clean-team restrictions, and any legal advice governing unusual pre-close coordination.

## Public boundary

Do not publish clean-team membership, confidential diligence contents, customer information, competitive strategy, transaction negotiations, or legal advice.

This document describes governance only and does not assert that any ORCHORDS transaction is subject to HSR review or that a clean-team arrangement is required in every transaction.
