# Foreign Investment Screening Governance

- U.S. Department of the Treasury — CFIUS voluntary notice filing instructions, 31 C.F.R. part 800: https://home.treasury.gov/policy-issues/international/the-committee-on-foreign-investment-in-the-united-states-cfius/voluntary-notice-filing-instructions-part-800
- U.S. Department of the Treasury — CFIUS declarations FAQ: https://home.treasury.gov/faq-item/international-affairs/for-what-type-of-transactions-can-parties-submit-a-declaration
- U.S. Department of the Treasury — CFIUS FAQs: https://home.treasury.gov/policy-issues/international/the-committee-on-foreign-investment-in-the-united-states-cfius/cfius-frequently-asked-questions

## Purpose

Some transactions involving foreign persons and U.S. businesses or U.S. real estate can fall within the jurisdiction of the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States (CFIUS). Treasury explains that certain covered transactions may be submitted through a notice or short-form declaration, and that declarations are mandatory in specified circumstances, including certain transactions involving critical technologies or substantial foreign-government interests.

Foreign-investment screening rules are transaction- and fact-specific. A general corporate-development checklist is not a substitute for qualified legal analysis of the applicable regulations.

## Screening triggers

A transaction with a U.S. nexus should be screened early when it involves, among other relevant facts:

- a foreign acquirer, investor, or ownership chain;
- possible foreign control of a U.S. business;
- non-controlling rights involving a U.S. business in areas addressed by the CFIUS regulations;
- critical technologies, covered investment critical infrastructure, or sensitive personal data;
- a foreign government with a material direct or indirect ownership interest;
- U.S. real estate that may fall within the CFIUS real-estate rules; or
- transaction rights that change access, governance, board participation, or substantive decision-making authority.

## Governance expectations

Where a possible CFIUS nexus exists, the transaction file should record:

- the parties and relevant ownership chain at the level necessary for legal review;
- the nature of the U.S. business or real-estate interest;
- whether critical-technology, infrastructure, or sensitive-data considerations were assessed;
- whether a mandatory declaration analysis was performed;
- whether a voluntary declaration or notice was considered;
- required filing and review milestones, if any;
- responsible internal and external reviewers; and
- any closing condition tied to foreign-investment clearance or risk mitigation.

## Current-law requirement

Use the current Treasury regulations, filing instructions, and FAQs when evaluating a transaction. Do not assume that a prior transaction's CFIUS conclusion applies to a later transaction with different parties, ownership, technology, data, rights, or real estate.

## Public boundary

Do not publish confidential ownership details, sensitive technology classifications, transaction-party data, filing contents, agency communications, mitigation terms, or legal advice.

This document describes governance only and does not assert that any ORCHORDS transaction is a covered transaction, subject to mandatory filing, filed with CFIUS, cleared, mitigated, or exempt.
