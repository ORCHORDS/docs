# Language Interpreter Governance

## Purpose

Govern the use of third-party language interpreters and translation services in customer support so that customers who do not communicate in the company's primary working languages receive effective support, while personal data, sensitive case content, and recording rules remain protected.

## Scope

This article covers engagement of any third-party interpreter, translator, or real-time translation service for the purpose of communicating with a customer or understanding a customer's communication. It covers spoken-language interpretation, signed-language interpretation where the company commits to it, and written translation. It applies whether the service is reached by phone, video, chat, or in-person, and whether the interpreter is a live human, an automated translation system, or a hybrid. It does not govern the company's own multilingual employees acting in the ordinary course of their role, except where they are supported by a third-party tool.

## Requirements

This article sets the following obligations for the covered support activity. MUST/SHOULD/MAY statements throughout the body of this article are part of these requirements.


## When an interpreter is offered

The company SHOULD offer interpretation or translation when:

- the customer requests it;
- the customer communicates in a language the assigned agent cannot serve at the level required for the case;
- the case involves a sensitive action (identity verification, security change, account recovery, financial action, accessibility accommodation, legal demand) where misunderstanding has material consequences;
- the case is in a regulated context where effective communication is a condition of valid consent, valid notice, or valid acceptance.

The offer MUST be made without the customer having to insist, MUST be available at no cost to the customer, and MUST not be conditioned on the customer abandoning the channel they chose.

## Qualifications and suitability

Interpreters and translators MUST be qualified for the language pair, the register, and the subject matter. Interpreters MUST be screened for conflicts of interest (for example, an interpreter who is also a customer, employee of a competitor, or party to the case). Where signed-language interpretation is offered, the interpreter MUST hold a credential recognized by the relevant jurisdiction. The company SHOULD retain a roster of pre-approved interpreters or providers and SHOULD track performance.

## Data handling

Third-party interpreters MUST be bound by a written agreement that addresses:

- confidentiality of all information they encounter, with survival of obligations after the engagement ends;
- prohibition on recording, transcription, summarization, or training-data reuse beyond what the engagement explicitly allows;
- restrictions on subcontracting and on the use of personal devices;
- data-breach notification timelines consistent with the company's policy and applicable law;
- audit rights, including the right to inspect the interpreter's handling of a specific engagement;
- data-residency commitments where the customer or the case requires them;
- secure transfer of documents and the prohibition on personal email, consumer cloud storage, or unapproved channels.

Automated translation services that process customer content MUST be assessed for the same criteria, with the additional consideration that the data may be retained by the service for model improvement unless the contract forbids it. Customers SHOULD be told when an automated translation service is in use, and SHOULD be offered a human alternative on request.

## Recording restrictions

Calls or sessions involving an interpreter MUST NOT be recorded in a way that captures the interpreter's voice or personal data without a documented basis, and MUST follow any consent rules applicable in the customer's jurisdiction. Where the company has a general recording notice, that notice MUST be confirmed or repeated in the customer's language before the conversation proceeds. Recordings MUST be stored under the same classification as the underlying case content.

## Customer-facing conduct

The interpreter MUST interpret faithfully, including unflattering or emotional statements. The interpreter MUST NOT add commentary, opinion, or advice. The interpreter MUST NOT share the customer's personal data with anyone outside the engagement. The agent MUST address the customer directly rather than the interpreter. The agent MUST record the interpreter's identifier, the language pair, and the duration in the case record.

## Refusal and safety

The interpreter MAY refuse to interpret if the content endangers the interpreter, the customer, or a third party; if the interpreter is unqualified for the subject matter; or if the interpreter identifies a conflict of interest. A refusal MUST trigger a defined back-up path so that the customer is not stranded.

## Audit and review

Interpreter engagements SHOULD be sampled for fidelity, data handling, recording compliance, and customer experience. Findings SHOULD feed the vendor review, the support-quality program, and the privacy program. Material breaches MUST trigger contract action.
