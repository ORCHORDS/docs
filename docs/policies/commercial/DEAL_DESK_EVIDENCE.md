# Deal Desk Evidence

## Purpose

Ensure that every deal-desk decision — approval, exception, conditional approval, or rejection — is supported by a complete, durable, and reviewable evidence record so that commercial governance is auditable, exceptions are traceable to their rationale, and post-hoc review can reconstruct why a specific commercial outcome was authorized.

## Scope

This article applies to all deal-desk activities across the company: pricing approvals and exceptions, non-standard term approvals, partner-of-record decisions, multi-year commitments, deferred-payment terms, expansion approvals, deviation from pricebook, and any case in which a deal-desk reviewer exercises judgment outside pre-approved parameters. It applies regardless of deal size when an exception is material in nature, and applies to all material deals regardless of whether an exception is needed.

## Requirements

- Deal-desk decisions MUST be captured in a system of record with: deal identifier, requester, reviewer(s), decision, decision date, conditions attached, expiration of approval, and links to supporting evidence.
- The evidence pack SHOULD include the commercial rationale, comparable transactions or benchmarks, customer-specific context, margin and cost-to-serve impact, delivery impact, partner or channel considerations, and any third-party approvals (legal, security, privacy, finance).
- Pricing rationale SHOULD distinguish cost-to-serve, value-realisation, competitive context, and strategic value, and SHOULD be free of internal-discount-rate references that would mislead the customer.
- Where a deviation is approved, the deviation log entry SHOULD record the precedent risk (e.g., "this discount is granted because of X and is not expected to be repeatable for Y") and any conditions required to manage that risk.
- Deal-desk evidence MUST be retained for the duration of the resulting contract plus any applicable warranty or dispute window, and SHOULD be protected commensurate with its sensitivity.
- Deal-desk decisions SHOULD be reviewable by second-line functions (finance, legal, internal audit) without dependence on the original reviewer's personal files.

## Workflow

A deal-desk request is initiated by the account team with a complete evidence pack. The deal-desk reviewer validates completeness, identifies the approval tier required by the related discount approval matrix, and routes the request to the appropriate approver(s). Reviewers may request additional evidence (e.g., comparable transactions, competitive intelligence, customer-disclosed constraints). Decisions are recorded with explicit conditions, and the conditions are tracked through closure. Where a deal-desk approval is conditional (e.g., contingent on security review, contingent on customer acceptance of a fallback clause), the conditionality is documented and tracked, and the deal MUST NOT be booked as closed until all conditions are met. Approvals SHOULD carry an expiration beyond which they must be re-validated; expired approvals MUST NOT be relied upon as if still current. Material exceptions SHOULD be reviewed periodically to identify patterns that warrant policy or pricebook updates.

## Controls

- Each deal-desk approval MUST have a unique identifier that ties the decision to the deal record, the contract record, and the booking record.
- Separation SHOULD exist between request and approval for material exceptions; the same individual SHOULD NOT both request and approve the same exception.
- Deal-desk evidence SHOULD be subject to periodic internal-audit review, including sampling of approvals, expirations, and conditions tracking.
- Pattern review SHOULD test whether certain customers, regions, or product lines generate disproportionate exception rates and whether that signals pricebook misalignment or governance weakness.
- The deal-desk system SHOULD prevent reliance on approvals that have expired or that have had their conditions violated.

## Anti-bribery, sanctions, and export-control evidence

Deal-desk evidence packs SHOULD include, where applicable, sanctions-screening status of the customer and any related third parties (resellers, agents, end-users), export-control classification of the items being sold, and confirmation that any third-party agents or intermediaries are subject to anti-bribery due diligence. Where the deal involves a higher-risk geography or a higher-risk sector, the evidence pack SHOULD include the heightened-due-diligence record. Deal-desk reviewers MUST NOT approve deals that bypass these controls on the basis of commercial pressure.

## Revenue-recognition alignment

Deal-desk evidence SHOULD be compatible with revenue-recognition requirements (IFRS 15 / ASC 606): variable consideration, material rights, significant financing components, allocation of transaction price, and identification of performance obligations. Pricing structures that bundle products or services SHOULD be supported by evidence that supports the allocation. Deal-desk approvals SHOULD NOT create terms that the finance organization cannot recognize in accordance with applicable accounting standards.
