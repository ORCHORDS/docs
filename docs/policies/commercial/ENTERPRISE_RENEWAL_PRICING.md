# Enterprise Renewal Pricing

## Purpose

Establish disciplined, evidence-based pricing for enterprise renewals so that the company captures fair value for continued delivery, recognizes the cost and risk of ongoing service, and avoids both opportunistic repricing and uncritical discounting of multi-year incumbency.

## Scope

This article applies to all renewals of material enterprise agreements: subscriptions, term licenses, multi-year service agreements, support and maintenance contracts, and any contract whose renewal is non-trivial in commercial terms. It applies whether the renewal is a flat continuation, an expansion, a contraction, a scope change, or a re-bid in the presence of competitive alternatives. It also applies to evergreen contracts at the points at which pricing or scope is revisited.

## Requirements

- Renewal pricing SHOULD be grounded in evidence: cost-to-serve, value-realization, market comparables where defensible, competitive context, and the customer's own usage and adoption data.
- Renewal pricing MUST NOT rely solely on the customer's perceived switching cost or inertia; the price SHOULD reflect the value of continued delivery and the cost of providing it.
- Renewal pricing SHOULD be proposed inside the customer's renewal window with adequate time for stakeholder review and procurement re-engagement.
- Material price increases SHOULD be supported by documented cost-to-serve changes (e.g., underlying infrastructure cost, security and compliance investments, support model changes) and SHOULD be communicated transparently.
- Multi-year renewals SHOULD be evaluated for present-value impact, commitment value, retention benefit, and any concessions or commitments exchanged for the longer term.
- Renewal concessions SHOULD follow the discount approval matrix and SHOULD NOT bypass deal-desk or finance review.
- Renewal pricing SHOULD be reviewed for revenue-recognition implications (variable consideration, material rights, significant financing components) and SHOULD be consistent with applicable accounting standards.

## Workflow

For each material upcoming renewal, the account team SHOULD assemble a renewal brief that includes: (a) the contract under renewal, (b) the customer's usage, adoption, and value-realization evidence, (c) the customer's recent commercial interactions and stakeholder sentiment, (d) competitive alternatives the customer has signaled or that are credible in the market, (e) the company's cost-to-serve for the next term, (f) proposed pricing, (g) any concessions or non-price terms, (h) approval-tier identification per the discount approval matrix, and (i) revenue-recognition notes. The brief is reviewed by deal-desk, finance, and legal as appropriate. The pricing proposal is communicated to the customer inside the renewal window. Where the customer pushes back on price, the negotiation SHOULD be supported by the value-realization evidence and SHOULD be escalated through the matrix where concessions are material. Renewal pricing decisions SHOULD be retained as part of the contract record.

## Controls

- Renewal pricing SHOULD be reviewed for consistency across customers with similar risk and value profiles, and SHOULD NOT be used as a vehicle for prohibited discrimination or coordinated competitor behavior.
- Material price increases SHOULD be benchmarked against cost-to-serve changes and SHOULD NOT be applied in a manner that creates surprise or that undermines the customer's ability to plan.
- Renewal concessions SHOULD follow the discount approval matrix and SHOULD be reviewed for precedent risk.
- Multi-year terms SHOULD be evaluated for early-termination protections, mid-term true-ups, and exit obligations.
- Renewal pricing SHOULD be cross-checked against the customer's overall commercial posture (other contracts, outstanding exceptions, payment history) before finalization.

## Multi-year commitment considerations

Multi-year renewals SHOULD be evaluated with explicit attention to: (a) the present-value impact of any upfront concession versus the retention benefit, (b) the cost-to-serve trajectory across the term, (c) the customer's own planning horizon and budget certainty, (d) any mid-term true-up or expansion provisions, (e) exit obligations and transition assistance, (f) any price escalator (fixed percentage, indexed, or capped) and its defensibility, and (g) revenue-recognition treatment of any discounts or concessions across the term. Multi-year commitments SHOULD NOT be used to lock in pricing that materially understates the value being delivered in later years.

## Antitrust, anti-bribery, and sanctions

Renewal pricing MUST NOT be coordinated with competitors. Communication of pricing strategy, customer-specific terms, or competitive intelligence to or from competitors is prohibited. Renewal interactions with public-sector customers MUST comply with applicable anti-bribery laws; any third-party intermediaries involved in renewal negotiations MUST be subject to anti-bribery and sanctions-screening due diligence. Renewal pricing with customers subject to sanctions or export-control restrictions MUST be screened before commitment.
