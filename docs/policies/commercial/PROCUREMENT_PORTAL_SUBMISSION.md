# Procurement Portal Submission

## Purpose

Govern the company's submissions to customer-side vendor portals (procurement platforms, vendor onboarding systems, security questionnaires, supplier diversity portals, and similar customer-managed intake systems) so that submitted information is accurate, authorized, evidence-based, and consistent with the executed or proposed commercial instruments.

## Scope

This article applies to all submissions the company makes to customer-side procurement portals and intake systems: vendor registration, security and privacy questionnaires, financial and insurance attestations, supplier diversity and ESG disclosures, product certifications, pricing submissions, contracting portals, and any other vendor-intake mechanism through which the company provides information to a prospective or current customer. It applies whether the submission is in response to a procurement event, a security review, a regulatory inquiry, or a routine onboarding requirement.

## Requirements

- Procurement-portal submissions SHOULD be reviewed for accuracy against the underlying source documents (executed agreements, audited financial statements, security certifications, insurance policies, regulatory filings).
- Submissions SHOULD be approved by the appropriate function (legal, security, privacy, finance, HR, sustainability) before they are transmitted, with the approving function recorded against the submission.
- Submissions SHOULD identify the version of the underlying source documents and SHOULD NOT include outdated or superseded information.
- Submissions SHOULD NOT include unsupported commitments, opinions framed as facts, or characterizations that would mislead the customer.
- Submissions SHOULD be retained as part of the deal record and SHOULD be referenceable for the duration of the customer relationship plus any applicable warranty or dispute window.
- Submissions SHOULD be tracked in a register so that the company can respond to follow-up requests coherently and so that outdated submissions can be retracted or updated.
- Submissions SHOULD NOT include information that would violate competition law (e.g., competitor-specific pricing, market-allocation data) or that would disclose sensitive personal data of customers or employees.

## Workflow

A portal submission is initiated by the account team with a complete draft. The draft is routed to the relevant subject-matter experts for validation against source documents. Each subject-matter expert reviews the relevant section, identifies any inaccuracies or unsupported claims, and either approves, requests revision, or rejects. The fully approved submission is transmitted through an authorized channel, and the transmission is recorded. The submission SHOULD be tracked with version, transmission date, approvers, and any follow-up cadence (e.g., annual refresh). When the underlying source document changes (e.g., a contract amendment, a security-certification renewal, an insurance-policy renewal), the next portal submission SHOULD reflect the updated information. Outdated or inaccurate submissions SHOULD be retracted or corrected proactively.

## Controls

- Portal submissions SHOULD be subject to segregation of duties: the requester SHOULD NOT be the sole approver of submissions that include material commitments.
- The submission register SHOULD be reviewed periodically for stale or inconsistent data.
- Subject-matter experts SHOULD be designated for each domain (security, privacy, finance, ESG, legal, HR) and SHOULD be accountable for the accuracy of submissions in their domain.
- The company SHOULD NOT submit information that creates an obligation inconsistent with the executed or proposed commercial instruments.
- The company SHOULD NOT use portal submissions to make commitments that would otherwise require deal-desk or contract review.
- The company SHOULD retain submission records for the duration of the customer relationship and any applicable warranty or dispute window.

## Anti-bribery, sanctions, and ESG-attestation integrity

Portal submissions often include anti-bribery, sanctions-compliance, and ESG representations. These representations MUST be true, accurate, and based on actual practice; they MUST NOT be boilerplate claims that misrepresent the company's program. Where a customer requests information about beneficial ownership, the company SHOULD provide accurate information and SHOULD NOT obscure ownership structures that would be material to the customer's counterparty-risk assessment. Where a portal submission includes a certification by an authorized signatory, the signatory SHOULD have actual knowledge of the underlying facts or SHOULD rely on a documented internal attestation.

## Cross-border and regulatory considerations

Portal submissions may include cross-border transfer mechanisms, data localization statements, and representations about the company's compliance with sector-specific regulations (e.g., HIPAA, GDPR, UK DPA, GLBA, FERPA, PCI-DSS where applicable). Each such representation SHOULD be reviewed by the appropriate subject-matter expert and SHOULD be consistent with the company's actual practices. Misrepresentations in these domains can create legal exposure for the company and should be treated as material commitments.
