# Pricelist Escalation

## Purpose

Define the conditions under which a non-standard pricing escalation — a price above or below the published list, a non-standard escalation mechanism, or a deviation from the standard pricing structure — may be proposed and approved, so that such escalations are intentional, evidence-based, and subject to appropriate governance.

## Scope

This article applies to all non-standard pricing escalations: above-list premiums, below-list reductions, multi-year escalation mechanisms that depart from standard indexation, ramped pricing that departs from the published schedule, regional price deviations, volume-pricing thresholds that depart from the published schedule, and any pricing structure that requires explicit justification outside the standard pricebook.

## Requirements

- Non-standard pricing escalations SHOULD be supported by documented justification: cost-to-serve, value-realization, market evidence, customer-specific commitment, or strategic value.
- Escalations SHOULD be reviewed by deal-desk per the discount approval matrix and SHOULD be subject to finance, legal, and product review as appropriate.
- Escalations SHOULD be evaluated for revenue-recognition implications (variable consideration, material rights, significant financing components).
- Escalations SHOULD be time-limited, with a defined expiration beyond which they must be re-validated.
- Escalations SHOULD NOT be used to evade deal-desk review, to create undisclosed preferential treatment, or to coordinate with competitors.
- Escalations SHOULD be recorded with their evidence pack and SHOULD be visible to leadership for pattern review.
- Escalations SHOULD be reviewed periodically for precedent risk and for opportunities to incorporate recurring escalations into the pricebook.

## Workflow

A pricing escalation is proposed by the account team with a complete justification pack: the proposed pricing structure, the rationale (cost, value, market, strategic), comparable transactions or benchmarks, customer-specific evidence (commitment size, term, exclusivity, case-study value), the cost-to-serve impact, the margin impact, the revenue-recognition implications, the precedent risk, and the proposed conditions and expiration. The request is routed to the appropriate approval tier and is reviewed by deal-desk, finance, legal, and product. Approval is recorded with conditions, and conditions are tracked through closure. Approved escalations SHOULD be re-validated at expiration or at any material change in the underlying assumptions; reliance on an expired escalation is itself a governance failure.

## Controls

- Escalations SHOULD be approved by roles with the appropriate delegated authority per the discount approval matrix.
- Escalations SHOULD be subject to segregation of duties; the requester SHOULD NOT be the sole approver.
- Escalations SHOULD be reviewed for compliance with applicable competition law (e.g., U.S. Robinson-Patman Act where it may apply, EU competition law on discrimination, UK Competition Act).
- Escalations SHOULD be reviewed for revenue-recognition compliance with applicable accounting standards (IFRS 15 / ASC 606).
- Escalations SHOULD be tracked in a register with effective period, evidence, approval identifier, and conditions.
- Escalations SHOULD NOT be used as a vehicle for bribery, sanctions evasion, or other prohibited conduct.

## Recertification and expiration

Escalations SHOULD carry an expiration; the expiration SHOULD be appropriate to the underlying commitment (e.g., end of the contract term, end of a multi-year period, end of a market-window). Recertification SHOULD require fresh evidence; assumptions and market conditions may have changed. Recertification SHOULD be reviewed by the same functions as the original escalation, with explicit attention to whether the rationale still holds and whether the conditions remain appropriate.

## Customer impact and communication

Escalations can affect customers in materially different ways. Above-list premiums SHOULD be communicated transparently and SHOULD be tied to a clear value or commitment exchange. Below-list reductions SHOULD be tied to specific conditions (e.g., volume commitment, multi-year term, case-study participation) so that the customer understands what is being exchanged. The escalation SHOULD be documented in the executed agreement or in an addendum; informal escalations that are not reflected in the executed instruments SHOULD be avoided.

## Antitrust and prohibited discrimination

Escalations MUST NOT be used to discriminate among similarly situated customers on a prohibited basis (e.g., protected classes under applicable equality law). Differences in pricing SHOULD be defensible based on legitimate commercial factors and SHOULD NOT be coordinated with competitors. Escalations involving public-sector customers or customers in higher-risk jurisdictions SHOULD be subject to heightened due diligence and SHOULD comply with applicable anti-bribery and sanctions regimes.
