# Renewal Termination Trigger

## Purpose

Define the conditions under which the company will not renew, will terminate, or will decline to extend a customer agreement, and the governance that must accompany such a decision, so that customer impact, transition obligations, and reputational considerations are managed thoughtfully and the decision is fully auditable.

## Scope

This article applies to all customer relationships in which non-renewal, termination for cause, termination for convenience, or non-extension is a contemplated action. It covers material commercial agreements across customer segments (enterprise, mid-market, public sector, education, healthcare, financial services) and across contract types (subscription, term license, services, support, partner-distributed). It applies to voluntary company-initiated termination and to non-renewal decisions, including non-renewal driven by commercial posture, risk, regulatory exposure, or strategic change.

## Requirements

- Non-renewal or termination SHOULD be supported by a documented trigger (e.g., persistent non-payment, material breach, sanctions exposure, regulatory directive, customer-side change of control to a sanctioned or higher-risk entity, change in strategic fit, end-of-life of a product line) and SHOULD be reviewed by the appropriate functions.
- Termination for cause SHOULD follow the notice and cure provisions of the underlying contract and SHOULD NOT bypass contractual obligations (e.g., data return, transition assistance, audit cooperation).
- Termination for convenience, where the contract permits it, SHOULD be reviewed for customer impact and SHOULD be communicated with adequate notice.
- Non-renewal SHOULD be communicated to the customer inside the contractual notice window and SHOULD NOT be communicated in a manner that misrepresents the reason.
- Termination and non-renewal decisions SHOULD be subject to segregation of duties; the requester SHOULD NOT be the sole approver.
- Termination and non-renewal SHOULD be evaluated for transition obligations (data return, migration assistance, support continuity, professional-services wind-down, partner obligations) and SHOULD include a transition plan.
- Termination and non-renewal SHOULD NOT be used as a vehicle for retaliation against a customer that has raised a complaint, made a regulatory disclosure, or exercised a contractual right (e.g., audit, indemnification claim).

## Workflow

A non-renewal or termination decision is initiated by the account team or the relevant commercial owner with a justification pack: the contract under termination/non-renewal, the trigger, the customer impact assessment, the transition plan, the regulatory or compliance considerations, the partner implications, the financial impact, and the proposed communication. The request is reviewed by deal-desk, legal, finance, security/privacy (where data is involved), customer success (where transition is material), and partner operations (where a partner is involved). Approval is recorded with conditions and is communicated to the customer in writing. The transition plan is executed against milestones, and the termination or non-renewal is recorded in the contract lifecycle system.

## Controls

- Termination or non-renewal SHOULD NOT bypass legal review for contracts that contain material obligations.
- Termination or non-renewal SHOULD be evaluated for sanctions and export-control implications, particularly where the customer's beneficial ownership or jurisdiction has changed.
- Termination or non-renewal SHOULD be evaluated for retaliation indicators (e.g., recent customer complaint, regulatory inquiry, audit exercise) and SHOULD be specifically reviewed where such indicators are present.
- Termination or non-renewal SHOULD be tracked in a register with effective date, trigger, evidence, approval identifier, transition milestones, and closure status.
- Termination or non-renewal decisions SHOULD be reviewed for compliance with applicable competition law; the company MUST NOT terminate or refuse to renew a customer in a manner that would constitute unlawful refusal to deal, exclusive dealing, or coordinated competitor behavior.

## Customer impact and transition obligations

Customer impact is a material consideration in any termination or non-renewal decision. Where the customer has stored data with the company, the transition plan SHOULD include data return in a usable format, deletion confirmation, and adequate time for the customer to migrate. Where the customer relies on the company for support or professional services, the transition plan SHOULD include service-continuity arrangements, knowledge transfer, and orderly handover to a successor provider (or to the customer's own operations). Transition obligations SHOULD be documented and SHOULD be honored even where the underlying commercial relationship is ending on difficult terms.

## Communication and tone

Termination and non-renewal communications SHOULD be professional, accurate, and respectful. The customer SHOULD be told the operative reason (e.g., "we are exiting this product line", "we are unable to continue under the current terms", "we are sunsetting this offering") without misrepresenting the facts or shifting blame. Communications SHOULD NOT disparage the customer or imply wrongdoing where none is alleged. Communications SHOULD be coordinated across the account team, customer success, partner operations (where applicable), and senior leadership to ensure consistency.

## Anti-bribery, sanctions, and ethics-screen considerations

Termination or non-renewal decisions SHOULD be screened for ethics signals: an attempt by a customer-side actor to extract a bribe in connection with the renewal; a sanctions-related change in beneficial ownership; an attempt to coerce improper behavior. Where such signals appear, the decision SHOULD be escalated to the appropriate ethics or compliance function, and the decision SHOULD NOT be used to retaliate against a customer that refused to make an improper payment. The company SHOULD document the basis for its decision in a manner that would withstand scrutiny by an internal auditor or regulator.
