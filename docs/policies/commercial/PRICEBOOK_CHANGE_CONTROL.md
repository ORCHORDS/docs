# Pricebook Change Control

## Purpose

Establish a controlled lifecycle for the published pricebook — the canonical commercial artifact that records list prices, tiers, and standard commercial terms — so that changes are intentional, authorized, and transparent to all stakeholders, and so that the company retains the ability to manage grandfathering, regional variations, and the integrity of contracts entered under prior revisions.

## Scope

This article applies to all changes to the published pricebook: new products or services, price changes, tier changes, packaging changes, regional variations, currency handling, terms changes (e.g., standard payment terms, support entitlements), sunset and end-of-life pricing, and promotional pricing. It applies to all channels through which the company publishes or represents pricing to customers, partners, or internal teams, including published lists, partner pricebooks, and self-service pricing.

## Requirements

- Pricebook changes SHOULD be approved through a defined change-control gate that includes product, finance, commercial, and legal as appropriate.
- Each pricebook revision SHOULD have an effective date, a supersession date (when applicable), a change summary, and a designated owner.
- Material pricebook changes SHOULD be communicated to customer-facing teams, partners, and (where applicable) customers in advance of the effective date.
- Grandfathering policy SHOULD be explicit: which existing contracts retain prior pricing, which move to new pricing, and how renewal pricing interacts with the change.
- Regional variations SHOULD be documented and SHOULD be defensible based on legitimate cost, market, or regulatory factors.
- Pricebook changes MUST NOT be applied retroactively to executed contracts unless the underlying contract permits repricing.
- Pricebook changes MUST NOT be coordinated with competitors.

## Workflow

A pricebook change is initiated with a change request that describes the trigger (cost change, market change, regulatory change, product change, post-incident learning), the affected products or services, the proposed new pricing, the rationale, the customer impact assessment (including renewal impact), the regional implications, the partner implications, and the proposed communication plan. The change is reviewed by product, finance, commercial, and legal; approval is recorded against the change identifier. Upon approval, the new revision is published with an effective date and a supersession path; the predecessor is retained for evidentiary use. Customer-facing teams and partners are notified in advance of the effective date, with sufficient time for in-flight deals to be evaluated against the new pricing. Changes to in-flight deals SHOULD be governed by the deal-desk evidence policy and SHOULD NOT be applied in a manner that distorts active negotiations.

## Controls

- The pricebook repository MUST be access-controlled, with write access limited to authorized owners.
- Each pricebook revision SHOULD have at least one designated successor in case the named owner is unavailable.
- Periodic review SHOULD occur at least annually, with priority given to product lines exposed to high-volume or high-risk customers.
- The system SHOULD prevent reliance on superseded revisions for new commitments.
- Internal-audit review SHOULD sample pricebook changes for evidence quality, communication adequacy, and absence of prohibited discrimination or coordination.

## Grandfathering and renewal interaction

Grandfathering policy SHOULD be explicit and SHOULD consider: (a) the customer's existing contract terms, (b) any contractual price-protection clauses, (c) the customer's renewal window and whether the change takes effect inside that window, (d) the impact on the customer's budget cycle and forecasting, and (e) any partner-attributed revenue impact. Renewal pricing SHOULD be evaluated against the current pricebook and against the prior revision where the customer is grandfathered; the customer SHOULD be informed of the applicable pricing for the renewal in advance of decision-making.

## Regional and currency considerations

Regional pricebook variations SHOULD reflect legitimate cost, market, regulatory, and currency factors. Where variations exist, the canonical parent SHOULD be the reference of authority for interpretation. Currency handling SHOULD be documented (e.g., pricing currency, billing currency, FX-sensitivity), and changes in FX SHOULD trigger pricebook review where material. Regional variations SHOULD NOT be used to evade competition-law limits, to discriminate on prohibited grounds, or to facilitate coordinated competitor behavior.

## Anti-bribery and sanctions screening

The pricebook SHOULD NOT contain terms that could be used to facilitate improper payments to officials or to evade sanctions (e.g., opaque rebating structures, undocumented discounts for higher-risk geographies). Customer and partner screening SHOULD be performed independently of the pricebook; the pricebook SHOULD NOT be a vehicle for sanctions evasion.
