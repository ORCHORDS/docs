# MSA vs Order Form Hierarchy

## Purpose

Define how the company documents the order of precedence between master service agreements (MSAs), license agreements, statements of work (SOWs), order forms, and ancillary instruments so that contractual obligations are unambiguous, customer-facing teams understand which document controls in case of conflict, and the company avoids the silent accumulation of inconsistent obligations across a customer relationship.

## Scope

This article applies to all commercial contract stacks involving a master agreement together with one or more ordering instruments: subscription order forms, SOWs, change orders, renewal riders, data processing addenda, security schedules, exhibits, and any other ancillary instrument that materially modifies the parties' obligations. It applies whether the master is company-paper or customer-paper, and whether the master is signed first or after an initial order.

## Requirements

- The order of precedence SHOULD be documented explicitly in the master agreement and SHOULD be consistent across the contract stack.
- An order form or SOW MUST NOT silently override material terms of the master (e.g., liability, indemnification, data protection, audit rights, governing law) without explicit identification and approval.
- The drafting standard SHOULD specify that conflicting terms in the ordering instrument yield to the master except where the ordering instrument expressly identifies the deviation and the parties have authorized it.
- Customer-facing teams SHOULD be able to identify, for any given relationship, the controlling master, the active ordering instruments, and any deviations in force.
- Each ordering instrument SHOULD reference the master by identifier and SHOULD state whether it modifies, supplements, or is silent on the master's terms.
- Deviation language SHOULD be drafted in a manner that is conspicuous (e.g., bolded, separately initialled) and SHOULD NOT be buried in standard-form boilerplate.
- The hierarchy SHOULD be reviewed by legal before any deviation is accepted and SHOULD be reflected in the deal-desk evidence pack.

## Workflow

When an order form or SOW is being drafted, the account team SHOULD identify the controlling master by reference and SHOULD draft the ordering instrument with explicit attention to whether the master is being modified, supplemented, or left silent. The deal-desk reviewer SHOULD verify that any deviation from the master is properly identified, justified, and approved. The executed ordering instrument SHOULD be filed alongside the master with explicit cross-references. Where a customer proposes a deviation embedded in standard-form boilerplate (e.g., a procurement portal's "click-through" terms), the deviation SHOULD be flagged for review and SHOULD NOT be accepted by silence. Material deviations from the master SHOULD be tracked in a deviation log so that the cumulative deviation posture for the customer is visible to leadership.

## Controls

- The company SHOULD maintain a register of executed masters and the ordering instruments against them, with the order of precedence visible.
- Deviations from the master SHOULD follow the nonstandard-terms escalation policy and the discount approval matrix where applicable.
- The drafting standard SHOULD be reviewed periodically for fitness and SHOULD be aligned with the contract-template version-control policy.
- Material deviations SHOULD be subject to legal review regardless of deal size.
- Deviations SHOULD be reviewed for compliance with applicable competition law and MUST NOT be used to coordinate with competitors or to allocate customers.

## Customer clarity

The order of precedence is a customer-protection mechanism as much as an internal-control mechanism. Customers SHOULD be able to read the contract stack and understand which document controls in case of conflict. Where the company prepares the stack, the company SHOULD err on the side of clarity: a clear hierarchy, conspicuous deviation language, and explicit cross-references between master and ordering instruments. Where the customer prepares the stack, the company SHOULD review the hierarchy as part of contract review and SHOULD propose corrections where the hierarchy is ambiguous or one-sided.

## Antitrust and prohibited terms

The order of precedence SHOULD NOT be used to evade legal limits (e.g., to bury anticompetitive exclusivity in an ordering instrument that escapes master-level review). Each ordering instrument SHOULD be reviewed in its own right for prohibited terms, including but not limited to: unlawful exclusivity, market allocation, price-fixing, refusal-to-deal clauses, IP-overreach, indemnification that exceeds lawful limits, audit rights that violate privacy law, and clauses that would interfere with the customer's or its end-users' legal rights. Deviations from the master SHOULD be evaluated against competition law regardless of their commercial attractiveness.
