# Loss-Reason Capture

## Purpose

Ensure that lost-deal reasons are captured with sufficient specificity, sensitivity, and governance to support honest retrospection, product and pricing feedback, and forecasting integrity — without exposing individual deal participants to retaliation and without using loss-reason data in a manner that would violate applicable competition law.

## Scope

This article applies to all material lost commercial opportunities across the company: net-new sales, expansions, renewals lost to the company or to a competitor, pilots not converted, partner-sourced deals that did not close, and any other category of material opportunity that ends without commitment. It applies regardless of the stage at which the loss occurred and regardless of whether the loss is to a competitor, to a do-nothing outcome, to a budget reallocation, or to a deferral.

## Requirements

- Loss reasons SHOULD be captured for every material lost opportunity and SHOULD be selected from a documented taxonomy that distinguishes customer-driven, competitor-driven, internal, and environmental reasons.
- Loss-reason capture SHOULD be timely (shortly after the loss is known) and SHOULD be updated when new information becomes available.
- Loss-reason capture SHOULD distinguish primary reason from contributing factors; multi-reason captures SHOULD be supported.
- Loss-reason data SHOULD be aggregated for trend analysis, with sensitivity to small samples (where a small number of deals could identify a specific customer or individual).
- Loss-reason data SHOULD NOT be used to coordinate with competitors on pricing, market allocation, or customer treatment.
- Loss-reason data SHOULD NOT be used as the sole basis for individual performance evaluation; it is one signal among several.
- Loss-reason data SHOULD be reviewed for product, pricing, and process feedback, and SHOULD feed into the relevant cross-functional retrospectives.

## Workflow

When a deal is closed as lost, the account team records the loss in the system of record with one or more loss reasons selected from the documented taxonomy, a brief free-text description (treated as sensitive but reviewable by authorized roles), and any supporting evidence (e.g., customer email, procurement notice, competitor reference). Where the loss is sensitive (e.g., involves a named competitor, a regulatory action, or an internal failure with reputational implications), the entry SHOULD be marked for restricted visibility. Loss-reason data is aggregated at the product, segment, region, and channel levels on a regular cadence; trend reports SHOULD be reviewed by commercial leadership and SHOULD be shared with product, pricing, and customer success for cross-functional feedback. Aggregations SHOULD follow statistical-disclosure controls so that small samples are not exposed in a way that would identify individuals or specific customers.

## Controls

- The loss-reason taxonomy SHOULD be version-controlled, with changes reviewed for consistency with prior periods to support trend analysis.
- Loss-reason data SHOULD be access-controlled; aggregated views SHOULD be appropriate to the audience (e.g., leadership, product, pricing).
- Loss-reason capture SHOULD NOT be used as a mechanism to assign blame for outcomes outside an individual's control.
- Loss-reason data SHOULD be retained for a defined period consistent with other commercial records and SHOULD be protected commensurate with its sensitivity.
- Reviews of loss-reason data SHOULD NOT be used to coordinate competitor-specific commercial responses in a manner that would violate competition law.

## Sensitivity and statistical-disclosure controls

When aggregating loss-reason data, the company SHOULD apply statistical-disclosure controls (e.g., minimum cell size, suppression of cells that would identify a specific deal or individual, aggregation across sufficiently broad dimensions). Where a small number of deals in a category could identify a specific customer or individual, the data SHOULD be aggregated only at higher levels or SHOULD be flagged for restricted access. Loss-reason reports SHOULD NOT name individual customers or individuals in a way that would expose them to retaliation or embarrassment, and SHOULD NOT be used outside the company in a manner that could embarrass the customer or competitor.

## Anti-bribery and ethics-screen considerations

Loss-reason capture SHOULD be used as a venue to identify losses that may be associated with attempted bribery, sanctions exposure, or other ethics concerns (e.g., a loss attributed to a customer's procurement official requesting an improper payment, a loss attributed to a refusal to pay a bribe). Where such signals appear, the entry SHOULD be escalated to the relevant ethics or compliance function. Loss-reason data SHOULD NOT be used to retaliate against individuals who refused to make improper payments.
