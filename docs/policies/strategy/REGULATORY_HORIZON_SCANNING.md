# Regulatory Horizon Scanning

## Purpose

Establish a recurring process for scanning the regulatory horizon so that emerging regulation is identified early enough to inform strategy, allocation, and advocacy decisions. Late awareness of regulatory change is a recurring source of avoidable strategic cost; this policy reduces that cost by making horizon scanning a routine discipline rather than a reaction to surprise.

## Scope

This policy applies to scanning for regulatory developments that could affect the organisation's strategy, capital allocation, operating model, or product design within a planning horizon. It covers source selection, prioritisation, response options, and decision routing. It does not replace continuous regulatory monitoring for compliance purposes, which is handled under compliance policy.

## Requirements

- The horizon-scanning function MUST maintain a curated set of primary sources (regulators, official journals, legislative trackers), reputable secondary sources (specialist law firms, industry associations, academic policy centres), and, where appropriate, tertiary sources (analyst summaries).
- Every scanned item MUST be tagged with jurisdiction, regulator, subject area, expected effective date (where known), potential impact, confidence, and source citation.
- Items MUST be triaged into categories: ignore, monitor, investigate, prepare response, or escalate to strategy.
- The triage threshold MUST be set in advance and SHOULD be reviewed annually; items crossing the threshold MUST be escalated within an agreed window.
- For each item escalated to strategy, the function MUST prepare a brief that includes the regulatory text or summary, the proposed response options, the cost of inaction, and the cost of each response.
- Response options SHOULD be developed in consultation with subject-matter experts (legal, compliance, finance, operations) before being presented to the strategy committee.
- The strategy committee decides which responses are funded, which are deferred, and which require further investigation; the decision is recorded with rationale.

## Workflow

1. The horizon-scanning function curates sources and runs a recurring scan; new items are logged in the standing register.
2. The function triages each item against the pre-set threshold and routes accordingly.
3. Items crossing the threshold are investigated; a brief is prepared with the regulatory text, response options, and cost analysis.
4. The strategy committee reviews the brief and decides on response; decisions are recorded with rationale and ownership.
5. Approved responses are tracked; progress is reported at the cadence agreed at decision.
6. Items that change materially (new effective date, scope change, withdrawal) are re-triaged and re-presented as needed.

## Controls

- Source integrity: the function SHOULD NOT rely on a single secondary source for consequential items; primary corroboration is required before escalation.
- Threshold discipline: the triage threshold MUST be applied consistently; ad hoc escalation by individual contributors is prohibited.
- Documentation: the register and briefs are retained for at least five years; retention supports both audit and learning.

## Linkages and roles

The horizon-scanning function interfaces with three adjacent capabilities: compliance, government relations, and strategy. Compliance owns the continuous monitoring of already-effective regulation and is the primary consumer of the scanning output for control design. Government relations owns relationships with regulators and industry associations and uses the scanning output to inform advocacy priorities. Strategy owns the interpretation of regulatory direction in the context of the organisation's posture. Where the three functions disagree on the implication of a development, the disagreement is recorded and routed to the strategy committee for resolution; the resolution and the reasoning behind it are retained so that subsequent interpretations can be tested against prior judgement.

The function SHOULD also maintain a record of false alarms, items that initially appeared material but on investigation proved unimportant, and SHOULD review that record annually to refine the triage threshold. A high false-alarm rate is itself a signal that the threshold or the source curation needs adjustment.

## Jurisdictional scope and translation

Regulatory developments arrive in the language and convention of their jurisdiction, and a scanning function that reports only in the original framing produces analysis that is harder to compare across jurisdictions. The function SHOULD therefore adopt a common analytical frame for every scanned item: the regulatory instrument, the authority issuing it, the population it applies to, the trigger that brings it into effect, the obligations it creates, and the remedies it provides. This frame allows the strategy committee to compare items across jurisdictions on a consistent basis and to identify patterns that no single jurisdiction's framing would reveal.

Where a development originates in a language other than the organisation's working language, the function SHOULD ensure the analysis is reviewed by a qualified translator before circulation; machine translation alone is not adequate for consequential items. The translator's name SHOULD be recorded on the brief so that the analysis is traceable to a human reviewer; this is especially important for items that may be cited as evidence in a later strategy decision.
