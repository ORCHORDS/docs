# Strategic Narrative Dissent

## Purpose

Provide a process for capturing, recording, and resolving dissent on strategic narratives so that disagreement is surfaced rather than suppressed and so that the organisation benefits from the dissent even when the narrative moves forward. Strategic narratives that have not been pressure-tested by structured dissent tend to ossify and to discount contradictory evidence; this policy counteracts that drift.

## Scope

This policy applies to dissent raised in the course of preparing, approving, or distributing a strategic narrative covered by [Executive Narrative Cadence](EXEC_NARRATIVE_CADENCE.md). It covers confidentiality, evidence standards, decision authority, and follow-up. It does not direct operational disagreement, which is handled under management processes.

## Requirements

- Dissent MUST be recorded whenever a named contributor or reviewer disagrees with a material element of a strategic narrative.
- Each dissent entry MUST record the dissenter, the date, the disputed element, the alternative view, the supporting evidence, and the dissenter's proposed test or experiment to resolve the disagreement.
- Dissent entries MUST be retained with the narrative and MUST be available to the strategy committee; they MUST NOT be silently edited or removed.
- Confidentiality MUST be maintained; the dissent record is shared with the named recipients of the narrative and SHOULD NOT be distributed further without the dissenter's consent.
- The decision authority for resolving dissent is the strategy committee; the committee may accept, partially accept, or override the dissent, but MUST record its rationale.
- The committee SHOULD define a follow-up action when dissent is overridden; the action SHOULD include the evidence that, if observed, would cause the committee to revisit the decision.
- The dissenter MUST be notified of the resolution and the rationale; the dissenter MAY request a review at the next recertification if new evidence emerges.

## Workflow

1. The narrative lead invites dissent at each review point; dissent is recorded in a structured template.
2. The lead compiles the dissent entries and submits them with the narrative to the strategy committee.
3. The committee deliberates the dissent alongside the narrative; each dissent entry receives a recorded disposition.
4. The committee's decision is communicated to the dissenter; the dissent entry, disposition, and follow-up action are filed.
5. At the next recertification, the strategy lead surfaces any follow-up evidence related to overridden dissent; the committee decides whether the underlying position should be revisited.
6. If new evidence materially changes the position, the narrative is revised and the dissent record is updated accordingly.

## Controls

- Record integrity: dissent entries MUST NOT be deleted; corrections are made by adding a new entry rather than altering history.
- Dissenter protection: dissenter identity is treated as confidential within the named distribution; breaches are escalated to the strategy committee chair.
- Follow-through: follow-up actions assigned at override MUST be tracked; failure to track follow-up is an escalation event.

## Quality of dissent

The value of recorded dissent depends on its quality. Useful dissent names a specific disputed element, offers a contrary view that the committee can engage with, and proposes a test that, if conducted, would resolve the disagreement; dissent that consists of generalised scepticism without an alternative view or a resolvable test SHOULD be returned for strengthening before it is recorded. The committee SHOULD encourage contributors to surface dissent early rather than late, because late dissent typically appears in the form of a launch objection rather than a substantive alternative that can be evaluated on its merits.

The dissent record SHOULD also include, where relevant, the dissenter's standing in the subject area; a dissent from a contributor with deep domain knowledge and a clear alternative view is treated differently from a dissent from a contributor without that grounding, not as a matter of voice weight but as a matter of how much further investigation is required before the committee can resolve the disagreement.

## Dissent over time

Dissent that is overridden at one recertification may become the prevailing view at the next, particularly when the evidence that would have resolved it becomes available or when the strategic context shifts. The policy SHOULD therefore ensure that overridden dissent is not just filed but revisited: at each subsequent recertification, the lead SHOULD surface the overridden dissent entries and report on the evidence, if any, that has emerged since the original decision. Where the evidence has shifted, the committee SHOULD give serious consideration to revisiting the original decision, not because dissent is presumptively correct, but because the conditions under which the dissent was overridden may no longer hold.

Dissent that has been overridden multiple times and that has not been borne out by subsequent evidence SHOULD NOT, on its own, be treated as a marker of failed dissent. Dissent that has been overridden multiple times and repeatedly vindicated by subsequent evidence SHOULD be treated as a marker that the committee's initial review process needs to be examined, with care taken not to punish individual dissenters but to identify systemic blind spots in how the committee weighs evidence.
