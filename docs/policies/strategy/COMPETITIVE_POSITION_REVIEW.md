# Competitive Position Review

## Purpose

Establish a recurring, evidence-driven assessment of how the organisation is positioned relative to competitors, substitutes, and adjacent entrants. The objective is to detect durable shifts in rivalry early enough to inform strategy and allocation choices, without overreacting to short-term noise that distorts executive judgement.

## Scope

This policy applies to all formal competitive-position reviews prepared for executive audiences. It covers signal sources, evidence standards, scenario planning around competitor moves, sensitivity labelling, and the authority that may approve a position change. It does not direct routine sales or marketing competitive intelligence activities, which remain operational, nor does it govern pricing tactics, which fall under commercial management.

## Requirements

- A competitive position review MUST be produced at least twice per year for any material business line and ad hoc when a triggering event is observed.
- Signal sources MUST include a mix of primary inputs (customer interviews, win-loss data, regulator filings, patent disclosures) and reputable secondary inputs (industry analyst notes, conference proceedings, peer-reviewed academic work) to avoid single-vendor dependency.
- Every cited signal MUST record source, recency, and confidence so readers can weigh evidence quality.
- Each review MUST distinguish between observed competitor behaviour, inferred intent, and speculative projection.
- Where a competitor's stated strategy is materially different from its observed capability, the review MUST label the divergence and propose a test that would resolve it within ninety days.
- Scenario planning SHOULD cover at least one cooperative scenario, one competitive scenario, and one disruption scenario in which a non-obvious entrant changes the basis of competition.
- Reviews MUST be classified for sensitivity before distribution; pricing-implication content MUST be restricted to the named strategy committee and SHOULD NOT be circulated as a generic market update.
- The named strategy committee retains authority to recertify or revise the position statement; any deviation from the recertification cadence MUST be recorded in the change log with rationale.

## Workflow

1. The strategy lead prepares a draft by gathering signals from the standing intelligence pool and tagging each item with source, date, and confidence.
2. The lead assembles a position map that contrasts the organisation's value proposition, cost position, capability profile, and customer relationships against the named peer set.
3. Strategic assumptions underlying the previous position statement are tested against the new evidence; failed assumptions are flagged for either retirement or further investigation.
4. Three scenarios are written at a common time horizon, each with explicit triggers and early-warning indicators that would shift the assessment from one scenario to another.
5. The draft is reviewed by finance, product, and operations to ensure numbers and operational constraints are accurate; substantive objections are recorded and either resolved or escalated.
6. The strategy committee meets, weighs dissenting views, and either approves the position, requests a revision, or defers pending an additional investigation.
7. The approved position, the dissenting views, and the change log are filed; derivative narratives inherit the approved framing and cannot widen the position without escalation.

## Controls

- Sensitivity: position reviews containing forward pricing implications are restricted to the strategy committee and a named distribution list; they MUST NOT be recirculated without explicit recertification.
- Recertification: reviews expire ninety days after approval and MUST be refreshed before any subsequent executive narrative relies on them.
- Audit trail: every approval, dissent, and revision MUST be retained with author, date, and rationale for at least three years.

## Using the review

A competitive position review is a tool for better decisions, not an artifact in its own right. The strategy committee SHOULD specify, at approval, the decisions the review is intended to inform and the horizon over which it is expected to remain relevant; the next review MUST evaluate whether those decisions actually used the review and whether it produced the expected improvement in decision quality. A review that is approved and then ignored is a signal that the framing or the audience is wrong, and the committee SHOULD investigate the cause rather than continue to produce reviews that fail to inform decisions.

Position reviews SHOULD also be tested for confirmation bias. A common failure mode is that reviews are organised around the existing position and consequently interpret ambiguous evidence as confirmation. The committee SHOULD require, at each review, a designated contrarian voice whose job is to argue, on the basis of the same evidence, for the strongest plausible alternative reading of the position; the contrarian argument is recorded alongside the position, and the committee's response to it is part of the approval record.
