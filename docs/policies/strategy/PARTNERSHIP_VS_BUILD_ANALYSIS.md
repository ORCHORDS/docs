# Partnership vs Build Analysis

## Purpose

Provide a structured, evidence-driven method for choosing among build, buy, partner, or platform-lease options so that sourcing decisions reflect total value rather than headline price. The policy counteracts two recurring distortions: optimising for the speed of a partner arrangement without weighing the dependency it creates, and overestimating the strategic control of an internal build without weighing the cost of operating it long-term.

## Scope

This policy applies to any capability decision that contemplates a non-trivial build, a commercial purchase, a long-term partnership, or a multi-year platform lease. It covers criteria, total-cost modelling, exit-cost analysis, and approval routing. It supplements [Build Buy Partner Decision Governance](BUILD_BUY_PARTNER_DECISIONS.md) by adding analytical depth required for higher-stakes or more reversible decisions.

## Requirements

- A sourcing proposal MUST evaluate each candidate option against a common set of criteria: strategic control, speed to value, total cost of ownership, expertise access, security and privacy posture, supplier risk, differentiation potential, exit options, and long-term maintenance burden.
- Total cost of ownership MUST include one-time acquisition, integration, ongoing operation, governance, and exit; calculations SHOULD span a horizon appropriate to the asset and discount at the organisation's standard rate.
- Exit-cost analysis MUST be first-class; each option MUST be scored for cost and time to exit, including the cost of stranded skill, data portability, and contractual lock-in.
- A build option MUST be accompanied by an honest assessment of organisational ability to retain the talent required to operate the capability over the asset's life.
- A partner option MUST be accompanied by an assessment of the partner's incentive alignment, durability, and concentration risk; a partner whose commercial model depends on the organisation's failure should be rejected.
- A lease option MUST be accompanied by a contract review for assignment, audit, data-return, and step-up clauses; hidden escalators MUST be quantified before approval.
- The proposal SHOULD include a sensitivity analysis that varies the two most consequential assumptions and reports how the recommendation changes.
- The proposal MUST be approved by the body with authority for the spend band and SHOULD be referred to the strategy committee when the option would create a material dependency.

## Workflow

1. The originating sponsor prepares the option set with criteria, total-cost model, and exit-cost analysis.
2. Finance validates the model assumptions and the discount rate; counsel reviews contractual terms for the partner and lease options.
3. The strategy committee (or appropriate delegated authority) reviews the proposal against the criteria; the committee may request sensitivity analyses or alternative options.
4. The committee records the basis for the decision, the dissent considered, and the conditions under which the decision would be revisited.
5. On approval, the sponsor executes the chosen option and reports progress against the milestones agreed at approval.
6. At each material milestone or at the agreed cadence, the committee retests the decision against current evidence.

## Controls

- Reverse stress test: each option MUST be subjected to a reverse stress test that names the conditions under which it would be a poor choice; the test is recorded at approval.
- Exit readiness: for non-build options, an exit playbook MUST be drafted within ninety days of approval; absence of a playbook is an escalation event.
- Documentation: the sourcing file MUST contain the criteria scores, the model, the dissent, and the approval; the file is retained for at least the life of the asset.

## Decision quality and post-decision review

Sourcing decisions deserve post-decision review as much as the decision itself, because the assumptions that supported the original choice tend to evolve differently from how the model predicted. The review SHOULD compare the realised total cost of ownership against the modelled cost, the realised substitution cost against the modelled cost, and the realised exit cost against the modelled cost. Significant variances SHOULD be investigated for their root cause; if the model systematically under-estimated a category of cost, that finding SHOULD feed back into the criteria and the model used for future sourcing decisions.

The review SHOULD also examine whether the chosen option delivered the strategic objectives it was intended to serve; a sourcing decision that met its cost and timeline targets but did not advance the strategy it was meant to support is a poor decision, not a successful one. The post-decision review is therefore not solely a financial exercise but a strategic one, and its findings SHOULD be considered alongside the financial evidence when the next sourcing decision of comparable type is made.
