# Innovation Portfolio Balance

## Purpose

Set the rules for allocating innovation capital across time horizons so that the organisation neither starves the future nor starves the present. The policy exists to make the trade-off explicit, to prevent the failure mode in which short-term operational pressure crowds out long-horizon bets, and to prevent the symmetric failure in which long-horizon rhetoric crowds out execution that funds the present.

## Scope

This policy applies to the explicit innovation portfolio, including incremental, adjacent, and transformational bets. It covers horizon definitions, evidence thresholds, allocation rules, review cadence, and the linkage between the innovation portfolio and the core operating plan. It does not apply to maintenance or compliance investment, which is governed by operating policy and is not subject to the innovation allocation rules.

## Requirements

- Horizon definitions MUST be explicit: Horizon 1 (incremental, typically within twelve months), Horizon 2 (adjacent, typically within thirty-six months), and Horizon 3 (transformational, beyond thirty-six months).
- Each horizon MUST have a defined share of innovation capital expressed as a range rather than a point estimate; the range should be reviewed annually against strategic posture.
- Every bet MUST be classified into a horizon at commitment and MAY be reclassified only with a written justification reviewed by the strategy committee.
- Evidence thresholds MUST increase with horizon length: longer horizons require larger learning milestones per unit of capital and longer periods before scale decisions.
- The portfolio MUST be reviewed at a cadence appropriate to horizon: Horizon 1 quarterly, Horizon 2 semi-annually, Horizon 3 annually, with event-driven reviews when a material assumption fails.
- The portfolio SHOULD include kill criteria for each bet; capital released by a kill MUST be redeployed within the same horizon or returned to the innovation pool rather than absorbed into core operations.
- The portfolio balance SHOULD be reported alongside the operating plan so that trade-offs between innovation and execution are visible at the same decision table.

## Workflow

1. The strategy lead, with finance, sets the horizon ranges and the total innovation pool for the upcoming cycle.
2. Sponsors submit bet proposals with explicit horizon classification, evidence thresholds, kill criteria, and learning milestones.
3. The strategy committee reviews proposals against the horizon ranges and either approves, defers, or rejects each one; deviations from the horizon ranges require committee justification.
4. Approved bets are added to the portfolio register with the agreed evidence gates, the kill criteria, and the named owner.
5. At each review, the committee tests live evidence against the gates, retires bets that fail, and reallocates released capital within the same horizon.
6. The portfolio balance is reported alongside the operating plan; the report highlights horizons that are under- or over-allocated and the steps taken to address the imbalance.

## Controls

- Horizon protection: capital released by a kill MUST NOT be silently diverted to operations; it MUST be redeployed within the innovation pool or returned to it.
- Reclassification discipline: bets that drift toward a different horizon MUST be reclassified through the committee rather than quietly redefined.
- Visibility: the portfolio report is presented at the same meeting as the operating plan so that the trade-off is explicit.

## Learning and option value

Innovation capital exists in part for the learning it produces, and the learning may matter more than the immediate commercial outcome. Each bet SHOULD therefore state, at commitment, what the organisation expects to learn if the bet succeeds, what it expects to learn if the bet fails, and how that learning would feed into subsequent bets. A portfolio in which every bet succeeds on its original commercial thesis is unusual and SHOULD trigger a check on whether the bets have been set up to test anything; a portfolio in which many bets fail but produce usable learning is functioning as intended. The committee SHOULD treat the cadence of learning as a portfolio metric in its own right, alongside capital deployment and horizon balance.

Where possible, bets SHOULD be structured as options rather than as binary commitments. A small initial outlay that preserves the right to scale later is typically more valuable than a large commitment that cannot be cancelled without penalty, particularly in early horizons. The portfolio SHOULD report the option-value framing of each bet at commitment and at each review, so that the committee can see whether the organisation is preserving or consuming optionality over time.
