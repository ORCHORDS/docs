# Geographic Expansion Governance

## Purpose

Provide a structured process for evaluating, approving, executing, and exiting geographic expansion decisions so that the organisation enters new jurisdictions with realistic expectations and clear exit triggers. The policy prevents over-extension driven by anecdotal demand signals and ensures the cost of compliance, hiring, and local partnership is weighed against credible market evidence.

## Scope

This policy applies to any proposal to establish a legal presence, hire local employees, sign a long-term local lease, or commit material capital in a jurisdiction where the organisation does not currently operate at scale. It covers entry criteria, regulatory mapping, partnership posture, and exit triggers. It does not govern occasional cross-border sales that are properly handled under existing commercial policy.

## Requirements

- A geographic expansion proposal MUST begin with a market evidence package that distinguishes observed customer demand, latent demand inferred from comparable markets, and demand invented to support the proposal.
- Each proposal MUST include a regulatory mapping that names the licenses, registrations, and supervisory regimes the organisation would be subject to and an honest assessment of readiness to satisfy them.
- Partnership posture MUST be explicit: the proposal MUST state whether local presence will be built, partnered, acquired, or served remotely, and the rationale for the chosen posture.
- Total cost of operating in the new jurisdiction MUST be modelled over at least five years and SHOULD include one-time setup, recurring compliance, currency, repatriation, talent, and exit cost.
- Entry MUST be staged; a meaningful proportion of capital MUST be subject to a defined milestone that, if missed, triggers either pause or exit without further escalation.
- Exit triggers MUST be pre-registered and SHOULD include demand thresholds, regulatory changes, geopolitical shifts, and reputational events.
- The proposal MUST identify the named accountable executive, the standing review cadence, and the conditions under which the cadence accelerates.
- Proposals involving sanctions-sensitive jurisdictions MUST be reviewed by counsel before submission and MUST NOT proceed without a written legal opinion.

## Workflow

1. Originating sponsor prepares the market evidence package and the regulatory mapping; finance prepares the five-year cost model; counsel prepares the legal opinion.
2. The strategy committee reviews the package against the entry criteria; approval may be granted, denied, or deferred for further evidence.
3. On approval, the sponsor stands up the local presence according to the staged plan and reports progress at the agreed cadence.
4. At each milestone, the committee tests the live evidence against the original thesis; if the thesis has materially drifted, the committee chooses among continue, adapt, pause, or exit.
5. At any pre-registered exit trigger or any committee-decided exit, the sponsor executes the pre-registered wind-down plan without further escalation requirements.

## Controls

- Staged capital: capital release is gated by milestones; milestones may not be redefined without committee approval.
- Watch list: each active expansion is added to a watch list that records live evidence against the original thesis; the watch list is reviewed at the cadence set at approval.
- Audit trail: every entry, milestone, and exit decision is retained with date, evidence, and rationale for at least five years.

## Local integration and partnership conduct

Geographic expansion introduces obligations to local stakeholders that go beyond the immediate commercial opportunity. The proposal SHOULD therefore include a local-integration plan that covers employment practices, supplier diversity, community engagement, and the organisation's posture on issues that local stakeholders are likely to care about. Where the expansion is built through partnership, the partnership conduct itself becomes a reputational exposure: a partner that operates to a lower standard than the organisation would accept internally can damage the organisation's standing with regulators, customers, and the local community. The committee SHOULD evaluate the partner against the organisation's standards, not only against its commercial offering, and SHOULD require contractual mechanisms for audit, remediation, and exit where standards are not met.

The proposal SHOULD also describe the local leadership plan: who will lead the local operation, how authority is delegated, what decisions can be made locally, and which decisions must be referred to the parent. Unclear delegation is a frequent cause of slow local execution and of strategic drift from the parent posture; the local leadership plan is the document that resolves it at entry and that the parent reviews on the standing cadence thereafter.
