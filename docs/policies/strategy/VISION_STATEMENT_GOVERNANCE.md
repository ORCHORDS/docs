# Vision Statement Governance

## Purpose

Govern the structure, evidence basis, version history, dissemination, and recertification of organisational vision statements so that vision remains a useful, durable guide for strategy and not a marketing artifact that drifts out of contact with reality. A vision statement that is not governed becomes either too generic to guide decisions or too specific to outlast change; this policy maintains the right balance.

## Scope

This policy applies to all formal organisational vision statements, including enterprise-level statements and statements for material business units. It covers structure, evidence basis, version history, dissemination, and recertification. It does not govern mission statements, which are addressed separately, nor does it govern values statements, which have a different lifecycle.

## Requirements

- A vision statement MUST be expressed in terms of the outcomes the organisation seeks for its stakeholders and MUST be testable over a long horizon.
- The statement MUST be supported by an evidence basis that explains the strategic reasoning; the evidence basis SHOULD cite market context, stakeholder analysis, and the capability trajectory required.
- Each vision statement MUST have a version history that records what changed, why, and the date of approval; previous versions MUST be retained for reference.
- Dissemination MUST be controlled; derivative communications MUST inherit the approved wording rather than introduce variations; visual and language adaptations SHOULD be approved through the same change process.
- A vision statement MUST have a recertification cadence; longer intervals MUST be justified with evidence that the underlying strategic context has not materially shifted.
- Re-certification SHOULD test whether the vision continues to align with stakeholder expectations, market direction, and the organisation's capability trajectory.
- Material changes to the vision statement SHOULD involve a consultation process with named stakeholders; the consultation SHOULD be documented and the responses SHOULD be retained.
- The vision statement MUST NOT be modified between recertifications except through the formal change process; ad hoc rewording is prohibited.

## Workflow

1. The strategy lead prepares a draft vision statement using the standing template and supports it with the evidence basis.
2. The lead consults named stakeholders; feedback is recorded and either incorporated or recorded as dissent.
3. The strategy committee reviews the draft, considers the consultation results, and either approves, defers, or requires revision.
4. On approval, the statement is filed with a version history entry and a recertification date; derivative communications are generated from the approved version.
5. The statement is disseminated through controlled channels; derivative adaptations are reviewed and approved through the same change process.
6. At recertification, the strategy lead retests the statement against current evidence and recommends continue, amend, or retire; the committee decides.

## Controls

- Version integrity: prior versions MUST be retained; rewording without version history is prohibited.
- Dissemination discipline: derivative communications MUST inherit the approved wording; variations require committee approval.
- Recertification discipline: vision statements that have passed their recertification date MUST be withdrawn or refreshed before continued use.

## Connection to strategy and operations

A vision statement that does not visibly connect to strategy and operations becomes ornamental. The governance of the vision statement SHOULD therefore include a periodic check that the strategy documents and the operating plan can be traced back to the vision through a clear line of reasoning; if they cannot, either the vision has drifted away from the work or the work has drifted away from the vision, and either finding is an event that warrants committee attention. Derivative strategy documents SHOULD cite the version of the vision statement they were written against; a strategy document that cites a superseded vision version is a recertification trigger.

The vision statement SHOULD also be tested for stakeholder salience: do the people the organisation depends on recognise themselves in the statement, and do they find it credible as a description of where the organisation is going? The consultation process at adoption and at each material revision SHOULD include a representative sample of stakeholder groups and SHOULD record the feedback and the committee's response to it. Silence from stakeholders SHOULD NOT be treated as endorsement; it is more often disengagement, and a vision statement that does not engage its stakeholders SHOULD be re-examined.
