# Product Strategy Document

## Purpose

Define the standards for an authoritative product-strategy document so that every product area can be evaluated on a common basis and so that downstream planning, investment, and performance reviews can rely on a stable artifact. A product strategy document that lacks structure, evidence, or ownership produces inconsistent decisions; this policy prevents that drift.

## Scope

This policy applies to every product-strategy document used to drive investment, resourcing, or roadmap decisions at the product-line level. It covers structure, evidence basis, ownership, review cadence, and outcome metrics. It does not direct the content of marketing collateral or sales enablement, which derive from the strategy document rather than governing it.

## Requirements

- A product-strategy document MUST contain a defined set of sections: purpose, target customer, problem addressed, evidence of demand, competitive context, capability dependencies, investment thesis, milestones, owners, risks, outcome metrics, and review triggers.
- Each section MUST cite its evidence basis; sections that rely on belief rather than observation MUST be labelled as such and SHOULD NOT drive consequential decisions until the belief is tested.
- The document MUST identify the accountable executive and the named author; both MUST be recorded in the document's metadata.
- Outcome metrics MUST be defined at the time of adoption and MUST distinguish leading indicators, lagging indicators, and counter-indicators.
- The document MUST have a defined review cadence appropriate to its horizon and a defined trigger for event-driven reviews.
- The document SHOULD be reviewed at least annually and MUST be recertified at each major strategic shift in the parent business line.
- A change log MUST be maintained; revisions MUST record what changed, why, and which downstream artifacts must be updated.

## Workflow

1. The named author drafts the document using the required structure and draws only from approved upstream strategy, segmentation, and customer-evidence sources.
2. The author validates each section with the relevant subject-matter experts; disagreements are recorded in the dissent section rather than silently resolved.
3. The strategy committee reviews the document against the standards; the committee may approve, defer, or require revision.
4. On approval, the document is filed with metadata; downstream artifacts (roadmaps, narratives, business cases) cite the approved version.
5. At each review, the author updates the document, refreshes the evidence, and submits for recertification; if recertification is denied, the document is retired and any dependent artifacts are flagged for revision.
6. The author maintains the change log; the strategy committee may request an interim review if the parent business line changes materially.

## Controls

- Source integrity: the document MUST NOT reference upstream artifacts that are themselves expired or under review; the author verifies currency before drafting.
- Outcome integrity: outcome metrics MUST be drawn from the approved metric register; ad hoc metrics introduced in the strategy document are prohibited.
- Retirement discipline: when a strategy document is retired, dependent artifacts MUST be updated within ninety days; the strategy committee tracks compliance.

## Evidence and uncertainty

A product-strategy document that presents a confident narrative without disclosing the strength of the evidence behind each claim creates downstream planning that assumes more certainty than the strategy actually carries. The document SHOULD therefore use a simple evidence labelling convention for material claims: directly observed, supported by primary research, supported by reputable secondary sources, inferred, or speculative. The labelling MUST be visible to consumers so that roadmaps and business plans built on speculative claims do not inherit the apparent confidence of the surrounding narrative. Where the strategy rests on speculative claims, the document SHOULD name the experiment or evidence event that would resolve the speculation and SHOULD set a horizon for resolution; speculative claims that remain unresolved past their horizon are a trigger for recertification rather than for confident continuation.

The outcome metrics defined in the document SHOULD also expose the basis for their targets. A target that has been derived from observed performance of an analogous product is more credible than a target that has been chosen to match investor expectations, and the document SHOULD make that distinction explicit. Targets that are chosen without an evidence base SHOULD be flagged as aspirational, and the strategy committee SHOULD decide whether aspirational targets are appropriate in that section or whether the document should hold the target until evidence supports it.
