# Market Segmentation Review

## Purpose

Ensure market-segmentation assumptions on which strategy depends remain current, sourced, and applicable. Segmentation that has not been refreshed becomes folklore: customer labels ossify, growth pockets are misidentified, and resource allocation drifts away from the highest-return opportunities. This policy defines a recurring review that keeps segmentation honest.

## Scope

This policy applies to every segmentation that materially drives strategy, resourcing, or product investment decisions. It covers sizing source, refresh cadence, applicability tests, and governance of changes. It does not govern operational CRM segmentation that exists purely for sales execution, which follows a separate operational review.

## Requirements

- Each material segmentation MUST have a documented owner, a documented refresh cadence, and a documented source basis for both sizing and behavioural assumptions.
- Sizing sources MUST distinguish primary measurement (transactional or survey data the organisation controls), secondary measurement (analyst or third-party datasets), and inferred sizing derived from secondary inputs.
- A segmentation SHOULD NOT be used to drive strategy for more than one refresh cycle beyond its last recertification; stale segmentations MUST either be refreshed or marked provisional.
- Each segmentation MUST record its applicability: the business questions it can answer, the questions it cannot, and the conditions under which the segmentation would have to be re-cut.
- Refresh cadence MUST be set at the time of segmentation adoption; longer intervals MUST be justified with evidence that the segment boundaries are stable.
- Re-cut criteria MUST be pre-registered; they SHOULD include measurable shifts in customer behaviour, the emergence of a new customer class, or a material change in the basis of competition.
- Changes to segmentation SHOULD be communicated in a controlled manner that explains what changed, what did not, and which decisions should be revisited.

## Workflow

1. The strategy lead maintains a register of material segmentations, each with owner, source, refresh cadence, and re-cut criteria.
2. The lead schedules each segmentation for refresh in line with its cadence; the refresh draws on the primary and secondary sources specified at adoption.
3. The lead prepares a refresh report that compares current evidence with the standing segmentation, identifies any drift, and recommends continue, amend, or re-cut.
4. The strategy committee reviews the refresh, considers dissent, and either approves the recommendation or commissions further analysis.
5. Approved changes are recorded in the segmentation register; the lead communicates the change to downstream stakeholders with a clearly stated effective date.
6. Decisions that depended on the previous segmentation are reviewed in light of the change; the review's conclusions are recorded alongside the change.

## Controls

- Currency check: every strategy document that uses a segmentation MUST cite the segmentation's recertification date; documents that reference stale segmentations MUST be revised before approval.
- Owner accountability: the named owner is responsible for ensuring refresh occurs; missing a refresh triggers a notification to the strategy committee chair.
- Communication discipline: changes to segmentation are communicated once and consistently; derivative communications MUST NOT introduce alternative cuts without committee approval.

## Quality of segmentation evidence

Segmentation is only as useful as its evidence. A segmentation that mixes observed customer behaviour with inferred commercial attractiveness tends to drift toward the latter, because the inferred dimension is easier to use rhetorically and harder to falsify. The refresh review SHOULD distinguish between dimensions grounded in directly observed behaviour, dimensions grounded in declared preference, and dimensions inferred from secondary data; each SHOULD carry a confidence rating, and the confidence rating SHOULD be visible to consumers of the segmentation so that downstream uses do not over-trust the lower-confidence dimensions.

Where the segmentation underwrites investment, the refresh SHOULD include a backward look: did the segments we believed would grow actually grow, and did the segments we deprioritised respond as the model predicted? Significant miss rates are a signal that the segmentation is mis-specified or that the operating model is not acting on it; either way the conclusion is that the segmentation is no longer credible and SHOULD be re-cut or retired.

## Segmentation and stakeholder communication

A segmentation is also a communication artifact, and the language used to describe segments has an outsized effect on how the organisation behaves toward the customers in each segment. The refresh SHOULD test the labels for unintended consequence: a label that is internally neutral may read differently to a customer who sees the label reflected in service quality or product investment. Where a label has acquired a meaning in operations that was not intended at segmentation, the refresh SHOULD consider renaming the segment or changing the operating treatment so that the label and the reality are aligned.

The refresh SHOULD also consider whether the segmentation is being used as a proxy for commitments the organisation has not actually made. A segmentation that distinguishes a "priority" segment from other segments tends, over time, to be read as a service-level promise even when no such promise was made; the strategy committee SHOULD be explicit about whether priority labels carry operational commitments and, if they do, whether those commitments are being met on a measured basis rather than asserted.
