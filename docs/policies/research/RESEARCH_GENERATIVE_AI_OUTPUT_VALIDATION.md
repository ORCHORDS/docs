# Generative AI Output Validation in Research

Generative AI may assist research analysis, but its outputs are not evidence by themselves. NIST identifies confabulation as a characteristic risk of generative AI: systems can confidently produce false, internally inconsistent, or unsupported content, including invented citations or reasoning.

## Governance requirements

- Treat generated summaries, classifications, interpretations, and citations as provisional until independently checked.
- Validate factual claims against the underlying source material or another authoritative source before incorporating them into a research finding.
- Verify citations by opening the cited source and confirming that it exists and supports the stated claim.
- Preserve enough information to distinguish researcher-authored conclusions from AI-assisted intermediate work.
- Apply stronger human review where an incorrect output could materially change a consequential finding or decision.
- Do not use model confidence, fluent wording, or apparent chain-of-reasoning as evidence of factual correctness.

## Validation record

For material AI-assisted analysis, record as appropriate:

- the research task and evidence set;
- the model or service used when disclosure is permitted and useful;
- the verification method;
- material errors discovered during review;
- the human decision on whether the generated output was accepted, corrected, or rejected.

## Claims boundary

AI assistance does not increase the strength of the underlying evidence. Research conclusions should continue to reflect the quality, representativeness, limitations, and uncertainty of the source data.
