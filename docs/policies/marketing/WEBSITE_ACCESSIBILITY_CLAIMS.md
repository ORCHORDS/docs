# Website Accessibility Claims

## Purpose

Public claims about the accessibility of a website ("WCAG 2.2 AA compliant", "accessible to all", "screen-reader friendly", "ADA compliant") carry legal and reputational weight. A claim that is overstated, outdated, or unsupported exposes the organisation to advertising-regulator and disability-discrimination claims. This article sets out the conformance target, testing method, limitations, recertification cadence, and feedback channel that govern accessibility claims. The objective is to publish accessibility claims that are defensible, current, and proportionate to the actual state of the site.

## Scope

This article applies to every public statement that describes the accessibility of any website, microsite, landing page, application surface, document, video, or downloadable artefact for which Marketing is the publisher or co-publisher. It covers claims on the organisation's own sites, on packaging, in sales collateral, in paid media, in press releases, and on partner sites where the organisation's accessibility statement is mirrored. It applies regardless of jurisdiction and regardless of whether the jurisdiction has codified accessibility standards.

## Requirements

- Every accessibility claim MUST be backed by a documented conformance assessment. The assessment MUST name the standard (for example, WCAG 2.2 Level A, AA, or AAA), the version, the scope of the assessment, the testing method, the date, and the assessor.
- Marketing SHOULD target WCAG 2.2 Level AA as the default claim. Level AAA conformance is rare and SHOULD NOT be claimed for an entire site unless the site has been independently audited.
- The testing method MUST be disclosed. Automated testing alone is insufficient; the assessment MUST include manual review of representative pages and SHOULD include testing with assistive technologies used by people with disabilities (for example, screen readers, voice control, switch input).
- Limitations and known gaps MUST be disclosed. A claim of full conformance with a list of known exceptions is more accurate than an unqualified claim.
- The accessibility claim MUST be paired with a feedback channel that allows users to report barriers and request alternative formats. The feedback channel MUST be monitored and MUST trigger remediation.
- The claim MUST be recertified at least annually, or more frequently if the site experiences a material redesign, a major content change, or a third-party audit that alters the conformance posture.
- Independent audits MAY be commissioned; the audit firm's credentials, the scope, and the resulting report SHOULD be available on request.
- Accessibility claims MUST be removed or corrected promptly when the underlying conformance changes. Continued publication of an outdated claim is misleading.
- Marketing MUST NOT claim conformance with a future version of a standard (for example, "WCAG 3.0 compliant") before the standard is finalised and an assessment against it is possible.
- Claims that touch regulated accessibility law (for example, the European Accessibility Act, the Americans with Disabilities Act, the UK Equality Act 2010, the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act) MUST be reviewed by the Legal function for compliance in each jurisdiction.
- The accessibility statement page MUST itself be accessible and MUST link to the claim substantiation package on request.
- Cross-border claims MUST distinguish between jurisdictions with codified accessibility law and those without, and MUST NOT promise more than the underlying assessment supports.

## Workflow

1. The accessibility owner commissions or performs the conformance assessment; the assessment report names the standard, the scope, the method, the date, and the assessor.
2. The Legal function reviews the proposed claim language against the assessment and the jurisdictional watchlist.
3. The claim is published on the accessibility statement page with the limitations and feedback channel.
4. The owner schedules the recertification at the cadence required by the claim type and any jurisdictional requirements.
5. User feedback is routed to a triage queue that is monitored at the cadence required by the Marketing Incident Response policy; feedback that identifies a barrier triggers a remediation task.
6. At recertification, the assessment is refreshed and the claim is either continued, modified, or retired.

## Controls

- The accessibility claim record is versioned in the Claim Substantiation system and includes the assessment report, the legal review, and the recertification due date.
- The feedback channel is tested for availability at least monthly; failed feedback routes are restored within the incident response SLA.
- Remediated barriers are recorded; the remediation history supports the next recertification.
- Quarterly, the accessibility owner audits a sample of accessibility claims across surfaces to confirm they are current.
