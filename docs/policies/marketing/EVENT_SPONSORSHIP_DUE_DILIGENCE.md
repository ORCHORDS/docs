# Event Sponsorship Due Diligence

## Purpose

Sponsoring an event — paying for branding, speaking, booth space, hospitality, or co-marketing — associates the organisation with the event, its organiser, its audience, and the other sponsors. A sponsorship decision that is rushed, undocumented, or driven by convenience can expose the organisation to reputational, regulatory, and contractual harm. This article defines the due-diligence steps, contractual controls, and recertification cadence required before the organisation commits to a sponsorship.

## Scope

This article applies to any sponsorship, paid speaking slot, branded hospitality, branded activation, or co-marketing arrangement with an event organiser or venue. It covers physical events, virtual events, hybrid events, conferences, trade shows, community gatherings, awards ceremonies, and hosted dinners. It applies whether the sponsorship is paid in cash, in product, in services, or in speaking/visibility in kind.

## Requirements

- Marketing MUST complete a documented due-diligence review of the event, the organiser, and the venue before committing to the sponsorship. The review MUST cover reputation, ownership, financial standing, audience profile, sponsor history, compliance posture, accessibility, and security.
- The due-diligence review MUST confirm that the event's content does not contravene the organisation's policies on claims, sustainability, accessibility, or partnerships, and does not require participation in unlawful activity.
- Marketing MUST verify the organiser's legal authority to grant the sponsorship rights offered (for example, exclusivity, logo usage, attendee data rights). The verification includes reviewing the organiser's corporate status and contractual history.
- The sponsorship contract MUST be reviewed by the Legal function and MUST contain, at minimum, scope of rights, exclusivity (if any), duration, payment terms, cancellation and refund terms, indemnity, data rights, compliance with applicable advertising and accessibility law, and audit rights.
- Where the sponsorship grants access to attendee data, the contract MUST specify the lawful basis, the permitted uses, the retention period, the security obligations, the prohibition on resale, and the deletion obligation at the end of the sponsorship.
- Marketing MUST NOT sponsor events that target audiences excluded by the organisation's policies (for example, minors without appropriate guardianship, audiences restricted by sanctions, audiences that can only be reached through deceptive recruitment).
- Hospitality and travel benefits associated with the sponsorship MUST comply with the organisation's anti-bribery and anti-corruption policies and the laws of the relevant jurisdictions.
- Marketing MUST publish or disclose sponsorship as required by applicable advertising and disclosure rules; the disclosure is co-ordinated with the Legal function.
- Accessibility is a hard requirement. Marketing MUST confirm that the event provides equivalent access for attendees with disabilities and that sponsored content (talks, signage, collateral) is accessible. Lack of accessibility MAY be a blocker.
- The sponsorship MUST be recertified at least annually for recurring events and before each new edition of an annual event. Recertification re-runs the due-diligence review and confirms the contractual terms are still appropriate.
- A register of active sponsorships is maintained; the register includes contract value, term, owner, and recertification date.

## Workflow

1. The sponsorship requestor identifies the event, the proposed level of sponsorship, the audience, and the strategic rationale.
2. The requestor performs the due-diligence review using the standard checklist and attaches the findings to the request.
3. The Legal function reviews the contract; the Privacy function reviews any data-rights clauses; the Accessibility reviewer evaluates the accessibility of the event and the sponsored content.
4. The request is reviewed by the Marketing Lead and, for material sponsorships, by the relevant business owner and the Finance function.
5. On approval, the contract is executed and the sponsorship is recorded in the register with the owner, the recertification date, and the data-rights summary.
6. At recertification, the owner re-runs the review and either confirms continuation, modifies the sponsorship, or terminates it; the outcome is logged.

## Controls

- The due-diligence checklist is versioned and published; each published version has an owner and a "next review" date.
- Sponsorship payments and in-kind benefits are tracked against the contract value; deviations trigger a contract review.
- The recertification register is reconciled against active contracts at least quarterly; expired recertifications are escalated to the Marketing Lead.
- The Marketing Incident Response policy applies if a sponsored event generates a public incident.
