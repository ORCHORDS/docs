# Partner Brand License Scope

## Purpose

Granting a partner the right to use our brand — names, logos, product names, trademarks, imagery, or distinctive trade dress — creates reputational, legal, and commercial exposure that is bounded by the scope of the licence. Unbounded or imprecise licences can result in inappropriate use, dilution, or infringement claims. This article defines the permissible uses, expirations, termination triggers, and audit rights that govern partner brand licences. The objective is to enable valuable co-marketing while protecting the integrity of the brand and the organisation's ability to enforce its marks.

## Scope

This article applies to every written grant of brand-licensing rights to a third party. It covers reseller and distributor rights, technology and integration partners, co-marketing arrangements, event sponsorships with brand rights, referral partners, agency partners, and any other arrangement in which the partner is permitted to use the organisation's marks in external communications. It applies regardless of whether the licence is commercial (paid), in-kind (reciprocal), or one-sided (no consideration).

## Requirements

- Every partner brand licence MUST be granted in a written contract reviewed by the Legal function. The contract MUST name the marks covered, the permitted uses, the prohibited uses, the territory, the channels, the duration, the termination triggers, and the audit right.
- The permitted uses MUST be specific and enumerated. Open-ended rights (for example, "any marketing purpose") are prohibited; the contract MUST list or reference a style guide that lists the permitted categories.
- The contract MUST require the partner to use the marks only in the form and manner specified by the style guide. Modifications to the marks (colour, proportion, juxtaposition, animation) MUST be pre-approved.
- The partner MUST NOT sub-licence, assign, or transfer the rights without prior written consent. The contract MUST prohibit sub-licensing by default.
- The duration MUST be time-bounded. The default maximum is 36 months; longer durations require renewal and an explicit business rationale recorded in the contract file.
- Termination triggers MUST be defined and MUST include, at minimum: breach of the style guide, breach of confidentiality, reputational harm, change of control of the partner, insolvency, regulatory action against the partner, and material breach of any other contract between the parties.
- Upon termination, the partner MUST cease use of the marks within a defined wind-down period (typically 30 calendar days) and MUST remove the marks from digital, print, and physical surfaces. The contract MUST include audit rights to confirm wind-down.
- The contract MUST include quality control rights. The organisation retains the right to review and require changes to partner use of the marks; the partner MUST comply within a defined timeframe.
- The contract MUST prohibit use of the marks in any communication that disparages the organisation, makes unverified claims, or implies endorsement of activities outside the scope of the licence.
- Where the partner is in a regulated industry, the contract MUST require compliance with the applicable sector regulations (for example, financial promotion rules, health-claim rules).
- A register of active partner brand licences is maintained; the register includes the partner, the marks, the territory, the expiry date, and the renewal owner.

## Workflow

1. The business owner proposing the partner relationship requests a brand licence through the standard intake process.
2. The Legal function drafts the licence contract using the approved template; deviations from the template are flagged and require additional review.
3. The brand team reviews the proposed use cases against the style guide and confirms the marks, formats, and channels.
4. The contract is executed and the licence is recorded in the register with the partner, the marks, the territory, the expiry date, and the renewal owner.
5. During the licence term, the brand team performs periodic usage reviews against the register and the style guide.
6. At expiry or termination, the partner is notified, the wind-down period begins, and the audit right is exercised as needed.

## Controls

- The licence register is reconciled against the contract repository at least quarterly; orphan licences are escalated.
- The brand team performs a periodic sampling of active partner uses against the style guide; deviations trigger a remediation request.
- Termination triggers are tracked in a workflow so that the brand team is notified promptly when a trigger event occurs.
- The audit right is exercised on a sample basis annually and on every termination event.
