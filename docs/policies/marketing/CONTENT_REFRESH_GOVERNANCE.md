# Content Refresh Governance

## Purpose

Marketing content decays. Statistics age, screenshots change, product names shift, regulatory references become outdated, and previously accurate claims become misleading as context evolves. This article establishes the cadence, signals, ownership, and archival standards that keep published marketing content current, accurate, and compliant. A predictable refresh rhythm protects the audience from stale information and protects the organisation from advertising-regulator and consumer-protection exposure.

## Scope

This article applies to all published marketing content that makes factual, performance, comparison, ranking, or time-sensitive claims. It covers website pages, microsites, blog posts, whitepapers, case studies, social posts that reference a claim, sales decks, brochures, packaging, event materials, and email templates. It applies regardless of the channel or whether the content is owned, paid, earned, or shared. It does not govern pure opinion or aspirational statements that do not assert a verifiable fact.

## Requirements

- Every published content asset MUST carry a "last reviewed" date, the owner of record, and the next scheduled review date. The metadata is surfaced to readers where the asset contains a time-sensitive claim.
- Content MUST be assigned a freshness class at publication. Class A (high urgency — rankings, prices, performance benchmarks, regulatory references) is reviewed at least every 90 days. Class B (general narrative and explainers) is reviewed at least every 180 days. Class C (evergreen opinion or stylistic content) is reviewed at least every 365 days.
- A refresh MUST be triggered earlier than the scheduled cadence when any of the following signals occur: a substantive change in the product or service, a change in regulatory classification, a competitor material event that affects a comparative claim, an unresolved reader correction, or the discovery that the underlying evidence is unsound.
- Marketing MUST maintain a content register that maps every published asset to its owner, freshness class, last review date, and next review date. The register is the system of record.
- The owner of record is accountable for the accuracy of the content they own. Owners leaving the organisation MUST transfer ownership formally and the transfer MUST be logged in the register.
- Refresh reviews MUST consider accuracy, currency, claim substantiation, accessibility, and brand voice. A review that considers only stylistic concerns is incomplete.
- Content that is no longer accurate or that cannot be refreshed with current evidence MUST be corrected, retired, or archived. Stale content left in place is misleading.
- Archived content that remains publicly accessible MUST carry an archive notice that communicates the date the content was last accurate and the reason for archival.
- Refresh outcomes MUST be logged: either "confirmed current", "edited and republished", or "retired/archived". The log is auditable.
- Automated link-checking and broken-reference detection SHOULD run at least weekly on content properties; broken references on assets with factual content MUST be repaired within the freshness class SLA.
- Where a refresh identifies a substantive error, the Marketing Incident Response policy applies and the correction is handled as an incident.

## Workflow

1. The content register schedules each asset for review according to its freshness class and any ad-hoc signals.
2. The owner of record receives a review task at the scheduled time with the asset, the prior review notes, and the freshness-class checklist.
3. The owner performs the review, consulting the substantiation record where the asset contains a claim, and the legal record where the asset references regulation.
4. The owner logs the outcome and either edits the asset (with a record of the changes), confirms currency, or initiates archival.
5. The register is updated with the new "last reviewed" date and the next scheduled date; the asset metadata is republished if the content changed.
6. The Marketing Lead receives a weekly roll-up of all review outcomes and any overdue reviews; overdue reviews are escalated.

## Controls

- The content register is the authoritative source; any publication system that lists live assets is reconciled against the register at least quarterly.
- Overdue reviews trigger an automatic reminder to the owner, the owner's manager, and the Marketing Lead after a configurable grace period.
- Accessibility is a required dimension of every refresh review; the Marketing Accessibility checklist is appended to the review task.
- Quarterly, the Marketing Lead audits a sample of assets across freshness classes to confirm the review process is functioning.
