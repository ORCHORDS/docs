# Customer Case Study Consent

## Purpose

A case study uses a customer's name, identity, voice, results, or operational detail to illustrate the value of a product or service. Using customer information in marketing without informed, specific, and revocable consent exposes the organisation to privacy regulator action, breach-of-contract claims, and reputational harm. This article sets out the scope of consent required for case studies, the approval authority, the permissions for logos and metrics, the expiration of consent, and the takedown process. The objective is to ensure that case studies reflect a documented partnership rather than an assumption.

## Scope

This article applies to any marketing artefact that identifies a customer by name, brand, logo, voice, image, or specific outcome. It covers written case studies, video case studies, recorded interviews, podcast episodes, social posts, paid promotion, event presentations, sales decks, and pages on the organisation's own website. It applies to current customers, former customers, prospects who participated in pilots, and named individuals who speak on behalf of a customer organisation. It does not apply to anonymous references or composite illustrations that do not identify a specific customer.

## Requirements

- Marketing MUST obtain documented, specific, informed, and revocable consent from the customer before any case study is published. The consent MUST name the channels of publication, the content covered by the consent, the duration, and the takedown procedure.
- For named individuals, Marketing MUST obtain consent directly from the individual in addition to any organisational authorisation. The individual consent covers voice, image, and personal attributes; the organisational consent covers logos, trademarks, and corporate identity.
- Consent MUST be recorded in the customer relationship record and the marketing artefact record; the record MUST include the consent artefact (signed form, recorded agreement, or system-of-record entry) and the date.
- Metrics and outcomes used in case studies MUST be substantiated. Marketing MUST source the metric from the customer's own data, with the customer's permission, or from a measurement that the customer has validated. Exaggerated or unverified outcome claims are prohibited.
- Use of customer logos MUST be limited to the scope of the consent. Where the consent does not cover a particular channel or format, the case study MUST NOT appear in that channel.
- Consent MUST be time-bounded. The default maximum duration is 24 months from publication; longer durations require renewal and are recorded as exceptions.
- Marketing MUST honour a customer request to amend, retire, or take down a case study within 14 calendar days of the request, regardless of the consent duration. The takedown procedure MUST be capable of locating and removing the asset across all channels, including archived copies and third-party reposts where the organisation has control.
- Cross-border case studies MUST meet the lawful-basis and consent requirements of every jurisdiction in which the asset is published.
- Customer case studies that include sensitive categories of information (health, financial difficulty, legal proceeding, personal crisis) MUST be reviewed by the Legal function before publication.
- Case studies involving minors require consent from a parent or legal guardian and MUST follow additional safeguards, including review by the Privacy function.
- Where a case study references a regulated outcome (clinical outcome, regulatory approval, financial return), the Legal function MUST confirm the permitted framing.

## Workflow

1. The case study owner identifies the candidate customer and confirms a willing participant through the customer success channel.
2. The owner sends the consent form, which names the channels, the content, the duration, the metrics, and the takedown procedure; the form is reviewed by the Legal function before it is sent.
3. The customer signs the consent form, returns it, and the artefact is stored in both the customer record and the marketing record.
4. The case study draft is written, reviewed for substantiation and voice, and circulated to the customer for factual review before publication.
5. The customer confirms factual accuracy in writing; the case study is published and the publication date and channels are recorded.
6. The case study is scheduled for a consent check at the consent expiration date; the owner reaches out to the customer to renew or retire the consent.

## Controls

- The case study register maps each case study to its consent artefact, owner, publication channels, and expiration date.
- A weekly job reconciles active case studies against the consent register; expired or missing consents trigger an automatic pause.
- The takedown procedure is documented and tested at least annually with a sample case study.
- Where the customer relationship ends, the consent remains valid for the duration stated in the consent form unless the customer requests earlier retirement.
