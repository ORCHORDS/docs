# Competitive Intelligence Ethics

## Purpose

Competitive intelligence informs marketing strategy, positioning, and differentiation when it is gathered through legitimate, public, and ethical channels. This article defines the permissible sources, restrictions, confidentiality expectations, and escalation paths that govern competitive intelligence activities. The objective is to ensure that the organisation's market insights are defensible, do not expose it to trade-secret or unlawful-means claims, and do not damage relationships with customers, partners, or former employees who are bound by their own confidentiality obligations.

## Scope

This article applies to every employee, contractor, or agent who gathers, analyses, stores, shares, or acts upon information about competitors of the organisation. It covers primary research, secondary research, third-party reports, social listening, public-record collection, customer-conversation analysis, and any intelligence obtained through events, hiring practices, partnerships, or product trials. It applies regardless of the source's geographic location or the medium in which the information is captured.

## Requirements

- Competitive intelligence MUST be gathered through open and lawful means. Public websites, regulatory filings, published reports, investor presentations, conference talks, and customer-authorised disclosures are permissible sources.
- Marketing MUST NOT solicit, accept, or use information obtained through misrepresentation, breach of confidentiality, theft, hacking, eavesdropping, or unlawful access. Information volunteered by a current or former employee of a competitor MUST be presumed confidential unless the source has a documented right to disclose it.
- Marketing MUST NOT ask candidates, contractors, partners, or third parties to disclose confidential information of a competitor. Interview questions MUST be limited to the candidate's own experience and skills.
- Information about a competitor's customers MUST be gathered only from publicly available sources or from customers who have explicitly authorised the disclosure; customer lists obtained through unauthorised channels are prohibited.
- Marketing MUST NOT use competitor confidential information even if it is received unsolicited; the recipient MUST promptly notify the Marketing Lead, the Legal function, and where appropriate the originating party, and MUST return or destroy the information.
- Outputs of competitive intelligence activities MUST be classified and stored according to the Marketing Disclosure Traceability policy; raw notes and source materials are retained only as long as necessary to support the documented analytical use.
- Marketing MUST NOT disparage competitors or use deceptive framing to suggest capabilities that derive from improperly obtained information.
- Competitive intelligence findings used in public marketing claims MUST meet the Comparative Claim Substantiation requirements.
- Marketing MUST NOT use competitive intelligence to facilitate anticompetitive agreements (price fixing, market allocation, bid rigging, group boycotts). Suspected violations MUST be escalated immediately to the Legal function.
- Where competitive intelligence activity occurs across borders, Marketing MUST confirm that the methods used are lawful in each jurisdiction and respect local privacy and trade-secret protections.
- Marketing SHOULD maintain a documented ethics review for high-risk intelligence activities, including reverse engineering, mystery shopping, or contracting with specialist research firms.

## Workflow

1. The requestor identifies the intelligence objective, the proposed sources, and the intended use.
2. The requestor confirms that each source is open and lawful and documents the confirmation in the intelligence record.
3. Where the activity is high-risk (reverse engineering, mystery shopping, contracted research), the Marketing Lead and Legal function review the plan before fieldwork begins.
4. The intelligence is gathered, redacted where necessary, and recorded in the marketing intelligence repository with provenance notes.
5. Outputs are reviewed for compliance with comparative claim, disparagement, and disclosure rules before any external use.
6. The record is retained for the period required by the Marketing Data Retention policy and then deleted.

## Controls

- Annual training is required for every member of the marketing organisation with competitive intelligence responsibilities; training completion is recorded and audited.
- Third-party research vendors are bound by the Marketing Vendor Governance policy and a confidentiality clause; the clause is non-negotiable.
- A confidential intake channel exists for employees to report suspected unethical collection; reports are investigated by the Legal function and the Marketing Lead.
- A register of high-risk intelligence activities is maintained; the register is reviewed quarterly.
