# Copywriting Brand Voice

## Purpose

Brand voice is the consistent, recognisable manner in which an organisation speaks to its audiences. Operating discipline around voice prevents drift, dilution, and accidental inconsistency across copywriters, agencies, and channels. This article defines the operating expectations for copywriting against the documented brand voice: lexicon, inclusive language, review process, and exceptions. The objective is to ensure that every external communication reads as the organisation, not as a sequence of independent authors.

## Scope

This article applies to all externally facing marketing copy: headlines, body copy, calls to action, microcopy, social posts, scripts, package text, in-product marketing messages, event signage, and partner co-branded copy where the organisation is named. It applies to copy written by employees, contractors, agencies, and AI-assisted tools. It does not govern legal notices, regulatory disclosures, technical documentation, or press releases, which follow their own dedicated policies.

## Requirements

- Marketing MUST maintain a documented Brand Voice Guide that defines the voice attributes (for example, tone, register, formality, directness, warmth, confidence), the lexicon of approved and prohibited words, and a baseline set of example passages that illustrate the voice in context. The guide is the authoritative reference.
- Copy MUST be reviewed against the Brand Voice Guide by a designated reviewer before publication. The reviewer is named in the workflow record and the review outcome is logged.
- Inclusive language is required. Copy MUST avoid gendered defaults, ableist framings, ageist assumptions, racial or ethnic stereotypes, and idioms that exclude audiences. Preferred alternatives are listed in the Brand Voice Guide.
- Copy MUST be plain-language first. Jargon, acronyms, and idioms SHOULD be replaced with everyday language unless the audience is technical and the term is precise.
- Copy MUST distinguish capability from aspiration. Aspirational statements MUST be recognisable as such (for example, by tense, qualifiers, or labelling); conflating roadmap items with current capability is prohibited.
- Marketing MUST maintain an exception register for deliberate voice deviations. Each exception names the rationale (regulatory, technical, audience-specific) and the approving reviewer.
- AI-generated or AI-assisted copy MUST be reviewed by a human reviewer against the Brand Voice Guide; the human reviewer is accountable for the published copy regardless of the source.
- The Brand Voice Guide MUST be reviewed at least every 180 days and updated when the organisation's positioning, audience, or product mix materially changes.
- Voice consistency is a measurable property. Marketing SHOULD maintain a periodic voice audit that scores a sample of recent copy against the guide and reports drift to the Marketing Lead.
- Copy that contains a claim is subject to the Claim Substantiation policy in addition to the voice review; the two reviews are recorded separately.
- Copy that is translated or localised MUST be reviewed by a localisation reviewer against both the Brand Voice Guide and the Marketing Localisation policy.

## Workflow

1. The copywriter drafts the copy against the brief and the Brand Voice Guide.
2. The designated reviewer evaluates the draft against the guide's voice attributes, lexicon, inclusive-language checklist, and any campaign-specific constraints.
3. Where the copy contains a claim, the claim substantiation review runs in parallel; both reviews must clear before publication.
4. Where the copy requires localisation, the localisation reviewer validates voice equivalence across target languages.
5. The reviewer logs the outcome (approved, revise, reject) in the campaign record; for an exception, the rationale and approver are recorded.
6. The published copy is sampled into the periodic voice audit, and the audit findings inform updates to the Brand Voice Guide.

## Controls

- The Brand Voice Guide is versioned and published; each published version has an owner and a "next review" date.
- The exception register is reviewed quarterly; recurring exceptions MAY be folded into the next version of the Brand Voice Guide.
- Voice-audit results are reported to the Marketing Lead; a sustained drift triggers a mandatory guide update.
- Where AI-generated copy is used, the audit checks for tell-tale phrasings, generic claims, and ungrounded superlatives in addition to voice consistency.
