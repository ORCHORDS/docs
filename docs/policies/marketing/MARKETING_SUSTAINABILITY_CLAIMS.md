# Marketing Sustainability Claims

## Purpose

Sustainability claims ("eco-friendly", "carbon neutral", "100% recyclable", "net zero", "green", "sustainable", "responsibly sourced") carry heightened scrutiny from advertising regulators, environmental NGOs, and consumer-protection authorities because they can materially influence purchase decisions and obscure substantive environmental performance. Vague or unsupported claims expose the organisation to enforcement action under unfair-trading laws and erode trust in genuine sustainability efforts. This article sets out the recognised standards, scope, verification, and disclaimer obligations required before a sustainability claim may be published.

## Scope

This article applies to any external marketing communication that makes a claim about the environmental, social, ethical, or sustainability characteristics of a product, service, packaging, operation, supply chain, partner, or the organisation as a whole. It applies across all channels and formats: advertising, packaging, labels, websites, point-of-sale, social posts, sales decks, investor communications, and event signage. It does not apply to internal communications or to non-promotional disclosures required by securities, sustainability, or financial regulation, which follow their own dedicated policies and disclosures.

## Requirements

- Every sustainability claim MUST be substantiated by evidence that is current, complete, and methodologically sound. The evidence MUST be retained for the lifetime of the claim plus the period required by the Marketing Data Retention policy.
- Recognised standards and frameworks SHOULD be used wherever available. Examples include ISO 14021 (self-declared environmental claims), ISO 14064 (greenhouse gases), ISO 14067 (carbon footprint of products), the GHG Protocol, the EU Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition Directive, the UK CMA Green Claims Code, and equivalent national frameworks.
- The scope of every claim MUST be explicit. Marketing MUST name the lifecycle stage, the boundary (product, packaging, operation, supply chain), the time window, and the methodology. Vague claims that omit scope are prohibited.
- Comparative environmental claims MUST follow the Comparative Claim Substantiation policy in addition to this article; the comparison MUST be on like-for-like terms.
- Claims that depend on offsetting, carbon credits, or renewable energy certificates MUST disclose the mechanism, the registry, the vintage, the project type, and any limitations (additionality, permanence, leakage).
- Forward-looking claims (for example, "by 2030 we will be net zero") MUST be time-bounded, accompanied by a credible transition plan, and recertified at planned intervals; vague forward claims without a plan are prohibited.
- Third-party certifications, eco-labels, and endorsements MAY be used, but only where the certification is from a recognised body, applies to the specific product or service in question, and is currently in force. Expired certifications MUST NOT be cited.
- Disclaimers and qualifications MUST be legible and proximate to the claim. Footnotes that are visually separated, in significantly smaller type, or hidden behind interaction are not acceptable disclosures.
- Sustainability claims MUST NOT use imagery, colour, or symbolism that conveys environmental benefit that the product or service does not substantiate (for example, leaf imagery on a product that is not independently verified).
- Sustainability claims that target vulnerable audiences or that exploit environmental anxiety MUST be reviewed for appropriateness before publication.
- Cross-border claims MUST be reviewed for compliance with the green-claims rules of every jurisdiction in which the claim will appear. Several jurisdictions are tightening rules and may deem common claims (for example, "climate positive", "100% green") to be unfair commercial practices.

## Workflow

1. The claim owner drafts the claim and assembles the substantiation package: methodology, data, scope, third-party verification, and disclaimers.
2. The Sustainability Reviewer evaluates the package against the recognised standards and the jurisdictional watchlist; the Legal function performs a parallel review for compliance with green-claims law in each target jurisdiction.
3. Where third-party verification is required, the verification is sourced from an accredited body and the verification statement is retained with the claim record.
4. The claim, disclaimer copy, and substantiation package are stored together and referenced from the campaign record.
5. The owner schedules recertification at the cadence appropriate to the claim type (at least annually, sooner for forward-looking or offset-based claims).
6. At recertification, the substantiation is refreshed and the claim is either continued, modified, or retired.

## Controls

- Sustainability claim records are versioned in the Claim Substantiation system; each record includes the methodology, the verifier, and the recertification due date.
- A quarterly report flags sustainability claims that depend on offsetting or forward-looking commitments; these are prioritised for recertification.
- The jurisdictional watchlist is maintained by the Legal function; new entries trigger an automatic review of active claims.
- Sustainability incidents follow the Marketing Incident Response policy; the correction is published through the same channels as the original claim.
