# Comparative Claim Substantiation

## Purpose

Comparative claims ("better than", "faster than", "cheaper than", "#1", "industry-leading") carry heightened substantiation and disclosure obligations because they explicitly position our offering against named or unnamed competitors. Misleading comparative claims expose the organisation to advertising regulator action, competitor complaints, and consumer-protection litigation. This article sets out the scope, methodology, currency, and disclaimer standards required before a comparative claim may be published.

## Scope

This article applies to any marketing communication that directly or indirectly compares our organisation, products, services, capabilities, or pricing against one or more identified or unidentified competitors. It applies to all channels — paid media, owned digital surfaces, print, broadcast, packaging, sales collateral, and event materials — and to claims made by creators, influencers, partners, or third parties acting on our behalf. It does not replace the Marketing Claim Substantiation Recertification policy; comparative claims have additional requirements layered on top of general substantiation.

## Requirements

- Every comparative claim MUST have a written substantiation record that names the comparison axis (price, speed, reliability, capacity, customer outcome, market share), the data source, the methodology, the sample size or coverage, and the date the evidence was generated.
- The data MUST be of equal recency for the parties being compared. Where competitor data is older than our own evidence, Marketing MUST either refresh the competitor evidence, narrow the claim, or add an explicit currency disclaimer.
- Where the claim is based on a single study or a single cohort, Marketing MUST disclose the study conditions, sample, geography, and time window; broad claims derived from narrow studies are prohibited.
- Marketing MUST verify the trademark, naming, and reputation consequences of naming a competitor. Claims that disparage competitors by reference to a single defect or adverse event without context MAY NOT be published.
- Headline rankings (for example, "#1 in category" or "leader") MUST cite the source publication, the ranking date, and the eligibility criteria; rankings from sponsored studies or pay-to-play lists MUST NOT be used as the sole basis for a headline claim.
- Methodology choices MUST be defensible to an independent reviewer. Selecting a benchmark that materially favours our offering without disclosure is a misrepresentation and is prohibited.
- Where the comparison turns on price, the comparison MUST be on like-for-like terms (same period, same billing model, same scope, same customer segment) and MUST disclose fees, taxes, and material exclusions.
- Where the comparison turns on performance, the conditions of measurement (hardware, network, configuration, workload mix) MUST be either standardised for both parties or disclosed to the audience.
- Disclaimers MUST be legible and proximate to the claim. Footnotes that are visually separated, in significantly smaller type, or hidden behind interaction are not acceptable disclosures.
- Comparative claims MUST be recertified at least every 90 days; a claim that depends on a third-party ranking MUST be recertified within 30 days of the next ranking publication.
- Cross-border comparative claims MUST be reviewed by the Legal function for compliance with the laws of each jurisdiction in which the claim will be published; some jurisdictions restrict superlatives and certain comparison structures.

## Workflow

1. The campaign owner or copywriter drafts the comparative claim and submits it with the substantiation package (evidence, methodology, source data, recency).
2. The Claim Substantiation Reviewer evaluates the package against the requirements above and either approves, requests revision, or rejects.
3. Where the claim names a competitor or relies on a third-party ranking, the Legal function performs a parallel review.
4. The claim, disclaimer copy, and substantiation package are stored together and referenced from the campaign record.
5. The owner schedules a recertification task in the Marketing Calendar with the cadence required by the claim type.
6. At recertification, the same workflow repeats; claims that fail recertification are paused and either corrected or retired.

## Controls

- Comparative claim records are versioned in the Claim Substantiation system; each record MUST include the approver, the evidence hash, and the recertification due date.
- A weekly report flags comparative claims that are within 14 days of their recertification due date; the owner receives an automated alert.
- Disclaimers are tested for legibility on representative displays (mobile, print, projection) before publication; legibility failures block publication.
- The Legal function maintains a comparative-advertising watchlist of jurisdictions with heightened restrictions; the watchlist is consulted at every review.
