# Partner Insurance Review

## Purpose

This policy establishes how partner insurance coverage is validated at onboarding and on each renewal, including policy types, minimum limits, additional-insured provisions, notice-of-cancellation provisions, and the records that demonstrate currency. It ensures that the organization is protected against foreseeable partnership risks to a degree proportionate to the engagement, that lapses or coverage reductions are detected early, and that the insurance position remains enforceable throughout the partnership.

## Scope

This policy applies to all partners required by policy, regulation, or contract to maintain insurance, including commercial general liability, professional liability (errors and omissions), cyber liability, crime/fidelity, workers' compensation, automobile liability, and any specialty coverage required by the engagement. It covers minimum-limit determination, additional-insured provisions, waiver of subrogation, notice of cancellation, evidence collection, annual renewal review, and the records that demonstrate compliance. It does not prescribe underwriting decisions of any insurer or the contents of any specific policy beyond what is required to verify compliance with this policy.

## Requirements

- Each partner required to maintain insurance MUST provide a certificate of insurance evidencing the required policy types, limits, additional-insured status, and notice-of-cancellation provisions.
- Minimum insurance limits MUST be set proportionate to partnership risk, the data classes handled, and the regulatory profile of the engagement.
- The organization MUST be named as an additional insured on the partner's commercial general liability and, where appropriate, on automobile and umbrella policies; the additional-insured status MUST be reflected on the certificate.
- The partner MUST provide notice of cancellation, non-renewal, or material change at least thirty days before the effective date, or ten days for non-payment of premium.
- The Partnerships Lead MUST review insurance evidence at onboarding and at each policy renewal, and confirm currency at least annually.
- Any lapse, reduction, or material change in coverage MUST trigger an immediate risk assessment and remediation plan; high-severity gaps MAY result in suspension or termination of the partnership.
- The Partnerships Lead SHOULD retain certificates, endorsements, and any related correspondence for the period required by the records-retention schedule.
- The organization MAY require that cyber liability coverage be at a level consistent with the volume and sensitivity of data handled and may require coverage of regulatory fines and penalties where lawful.

## Workflow

1. **Coverage requirement definition.** During onboarding, the Partnerships Lead defines required coverage types and minimum limits based on partnership risk.
2. **Evidence request.** The partner is asked to provide a certificate of insurance and, where required, policy endorsements.
3. **Review.** The Partnerships Lead verifies coverage against the requirements, including additional-insured status and notice provisions.
4. **Approval.** Coverage is approved with any required endorsements identified and tracked to receipt.
5. **Renewal tracking.** Renewal dates are entered into a tracker and reviewed at least thirty days before renewal.
6. **Annual review.** Coverage is revalidated annually and at any renewal.
7. **Lapse handling.** A reported lapse triggers a risk assessment, a remediation plan, and, where required, suspension or termination.
8. **Records.** Certificates, endorsements, and reviews are retained per the records-retention schedule.

## Controls

- An insurance tracker exists with required coverage, current evidence, renewal date, and review status for every insured partner.
- Additional-insured status is documented on the certificate of insurance.
- Notice-of-cancellation provisions are recorded in the tracker.
- High-severity gaps trigger formal remediation under a documented escalation path.

## Coverage tailoring by risk

Minimum insurance limits are set proportionate to partnership risk. Engagement factors considered include the data classes the partner handles, the regulatory regime applicable to the engagement, the volume and frequency of customer interaction, the use of subcontractors, the geographic spread of operations, the historical incident record of the partner, and any specialty exposure such as professional services, security operations, or autonomous decisioning. Higher-risk engagements MAY require higher minimum limits, additional coverage types, broader additional-insured provisions, or a higher deductible absorbed by the partner.

## Lapse, suspension, and termination

A lapse in coverage, a coverage reduction, or a material change in policy terms triggers a documented risk assessment and a remediation plan. The risk assessment considers the duration of the lapse, the affected coverage type, the data classes handled during the lapse window, and any incidents that occurred during the window. Depending on severity, the organization MAY suspend new work, restrict access, require evidence of retroactive coverage, place the partner on a corrective-action plan, or terminate the partnership. The decision and rationale are recorded in the insurance tracker and the partnership record.
