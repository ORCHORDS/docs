# Partner Subcontractor Disclosure

## Purpose

This policy establishes the disclosure and approval requirements for partner use of subcontractors in the performance of obligations to the organization or jointly to customers. It ensures that subcontractor identity, scope, location, and flow-down obligations are known, that the organization retains an opportunity to object to a proposed subcontractor, and that subcontractor engagement does not dilute the partner's accountability for delivery, security, privacy, and compliance.

## Scope

This policy applies to every partner that engages a subcontractor to perform any portion of its obligations under the partnership agreement, including delivery, support, hosting, data processing, security operations, development, integration, and customer success. It covers disclosure of subcontractor identity, scope of services, geographic location, data access, flow-down obligations, approval, objection, ongoing monitoring, and the records that demonstrate compliance. It does not cover the partner's internal staffing, parent-subsidiary arrangements disclosed in the partnership agreement, or subcontracting for purely commercial back-office functions unrelated to the partnership obligations.

## Requirements

- Each partner MUST disclose, before engagement, the identity of any proposed subcontractor, the scope of services, the geographic location of performance, the data classes the subcontractor will access, and the flow-down obligations the partner will impose.
- The organization MUST have the right to object to a proposed subcontractor on grounds of risk, security, privacy, regulatory, or compliance concerns; the objection MUST be communicated in writing with rationale within ten business days of disclosure.
- The partner MUST impose flow-down obligations on the subcontractor that are at least equivalent to those imposed on the partner by the partnership agreement, including security, privacy, confidentiality, audit, and termination provisions.
- The partner MUST maintain a current list of approved subcontractors and provide it to the organization on request and at least annually.
- The partner MUST notify the organization within ten business days of any change in subcontractor status, including new engagement, replacement, or termination.
- The Partnerships Lead MUST evaluate each disclosure against the partnership risk profile and the data classes handled, and document the decision.
- The organization SHOULD require that high-risk subcontractor relationships be subject to an assessment equivalent to the partner's own onboarding assessment.
- The organization MAY require the right to conduct an independent audit of a subcontractor where the engagement involves regulated data, significant volume, or material risk.

## Workflow

1. **Disclosure.** The partner submits a subcontractor disclosure with identity, scope, location, data access, and proposed flow-down.
2. **Review.** The Partnerships Lead reviews the disclosure against risk, security, privacy, and regulatory considerations.
3. **Objection window.** The organization has ten business days to object; silence beyond the window constitutes no objection unless the agreement specifies otherwise.
4. **Flow-down verification.** The Partnerships Lead verifies that the partner's flow-down obligations are at least equivalent to those of the partnership agreement.
5. **Approval.** The subcontractor is approved, conditionally approved, or rejected; the decision is recorded with date and approver identity.
6. **Engagement.** The partner engages the subcontractor under the agreed flow-down.
7. **Ongoing monitoring.** The partner maintains the subcontractor list and notifies the organization of changes.
8. **Independent audit.** Where required, the organization conducts or commissions an independent audit of the subcontractor.
9. **Records retention.** Disclosures, decisions, flow-downs, and notifications are retained per the records-retention schedule.

## Controls

- A subcontractor register exists for every partner that uses subcontractors, with identity, scope, location, data access, and approval status.
- A documented objection window and right of objection is incorporated into every partnership agreement.
- Annual refresh of subcontractor lists is performed and retained.
- Independent audit rights are included where the engagement risk warrants.

## Concentrated subcontractor exposure

Where multiple partners rely on the same subcontractor for the same capability, the organization MAY be exposed to correlated risk. The Partnerships Lead maintains an awareness of concentrated subcontractor exposure across the partner portfolio and reports material concentrations to the governance body. Where concentrated exposure creates unacceptable risk, the organization MAY require partners to qualify a secondary subcontractor, to maintain documented business-continuity arrangements for the primary subcontractor, or to limit the scope of work performed by the concentrated subcontractor.

## Change handling

Subcontractor changes — including new engagement, replacement, scope expansion, or termination — are subject to the same disclosure and approval process as the initial engagement. Material changes, including a change in the data classes the subcontractor handles or a change in geographic location, trigger an interim risk assessment. The Partnerships Lead reviews the change against the existing approval and updates the subcontractor register and the partnership record accordingly.
