# Partner Conflict of Interest

## Purpose

This policy establishes how conflicts of interest involving partners are identified, declared, scoped, mitigated, and disclosed. It protects the integrity of partnership decisions by ensuring that personal, organizational, financial, or competitive relationships that could bias a partner's or an employee's judgment are surfaced early, managed transparently, and resolved in a manner consistent with fiduciary and contractual obligations.

## Scope

This policy applies to all partners and to all employees, contractors, and authorized agents of the organization who participate in partner selection, partner governance, partner performance review, or any decision in which a partner has a material interest. It covers personal conflicts, organizational conflicts, competitive conflicts, financial conflicts, and conflicts arising from concurrent or sequential engagements. It does not replace any procurement-specific conflict policy; it harmonizes with it.

## Requirements

- Every partner MUST disclose, at onboarding and annually thereafter, any relationship that could give rise to a conflict of interest involving the partnership.
- Every employee or contractor who participates in a partnership decision MUST disclose any personal, financial, or familial relationship with the partner under consideration, including prior employment, equity holdings, board service, or close personal ties.
- The Partnerships Lead MUST evaluate each disclosed conflict, assign a severity rating, and document the mitigation, including recusal, additional review, written disclosure to governance, or prohibition.
- A conflict that cannot be mitigated to an acceptable residual risk MUST result in recusal of the conflicted party from the relevant decision and MAY result in termination of the partnership if no alternative is feasible.
- All disclosed conflicts and their resolution MUST be recorded in a conflict log accessible to authorized reviewers; personal identifiers MUST be limited to those with a need to know.
- The Partnerships Lead MUST review the conflict log at least quarterly and report material or persistent conflicts to the governance body.
- The organization SHOULD require periodic refresh of conflict disclosures from each partner and from each employee participating in partnership decisions.
- The organization MAY decline to enter into or continue a partnership where the disclosed conflict cannot be managed consistent with this policy.

## Workflow

1. **Disclosure.** The partner or employee submits a conflict disclosure describing the relationship, the parties, and the nature of the potential conflict.
2. **Triage.** The Partnerships Lead triages the disclosure within five business days, gathering supporting information as needed.
3. **Severity assessment.** The Partnerships Lead rates the conflict as low, medium, or high based on the ability of the relationship to influence the partnership decision.
4. **Mitigation design.** A mitigation plan is documented, including controls such as recusal, dual review, information barriers, disclosure to governance, or contract amendment.
5. **Decision.** The Partnerships Lead decides whether the conflict is acceptable with mitigation, requires escalation, or disqualifies the partner or employee from the engagement.
6. **Monitoring.** Open conflicts are reviewed at each quarterly governance cycle; changes in circumstance trigger interim review.
7. **Records.** The conflict record, mitigation plan, and decisions are retained per the records-retention schedule and reviewed on request by authorized reviewers.
8. **Escalation.** Material or persistent conflicts are escalated to the governance body with recommendations for action.

## Controls

- A conflict log exists with severity rating, mitigation plan, and status for every disclosed conflict.
- Conflict disclosures are refreshed annually for every active partner and every participating employee.
- Material conflicts are reported to the governance body at least quarterly.
- The Partnerships Lead can produce the conflict log for any active partner on request within two business days.

## Categories of conflict

The Partnerships Lead evaluates conflicts across several categories. Personal conflicts include familial, romantic, or close personal relationships between partner personnel and organization personnel with decision authority over the partnership. Financial conflicts include equity holdings, debt, board service, paid advisory roles, or material commercial arrangements that could bias judgment. Organizational conflicts include concurrent or sequential engagements in which the partner (or an affiliate) supports a competing offering, a successor offering, or a regulatory matter involving the organization. Competitive conflicts include situations in which a partner competes for the same customer, segment, or opportunity as the organization. Each category has a documented mitigation playbook; mitigations MAY be combined where the conflict spans categories.

## Failure to disclose

Failure by a partner or by an employee to disclose a known conflict is itself a partnership-performance or conduct issue. Where non-disclosure is discovered, the Partnerships Lead reopens the conflict log, performs an impact assessment on prior decisions, and recommends corrective action proportionate to the impact. Corrective action MAY include voiding affected decisions, recovering any benefit conferred, reassigning decision authority, retraining, or termination of the partnership or employment.
