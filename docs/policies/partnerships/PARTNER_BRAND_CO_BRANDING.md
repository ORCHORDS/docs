# Partner Brand Co-Branding

## Purpose

This policy governs the creation, approval, distribution, expiration, and takedown of co-branded assets produced jointly by the organization and its partners. It protects brand integrity for both parties, ensures that customer-facing representations remain accurate, and provides a documented process for retiring co-branded material when a partnership ends or a campaign concludes.

## Scope

This policy applies to all co-branded assets, including logos, lockups, wordmarks, color treatments, imagery, video, web pages, microsites, social posts, paid-media creatives, sales decks, datasheets, packaging, event signage, and printed collateral that display the marks of both parties. It applies whenever such assets are produced by either party, jointly, or by a third party on either party's behalf. It does not cover single-party assets that merely mention the other party in text.

## Requirements

- Co-branded assets MUST be reviewed and approved by both parties' brand or marketing leads before public release; approvals MUST be recorded with date, asset identifier, and approver identity.
- Each co-branded asset MUST identify the marks used, the trademark or registration status, and the geographic scope of permitted use.
- Either party MAY require changes for clarity, brand-standards conformance, or legal sufficiency; the requesting party MUST document the change request and the resolution.
- Co-branded assets MUST carry an expiration or review date not later than twelve months from publication, after which the asset MUST be re-approved or retired.
- The organization MUST publish a brand-use guide that defines clear space, color reproduction, minimum size, prohibited alterations, and lockup rules; partners MUST follow it.
- Upon partnership termination, campaign end, or any material change in the relationship, the party discovering the change MUST trigger takedown within ten business days and confirm completion.
- The Partnerships Lead MUST maintain a co-branded asset register with at least the following fields: asset identifier, parties, approval date, expiration date, distribution channels, and takedown status.
- The organization SHOULD conduct an annual co-branded asset audit to verify that no expired or unauthorized asset remains in distribution.
- The organization MAY require that high-risk or regulated claims (for example, performance, security, or financial claims) be reviewed by Legal before approval.

## Workflow

1. **Request intake.** A partner or internal team submits a co-branding request describing the asset, audience, channels, intended lifespan, and any regulated claims.
2. **Pre-flight review.** Brand and Legal complete a pre-flight review covering trademark availability, claims, regulated-industry rules, and existing approvals.
3. **Draft production.** The asset is produced in conformance with the brand-use guide; working files are versioned.
4. **Approval.** Both brand leads approve the final asset in writing; approvals are stored with the asset record.
5. **Distribution.** The asset is released through approved channels; distribution is logged so takedown later can be comprehensive.
6. **Re-review.** On or before the expiration date, the asset is re-approved or retired.
7. **Takedown.** On partnership termination or any trigger event, the Partnerships Lead notifies all known distributors and confirms removal; completion evidence is archived.
8. **Audit.** An annual audit samples the asset register against observed public exposure and reports discrepancies.

## Controls

- A versioned co-branded asset register exists; each entry has approval evidence and an expiration date.
- A documented takedown procedure with named owners and target completion windows exists for each trigger event.
- Annual audit results are retained and reviewed by the Partnerships Lead.
- Brand-use guide version is referenced in every approval record so later reviewers can reproduce the conformance check.

## Endorsement and disclosure

Co-branded assets that include endorsement-style content, customer testimonials, or third-party statements MUST comply with applicable endorsement and disclosure rules. Material connections between the organization and any endorser MUST be clearly disclosed, and the language used MUST be truthful and non-deceptive. Co-branded assets referencing measurable performance, security, financial, or comparative claims MUST be substantiated before approval and revalidated at each refresh.

## Localization

Where a co-branded asset is localized for a market, the localization MUST preserve the meaning of the source asset and the integrity of the marks, and any required local disclosures MUST be added. Localized versions are tracked in the co-branded asset register with a reference to the source asset and the responsible translator or localization owner.
