# Partner Pricing Alignment

## Purpose

This policy establishes how partner pricing is aligned with the organization's published pricebooks, discount guardrails, margin expectations, and customer-facing integrity requirements. It ensures that partners present prices that customers can rely on, that discount authority is bounded, that margin and channel economics remain within agreed parameters, and that deviations are approved and documented before customer exposure.

## Scope

This policy applies to all partners authorized to quote, sell, or resell the organization's offerings, including resale, distribution, referral, marketplace, OEM, and managed-services partners. It covers pricebook publication, partner pricing schedules, discount approval authority, margin and channel-economics expectations, customer-facing presentation, currency and locality, and the records that demonstrate compliance. It does not govern the internal pricing strategy itself; it governs how that strategy is communicated to and applied by partners.

## Requirements

- The Partnerships Lead MUST publish a current pricebook to each authorized partner and a clear process for refreshing it; partners MUST NOT quote from outdated pricebooks.
- Each partner MUST operate within the published pricebook and the partner-specific pricing schedule; deviations require written approval before customer exposure.
- Discount approval authority MUST be set by engagement tier, deal size, and risk; partners MUST request approval through the documented channel and retain evidence.
- Customer-facing price quotes MUST identify the offering, the unit price, the discount (if any), the resulting net price, the validity period, and any terms that affect the price.
- Currency, locality, and tax treatment MUST be specified in the pricebook and applied consistently; partners MUST NOT misrepresent them.
- Margin and channel-economics expectations MUST be defined in the partnership agreement; persistent under-margin deals MUST be reviewed for cause and remediation.
- The Partnerships Lead MUST conduct a quarterly pricing compliance review, including a sample of partner quotes against the pricebook and the discount guardrails.
- The organization SHOULD publish a pricebook change calendar so partners can plan for refresh.
- The organization MAY require deal-registration, two-quote minimum, or pricing-floor protections where the channel economics warrant.

## Workflow

1. **Pricebook publication.** The Partnerships Lead publishes the current pricebook to each authorized partner.
2. **Partner schedule.** A partner-specific pricing schedule is set in the partnership agreement and refreshed on a defined cadence.
3. **Quote preparation.** The partner prepares a customer-facing quote using the current pricebook and schedule.
4. **Discount check.** Any discount within the partner's authority is applied; any discount beyond the partner's authority is submitted for approval.
5. **Approval.** Discount approval is granted or denied through the documented channel; approval evidence is retained.
6. **Quote issuance.** The partner issues the customer-facing quote with all required disclosures.
7. **Quarterly review.** The Partnerships Lead reviews a sample of quotes against the pricebook and the discount guardrails.
8. **Non-conformance handling.** Non-conforming quotes are addressed under the documented escalation path, including corrective action, retraining, or termination.
9. **Records retention.** Quotes, approvals, and reviews are retained per the records-retention schedule.

## Controls

- A current pricebook exists for every authorized partner and a refresh cadence is documented.
- Discount approval authority is documented for each partner tier and reviewed at least annually.
- Quarterly pricing compliance review findings are reported to Finance and the governance body.
- Non-conformance findings trigger documented corrective action with owners and deadlines.

## Customer-facing integrity

Customer-facing price quotes MUST be presented in a manner that is not misleading and that supports informed customer decisions. Each quote MUST identify the offering, the unit price, any discount and resulting net price, the validity period, the term and renewal terms where applicable, the support tier, and any conditions affecting price. Where a partner bundles the organization's offering with its own services, the bundled price MUST be presented transparently with the components and allocation disclosed. Bundling MUST NOT obscure the price of the underlying offering, and any volume, term, or commitment discount applied to the bundle MUST be reconciled to the underlying pricebook rules.

## Currency, locality, and tax

The pricebook specifies the currency, the locality, and the tax treatment for each offering. Partners MUST NOT misrepresent currency, apply an incorrect tax treatment, or quote in a currency inconsistent with the customer's contract. Where the customer's contract requires a specific currency, the partner MUST honor that requirement. Currency conversions, where applicable, use the source rate defined in the partnership agreement or the customer contract.
