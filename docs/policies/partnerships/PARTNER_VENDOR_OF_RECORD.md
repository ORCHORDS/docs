# Partner Vendor-of-Record Decision

## Purpose

This policy establishes the criteria, decision process, financial-control implications, customer-clarity considerations, and approval authority for choosing the vendor-of-record model in a partnership. It ensures that the entity that contracts with the customer is selected on the basis of risk, financial control, regulatory clarity, support responsibility, and customer benefit rather than on convenience alone, and that the decision is documented and revisable.

## Scope

This policy applies to all partnerships in which the organization and a partner deliver a combined offering to a customer, and the vendor-of-record choice is open. It covers the criteria for selecting the organization, the partner, or a jointly formed entity as the contracting party, the implications for invoicing, tax, support, liability, regulatory exposure, and customer-facing clarity, and the approval authority required for the choice. It does not govern the underlying pricing or commercial terms, which are negotiated separately.

## Requirements

- The vendor-of-record decision MUST be made before customer commitment and documented in a vendor-of-record memo identifying the recommended party, the rationale, the financial-control implications, the regulatory implications, the support responsibility, and the customer-clarity considerations.
- The decision MUST be approved by the Partnerships Lead, Finance, Legal, and the appropriate business owner before customer commitment.
- The vendor-of-record MUST be the party that bears the contractual liability to the customer for the offering, that issues the invoice, that collects payment, and that holds the customer-facing support obligation unless the agreement specifies otherwise.
- The vendor-of-record decision MUST consider the customer's interest in having a single accountable contracting party, the regulatory implications of the chosen party (for example, data protection, financial regulation, export control), and the financial control implications of the chosen party (for example, revenue recognition, tax treatment, accounts receivable).
- Where the partner is the vendor-of-record, the partnership agreement MUST specify how the organization is protected against partner default, including indemnification, escrow, insurance, and termination rights.
- Where the organization is the vendor-of-record, the partnership agreement MUST specify how the partner is compensated, including referral fees, revenue share, or service fees.
- The Partnerships Lead MUST revisit the vendor-of-record decision at each major partnership milestone and at any material change in the regulatory or financial environment.
- The organization SHOULD ensure that customer-facing contracts clearly identify the vendor-of-record and the support escalation path.
- The organization MAY decline to enter into a partnership in which the proposed vendor-of-record model is inconsistent with this policy.

## Workflow

1. **Trigger.** The Partnerships Lead identifies a partnership opportunity that requires a vendor-of-record decision.
2. **Memo drafting.** A vendor-of-record memo is drafted identifying the candidate parties, the criteria, the financial-control implications, the regulatory implications, the support responsibility, and the customer-clarity considerations.
3. **Consultation.** Finance, Legal, and the business owner review the memo and provide input.
4. **Decision.** The Partnerships Lead, Finance, Legal, and the business owner jointly approve the recommended vendor-of-record.
5. **Documentation.** The decision is recorded in the partnership record with date, attendees, and rationale.
6. **Customer-facing clarity.** The chosen vendor-of-record is identified in the customer-facing contract and in any sales or support materials.
7. **Periodic review.** The decision is revisited at each major partnership milestone and at any material change in the regulatory or financial environment.
8. **Records retention.** Memos, decisions, and supporting documents are retained per the records-retention schedule.

## Controls

- A vendor-of-record memo exists for every partnership where the vendor-of-record choice is open.
- Joint approval by Partnerships Lead, Finance, Legal, and the business owner is documented.
- The chosen vendor-of-record is clearly identified in the customer-facing contract.
- Periodic review of the decision is documented with attendees and rationale.

## Sub-delivery and pass-through

Where the vendor-of-record engages the other party as a sub-supplier, the customer-facing contract MUST identify the vendor-of-record as the contracting party while internal arrangements specify the sub-supplier relationship. Pass-through obligations — including security, privacy, support response, and incident coordination — MUST be documented in the partnership agreement and reconciled with the customer-facing contract. The Partnerships Lead ensures that the customer is never asked to deal directly with a sub-supplier on matters that affect contractual liability, and that the vendor-of-record retains responsibility for coordination.

## Reversal

Reversing an established vendor-of-record decision is treated as a material change and follows the same approval path as the original decision, with documentation of the rationale, customer impact, transition plan, and timing. Reversal is normally contemplated only at major partnership milestones (renewal, scope change, or regulatory change) and not during the active contract term, except where required by law, regulation, or material change in circumstance. Where reversal is required mid-term, the Partnerships Lead coordinates with Legal, Finance, and the customer to manage the transition.
