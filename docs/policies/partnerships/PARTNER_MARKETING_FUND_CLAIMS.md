# Partner Marketing Fund Claims

## Purpose

This policy governs how partner marketing-development-fund (MDF) claims are submitted, evaluated, accrued, approved, paid, and audited. It ensures that MDF is spent on eligible activities, supported by sufficient evidence, consistent with the partner agreement and program rules, and reconciled with the financial books so that accruals and disbursements are accurate and disputes can be resolved promptly.

## Scope

This policy applies to all MDF programs operated by the organization with any partner, including program definitions, claim eligibility, evidence requirements, accruals, approval limits, payment timing, audit, and dispute resolution. It covers all eligible activity categories defined in the program, including demand generation, events, content production, digital advertising, training, and translation. It does not cover partner incentive payments, rebates, or revenue share, which are governed by their respective policies.

## Requirements

- Each MDF program MUST publish a program guide stating eligible activities, ineligible activities, claim limits per period, required evidence, submission deadlines, and approval authority.
- A partner MUST submit a claim with the activity description, intended audience, planned spend, expected outcomes, and supporting evidence before incurring the expense, unless the program guide expressly allows reimbursement after the fact.
- The organization MUST evaluate each claim against eligibility, evidence sufficiency, brand and message conformance, and budget availability before approval.
- The Partnerships Lead MUST accrue approved claims in the financial period in which they are approved and reconcile disbursements against accruals.
- The organization MUST require post-activity evidence for each approved claim, including receipts, attendance, impressions, leads, or other measurable outcomes defined in the program guide.
- Disputes regarding eligibility, evidence sufficiency, or amount MUST be logged, severity-rated, and resolved within thirty business days.
- The Partnerships Lead MUST audit a sample of claims at least annually and report results to Finance and the governance body.
- The organization SHOULD publish an MDF dashboard with budget, committed, accrued, paid, and disputed amounts.
- The organization MAY require pre-funding, holdback of a portion of the claim, or clawback provisions where the program risk warrants.

## Workflow

1. **Program publication.** The Partnerships Lead publishes the MDF program guide and the annual budget per partner.
2. **Pre-claim intake.** The partner submits a claim before incurring the expense; the Partnerships Lead confirms eligibility and budget.
3. **Claim evaluation.** The claim is evaluated for eligibility, evidence sufficiency, and brand conformance.
4. **Approval.** The claim is approved by the authority defined in the program guide; approval is recorded with date and approver identity.
5. **Accrual.** The approved claim is accrued in the financial period of approval.
6. **Activity execution.** The partner executes the activity and submits post-activity evidence within the program-defined window.
7. **Post-activity review.** The Partnerships Lead reviews the post-activity evidence against the approved claim.
8. **Payment.** Payment is processed per the program guide and reconciled against accruals.
9. **Audit.** An annual audit samples claims for evidence sufficiency, conformance, and accuracy of accruals and disbursements.
10. **Dispute handling.** Disputes are logged, investigated, and resolved within the program-defined window.

## Controls

- A program guide exists for each MDF program with eligibility, evidence requirements, and approval authority.
- An accrual-to-disbursement reconciliation is performed at least monthly.
- An annual audit reports findings to Finance and the governance body.
- A dispute log captures every open dispute with owner, deadline, and status.

## Eligible and ineligible activities

The program guide defines which activities are eligible for MDF support. Eligible activities typically include demand-generation programs, jointly funded events, content production and localization, paid-media placements targeted at defined audiences, partner-enablement activities that are jointly beneficial, and translation of approved assets. Ineligible activities typically include the partner's general operating expenses, internal staff time not allocated to the approved activity, activities not aligned with the approved plan, activities inconsistent with the brand-use guide, gifts and entertainment that do not meet the gift-and-entertainment policy, and any activity that violates applicable law.

## Clawback and recovery

The organization reserves the right to claw back paid MDF where post-activity evidence is not provided within the program-defined window, where the activity is materially different from the approved claim, where the activity is later found ineligible, or where the partner is in material breach of the partnership agreement. Clawback events are documented with the basis, the amount, the notification to the partner, and the recovery method. Aging clawbacks are escalated to the governance body.
