# Partner Go-To-Market Alignment

## Purpose

This policy establishes how partner go-to-market plans are aligned with the organization's strategic priorities, segmentation, message house, and lead-handling expectations. It ensures that partner-led and joint go-to-market activity reinforces the organization's positioning, that lead routing and protection are clear, that claims are reviewed for accuracy, and that conflicts in the field are resolved on a documented basis.

## Scope

This policy applies to all partners with go-to-market activity, including joint campaigns, partner-led campaigns, joint events, partner events, paid-media activity, content syndication, account-based motions, lead-generation programs, and field engagement. It covers segmentation alignment, message approval, lead handling, conflict resolution, and the records that demonstrate alignment. It does not cover single-party marketing activity that does not reference the other.

## Requirements

- Each active go-to-market plan with a partner MUST identify target segments, value proposition, offer, channel mix, lead-handling expectations, and success metrics.
- The organization MUST provide partners with a current message house and approved claims list; partners MUST use only approved claims in customer-facing materials.
- The organization and the partner MUST define a lead-routing and lead-protection agreement that covers registration, acceptance criteria, aging, and conflict resolution.
- Co-branded campaign assets MUST be approved under the co-branding policy before release.
- The Partnerships Lead MUST review and approve the go-to-market plan at least annually and whenever a material change in segmentation, offer, or channel mix is proposed.
- Field conflicts, including duplicate account engagement or messaging inconsistency, MUST be logged, triaged within five business days, and resolved under the documented escalation path.
- The organization SHOULD publish a shared calendar of joint go-to-market activity with named owners and milestone dates.
- The organization MAY require that joint programs use only approved channels, list sources, and analytics tools to ensure data integrity.

## Workflow

1. **Plan intake.** The partner or joint planning team submits the go-to-market plan to the Partnerships Lead.
2. **Alignment review.** The Partnerships Lead reviews the plan against segmentation, message house, claims, and lead handling.
3. **Approval.** The plan is approved with any required changes documented; approval is recorded with date and approver identity.
4. **Asset production.** Joint assets are produced under the co-branding policy and claims review.
5. **Execution.** The plan is executed against the published calendar; weekly or biweekly standups track progress.
6. **Lead handling.** Leads are routed according to the lead-routing agreement; conflicts are logged and triaged.
7. **Mid-cycle review.** A mid-cycle review assesses progress against plan and adjusts scope, message, or channel as needed.
8. **Post-program review.** After program close, outcomes are reconciled against success metrics, lessons recorded, and the partner record updated.

## Controls

- A documented go-to-market plan exists for every active partner with go-to-market activity.
- Lead-routing and lead-protection rules are documented and reconcile to actual routing behavior in the CRM.
- A field-conflict log captures every reported duplicate engagement or messaging inconsistency.
- A shared calendar reflects joint activity with named owners and milestone dates.

## Brand and claims integrity

All joint go-to-market activity MUST conform to the brand-use guide and the approved claims list. Any quantitative, comparative, performance, security, or financial claim MUST be substantiated before release and revalidated before each refresh. Endorsement-style content created by or on behalf of a partner MUST comply with applicable endorsement and disclosure rules, including clear identification of the material connection between the organization and the partner and the use of truthful, non-deceptive language. The Partnerships Lead maintains a claim-substantiation file for each approved claim, including the source data, the date of substantiation, and the reviewer identity, and revalidates the file at each refresh.

## Program measurement

Each go-to-market plan defines success metrics at the program and channel level. Common metrics include pipeline created, marketing-qualified leads, sales-qualified leads, sourced and influenced revenue, deal-cycle time, and partner-satisfaction indicators. The Partnerships Lead publishes a program-measurement dashboard and reconciles reported results against source data each cycle. Where results fall materially short of plan, a corrective-action plan is documented, including root-cause analysis, owner, and deadline.
