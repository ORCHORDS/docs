# Partner Compliance Recertification

## Purpose

This policy establishes the periodic recertification of each partner's compliance posture, covering the controls, attestations, and evidence that demonstrate the partner continues to satisfy the obligations assumed at onboarding. It ensures that compliance currency is verified rather than assumed, that evidence is current, and that any remediation required is owned, scheduled, and tracked to completion.

## Scope

This policy applies to every active partner whose engagement requires compliance with internal policy, regulatory regimes, certification standards, or contractual compliance obligations. It covers evidence collection, attestation refresh, scope verification, control retesting, gap remediation, and the records that demonstrate currency. It does not replace underlying audit activities; it orchestrates the cadence and evidence handling that allow the organization to rely on partner compliance at any point in time.

## Requirements

- The Partnerships Lead MUST schedule a recertification cycle for each active partner at least annually and more frequently where risk warrants.
- Each recertification MUST collect current evidence for every applicable control family, including at minimum: security, privacy, business continuity, financial integrity, ethics, and regulatory permits.
- The partner MUST provide an attestation signed by an authorized representative, dated within the recertification window, confirming the accuracy of submitted evidence.
- Where the partner holds third-party certifications (for example, ISO/IEC 27001, SOC 2, PCI DSS), the recertification MUST verify that the certification is current, in scope, and not suspended or withdrawn.
- Any compliance gap identified during recertification MUST be logged, severity-rated, assigned an owner, given a remediation deadline, and tracked until closure.
- The Partnerships Lead MUST escalate unresolved high-severity gaps to the appropriate governance body within five business days of identification.
- The organization SHOULD maintain a compliance evidence record per partner with versioned artifacts, attestation dates, and reviewer identity.
- The organization MAY require independent attestation, audited financial statements, or specialized assessments where the partnership risk warrants.

## Workflow

1. **Cycle scheduling.** The Partnerships Lead drafts an annual recertification calendar aligned with partner contract anniversaries and risk tiering.
2. **Evidence request.** A formal evidence request is sent to the partner listing the required controls, acceptable evidence types, and submission deadline.
3. **Evidence intake.** Submitted evidence is logged, hashed for integrity, and routed to the appropriate reviewers (Security, Privacy, Legal, Finance).
4. **Review and testing.** Reviewers evaluate evidence against control statements, retest where feasible, and identify gaps.
5. **Attestation refresh.** The partner provides a signed attestation confirming current compliance status.
6. **Decision.** The Partnerships Lead issues a recertification decision: pass, conditional pass, or fail, with conditions and deadlines documented.
7. **Remediation tracking.** Conditional or failed recertifications enter the remediation tracker; progress is reviewed at least monthly.
8. **Records retention.** All evidence, attestations, and decisions are retained for the period required by the records-retention schedule.

## Controls

- An annual recertification calendar exists and is reviewed quarterly.
- Each active partner has a current recertification record with attestation, evidence inventory, and decision.
- A remediation tracker records every open gap with owner, deadline, and status.
- Suspension or withdrawal of any partner's third-party certification triggers an immediate interim recertification.

## Triggered interim recertification

A recertification cycle is initiated outside the planned cadence whenever a material event occurs. Triggers include the loss, suspension, or scope reduction of any third-party certification; a reportable security or privacy incident involving the partner; a material change in the partner's ownership, control, or corporate structure; a regulatory enforcement action; a material change in the data classes the partner handles; a change in the regulatory regime applicable to the engagement; or an escalation from the partner-incident coordination process. The interim recertification focuses on the changed scope and is documented with a separate record and decision.

## Scope of evidence

The Partnerships Lead defines the evidence required for each control family in a control-evidence matrix. The matrix identifies acceptable evidence types (for example, policy documents, procedure documentation, configuration exports, audit reports, attestation letters, training records, and screenshots), the freshness requirement, and the reviewer. The matrix is reviewed at least annually and updated when the regulatory regime, the threat landscape, or the partnership scope changes.
