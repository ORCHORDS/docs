# NIST CSF 2.0 — Govern Function

## Purpose

Provide public guidance for using the **Govern** function introduced in NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0 to establish and monitor cybersecurity risk-management direction.

## Standard context

NIST CSF 2.0 is a voluntary, outcome-based framework for managing cybersecurity risk. It is intended for organizations of any size, sector, or maturity and does not prescribe a single implementation method.

The CSF 2.0 Core contains six Functions: **Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover**. Govern is designed to inform and support the other five Functions by addressing cybersecurity risk strategy, expectations, policy, roles, oversight, and supply-chain risk management.

## Governance baseline

An organization applying the Govern function should establish documented practices for:

- understanding organizational context and stakeholder expectations;
- defining cybersecurity risk-management strategy and risk appetite or tolerance where applicable;
- assigning cybersecurity roles, responsibilities, authorities, and accountability;
- maintaining cybersecurity policy and reviewing it when conditions change;
- overseeing cybersecurity risk-management performance and significant risk decisions;
- integrating cybersecurity supply-chain risk into governance and procurement processes.

## Implementation guidance

1. Connect cybersecurity objectives to mission, business, legal, contractual, and stakeholder requirements.
2. Define who owns cybersecurity-risk decisions and who provides independent challenge or oversight where appropriate.
3. Establish escalation criteria for material cybersecurity risks and exceptions.
4. Review policy and risk assumptions after significant organizational, regulatory, technology, threat, or supplier changes.
5. Use measurable outcomes rather than treating governance as a policy-document exercise only.
6. Record dependencies between governance decisions and Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover activities.

## Evidence examples

Evidence may include approved risk-management strategy, role assignments, risk registers, policy-review records, governance meeting records, supplier-risk criteria, exception decisions, and documented escalation thresholds.

## Verification

Confirm that governance decisions are current, assigned to accountable owners, reflected in operating practices, and periodically reviewed. Do not claim CSF implementation or conformance solely because policies exist.
