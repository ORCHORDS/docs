# IFAC/IAASB ISA 315 Risk Assessment Governance

## Purpose

Govern the application of ISA 315 (revised 2019), identifying and assessing the risks of material misstatement, as adopted by the IAASB under IFAC's standard-setting structure, so that risk assessment in financial statement audit engagements is performed as the standard requires: understanding the entity and its environment, identifying risks of material misstatement through defined lenses, and linking assessed risks to further audit procedures.

## Scope

Applies to audit engagement methodology where the studio is the auditor or where the studio, as auditee, must understand what the auditor will do. Covers the understanding required, risk identification procedures, inherent risk factors and likelihood of significant risks, and the documentation ISA 315 requires. Does not cover further audit procedures (ISA 330) or the audit opinion itself.

## Workflow

1. Obtain the required understanding per ISA 315's categories: the entity, its environment, the applicable financial reporting framework, and the entity's system of internal control — including the five components of internal control and the IT environment's role.
2. Perform risk assessment procedures: inquiry, analytical procedures, observation and inspection — and, for the IT environment, walkthroughs that trace transactions through the system.
3. Identify risks of material misstatement through the standard's inherent risk factors: complexity, subjectivity, change, uncertainty, and susceptibility to management bias — recording which factors elevate each risk.
4. Assess identified risks: determine which are significant risks (higher likelihood and materiality of impact, requiring special audit consideration) and which risks are pervasive rather than assertion-specific.
5. Understand controls relevant to the audit for significant risks: evaluate design and determine implementation — noting the standard's separation between evaluating design (walkthrough or otherwise) and testing operating effectiveness (ISA 330 territory).
6. Document per the standard's requirements: the understanding obtained, the risks identified and assessed, the significant risks with their inherent risk factors, and the rationale — documentation that supports the assessment, not just records it happened.
7. Re-assess when conditions change: new systems, new revenue streams, control changes, or findings during the audit re-open the risk assessment rather than being deferred to next year.

## Controls and evidence

- Understanding documentation across ISA 315's required categories.
- Risk assessment procedure records (inquiries, analytics, walkthroughs) with sources.
- Risk register for the engagement: risks, inherent risk factors, assertions, and significant-risk classification.
- Control design evaluation records for significant risks.
- Re-assessment records triggered by engagement changes.

## Validation

- Sample identified significant risks and confirm each has recorded inherent risk factors and planned special consideration.
- Confirm walkthrough coverage of the IT environment where the system processes significant transactions.
- Confirm re-assessments occurred after material engagement changes, with updated risk conclusions.

## Failure correction

- **Significant risk without inherent risk factors documented** → complete the factor analysis; if factors cannot be articulated, re-examine the classification.
- **Walkthrough gap in the IT environment** → perform the walkthrough before relying on system-generated evidence.
- **Risk assessment stale after system change** → re-assess immediately and evaluate whether procedures already performed still address the changed risk.

## Limitations

- ISA 315 governs auditors' risk assessment; as auditee, understanding the standard prepares the studio for what auditors will examine, not a substitute for it.
- The standard is part of a suite: ISA 330 (responses), ISA 240 (fraud), ISA 540 (estimates) interlock with it.
- Jurisdictional adoption of IAASB standards varies; local adoption status determines the binding text.
