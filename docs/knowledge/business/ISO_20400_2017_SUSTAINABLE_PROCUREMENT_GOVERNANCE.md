# ISO 20400:2017 Sustainable Procurement Guidance Governance

## Purpose

Govern the application of ISO 20400:2017 (sustainable procurement — guidance) so that sustainability considerations are integrated into the studio's procurement function systematically: policy and strategy set direction, integration touches the procurement process at its defined stages, and supplier engagement raises performance rather than merely collecting questionnaires.

## Scope

Applies to the studio's procurement function and all categories of spend. Covers policy, integration into the procurement process, and supplier engagement per the standard's framework. Does not cover supplier social responsibility audit regimes (covered separately) or procurement law.

## Workflow

1. Establish the sustainable procurement policy and commitment at the level the standard directs: govern the policy from top management, define its scope across spend categories, and publish it internally and to suppliers.
2. Analyze spend and prioritize: categorize spend by sustainability risk and opportunity (environmental, social, economic) so integration effort concentrates where impact is material, not spread uniformly across low-risk categories.
3. Set sustainability criteria per prioritized category: define the environmental and social requirements, verification methods, and weightings that will apply in sourcing decisions — before the tender, not negotiated in after award.
4. Integrate at the procurement process stages per the standard: pre-solicitation (market engagement), solicitation (criteria and award weighting), evaluation (verification of claims), and contract management (obligations that survive award).
5. Engage suppliers on performance: communicate expectations, verify compliance proportionate to risk, and work with strategic suppliers on improvement plans; disengagement without engagement first wastes the leverage procurement holds.
6. Measure and review: category-level indicators (percentage of spend with sustainability criteria applied, supplier conformance rates, improvement plan progress) reported to management on a defined cadence.
7. Manage claims discipline: sustainability requirements imposed on suppliers must be verifiable, and supplier self-declarations are verified proportionate to risk; unverified claims propagate through the studio's own reporting.

## Controls and evidence

- Sustainable procurement policy with governance and scope.
- Spend analysis and category prioritization records.
- Category-level sustainability criteria definitions with verification methods.
- Procurement stage integration records: criteria in tenders, award decisions, contract clauses.
- Supplier engagement and improvement plan records.
- Category indicators reported to management.

## Validation

- Sample three tenders from prioritized categories and confirm sustainability criteria and weightings were present before award decisions.
- Confirm supplier sustainability claims in the sample have proportionate verification.
- Confirm category indicators reported on cadence with management decisions recorded.

## Failure correction

- **Criteria absent from a prioritized category's tender** → retrofit verification through contract variation where possible and fix the category criteria definition gap.
- **Unverified supplier self-declaration relied on** → verify retroactively, adjust reliance in the studio's own reporting, and set the category's verification depth.
- **Improvement plan stalled** → escalate through supplier management; a stalled plan with unchanged sourcing is engagement theater.

## Limitations

- ISO 20400 is guidance, not certifiable requirements; conformance claims are self-declared against its framework.
- Sustainable criteria interact with cost and delivery constraints; the policy governs trade-offs rather than eliminating them.
- Category prioritization quality bounds everything; stale spend analysis misdirects the whole programme.
