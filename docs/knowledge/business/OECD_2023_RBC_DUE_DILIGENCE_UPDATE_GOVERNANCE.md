# OECD 2023 Responsible Business Conduct Due Diligence Update Governance

## Purpose

In 2023 the OECD updated its due diligence materials for responsible business conduct (RBC), issuing targeted updates that align RBC due diligence with the OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises (2023 edition) and providing practical clarifications for industry-specific applications. The updates refine the six-step process, strengthen the linkage to risk-based due diligence, and adjust guidance on stakeholder engagement, leverage, and grievance mechanisms. This article governs the application of the 2023 OECD RBC due diligence updates so an organization's due diligence practice stays aligned with the current OECD position.

## Scope

The 2023 updates apply to enterprises conducting RBC due diligence under the OECD framework. Within this knowledge base, the article covers the 2023 updates to the six-step process, the strengthened emphasis on risk-based prioritization, the clarifications on stakeholder engagement, the operational guidance on leverage and disengment, and the updated treatment of grievance mechanisms. It does not replace the 2017 guidance as the base reference; it is an update to be applied together with the 2017 guidance.

## Workflow

1. Adopt the 2017 OECD guidance as the base RBC due diligence process.
2. Apply the 2023 updates to each step as relevant:
   - Embedding: clarify the linkage between RBC commitments and corporate strategy.
   - Identifying and assessing: prioritize by severity and by risk of occurrence, with documented criteria.
   - Ceasing, preventing, mitigating: use leverage where the organization has not caused or contributed; consider responsible disengment where leverage is exhausted.
   - Tracking: use the updated indicators and the increased emphasis on outcomes rather than outputs.
   - Communicating: adopt the updated stakeholder communication guidance.
   - Remediation: ensure grievance mechanisms are legitimate, accessible, predictable, equitable, transparent, rights-compatible, and a source of continuous learning (per the UNGP effectiveness criteria the OECD now references).
3. Document the changes to the organization's due diligence practice produced by the 2023 update.
4. Train the relevant functions (procurement, compliance, sustainability, legal) on the updates.
5. Review the updated practice on the next due diligence cycle.

## Controls and evidence

Update evidence includes the documented changes to the due diligence practice, the training records, the updated risk-based prioritization criteria, the leverage assessments, the updated grievance mechanism design, and the next-cycle review record. The documentation should make it possible to see what changed from the prior practice.

## Validation

Validation should confirm the 2023 updates are reflected in the policy and the process, the updates are operationalized in current due diligence activities, the grievance mechanism meets the UNGP effectiveness criteria the OECD now references, and the changes are reviewed on the next cycle. Spot checks should confirm a current due diligence case reflects the updated practice.

## Failure correction

Common failure modes: the 2023 updates are not integrated into the existing practice (corrective: map the updates to the current process and update the affected steps); the organization continues to follow pre-2023 practice without acknowledging the updates (corrective: document the gap and apply the updates at the next cycle); grievance mechanisms are nominal (corrective: assess the mechanism against the UNGP effectiveness criteria and remediate gaps); risk-based prioritization is uneven (corrective: define and document the prioritization criteria).

## Limitations

The 2023 OECD materials are updates and clarifications; they do not replace the 2017 guidance as the authoritative framework. The OECD's due diligence guidance remains non-binding and does not certify due diligence practice. Sector regulations (EU CSDDD, German Supply Chain Act, US conflict minerals regulations) impose their own obligations; the OECD updates do not satisfy them.

## Scope note

This article summarizes project-neutral business use of the OECD 2023 RBC due diligence updates. It does not assert any specific organization's due diligence conformance or claim any certification outcome.
