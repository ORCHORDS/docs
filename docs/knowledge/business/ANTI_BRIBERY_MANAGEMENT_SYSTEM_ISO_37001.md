# Anti-Bribery Management System Governance

## Scope

This article covers the umbrella governance practices that prevent bribery and corrupt dealing across an organisation. It draws on ISO 37001:2025, *Anti-bribery management systems — Requirements with guidance for use*, and on the OECD's anti-corruption instruments. The article is positioned above commercial, partnerships, and procurement activities but treats bribery risk as a governance-level concern because of its potential impact on reputation, legal exposure, and licence to operate.

## Workflow

ISO 37001 specifies the requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, reviewing, and improving an anti-bribery management system (ABMS). The recurring workflow has the following elements:

1. **Context of the organisation.** The organisation identifies the issues that may affect its anti-bribery objectives, the interested parties (including public-sector customers, regulators, and joint-venture partners), and the scope of the ABMS. ISO 37001 treats bribery risk as dependent on the geography, sector, counterparties, and transactions of the organisation.
2. **Leadership and governance.** Top management demonstrates leadership by establishing an anti-bribery policy, ensuring that the ABMS is integrated with other management system processes, and assigning responsibility and authority for anti-bribery at a senior level. The standard requires anti-bribery oversight that is independent of operational management.
3. **Risk assessment and due diligence.** The organisation identifies and assesses bribery risks in its activities, transactions, counterparties, and jurisdictions, and applies due diligence proportionate to those risks. Higher-risk relationships — agents, intermediaries, joint-venture partners, politically exposed counterparts, sponsors, and charitable contributions — receive enhanced scrutiny.
4. **Controls.** The ABMS includes financial and non-financial controls designed to prevent, detect, and respond to bribery. These include gifts and hospitality policies, segregation of duties, procurement controls, contracting controls that permit termination for corrupt conduct, whistleblower channels, and investigation procedures.
5. **Training and communication.** Anti-bribery training is provided to directors, officers, employees, and business associates on a risk-based basis, with content tailored to the bribery risks relevant to each role.
6. **Monitoring, evaluation, and continual improvement.** Internal audits, management reviews, performance evaluation, and corrective action support continual improvement of the ABMS.

## Controls and evidence

Practitioners typically rely on the following evidence:

- An anti-bribery policy approved at the highest governance level and communicated across the organisation.
- A register of bribery risks and a documented risk assessment, refreshed when the organisation enters new markets, sectors, or significant transactions.
- Due-diligence records for agents, intermediaries, joint-venture partners, and significant suppliers, including background checks and periodic re-screening.
- A gifts, hospitality, donations, and sponsorship register with thresholds and a pre-clearance or post-disclosure mechanism.
- A confidential reporting channel and an investigation procedure that protects confidentiality and prohibits retaliation.
- Records of anti-bribery training completion and competency assessments.
- Internal audit findings, management responses, and remediation evidence.

## Validation

Validation of the ABMS draws on:

- External certification to ISO 37001 by an accredited certification body, which provides assurance on the design and operating effectiveness of the ABMS.
- Internal audits conducted against the standard's requirements and the organisation's own anti-bribery procedures.
- Periodic independent reviews of high-risk areas such as agent networks and government-facing operations.
- Regulatory and enforcement actions — including deferred prosecution agreements and anti-corruption enforcement by agencies such as the U.S. Department of Justice and the UK Serious Fraud Office — which may require ongoing monitoring reports.
- Benchmarking against the OECD Anti-Bribery Convention's recommendations on enforcement and corporate compliance.

## Failure correction

When bribery incidents or control breakdowns occur, the corrective process typically includes:

- Suspension or termination of the implicated counterparties and individuals, in accordance with local employment law.
- A documented investigation conducted by qualified internal or external investigators, with findings protected by legal privilege where appropriate.
- Self-reporting where applicable to law enforcement or regulators, consistent with the organisation's legal obligations and the guidance of counsel.
- A root-cause analysis that asks why existing controls did not prevent or detect the incident and whether management override, weak tone from the top, or process gaps were involved.
- A remediation plan with owners, deadlines, and verification of completion, reported to senior management and, where appropriate, to the audit committee or the board.

## Limitations

ISO 37001 is a management-system standard and does not displace national anti-bribery laws. The OECD Anti-Bribery Convention focuses on the supply side of bribery — the bribery of foreign public officials by companies domiciled in signatory countries — and is supplemented by domestic offences and by regional instruments such as the African Union Convention on Preventing and Combating Corruption. The ABMS does not, by itself, absolve an organisation of criminal liability; prosecutors commonly evaluate corporate compliance programmes against guidance such as the U.S. DOJ *Evaluation of Corporate Compliance Programs* and the UK Bribery Act 2010 *adequate procedures* defence.
