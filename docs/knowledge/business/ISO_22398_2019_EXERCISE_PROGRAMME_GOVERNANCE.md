# ISO 22398:2019 Exercises and Testing Programme Governance

## Purpose

Govern the application of ISO 22398:2019 (security and resilience — community resilience — guidelines for conducting exercises) adapted to organizational resilience exercises, so that exercises are designed instruments with defined objectives, controlled conditions, and evaluated outcomes — producing evidence about response capability rather than unstructured rehearsals that test nothing in particular.

## Scope

Applies to the studio's exercise and test programme for continuity, incident response, crisis management, and recovery arrangements. Covers exercise design, conduct, evaluation, and programme management. Does not cover the plans being exercised (each plan's own governance covers that) or live incident response.

## Workflow

1. Derive the exercise programme from risk and capability priorities: what must be proven (evacuation, failover, decision-making under pressure, communication cascade) determines exercise types and cadence — not the calendar's convenience.
2. Select exercise types deliberately per the standard's spectrum: discussion-based (seminars, workshops, tabletops) to operations-based (drills, functional, full-scale), matched to the maturity of the capability being exercised.
3. Design each exercise with defined objectives, scenario foundations, and scope; objectives state what capability evidence the exercise will produce, in observable terms.
4. Control conditions and safety: exercise rules of engagement, safety constraints, abort criteria, and the distinction between exercise play and real response are briefed and recorded before conduct.
5. Facilitate and document conduct: timelines, injects, decisions made, and actual performance against expected performance — documentation during conduct, not reconstructed afterward.
6. Evaluate against the objectives: an after-action review comparing performance to expectations, identifying gaps, and classifying them (plan gap, training gap, resource gap) with owners and dates.
7. Close the improvement loop: corrective actions tracked to completion, and the next exercise in the programme tests whether the corrective actions held — an exercise whose findings recur is a programme failure.

## Controls and evidence

- Exercise programme plan derived from risk/capability priorities with cadence per capability.
- Exercise design documents: objectives, scenario, scope, rules of engagement, safety and abort criteria.
- Conduct records: timeline, injects, decisions, performance notes.
- After-action evaluations with gap classification, owners, and dates.
- Corrective action closure records and re-test evidence.

## Validation

- Sample two exercises and confirm each had pre-stated objectives in observable terms and an evaluation against them.
- Confirm corrective actions from the previous exercise for a capability were re-tested in a later exercise.
- Confirm every exercise had briefed safety and abort criteria before conduct.

## Failure correction

- **Exercise without stated objectives** → treat as a discussion session, not an exercise; re-design before counting it toward programme coverage.
- **Recurring finding across exercises** → escalate the corrective action's root cause; the fix is programme-level, not another action item.
- **Conduct documentation missing** → reconstruct from participant notes with gaps marked, and assign conduct documentation as a facilitator duty going forward.

## Limitations

- Exercises sample capability under simulated conditions; real events differ in stress, information quality, and stakes.
- Full-scale exercises carry operational cost and risk; the type ladder exists so capability is proven proportionately.
- Scenario realism trades against control; design deliberately on that spectrum per objective.
