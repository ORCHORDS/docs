# HIPAA Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act Governance

## Purpose

The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) Privacy, Security, and Breach Notification Rules establish requirements for covered entities and business associates that handle Protected Health Information (PHI). Governance ensures that an in-scope organization implements administrative, physical, and technical safeguards, conducts risk analyses, manages business associate agreements, and reports breaches as required.

## Current context and source status

HIPAA was enacted in 1996; the Privacy Rule was published in 2000 and the Security Rule in 2003, with subsequent modifications including the HITECH Act modifications, the Omnibus Rule of 2013, and the Right of Access and Information Blocking rules. The Office for Civil Rights (OCR) enforces HIPAA. Verify the current HIPAA Rules and OCR guidance before treating any specific requirement as a current requirement.

## Governance workflow and controls

### 1. Determine applicability

Determine applicability: covered entity (healthcare provider, health plan, healthcare clearinghouse) or business associate (entity that creates, receives, maintains, or transmits PHI on behalf of a covered entity). Apply the requirements accordingly.

### 2. Implement the Privacy Rule

Implement the Privacy Rule: notice of privacy practices, individual rights (access, amendment, accounting of disclosures, restriction, confidential communications, complaint), uses and disclosures, minimum necessary, business associate agreements.

### 3. Implement the Security Rule

Implement the Security Rule safeguards:

- administrative safeguards (security management, workforce security, information access management, training, incident procedures, contingency plan, evaluation);
- physical safeguards (facility access, workstation use, workstation security, device and media controls);
- technical safeguards (access control, audit controls, integrity, person or entity authentication, transmission security).

### 4. Conduct risk analysis

Conduct an accurate and thorough risk analysis per 45 CFR § 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A). Use the analysis to identify risks and implement risk management measures.

### 5. Manage business associate agreements

Maintain a register of business associate agreements (BAAs). Verify each BAA meets the requirements of 45 CFR § 164.504(e). Update BAAs when relationships or services change.

### 6. Implement the Breach Notification Rule

Implement the Breach Notification Rule: notification to affected individuals, HHS, and (for breaches affecting 500 or more individuals) the media. Document breach risk assessments.

### 7. Train workforce

Train workforce members on HIPAA requirements. Tailor training by role. Document training completion.

### 8. Manage individual rights requests

Manage individual rights requests within the required response times. Document the response.

## Validation and evidence

- Privacy Rule implementation evidence.
- Security Rule safeguard implementation evidence.
- Risk analysis and risk management plan.
- Business associate agreement register.
- Breach log and notification records.
- Training records.

## Failure correction

Common defects include stale risk analyses, missing BAAs, and breach notification that exceeds the 60-day deadline. Corrective actions include a risk analysis refresh, a BAA register audit, and a breach notification procedure review.

## Limitations

- HIPAA applies to specific entities; other healthcare data may be covered by other regulations.
- The HIPAA Rules are minimum standards; sectoral regulators may impose more.
- The Privacy Rule includes de-identification standards; verification of de-identification requires care.
- Individual rights requests may interact with state laws that are more protective.
