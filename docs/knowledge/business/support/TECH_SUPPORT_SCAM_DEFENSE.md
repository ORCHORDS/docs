# Tech-Support Scam Defense

## Purpose

Support teams and employees should be prepared for unsolicited calls, emails, and pop-ups that falsely claim a device or account has a technical problem and then seek payment, credentials, or remote access.

## Defensive pattern

- Treat unexpected technical-support contact as untrusted, even when caller ID or branding looks familiar.
- Do not click links, call numbers, or install software supplied by an unsolicited message or pop-up.
- Never disclose passwords or authentication codes to an unexpected support contact.
- Do not grant remote access to someone who initiated the contact unexpectedly.
- Verify a vendor through a known official channel before taking action.
- If interaction occurred, disconnect unauthorized remote access, preserve evidence, rotate affected credentials, and escalate to security.
- Train staff with realistic examples because these scams rely heavily on urgency and authority cues.

## Customer-support use

When customers report this scam, support should distinguish the legitimate company's official channels from the impersonator and provide concrete containment steps rather than merely saying the message was fake.
