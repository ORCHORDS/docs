# Customer Contact Centre Service Management

## Purpose

ISO 18295-1:2017 specifies service requirements for customer contact centres and provides a framework intended to help centres consistently meet customer and client needs across interaction channels. ISO lists the 2017 edition as still published, but at stage 90.92 (to be revised), with a second edition currently under development as ISO/AWI 18295-1.

## Use the current published edition carefully

A support organization can use the published standard as a reference point for structuring contact-centre service management while tracking the revision separately. Do not describe the draft second edition as a published requirement or assume its eventual content will remain unchanged.

The current ISO description says the 2017 edition applies to in-house and outsourced customer contact centres of all sizes and across sectors, covering inbound and outbound interactions and multiple channels. It also specifies performance metrics where required.

## Operational design considerations

A practical contact-centre management model should make responsibilities and service expectations explicit. Useful areas to structure include:

1. **Customer access and interaction channels** — define which channels are supported and how customers move between them.
2. **Service objectives** — document the intended level and consistency of service instead of relying on informal expectations.
3. **Competence and enablement** — give personnel the information, training, authority, and escalation routes needed to handle contacts effectively.
4. **Performance measurement** — choose indicators that reflect customer outcomes and service quality rather than optimizing a single speed metric in isolation.
5. **Escalation and handoff** — define when an interaction needs specialist, supervisory, complaint, security, or external-dispute handling.
6. **Outsourced service governance** — when a third party operates a contact centre, define how service requirements, reporting, review, and accountability are maintained.
7. **Continuous improvement** — use complaints, contact reasons, repeat-contact patterns, and customer feedback to identify systemic service problems.

## Revision watch

ISO approved work on edition 2 in April 2026. The replacement project is currently an Approved Work Item, not a published International Standard. Organizations referencing ISO 18295-1 should therefore record the edition used and re-check ISO's catalogue before treating any revision as final.
