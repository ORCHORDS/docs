# Customer-Service Impersonation Defense

## Purpose

Attackers can impersonate a company's support channel to trick customers into sending money, revealing credentials or personal information, or granting remote access. Support operations should make official contact paths easy to verify and avoid practices that train customers to trust risky requests.

## Publish authoritative contact paths

- Keep official support phone numbers, email addresses, domains, and help-center links current on the company's own website and product surfaces.
- Use consistent sender domains and branding for outbound support messages.
- Avoid asking customers to locate sensitive support channels through ads or unverified search results when an authenticated in-product route is available.
- Provide a simple way for customers to report suspected impersonation.

## Safe support behavior

Support agents should not ask customers to:

- send passwords, recovery codes, or full authentication secrets;
- transfer money to "protect" an account;
- pay through gift cards, cryptocurrency, or unusual payment methods to resolve an ordinary support issue;
- install remote-access software without a clearly documented, authenticated support process; or
- bypass normal account-security controls because a request is urgent.

## Verification workflow

1. Identify the customer through the approved support and authentication process.
2. If the conversation began through an unexpected message, give the customer a way to independently re-enter through an official channel.
3. Treat urgent payment, credential, or remote-access requests as high-risk signals.
4. Escalate suspected impersonation to the appropriate fraud, security, or abuse function.
5. Preserve evidence such as the impersonating domain, phone number, message headers, screenshots, timestamps, and reported payment destination where lawful and appropriate.
6. Warn affected customers through official channels when a material impersonation campaign is confirmed.

## Brand-protection controls

Where appropriate, use email-authentication controls, monitor for lookalike domains and fake support listings, and coordinate takedown or abuse reports with registrars, platforms, search providers, and relevant authorities. Technical controls complement, but do not replace, clear customer-facing verification guidance.
