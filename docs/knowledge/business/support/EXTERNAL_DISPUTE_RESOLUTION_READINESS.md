# External Dispute Resolution Readiness

## Purpose

Some customer complaints cannot be resolved through an organization's internal complaints process. A support organization should be prepared to identify when external dispute resolution is appropriate and to preserve the information needed to participate fairly and consistently.

## Standards context

ISO 10003:2018 provides guidelines for dispute resolution external to organizations when complaints have not been resolved internally. ISO states that the standard applies to disputes arising from domestic or cross-border business activities, including electronic commerce, and addresses participation decisions, provider selection, fairness, accessibility, transparency, monitoring, and improvement.

## Readiness controls

A reusable readiness process should define:

1. which unresolved complaints may be eligible for external dispute resolution;
2. who is authorized to approve participation or referral;
3. what records must be preserved from the internal complaints process;
4. how relevant facts, communications, and proposed remedies are assembled without altering the record;
5. how customers are given clear information about applicable escalation routes;
6. how deadlines and provider requirements are tracked;
7. how confidential or personal information is shared only when necessary and permitted; and
8. how outcomes are reviewed for recurring service or process problems.

## Separation from internal complaint handling

External dispute resolution should not replace a functioning internal complaints process. It is an escalation path for disputes that remain unresolved after reasonable internal handling or where another applicable process requires external referral.

Available dispute-resolution mechanisms, mandatory schemes, deadlines, and legal consequences differ by jurisdiction and sector. This article provides general operational guidance and is not legal advice.
