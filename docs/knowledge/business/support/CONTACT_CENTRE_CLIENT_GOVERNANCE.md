# Contact Centre Client Governance

## Purpose

ISO 18295-2:2017 specifies requirements for organizations that use the services of customer contact centres. ISO describes the standard as applicable to clients using in-house or outsourced contact centres of all sizes and across multiple voice and non-voice channels.

ISO currently lists the 2017 first edition as **Published**, but at stage 90.92, meaning the International Standard is to be revised. ISO/AWI 18295-2, edition 2, is under development at stage 10.99 and is intended to replace the 2017 edition. The draft project should therefore be tracked, but not treated as a published requirement.

## Client-side governance responsibilities

A business using a customer contact centre should govern the service deliberately rather than treating the centre as an isolated operational function. Useful areas include:

1. **Service requirements** — define the customer outcomes, supported channels, service scope, operating assumptions, and escalation expectations the contact centre is expected to meet.
2. **Roles and accountability** — make clear which responsibilities belong to the client organization and which belong to the contact-centre operator.
3. **Information quality** — provide current product, policy, service, and escalation information so contact-centre personnel can give customers accurate guidance.
4. **Performance management** — use agreed measures that reflect service quality and customer outcomes, not only speed or volume.
5. **Change management** — communicate changes in products, policies, campaigns, incidents, operating hours, or customer obligations before they create inconsistent support responses.
6. **Complaint and escalation integration** — connect contact-centre handling to complaint-management, specialist, security, legal, and external-dispute processes where relevant.
7. **Outsourced-provider oversight** — when a third party operates the centre, maintain governance of service expectations, reporting, issue handling, customer protections, and continuous improvement.
8. **Review and improvement** — use contact reasons, repeat contacts, escalations, complaints, customer feedback, and service failures to identify systemic improvements.

## Relationship to ISO 18295-1

ISO 18295-1:2017 addresses requirements for the customer contact centre itself, while ISO 18295-2:2017 addresses the organizations using those services. The two perspectives are complementary: contact-centre capability does not remove the client's responsibility to define, govern, and maintain appropriate arrangements.

## Revision watch

ISO approved the edition 2 project on April 17, 2026. As of the current ISO catalogue, ISO/AWI 18295-2 remains an Approved Work Item at stage 10.99. Do not describe the second edition as published until ISO advances it through the publication process.
