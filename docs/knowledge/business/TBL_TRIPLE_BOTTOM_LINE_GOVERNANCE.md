# Triple Bottom Line Sustainability Governance

## Purpose

The Triple Bottom Line (TBL) concept holds that organizations should measure success not only by financial performance but also by social and environmental performance. Governance ensures that the enterprise applies TBL thinking to strategy, that reporting covers the three dimensions, and that trade-offs among the dimensions are explicit and documented.

## Current context and source status

The Triple Bottom Line concept was articulated by John Elkington in 1994 and revisited in his 2018 reflection calling for a reset. The concept is widely cited but is not a formal reporting standard. TBL thinking overlaps with, but is distinct from, formal sustainability reporting frameworks (GRI, IFRS S1/S2, ESRS). Verify the current application of TBL in the enterprise's reporting before treating any specific measure as a current requirement.

## Governance workflow and controls

### 1. Define the three dimensions

Apply the three TBL dimensions:

- economic (or prosperity): financial value creation;
- social (or people): social impacts on communities, workers, and customers;
- environmental (or planet): environmental impacts on ecosystems, climate, and resources.

### 2. Apply TBL to strategy

Apply TBL thinking to strategy development. Identify how the strategy affects each dimension. Identify trade-offs.

### 3. Develop TBL metrics

Develop metrics for each dimension. Document the metric definition, data source, and calculation. Where possible, align metrics with formal sustainability reporting standards.

### 4. Apply TBL to capital allocation

Apply TBL thinking to capital allocation. Include externalities in investment decisions where measurable.

### 5. Report TBL performance

Report TBL performance in sustainability reports, integrated reports, or annual reports. Use the enterprise's chosen reporting framework.

### 6. Apply assurance

Apply internal or external assurance to TBL metrics where material. Disclose assurance scope.

### 7. Avoid greenwashing and social-washing

Avoid unsubstantiated claims about social or environmental performance. Apply verification to claims. Align marketing claims with disclosed performance.

## Validation and evidence

- TBL strategy documentation.
- TBL metrics with definitions and data sources.
- TBL performance report.
- Marketing-claim verification process.

## Failure correction

Common defects include TBL metrics that are not measurable, TBL reporting that does not match strategy, and marketing claims that diverge from disclosed performance. Corrective actions include a metric measurability review, a strategy-metric alignment check, and a marketing-disclosure consistency review.

## Limitations

- TBL is a concept, not a standard; application varies.
- Some TBL metrics (especially social) are difficult to measure consistently.
- Trade-offs among dimensions are not always resolvable; TBL does not prescribe weighting.
- TBL does not replace formal sustainability reporting frameworks.
