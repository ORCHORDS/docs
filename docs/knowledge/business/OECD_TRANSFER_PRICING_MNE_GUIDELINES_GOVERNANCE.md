# OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines Governance

## Purpose

Govern the application of the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises and Tax Administrations so that the studio's intercompany transactions with related parties are priced per the arm's length principle, documented contemporaneously, and defended with functional analysis — rather than priced for convenience and documented when challenged.

## Scope

Applies to every cross-border transaction between the studio and its related parties (service fees, royalties, intercompany financing, product transfers). Covers functional analysis, transfer pricing method selection, documentation, and country-by-country reporting linkage. Does not constitute tax advice; jurisdictional rules apply alongside.

## Workflow

1. Inventory intercompany transactions: what crosses between related entities — services, licenses, financing, goods — with amounts, flows, and the entities involved; un-inventoried flows are the primary audit exposure.
2. Perform functional analysis per the guidelines: functions performed, assets used, and risks assumed by each entity — the analysis drives profit allocation, and functions assumed without substance behind them fail scrutiny.
3. Select the most appropriate transfer pricing method per the guidelines' hierarchy of methods and the transaction's character: comparable uncontrolled price, resale price, cost plus, transactional net margin, or transactional profit split — documenting why the selected method fits.
4. Benchmark where comparables are used: the comparability analysis records search criteria, screened comparables, and adjustments; benchmarking performed after the fact to justify a preset price is visible to tax administrations.
5. Document contemporaneously: master file and local file per the OECD framework (BEPS Action 13), prepared for the fiscal year as transactions occur and finalized with the return, not years later.
6. File country-by-country reporting where thresholds are met: revenue, profit, taxes paid, and indicators of economic activity per jurisdiction, exchanged between tax administrations automatically.
7. Monitor change triggers: business restructuring, new intercompany flows, or material profitability shifts re-open method selection and documentation.

## Controls and evidence

- Intercompany transaction inventory with flows and amounts per period.
- Functional analysis documentation per entity and transaction type.
- Transfer pricing method selection records with rationale.
- Benchmarking studies with search criteria and comparables documentation.
- Master file and local file completion records tied to fiscal periods.
- Country-by-country report filing confirmations where thresholds met.

## Validation

- Confirm the transaction inventory matches the entities' actual books (no unrecorded flows).
- Sample one intercompany transaction type and confirm the method, functional analysis, and benchmarking trail is current for the period.
- Confirm documentation was finalized in the return period, not after an inquiry.

## Failure correction

- **Un-inventoried intercompany flow discovered** → price it per a defensible method, document retroactively with the gap disclosed where required, and fix the inventory process.
- **Functional analysis without substance** → align substance and functions or reallocate profit consistent with actual functions; documentation cannot outrun reality.
- **Benchmarking stale** → refresh the study for the current period and assess whether conclusions change.

## Limitations

- The guidelines are not law; jurisdictions legislate their own transfer pricing rules, sometimes diverging (e.g., local deviations on method hierarchy).
- Controversy risk persists under correct application — dispute resolution mechanisms (MAP, arbitration) exist for double taxation.
- Permanent establishment and profit attribution questions sit adjacent to transfer pricing and need separate analysis.
