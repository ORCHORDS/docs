# FCPA and UK Bribery Act Anti-Corruption Governance

## Purpose

The U.S. Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA) and the UK Bribery Act 2010 are two of the most prominent anti-corruption regimes with extraterritorial reach. Governance ensures that an enterprise operating internationally has an anti-corruption program that meets both regimes' requirements, applies adequate-provisions requirements to third parties, and maintains accurate books and records.

## Current context and source status

The FCPA was enacted in 1977 with amendments since. The UK Bribery Act 2010 came into force in 2011. Both regimes are actively enforced. The U.S. Department of Justice and the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission jointly enforce the FCPA. The UK Serious Fraud Office and other UK agencies enforce the Bribery Act. Verify current enforcement guidance and any rule changes before treating any specific compliance step as a current requirement.

## Governance workflow and controls

### 1. Apply anti-corruption policy

Adopt a written anti-corruption policy that prohibits bribery of public officials (FCPA) and any person (UK Bribery Act). The policy should be communicated internally and to third parties.

### 2. Conduct risk-based due diligence on third parties

Apply risk-based due diligence on third parties (agents, consultants, joint venture partners, distributors, suppliers, customers). The level of due diligence should correspond to the risk profile.

### 3. Maintain accurate books and records

Maintain a system of internal accounting controls that provides reasonable assurance that transactions are executed and assets are recorded in accordance with management authorization. Record all transactions accurately.

### 4. Avoid facilitation payments

Adopt a strict prohibition on facilitation payments, even where local law permits them. Document the prohibition in the policy. Report any pressure to make facilitation payments.

### 5. Implement gifts and hospitality controls

Maintain a gifts and hospitality register. Apply thresholds and approval requirements. Review the register.

### 6. Train employees

Train employees on anti-corruption requirements. Tailor training by role (high-risk roles receive more intensive training).

### 7. Investigate and discipline

Investigate allegations of corruption. Apply discipline consistently. Report to authorities where required.

### 8. Audit and monitor

Audit anti-corruption controls. Monitor red flags (unusual payment patterns, requests for cash payments, requests to use third parties without explanation).

## Validation and evidence

- Anti-corruption policy.
- Third-party due diligence records.
- Books and records audit.
- Gifts and hospitality register.
- Training records.
- Investigation records.

## Failure correction

Common defects include third-party due diligence without follow-up, gifts and hospitality registers that are incomplete, and discipline that is not consistent. Corrective actions include a third-party due diligence effectiveness review, a register completeness audit, and a discipline consistency check.

## Limitations

- Anti-corruption laws have extraterritorial reach; small companies can be in scope.
- Local law may permit conduct that the FCPA or Bribery Act prohibits; follow the stricter law.
- Enforcement is fact-intensive; legal review is essential for specific cases.
- Anti-corruption controls should be integrated with broader compliance and ethics programs.
