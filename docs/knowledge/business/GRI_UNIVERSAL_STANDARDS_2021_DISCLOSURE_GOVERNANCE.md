# GRI Universal Standards 2021 Disclosure Governance

## Purpose

Govern the application of the GRI Universal Standards 2021 (GRI 1: Foundation 2021, GRI 2: General Disclosures 2021, GRI 3: Material Topics 2021) so that sustainability reporting follows the GRI system correctly: reporting principles applied, required general disclosures complete, material topics determined by defined process, and claims of "in accordance with GRI" meet the standard's actual conditions.

## Scope

Applies to the studio's sustainability reporting prepared under the GRI Standards. Covers reporting principles, general disclosures, materiality determination, and the in-accordance claims. Does not cover topic-specific GRI standards selection beyond the process (each topic standard's disclosure detail) or financial sustainability regulation.

## Workflow

1. Apply the reporting principles per GRI 1: accuracy, balance, clarity, comparability, completeness, sustainability context, timeliness, and verifiability — recording how each principle shaped the report's preparation, not just naming them.
2. Report the GRI 2 general disclosures completely: organizational details, entities in the report, restatement policy, contact point, reporting period, publication frequency, and the governance structure disclosures.
3. Determine material topics per GRI 3's process: identify topics from the GRI topic standards and the studio's context, assess significance by impact on the economy, environment, and people — with the process, evidence, and stakeholder input documented.
4. List material topics in the report with the process used to determine them; the list drives which topic-specific standards are reported.
5. Publish the GRI content index as GRI 1 requires, mapping disclosures to the locations in the published materials.
6. Make the in-accordance claim accurately: "in accordance with" requires all required disclosures, all material topics covered, and the content index published; claims that omit required elements are "with reference to," not "in accordance" — over-claiming is the primary failure mode.
7. Manage changes deliberately: restatements of prior-period information follow the stated restatement policy with reasons disclosed; changed material topics are disclosed as changes.

## Controls and evidence

- Reporting principles application notes per report cycle.
- GRI 2 general disclosure records for each disclosure item.
- Materiality determination file: topic identification sources, significance assessment, stakeholder input, and final list with rationale.
- GRI content index published with the report.
- In-accordance claim checklist completed before publication.
- Restatement records with reasons.

## Validation

- Walk the GRI 2 disclosure list against the report and confirm zero omissions for the claimed conformance level.
- Confirm the materiality file shows evidence for each listed topic's significance, not assertion only.
- Confirm the published claim level matches what the checklist supports.

## Failure correction

- **Required disclosure omitted under an "in accordance" claim** → correct the claim or complete the disclosure before next publication, and re-review the checklist gate.
- **Material topic without determination evidence** → re-run the assessment for that topic with documented input, or drop it with explanation.
- **Restatement made without policy disclosure** → disclose the restatement policy and the specific restatement reasons retroactively.

## Limitations

- GRI reporting is voluntary in most jurisdictions; regulatory sustainability regimes (e.g., ESRS under CSRD) impose different requirements and may need parallel preparation.
- Materiality under GRI centers on impacts, not only enterprise-value effects; financial-materiality frameworks differ.
- The topic standards library is extensive; completeness depends on disciplined content index maintenance.
