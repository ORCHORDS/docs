# Price Comparison and Sale Claims

## Purpose

Claims such as "sale," "was/now," percentage-off, or comparisons with another seller can mislead customers when the reference price is not genuine or the comparison is not meaningful.

## Review pattern

- Identify exactly what reference price or comparison the ad communicates.
- Confirm the former price, competitor price, or market comparison is real and supportable.
- Do not manufacture a temporary high price merely to advertise a later discount.
- State material qualifications clearly when products, quantities, locations, or time periods differ.
- Keep promotional pricing and checkout pricing synchronized.
- Preserve evidence for the reference price and campaign period.
- Re-check evergreen advertisements when prices or market conditions change.

## Operational check

Before publication, a reviewer should be able to answer: compared with what, during what period, for which product, and based on what evidence?

## Boundary

Federal truth-in-advertising principles apply alongside state and local pricing rules, which may impose additional requirements.
