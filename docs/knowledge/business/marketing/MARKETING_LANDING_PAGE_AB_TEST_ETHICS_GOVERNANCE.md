# Landing Page A/B Test Ethics Governance

## Scope

This control governs the design, exposure, measurement, and shutdown of A/B and multivariate tests on marketing landing pages. It applies to tests that vary creative, headline, body copy, offer, call-to-action wording, pricing presentation, form fields, layout, image selection, video, social proof placement, urgency language, and trust signals. It applies to tests on direct-response landing pages, paid search post-click pages, social destination pages, email click-through pages, partner referral pages, affiliate bridge pages, lifecycle and re-engagement pages, and any page variant generated by an automated optimization tool.

The governing baseline is the FTC's Native Advertising Guides, which describe how distinguishing sponsored content from surrounding editorial material is an important consumer-protection practice, and which the FTC has used to evaluate whether consumers can reasonably recognize the source and nature of what they are viewing on a marketing surface. The control treats an A/B variant that introduces or conceals paid, sponsored, or promotional content as a high-risk variant that requires elevated review.

## Workflow or implementation guidance

A landing page experiment proceeds through five phases.

1. Plan the test. The owner records the hypothesis, the audience, the traffic source, the variant count, the dependent variable, the statistical decision rule, the maximum runtime, the variant that serves as control, the data that will be measured, and the data that will be excluded (for example, internal employee traffic, known competitor traffic, or fraud-flagged sessions).
2. Review the variant content. Each variant is checked for sponsored-content clarity, headline substance, claim substantiation, and disclosure presence and prominence. Variants that change the disclosure language, the sponsored-content label, the source attribution, or the visual treatment of an "ad" or "Sponsored" marker are not released without a documented sponsor-content review.
3. Configure the test. Variant assignment is randomized at the variant level (not at the disclosure level). The control is treated as a baseline that includes the established disclosures and source labels. Variants that suppress a disclosure or rewrite a sponsored-content marker are blocked by editorial review.
4. Run, monitor, and stop. Monitoring confirms that variant exposure matches the experimental design, that no variant inadvertently changes disclosure language for a paid placement, and that traffic quality is acceptable. Stop rules reflect the a priori statistical decision criterion and an ethics stop rule if a variant produces a substantiated complaint pattern or a substantiation defect.
5. Document results and disposition. The closing record identifies the winning variant, the quality evidence collected, the disclosure language present in each variant, any segment differences observed, and the final deployed version.

## Controls

The most important controls for this governance relate to disclosure preservation, claim substantiation, and treatment of paid placements.

- A variant may not remove, weaken, or relocate a disclosure required by the FTC native advertising framing, including the "Ad" or "Sponsored" label or equivalent content-distinction marker.
- A variant may not introduce comparative, performance, or efficacy claims that the campaign team has not independently substantiated for that variant.
- A variant that differs only in disclosure design must include the disclosure in every variant, with prominence and clarity evaluated per variant.
- An ethical stop rule, separate from the statistical stop rule, halts the test if a substantiated complaint is filed, if a regulator issues a relevant public statement that affects the test, if any variant makes a claim the team cannot substantiate, or if a variant misrepresents the relationship between advertiser and content.
- Traffic excluded from analysis is documented, and any per-segment analysis is named in the test plan rather than discovered post hoc.
- Sample quality controls (fraud filtering, bot filtering, geo restrictions where appropriate, paid vs organic splits) are recorded, and the experiment is not used as the sole source of truth when those controls cannot be fully observed.
- Variant exposure data is preserved at the user level only where the team's privacy posture, consent capture, and retention rules permit; otherwise, only aggregated exposure counts and the experimental schedule are retained.

## Validation evidence

Evidence must demonstrate that disclosure markers, source attribution, and claim substantiation remained intact across variants and that the experimental design was not distorted by excluded traffic.

- Pre-launch evidence: each variant's HTML or rendered markup, a screenshot or rendered snapshot of each variant, a description of the disclosure markers present in each variant, the source attribution present in each variant, and the editorial reviewer who approved the variant.
- During-test evidence: daily traffic per variant, the number of unique visitors per variant, the statistical decision metric per variant, the fraud or bot filtering applied, and any stop-rule invocations.
- Post-test evidence: final hypothesis result, the deployed variant and its disclosures, the variants retired, and any complaints received during the test window linked to the variant exposure.

## Failure modes and correction

Frequent failures include running variants that suppress a paid-content label on a sponsored placement, accepting test results from traffic that contains paid or incentivized visits outside the intended sample, treating statistical significance as the only stopping rule, optimizing a variant for maximum conversion without confirming that the variant's claims are substantiated, extending tests indefinitely, and re-running a blocked variant without resolving the disclosure defect.

Correction begins with pausing exposure to any variant that has been identified as defecting. The original control variant is restored or a corrected variant is published. Analytics is reviewed for the period the defecting variant ran, with attention to any segment that received the variant disproportionately. The corrected variant is reviewed through the same controls as a new test. A root-cause entry identifies whether the failure was a planning defect, a copy defect, a tooling defect, or a review bypass, and updates the gating control accordingly. When the defect raises a consumer-protection concern, the team records the fact and refers the matter to legal counsel rather than attempting to adjudicate the question itself.

## Limitations

This control does not determine whether a particular landing page is regulated by the FTC under the FTC Act or by the Native Advertising Guides as applied, nor does it determine whether a state attorney general or another foreign regulator would view a particular variant as deceptive. It does not replace substantive disclosure design, claim substantiation, accessibility, or privacy review. It does not address every research method: holdout testing, bandit optimization, uplift modeling, or natural-traffic experiments carry their own design integrity requirements that should be incorporated alongside this control.
