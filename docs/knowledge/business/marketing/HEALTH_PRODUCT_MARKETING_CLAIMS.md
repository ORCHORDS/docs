# Health-Product Marketing Claims

## Purpose

Health-related marketing carries a higher substantiation burden because consumers may rely on claims about safety, efficacy, diagnosis, treatment, or prevention when making consequential decisions. The FTC's Health Products Compliance Guidance applies truth-in-advertising principles to foods, supplements, OTC drugs, health devices, diagnostic tests, health apps, and other health-related products.

## Review the net impression

Marketing review should identify both express and reasonably implied claims. Evaluate the advertisement as a whole, including text, product names, images, charts, endorsements, demonstrations, qualifiers, and surrounding context. A marketer is responsible for reasonable interpretations that the material communicates, not only for the words it intended to emphasize.

## Evidence before publication

1. List every objective health or safety claim the material communicates.
2. Identify the evidence needed for each claim before publication.
3. Confirm that the evidence is scientifically sound, relevant to the specific product, population, outcome, dosage or use condition represented, and consistent with the body of evidence.
4. Record important limitations, uncertainty, and contradictory evidence.
5. Reject claims that overstate what the evidence supports.
6. Re-review recurring claims when the product, evidence base, or relevant regulatory guidance changes.

## Disclosures and qualifications

A disclosure should prevent deception rather than contradict the headline claim. Where qualification is needed, make it clear, prominent, proximate, and understandable in the same context in which the claim is presented. Do not rely on a hyperlink or fine print to cure a misleading overall impression.

## Endorsements and testimonials

Testimonials, influencers, experts, and consumer experiences do not replace substantiation for objective product claims. Marketing teams should separately verify the underlying claim and ensure any material connection or atypical-result qualification is handled under the applicable endorsement rules.

## Governance record

For material health claims, retain:

- the exact approved claim and creative version;
- supporting studies or evidence references;
- review date and accountable reviewer;
- known limitations and required disclosures;
- approval or rejection decision; and
- the trigger for re-review.

This article summarizes reusable FTC-oriented marketing controls. Product-specific FDA requirements and non-U.S. laws may impose additional or different obligations.
