# Leather and Imitation-Leather Marketing Claims

## Purpose

The U.S. Federal Trade Commission's Guides for Select Leather and Imitation Leather Products address deceptive claims about the composition and characteristics of products such as luggage, handbags, wallets, footwear, and belts.

The Guides are advisory guidance under the FTC Act rather than a certification programme. They are currently listed by the FTC as the Leather Guides in 16 CFR Part 24.

## Composition claims

Marketing should accurately distinguish genuine leather from simulated, imitation, coated, bonded, or other non-leather materials when a product's appearance could otherwise create a misleading impression.

A reusable claim-review process should:

1. verify the material composition of the part being described;
2. make non-leather content clear when the product appears to be leather;
3. avoid implying that a surface finish, backing, trim, or component is leather when it is not;
4. ensure images, product titles, marketplace attributes, and comparison tables do not contradict the material disclosure; and
5. revalidate claims when suppliers or construction change.

## Performance and treatment claims

The Leather Guides also address misrepresentations about finish, processing, serviceability, and durability. Terms such as `waterproof`, `dustproof`, `warpproof`, `scuffproof`, `scratchproof`, `scuff resistant`, or `scratch resistant` should be used only when the product and evidence support the represented level of performance.

## Evidence

Retain appropriate specifications, supplier declarations, test evidence, bills of materials, or other records that support the composition and performance claims actually made. A brand name or supplier description should not be treated as proof when the representation could materially affect a buyer's decision.

## Scope note

The Leather Guides cover selected product categories and do not replace textile, wool, fur, country-of-origin, customs, safety, chemical, or state consumer-protection requirements.
