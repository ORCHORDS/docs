# Textile Fiber Advertising Disclosures

## Purpose

FTC Textile and Wool Rules require truthful fiber-content and origin information for covered textile and wool products. Written advertising, including internet advertising, can trigger disclosure duties when it mentions or implies the presence of a fiber.

## Advertising controls

When a written advertisement makes a fiber claim:

1. Use fiber names that match the product's required labeling information.
2. List fiber names in the required order when the ad must disclose content.
3. If a fiber trademark is used, place the generic fiber name immediately next to the trademark where required and make the disclosure legible and conspicuous.
4. Do not use a trademark, product name, image, or headline that implies a materially different fiber composition than the product actually has.
5. Keep catalog and internet origin statements consistent with the product's actual country-of-origin labeling.
6. Revalidate listings when suppliers, manufacturing locations, fiber composition, or labeling records change.

FTC guidance explains that written advertising includes internet advertising. When a fiber trademark appears in an ad, fiber-content information must be disclosed at least once; percentages generally do not have to be repeated in the advertisement even though they are required on covered product labels.

## Country-of-origin advertising

Mail-order and internet descriptions for covered textile products must disclose whether the item is made in the United States, imported, or both, using language that accurately reflects the product's origin. Unqualified U.S.-origin claims should not be used for items made with imported materials when qualification is required.

## Evidence and records

Marketing data should be traceable to the same source information used for labels and supply-chain records. Preserve current product specifications, origin information, responsible-party identity, and change history so advertising claims can be corrected when upstream facts change.

## Scope note

This article summarizes general U.S. FTC advertising and labeling concepts. Customs, Made in USA requirements, fur rules, care-label requirements, and state law may impose additional obligations.
