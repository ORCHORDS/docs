# FCC Sponsored Political Ad Identification

## Scope

This control applies to political advertising and sponsored political matter distributed through FCC-regulated broadcast, cable, satellite, or other covered programming contexts where sponsorship identification, political file, or related public inspection file obligations may be implicated. It is intended for station operations, ad traffic teams, political sales, legal review, public-file administrators, agencies, and campaign operations teams that prepare, accept, schedule, invoice, archive, or review sponsored political ad material.

The control is deliberately narrow. It does not claim that every online political ad is governed by FCC sponsorship identification rules, and it does not replace Federal Election Commission, state election, platform, or EU political advertising regimes. It focuses on the operational controls needed when FCC sponsorship identification and political-file workflows are relevant to the channel and entity.

Primary sources include FCC political programming guidance at [FCC: Political Programming](https://www.fcc.gov/media/policy/political-programming), FCC public inspection file materials at [FCC Public Inspection Files](https://publicfiles.fcc.gov/), and the sponsorship identification rule text in [47 CFR Section 73.1212](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-73.1212). FCC orders and public notices may further affect interpretation, so controls should be tied to dated legal review.

## Requirements Versus Recommendations

Internal requirements are mandatory gates for covered placements: identify the sponsor, classify the ad as candidate, issue, ballot, federal, state, local, or other political matter, capture order and schedule information, preserve required public-file data where applicable, verify that sponsorship identification content is present in the creative or associated announcement workflow, and document acceptance or rejection decisions. These are internal controls, not a blanket representation of legal compliance.

Recommendations include automated reconciliation of traffic logs to public-file records, periodic copy review, sampling of aired content, second-person review of high-volume political buys, and issue taxonomy normalization. These recommendations reduce operational error but should not be described as FCC requirements unless tied to a specific rule, order, or licensee obligation.

## Workflow

The workflow begins at ad intake. Sales or traffic staff must capture the purchaser, sponsor, agency, treasurer or responsible contact where applicable, candidate or issue reference, election date, office sought, jurisdiction, and whether the request involves legally qualified candidates, national legislative issues, ballot measures, or controversial issues of public importance. The buyer’s invoice name alone is not enough when the sponsor is a different person or entity.

Classification then determines the review path. Candidate uses, issue ads, and noncandidate political matter can have different public-file data requirements and timing expectations. The control should force a documented classification before trafficking. When classification is uncertain, the ad should be routed to the legal decision owner rather than published under a generic “political” label.

Creative review checks the sponsorship identification. The review should inspect the script, audio, video slate, lower third, end card, caption file, and any embedded graphic or announcement field used by the station. The sponsor name should be consistent with the order record unless legal review approves a difference. When an ad is supplied in multiple lengths or languages, each version must be reviewed. A thirty-second English spot approval does not automatically approve a fifteen-second Spanish cutdown.

Public-file preparation should happen in parallel with trafficking, not after the flight ends. Required documents, fields, or uploads should be prepared as close to acceptance as the operating procedure requires. The public-file owner should reconcile order details, rates or classes where applicable, dates and times requested, dates and times aired if maintained in the workflow, sponsor identity, and issue/candidate classification.

Post-air reconciliation should compare traffic logs, makegoods, cancellations, copy rotations, and public-file entries. Any discrepancy should receive a correction record. Political advertising often moves quickly near election dates; therefore, the control should prioritize fast exception handling over after-the-fact cleanup.

## Concrete Fields And Controls

Minimum intake fields are: order ID, advertiser, sponsor, agency, buyer contact, responsible organization, candidate name if applicable, office, election date, jurisdiction, issue or ballot measure, federal/state/local classification, ad title, creative ID, length, language, requested flight dates, requested dayparts, station or system, rate class, invoice reference, public-file status, legal review status, sponsorship ID text, approval timestamp, and archive location.

Controls include sponsor-name normalization, duplicate-order detection, mandatory political classification, creative lock after approval, public-file checklist completion, and traffic-to-file reconciliation. Sponsor-name normalization should prevent the same sponsor from appearing under avoidably different variants unless the variants are intentional and reviewed. Creative locking should prevent a revised ad from entering rotation without new review.

The public-file checklist should be specific enough for operators to act on it. It should not merely say “upload political file.” It should identify the file category, order document, sponsor identity record, issue or candidate classification, dates requested, schedule information, disposition, rate information where applicable, and responsible uploader. If a station or system uses vendor software, the vendor export should be reconciled to the FCC public-file state rather than assumed accurate.

## Validation Evidence And Tests

Validation evidence should include the signed or accepted order, sponsor identity source, ad copy or media file, transcript where available, sponsorship ID review notes, trafficking record, invoice or billing reference, public-file upload confirmation, and reconciliation report. Evidence should be date-stamped because political advertising obligations can be time-sensitive.

Tests should include creative-to-order sponsor matching, public-file completeness review, log-to-file reconciliation, language-version review, and post-air spot sampling. A creative-to-order test compares the visible or audible sponsor disclosure against the sponsor in the order record. A public-file test verifies that the required record exists in the correct entity’s public inspection file and is associated with the right political classification. A log-to-file test verifies that aired schedules and changes do not create unsupported records or missing records.

For high-risk periods, such as the weeks before an election, sample daily or near-daily depending on volume. Sampling evidence should record what was checked, what source system was used, what discrepancy was found, and how it was corrected.

## Failures And Corrections

Common failures include missing sponsor identification, inconsistent sponsor naming between order and creative, issue ads misclassified as ordinary commercial spots, missing public-file uploads, stale revised creative in rotation, wrong language version, missed makegood documentation, and vendor exports that fail to reach the public-file system. Corrections should be documented with dates and preserved evidence.

If the sponsorship identification is missing from a covered ad, stop further placement until review determines the correction path. If a public-file record is incomplete, upload or amend the record according to the entity’s procedure and preserve the reason for the late or corrected entry. If sponsor naming is inconsistent, determine whether it is a harmless formatting variation or a substantive identity issue. Substantive identity issues should be escalated before additional spots run.

If an ad was wrongly classified, the correction should update the order classification, public-file record, reporting workflow, and future campaign rules. Merely changing a label in the traffic system is insufficient if the public file, billing, and review evidence remain inconsistent.

## Limitations

This control does not determine whether a buyer is a legally qualified candidate, whether a communication triggers equal opportunities, lowest unit charge, federal election law, or state law obligations, or whether a specific public-file entry satisfies FCC requirements. Those determinations require qualified legal review using current rules and facts.

FCC obligations differ by service and context. Public-file systems, cable systems, broadcasters, and other covered entities may have different operational details. The control should be customized per entity, and every legal conclusion should cite the date, authority, reviewer, and affected stations or systems.
