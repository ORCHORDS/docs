# Influencer Payment Disclosure Tracking

## Scope

This control governs the recording, communication, and verification of payments, products, gifts, discounts, trips, hospitality, exclusive access, and other material considerations provided to influencers, creators, endorsers, reviewers, expert spokespeople, brand ambassadors, employees who are also creators, and the agencies, talent managers, and platforms that act as intermediaries. It applies to any material relationship that may affect how an audience member evaluates the resulting endorsement, including relationships that are not capturable in a single campaign budget.

The governing reference is the FTC Endorsement Guides at 16 CFR Part 255, which address endorsements and testimonials in advertising. The Guides describe what constitutes an endorsement, what counts as a material connection between an advertiser and an endorser, and what kind of disclosure is likely to be effective. A material connection includes a business or family relationship, a payment, free product, or other benefit that an audience member would not expect unless told about it; the guides address both payments and product seeding in similar terms.

## Workflow or implementation guidance

The influencer disclosure workflow proceeds in five phases.

1. Identify the relationship. Before any content is briefed, the marketing team records the type of relationship (paid campaign, gift seeding, affiliate commission, equity or options, hospitality, employment, family), the parties involved, the value where it is determinable, and the planned campaign window.
2. Classify the disclosure obligation. The owner determines whether the relationship must be disclosed, what category of disclosure is appropriate (paid, gifted, sponsored, partnership, ambassador), and what the local expectation is for the channel. Material connections are disclosed even if the law's text is unclear, because undisclosed relationships can mislead.
3. Brief the influencer. The brief includes the disclosure requirement, the disclosure language guidance for the channel (caption text, on-screen text, spoken phrase), the placement guidance (before the claim, not buried after the first paragraph), and any restriction on superlatives or claims that the influencer is not qualified to make.
4. Verify the published content. After publication, the team verifies that the disclosure is present where it appears (caption opening line, on-screen overlay, video frame during claim, podcast spoken phrase), that the disclosure is in the same language as the claim, and that the disclosure language is not buried in tags or behind a "see more."
5. Record and retain. The relationship record, the brief, the published content snapshot, and any correction are retained for a period consistent with the audit window and the organization's evidence-retention policy.

## Controls

The controls in this workflow protect the integrity of the endorsement and the audit trail that supports it.

- Every influencer relationship is recorded with the type of consideration, value, parties, planned window, and the disclosure obligation before creative briefing.
- Disclosures use language appropriate to the channel. Where the audience cannot see or hear the disclosure easily, the disclosure is repeated (for example, in every section of a long video, or in every post in a series).
- Superlatives, performance claims, comparative claims, efficacy claims, and claims about specific results are flagged in the brief and require substantiation that the endorser can speak to personally.
- The brief records the disclosure requirement and the channel-specific language guidance. The post-publication check verifies the disclosure.
- Affiliate links and discount codes are tracked at the influencer level; the marketing analytics pipeline records the disclosure alongside the click or conversion attribution.
- Corrections are tracked. When a post is corrected, the correction itself is subject to the same disclosure requirement.
- Payments to intermediaries (agencies, talent managers) do not displace the disclosure obligation; the disclosure is owed regardless of who handled payment.
- Employee creators and brand ambassadors are treated as having a material connection that must be disclosed.

## Validation evidence

Evidence is collected at each influencer relationship and audited on a defined cadence.

- Relationship log: influencer identifier, type of relationship, value, parties, window, disclosure obligation, and channel.
- Brief: disclosure requirement, language guidance, claim restriction list, and any claims the influencer agreed to substantiate.
- Published content: snapshot, screenshot, video frame, transcript, or audio file with timestamp.
- Disclosure check: location, language, prominence, repetition, format compared to channel expectation.
- Affiliate attribution: link or discount code per post, click and conversion events attributed, disclosure verified on the post.
- Corrections and removals: reason, date, content of correction, content of removed post, the user-visible artifact of correction.

## Failure modes and correction

Common failures include paying an influencer and not disclosing it, treating "gifted" relationships as non-material, sending product seeding without a record, relying on a platform-provided disclosure label without verifying it is visible in the rendering, putting the disclosure after the claim rather than before it, hiding the disclosure in a hashtag that cannot be read in the context, and not correcting a previously published post when the relationship changes. Other failures include assuming the disclosure obligation ends when the payment ends (it does not for residual relationships that are still material) and having a single disclosure policy that is not adapted to the channel.

Correction begins with the post. When a post is live without the required disclosure, the influencer is asked to add the disclosure in place where possible, or the post is corrected and the correction is published. Affiliate or retargeting audiences derived from the post are reviewed for whether they should be retained. The brief is reviewed for the omission, the disclosure rule is reaffirmed with the influencer, and any other recent posts by the same influencer are checked for the same issue. The root cause entry distinguishes planning, brief, and post-publication verification failures and updates the relevant control. Where the failure raises a regulatory concern, the matter is referred to counsel rather than adjudicated operationally.

## Limitations

This control does not decide whether a particular relationship is "material" within the meaning of the FTC Endorsement Guides, whether a particular post is an "endorsement," or whether a particular disclosure "clearly and conspicuously" communicates the relationship. It does not address every disclosure regime (for example, the varying requirements of state attorneys general, or non-US consumer protection regimes), non-disclosure obligations (such as tax or employment rules), or the interaction between compensation and intellectual-property assignment. It does not adjudicate compensation disputes with influencers. It assumes a workable publisher or platform that will preserve published content on demand.
