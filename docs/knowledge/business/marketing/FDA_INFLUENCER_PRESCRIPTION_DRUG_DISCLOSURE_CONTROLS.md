# FDA Influencer Prescription Drug Disclosure Controls

## Scope

This control applies to U.S.-directed influencer, creator, patient ambassador, healthcare professional, affiliate, paid social, short-form video, livestream, podcast, blog, and testimonial content that mentions or promotes a prescription drug, biologic, or prescription drug indication. It is designed for pharmaceutical marketing, medical/legal/regulatory review, agency operations, social media governance, and monitoring teams.

The control covers promotional disclosure governance, not every rule applicable to prescription drug promotion. FDA requirements and expectations can depend on whether the communication is labeling, advertising, reminder promotion, help-seeking content, disease-awareness content, broadcast advertising, internet/social media promotion, or another category. This document therefore avoids unsupported claims that a given post is compliant merely because the workflow was followed. The workflow is intended to produce reviewable evidence that benefit claims, risk information, material connections, product identity, indication, limitations, and correction steps were considered and documented.

Primary sources include FDA’s prescription drug promotion materials, including [FDA: Office of Prescription Drug Promotion Frequently Asked Questions](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/center-drug-evaluation-and-research-cder/opdp-frequently-asked-questions-faqs), [FDA: For Industry - Using Social Media](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/center-drug-evaluation-and-research-cder/industry-using-social-media), and FDA guidance on risk presentation such as [FDA: Presenting Risk Information in Prescription Drug and Medical Device Promotion](https://www.fda.gov/media/76269/download). Material-connection advertising disclosure should also be coordinated with FTC endorsement guidance, but this control focuses on FDA prescription drug promotional risk governance.

## Requirements Versus Recommendations

Internal requirements are mandatory gates before an influencer asset may publish. Each asset must identify the product, indication or approved-use context if claims are made, intended audience, channel, creator, compensation or material connection category, claim inventory, risk information placement, required safety links where used, medical/legal/regulatory approval, version, and expiration date. Any product-benefit claim must be mapped to approved labeling or a reviewed substantiation source. Any safety presentation must be reviewed for prominence, readability, proximity, and completeness relative to the format.

Recommendations include pre-publication comprehension checks, mock mobile rendering review, creator training refreshers, social listening for misleading reposts, and sampling of comments or duets where the creator or brand account responds. These are useful controls, but they should not be described as FDA requirements unless a qualified reviewer has tied them to a specific authority or commitment.

## Workflow

The workflow starts with campaign classification. The requester must state whether the content names a prescription drug, discusses a branded indication, compares therapies, includes patient experience, contains efficacy or safety claims, or is disease-awareness material without product mention. Classification determines the level of review and whether risk disclosure is required in the asset itself, linked material, or both.

Creator intake must capture legal name, public handle, platform IDs, role, audience profile, compensation type, relationship to the sponsor, prior content restrictions, healthcare professional status if applicable, conflicts, and training completion. The creator brief must distinguish approved messages from prohibited extrapolations. A patient may describe personal experience, but the final content should not imply typical outcomes, superiority, unapproved use, or absence of risk unless reviewed evidence supports that communication.

Creative development must use a claim matrix. Each sentence, caption line, superimposed text element, audio claim, spoken statement, visual demonstration, hashtag, and link label should be classified as product identification, efficacy, safety, dosing, patient experience, access, affordability, lifestyle, disease awareness, or relationship disclosure. The review record should show which claims are approved, modified, rejected, or escalated.

Risk review must be format-specific. For short video, reviewers should examine audio, on-screen text, caption, pinned comment, and landing page together. Risk information should not be hidden by fast scrolling, low contrast, tiny text, expired story placement, platform truncation, audio-only benefit claims, or a caption fold that users are unlikely to open before receiving the promotional message. For livestream or ephemeral content, the plan must define whether the format is allowed, how disclosures recur, who monitors deviations, and how recordings are retained.

Final approval must lock the creative, caption, hashtags, link target, posting account, posting window, paid amplification settings, and allowed creator interactions. Any change after approval requires re-review unless the change is in a pre-approved minor-edit category, such as correcting a typo that does not change meaning.

## Concrete Fields And Controls

Minimum fields are: asset ID, campaign ID, product name, established name if required by the review standard, indication context, target audience, platform, format, creator name, creator handle, sponsor, compensation category, material connection disclosure text, claim text, claim type, label support reference, risk disclosure text, safety-link URL, fair-balance review status, reviewer names, approval date, expiration date, publication date, monitoring owner, and takedown criteria.

Controls include locked copy decks, platform-specific rendering previews, disclosure placement rules, prohibited-claim dictionaries, adverse event intake routing, comment-response guardrails, and post-publication archive capture. The disclosure control should require both relationship disclosure and prescription drug promotional review where applicable. A clear paid relationship disclosure does not cure an unbalanced drug-benefit claim; conversely, fair-balance review does not replace material-connection disclosure governance.

Safety-link controls should verify that URLs resolve, are mobile readable, are not blocked by tracking redirects, and point to the approved prescribing information, medication guide, patient safety information, or campaign-specific safety destination approved by review. Hashtag controls should prevent unsupported tags such as broad cure claims, comparative superiority, or disease-state tags that could materially change the implied audience or use context.

## Validation Evidence And Tests

Validation evidence should include the approved script, caption, storyboard, final file, thumbnail, platform preview, risk text, safety links, claim matrix, creator agreement, training record, review approvals, and live-post capture. For video, retain a file copy or platform archive showing audio and visuals. For stories, disappearing posts, livestreams, or platform-specific interactive formats, archive the posted state as soon as practicable.

Tests should include claim-to-label mapping, risk-to-benefit balance review, readability review, link resolution testing, mobile truncation testing, and relationship-disclosure visibility testing. The reviewer should document whether the first promotional impression contains a benefit claim before risk information appears. If platform truncation hides material risk or relationship disclosure, the asset should be revised, blocked, or moved to a format that can carry the necessary information.

Monitoring tests after publication should compare the live asset to the approved asset, check captions and hashtags, confirm that paid amplification has not changed the target audience beyond approval, and sample creator replies. If comments include adverse event information, product complaints, misinformation, or off-label discussion, route them under the organization’s pharmacovigilance, quality, and medical information procedures.

## Failures And Corrections

Common failures include creators improvising claims, captions being edited after approval, risk text being placed only behind a fold, safety links breaking, thumbnails implying benefit claims not reviewed in the asset, reposts omitting disclosures, and creator replies expanding into medical advice. Each failure should receive an incident record with asset ID, platform, date found, screenshots, risk assessment, correction owner, correction deadline, and final disposition.

Corrective actions may include editing the caption, removing the post, disabling paid amplification, posting a corrected version, retraining the creator, updating the creator agreement, notifying internal regulatory owners, or escalating to pharmacovigilance or legal review. Do not silently overwrite the incident evidence. Preserve the original state, the corrected state, and the decision rationale.

## Limitations

This control does not determine whether a specific influencer communication is legally compliant, whether a submission to FDA is required, or whether a piece is exempt from prescription drug advertising requirements. It is not a substitute for FDA regulations, approved labeling, legal review, OPDP expectations, or platform rules. FDA guidance documents may contain recommendations as well as descriptions of statutory or regulatory requirements, so review records should distinguish mandatory rules, agency recommendations, internal policy, and channel preference.

The control should be maintained as a living operating procedure. Update it when FDA publishes new guidance, enforcement activity changes risk tolerance, the approved label changes, or platforms alter formats in ways that affect risk prominence or disclosure visibility.
