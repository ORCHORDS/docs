# UTM Campaign Parameter Governance

## Scope

This control governs the creation, approval, deployment, monitoring, and correction of UTM campaign parameters used on marketing URLs. It applies to paid media, owned social, email, affiliate, partner, QR, display, influencer, lifecycle, and offline-to-online links when the destination URL is intended to attribute traffic, sessions, leads, purchases, or other campaign outcomes. The scope includes links created manually, links generated by marketing automation tools, links appended by ad platforms, links embedded in creative files, and links distributed to partners. It does not decide whether a campaign is legally permissible, whether a disclosure is adequate, or whether an analytics platform may process a particular user signal. Those determinations require separate privacy, advertising, and measurement reviews.

UTM governance is a data quality and operational integrity discipline. Google Analytics describes campaign URL parameters as a way to identify the campaigns that refer traffic and explains that parameter values can appear in acquisition reporting after a user clicks a tagged link: [Google Analytics Help, URL builders: collect campaign data with custom URLs](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10917952). Google also provides an official Campaign URL Builder for formatting campaign URLs: [Google Analytics Demos and Tools, Campaign URL Builder](https://ga-dev-tools.google/campaign-url-builder/). These sources are used here only for parameter behavior and URL-building context, not as promises about reporting completeness, attribution accuracy, privacy compliance, or future product behavior.

## Required Fields And Controls

Every governed URL must have a recorded destination URL, owner, campaign purpose, launch date, intended channels, and source system. The required UTM fields are `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign`. `utm_id`, `utm_content`, and `utm_term` are recommended when they materially improve traceability, paid search keyword analysis, creative testing, or downstream reconciliation. A governed campaign must not use arbitrary one-off parameter names for the same concept unless an approved platform integration requires it. When non-UTM parameters are necessary, such as ad click identifiers or experimentation parameters, they must be documented separately from the campaign taxonomy.

`utm_source` identifies the traffic source or publisher. It must be selected from a controlled list such as `google`, `meta`, `linkedin`, `newsletter`, `affiliate_partner_name`, or `qr_event_booth`. `utm_medium` identifies the channel or delivery medium and must use approved values such as `cpc`, `paid_social`, `email`, `organic_social`, `display`, `affiliate`, `referral`, or `qr`. `utm_campaign` identifies the campaign initiative and must use a durable naming convention that survives creative refreshes, for example `fy27_q1_product_launch_us` rather than `newlaunch` or `test`. `utm_id` should carry the canonical campaign identifier from the planning system when available. `utm_content` should distinguish message, placement, or creative variant. `utm_term` should be limited to search terms or targeting descriptors where the receiving platform and internal policy permit that usage.

Parameter values must be lowercase unless the destination system has a documented case-sensitive requirement. Spaces must be replaced with underscores or hyphens according to the approved convention. Values must not contain personal data, email addresses, phone numbers, raw account IDs, names of individual consumers, sensitive inferences, coupon codes assigned to one person, or internal secrets. Values must be URL-encoded when required, but encoding is not a substitute for taxonomy approval. The final URL must preserve the destination path, fragment behavior, existing query parameters, and required platform click identifiers.

## Workflow

Campaign teams submit UTM requests before creative trafficking or link distribution. The request records the base URL, campaign owner, taxonomy fields, start and end dates, platform, intended audience segment at a non-personal level, and any downstream systems that will consume the values. Marketing operations validates naming, collisions, and completeness. Analytics validates that the requested fields can support reporting requirements without creating unnecessary cardinality. Privacy or legal reviewers are involved only when the proposed parameter values, campaign context, or data sharing path raises issues outside this control.

Approved links are generated from a controlled builder, spreadsheet with locked validation lists, marketing operations form, or platform workflow that enforces the same constraints. The generated URL is tested before release. The approver records a canonical link package containing the raw destination URL, final tagged URL, shortened URL if used, QR code target if used, and the date and tool used to create it. Any shortened URL must resolve to the approved final URL at launch and after campaign changes. Any partner-distributed link must be accompanied by instructions that prohibit rewriting UTM values without approval.

Changes after launch require versioning. Minor corrections, such as fixing a misspelled `utm_content`, are recorded with the original value, corrected value, reason, effective time, and downstream reporting note. Major taxonomy changes, such as moving a campaign from `paid_social` to `affiliate`, require analytics approval because they may split reporting. Emergency correction is allowed when a broken URL, misleading destination, or high-volume attribution defect is active, but the emergency owner must complete the change record afterward.

## Validation Evidence And Tests

Pre-launch evidence must include a rendered final URL, a successful HTTP response or destination load confirmation, and a parameter parse showing each expected field and value. The test must confirm that existing query parameters are preserved with the correct `?` and `&` separators, that fragments are not placed before query parameters, that encoded characters decode to the approved values, and that the URL does not exceed platform or application limits known to the team. For email, a proof must confirm that the email service provider did not rewrite or truncate the link incorrectly. For paid media, a trafficking preview or exported ad URL must show the final values after any platform macros are applied, or must document which macros are intentionally resolved at click time.

Post-launch validation must sample live click paths from each major channel and compare observed analytics values against the approved registry. Monitoring should flag unknown `utm_source`, unknown `utm_medium`, blank required fields, mixed-case variants, values with spaces, values containing `@`, unusually high-cardinality values, and campaign names not tied to an active campaign record. Reports used by finance, sales operations, or executive dashboards should include a freshness note and a known-data-quality section when UTM defects are present.

Evidence should be retained as campaign metadata: request record, approval, generated URL, test result, release timestamp, and correction log. The control requires evidence sufficient to reconstruct why a parameter value existed, who approved it, and how the final URL was verified. It does not require retaining every click event inside this governance document.

## Failures And Corrections

Common failures include source and medium reversal, use of display names rather than controlled values, accidental creation of multiple campaign spellings, omission of UTM fields from shortened URLs, unencoded characters, platform macro leakage, and inclusion of personal data in a parameter. Another frequent failure is copying an old URL and changing only visible ad copy while leaving an obsolete campaign value. These failures are corrected by replacing active links, updating trafficking assets, documenting the affected window, and annotating reporting where values cannot be merged safely.

If personal data or a secret appears in a live UTM value, the immediate correction is to stop distribution, replace the link, and notify the appropriate privacy, security, and platform owners. Analytics teams should assess whether the value entered logs, exports, dashboards, or third-party integrations. This document does not prescribe breach notification or legal conclusions; it requires operational escalation and factual evidence collection.

If a campaign was launched with inconsistent values across channels, the correction should prioritize forward-looking standardization. Historical data may be normalized in a reporting layer only when the mapping is deterministic and documented. Raw event data should not be silently overwritten unless the analytics data governance policy permits it. When normalization is used, dashboards must identify the mapping table or transformation version.

## Requirements Versus Recommendations

Required: use approved fields for governed campaign links; keep `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` complete; prohibit personal data and secrets; validate final URLs before launch; record approval and correction evidence; monitor for unknown or malformed values; and escalate privacy or security issues when parameter contents create risk beyond attribution quality.

Recommended: use `utm_id` as the durable planning-system key; reserve `utm_content` for creative and placement variants; use automated link builders; maintain dashboard-level anomaly detection; review high-cardinality values monthly; and publish channel-specific examples for campaign managers.

## Limitations

UTM parameters identify tagged link intent; they do not prove that all traffic came from the stated source, that every click produced a measurable session, that attribution is complete, or that downstream conversions are causally caused by a campaign. Browser behavior, consent settings, redirects, app handoffs, ad blockers, platform rewrites, and analytics configuration can change what is collected and reported. Governance improves consistency and auditability, but it cannot turn URL parameters into a complete measurement system.
