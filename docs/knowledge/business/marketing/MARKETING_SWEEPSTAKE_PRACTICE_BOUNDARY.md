# Marketing Sweepstake Practice Boundary

Promotion mechanics sit on a line. On one side is the lawful sweepstake: a prize awarded by chance, open to entrants without payment. On the other side is an illegal lottery: prize plus chance plus consideration, the third element being anything of value the promoter extracts for entry. The boundary is not about scale or intent; a modest social-media giveaway that requires a purchase crosses the same line as a large mail-in promotion. This article governs the practice boundary that keeps promotional drawings lawful: how consideration is identified, how free entry routes are made genuinely equal, and how the mechanics are documented so the boundary can be proven after the fact.

## Scope

This control covers chance-based promotions: sweepstakes, drawings, raffles run by or for the business, prize wheels, random-comment giveaways, social platform contests decided by chance, and promotional games awarding prizes randomly. It applies to the promotion design, entry routes, odds and winner-selection mechanics, prize descriptions, and the documentation retained from design through winner notification.

It addresses the lottery boundary and its adjacent disclosure duties. It does not govern skill-based contests, which avoid the chance element and raise different questions, and it does not administer the full promotion disclosure regime, which is covered by separate controls. Where a promotion mixes skill and chance, or where entry routes differ in value, counsel review is mandatory rather than optional.

## Workflow or implementation guidance

1. **Identify the three elements at design time.** Every promotion concept is annotated against the three lottery elements: prize (anything of value), chance (any material randomness in selection), and consideration (anything of economic value required to enter). The concept review names each element present; the design's job is to eliminate or neutralize consideration wherever prize and chance coexist.
2. **Interpret consideration broadly.** Consideration is not only money. Purchase requirements, subscription sign-ups, paid shipping, store visits, app downloads requiring payment, processing fees, and excessive informational demands have all been argued as consideration. Where any entry route extracts value, the design must supply an alternative that does not, and that alternative must be real.
3. **Make the free route genuinely equal.** An alternate entry method is lawful only if it gives equal dignity and equal odds: same prize pool, same odds per entry, same deadlines, no material handicap in effort so severe that a reasonable person would regard the free route as decorative. A free route buried in tiny print, restricted to business hours, or capped relative to purchase entries is treated as non-existent for boundary purposes.
4. **Publish entry routes and odds symmetrically.** Rules state all entry methods with equal prominence, the odds for each, entry limits, eligibility, drawing date, and how winners are notified and published. The official rules are the same document referenced in every ad for the promotion.
5. **Run selection with a documented random process.** Winner selection uses a verifiable random mechanism, a certified drawing tool, a documented random seed, or an equivalent auditable process. The record shows who ran it, when, with what input list, and what output was produced.
6. **Deliver the prizes as described.** Prize fulfillment records match the described prizes in value and form; substitutions are documented and of equal or greater value. Winner lists, releases where used, and notification records are retained.
7. **Re-review on any mechanic change.** Any change to entry routes, prize structure, or platforms during the flight re-triggers the element analysis, because mid-flight changes are where a compliant design quietly becomes a lottery.

## Controls

- Every chance-based promotion requires a signed boundary analysis before production: elements identified, consideration assessment, free-route equality assessment, and counsel sign-off where any element is contested.
- The free entry route ships with the same launch gate as the purchase route; a promotion cannot launch with the free route "coming soon."
- Official rules are versioned, and every ad referencing the promotion points at the current version.
- The random selection process is documented per drawing, with operator, method, and output retained.
- Platform-specific promotion requirements, including platform rules on contests, are checked at design, since platform prohibition can kill an otherwise lawful mechanic.
- Employees and their households are excluded by rule, and eligibility screening of winners is recorded.

## Validation evidence

- Boundary analyses for sampled promotions showing the three-element assessment and the consideration reasoning.
- Rendered entry surfaces for each route: purchase route and free route as actually displayed, captured across devices, demonstrating comparable prominence and odds.
- Official rules versions with effective dates, matched to the ads that referenced them during each version's window.
- Drawing records: input list size, method, operator, timestamp, output, and winner notification logs.
- Fulfillment records showing prizes delivered as described, with any substitutions documented.
- Mid-flight change records with re-review outcomes.

## Failure modes and correction

Common failures include a giveaway where entry requires any purchase with no free route at all, a free route that exists but is limited to one entry while purchases grant ten, an entry route requiring a paid subscription that is never canceled by entrants, rules stating one drawing date while the drawing runs early, a prize described as a product bundle but delivered as a gift card of lesser value, and a state-level registration or bond requirement missed for a promotion touching markets that regulate chance promotions. The most common design failure is the decorative free route: technically present, practically unusable.

Correction starts with stopping the defective mechanic: entry routes that extract value are closed or replaced with a genuine free route before the promotion continues. Entrants who paid to enter are evaluated for restitution, a decision escalated with counsel rather than made by the campaign team. Drawings already conducted on a defective basis are reviewed with counsel, and prize obligations are honored. Rules are corrected and republished, and every surface referencing the old version is updated. Recurrence requires the boundary analysis to move upstream into creative brief approval, so no chance mechanic reaches production without it.

## Limitations

Lottery and promotion law is jurisdiction-specific: some markets regulate chance promotions through registration, bonding, or disclosure statutes, some restrict them entirely, and the meaning of consideration varies by jurisdiction and by market practice over time. This control supplies a conservative operating boundary, not a multi-jurisdiction determination; counsel review remains mandatory for contested elements and for multi-market flights. Platform rules add independent constraints. Nothing here legitimizes a mechanic that complies with the letter of the boundary while manufacturing the impression that purchase improves the chance of winning.
