# Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule

## Purpose

The U.S. Federal Trade Commission's Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials addresses specified unfair or deceptive practices involving reviews and testimonials. The rule took effect on October 21, 2024. FTC guidance states that courts can impose civil penalties for knowing violations.

## Practices covered by the rule

A review or testimonial program should be designed to avoid prohibited conduct including:

- creating or selling fake or false reviews or testimonials;
- buying, procuring, or disseminating reviews or testimonials that a business knew or should have known were fake or false in circumstances covered by the rule;
- providing compensation or another incentive conditioned on a review expressing a particular positive or negative sentiment;
- using certain insider reviews or testimonials without required disclosure of the material connection;
- misrepresenting that a company-controlled review website or entity is independent;
- using unfounded or groundless legal threats, intimidation, or certain other tactics to prevent or remove negative reviews; and
- buying or selling fake indicators of social-media influence when the rule's conditions apply.

## Review-program controls

1. Do not create, script, or procure purported customer experiences that are fake or false.
2. If incentives are offered for reviews, do not condition the incentive on positive or negative sentiment.
3. Identify insider relationships and apply the disclosure and solicitation requirements relevant to officers, managers, employees, agents, and relatives.
4. Do not suppress truthful negative feedback through baseless legal threats or intimidation.
5. Review vendors that solicit, moderate, syndicate, or generate review content for signs of fake or false activity.
6. Distinguish obligations under the Reviews and Testimonials Rule from related FTC Endorsement Guides and other review laws.
7. Re-check review practices when vendors, incentive structures, moderation processes, or distribution channels change.

## Hosting versus using reviews in advertising

FTC staff guidance distinguishes merely hosting reviews from using review or testimonial content as advertising. Responsibilities can differ when a business creates, purchases, procures, disseminates, republishes, or otherwise incorporates review content into its own marketing. Check the current FTC Q&A for the specific fact pattern rather than assuming that all review activity is treated the same way.

## Scope note

This article summarizes selected U.S. federal requirements and FTC staff guidance. The FTC states that its Q&A is not definitive or comprehensive and does not provide a safe harbor. Platform rules, state law, sector-specific requirements, and non-U.S. regimes may impose additional obligations.
