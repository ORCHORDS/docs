# Jewelry and Gemstone Marketing Claims

## Purpose

The U.S. Federal Trade Commission's Jewelry Guides help marketers describe jewelry, precious metals, diamonds, gemstones, and pearls truthfully and disclose information that would be material to a reasonable buyer.

## Claim governance

Marketing teams should treat jewelry descriptions as product facts that require substantiation rather than as flexible promotional language.

1. Verify material, fineness, treatment, weight, origin, and grading claims before publication.
2. Use terms such as gold, silver, platinum, diamond, gemstone, pearl, plated, cultured, synthetic, or imitation only when the product meets the applicable description.
3. Disclose treatments, simulated characteristics, or other facts when omission could mislead a buyer about value, durability, rarity, care, or expected appearance.
4. Keep decimal weight claims accurate to the precision represented in the advertisement.
5. Ensure photographs, product names, filters, comparison charts, and marketplace attributes do not contradict written disclosures.
6. Update or withdraw claims when suppliers, product composition, treatment status, or substantiation changes.

## Digital product listings

Online product pages should keep material disclosures close enough to the claim that a customer can understand the product before purchase. A buried technical note should not be used to correct a headline, image, title, or price presentation that creates a materially different impression.

## Supplier evidence

For material claims, retain appropriate evidence such as supplier specifications, assay or grading information, treatment disclosures, invoices, certificates, or other records that support the representation actually made. Supplier terminology should be independently checked when it could create a deceptive consumer impression.

This article summarizes general U.S. FTC marketing principles. It does not replace the Jewelry Guides, assay or hallmark law, customs requirements, sanctions rules, or state consumer-protection law.
