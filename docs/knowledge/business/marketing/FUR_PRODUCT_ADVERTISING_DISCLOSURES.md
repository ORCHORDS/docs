# Fur Product Advertising Disclosures

## Purpose

The U.S. Federal Trade Commission's Fur Products Labeling Act and Fur Rules require material fur-product information to appear not only on product labels but also on invoices and in advertising for covered fur products.

## Advertising controls

For a covered product advertisement:

1. Use the animal name recognized by the Fur Products Name Guide.
2. Avoid geographic descriptions that create a false impression about where the fur originated.
3. Disclose artificial coloring when the fur is pointed, dyed, bleached, or otherwise artificially colored; when applicable, describe untreated fur as natural.
4. Disclose when the product contains more than the applicable amount of pieces, scraps, or waste fur.
5. Disclose when fur is used or damaged where required.
6. Keep textile or wool content and country-of-origin statements consistent with the other labeling rules that apply to the product.
7. Do not advertise dog or cat fur products; federal law prohibits their import, export, sale, trade, advertising, transport, and distribution.

## Catalog and remote-sale advertising

FTC guidance distinguishes general group advertising from catalog, mail-order, and similar advertising where the customer does not see the product label before delivery. Remote-sale descriptions should therefore carry the required origin and product facts with enough specificity for the buyer to understand what is being offered before purchase.

General institutional advertising that does not promote particular products may not require the full product information, but a color claim can still require disclosure when the color is artificial.

## Invoice and listing consistency

The product label, invoice, ecommerce record, marketplace listing, and advertisement should be generated from the same controlled product facts. A change in animal species, origin, coloring treatment, composition, or used/damaged status should trigger review of all customer-facing representations rather than only the physical label.

## Scope note

This article summarizes general U.S. FTC fur-advertising requirements. Wildlife-protection law, customs restrictions, sanctions, state law, textile and wool rules, and product-safety requirements may impose additional restrictions.
