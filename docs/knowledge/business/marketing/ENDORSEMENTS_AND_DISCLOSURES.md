# Endorsements and Material-Connection Disclosures

## Purpose

Endorsements, testimonials, influencer posts, reviews, and similar marketing can become misleading when a material relationship with the advertiser is hidden or when the endorsement communicates claims that are not truthful or supported.

## Material connections

Federal Trade Commission guidance explains that a material connection can include financial, employment, personal, family, free-product, discount, or other relationships that could affect how an audience evaluates an endorsement.

When disclosure is needed, make it:

- clear and understandable to the intended audience;
- hard to miss;
- close to the endorsement rather than buried in a profile, terms page, or long list of tags;
- presented in the same language as the endorsement; and
- suitable for the medium, including visual or repeated disclosure where viewers may encounter only part of a video or live stream.

Do not assume a platform's built-in disclosure control is sufficient in every context.

## Truthfulness of the endorsement

An endorser should not describe an experience they did not have or state an opinion they do not actually hold. Compensation does not permit an advertiser or endorser to make claims that would otherwise lack the required support.

## Operational workflow

1. Identify whether the content is an endorsement or testimonial.
2. Record any relationship or benefit that could be material to the audience.
3. Determine whether and how the connection should be disclosed.
4. Review the disclosure in the actual placement, not only in draft text.
5. Confirm objective product claims are independently substantiated.
6. Monitor recurring campaigns and third parties for continued compliance.

This article summarizes reusable marketing principles and does not replace jurisdiction-specific legal review. Other countries and sectors may impose different or additional disclosure requirements.
