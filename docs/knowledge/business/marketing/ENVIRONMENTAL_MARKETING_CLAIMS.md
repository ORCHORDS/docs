# Environmental Marketing Claims

## Purpose

Environmental claims can mislead when they are broad, unqualified, or unsupported. The FTC Green Guides explain how truth-in-advertising principles apply to environmental marketing and emphasize substantiation and clear qualification.

## Practical controls

1. **Avoid vague general claims.** Terms such as "green" or "eco-friendly" should not be used broadly without specific, supportable meaning.
2. **Substantiate before publishing.** Maintain competent and reliable evidence for environmental claims, especially scientific or lifecycle claims.
3. **State the exact benefit.** Explain whether a claim concerns the product, packaging, manufacturing process, disposal, recycled content, or another defined attribute.
4. **Qualify limitations prominently.** If recycling, composting, renewable content, or similar benefits depend on conditions or availability, disclose those limits clearly.
5. **Keep comparisons defined.** Claims such as "less waste" or "lower emissions" should identify the comparison basis and relevant period or product.
6. **Re-review changing claims.** Update marketing when product composition, suppliers, packaging, facilities, methodology, or evidence changes.

## Evidence to retain

Keep the source data, methodology, approvals, claim wording, qualifications, and review date for material environmental claims.

The Green Guides are U.S. FTC guidance. Environmental labeling and advertising obligations can differ by product and jurisdiction.
