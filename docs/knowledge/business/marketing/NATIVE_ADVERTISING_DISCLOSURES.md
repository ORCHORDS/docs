# Native Advertising Disclosures

## Purpose

Advertising should not be formatted in a way that materially misleads people into believing commercial content is independent, editorial, or otherwise non-advertising content. The Federal Trade Commission applies established truth-in-advertising principles to native advertising and other deceptively formatted advertisements.

## Start with the net impression

Review the advertisement as a whole rather than treating a disclosure as a cure-all. Relevant factors include the presentation, placement, surrounding content, visual style, headline, imagery, audio, and the path a consumer takes before reaching the full advertising message.

If the commercial nature of content is not otherwise apparent, a disclosure may be needed before or at the point where the consumer encounters the advertising message.

## Disclosure design

When a disclosure is necessary to prevent deception, it should be clear and prominent. Practical checks include:

- use plain, unambiguous language that communicates the content is advertising;
- place the disclosure where people are likely to notice it, close to the advertising message or focal point;
- ensure text is readable and visually distinguishable from its background;
- account for mobile, desktop, audio, video, and other formats independently;
- place multimedia disclosures close enough to the relevant message that viewers or listeners can connect them; and
- test whether the intended audience can actually notice, process, and understand the disclosure.

## Publication workflow

1. Identify content that resembles editorial, news, reviews, entertainment, or other non-advertising material.
2. Assess whether a reasonable member of the intended audience would recognize the commercial nature before engaging with it.
3. Add or strengthen disclosures where the format could otherwise mislead.
4. Review both the initial placement and any click-through or destination page.
5. Re-test when layouts, devices, distribution channels, or surrounding content change.
6. Preserve screenshots or other review evidence for material campaigns when useful for later substantiation.

## Scope note

This article summarizes U.S. federal guidance on advertising format and disclosure. Platform rules, sector-specific requirements, state law, and non-U.S. advertising regimes may impose additional obligations.
