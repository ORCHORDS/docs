# Product Launch Newsroom Embargo Governance

## Scope

This control governs the planning, communication, lifting, and audit of media and analyst embargoes associated with product launches, feature announcements, brand news, event-driven announcements, and related pre-release coverage. It applies to product launches, product refreshes, partnership announcements, financial announcements where a public-relations embargo is in place, embargoed reviews, embargoed briefings, embargoed product seeding, analyst-day materials, investor-day press kits, and any other release in which a reporter, analyst, influencer, blogger, or partner has been given information in advance under a "do not publish before" condition.

The governing reference is the FTC's Native Advertising Guides, which address how sponsored, paid, or otherwise promoted content should be distinguished from surrounding editorial material so that consumers can recognize what they are seeing. The framework is relevant here for the disclosure discipline that anchors an embargoed launch: terms that govern the distinction between what is on-the-record, what is on-background, what is on-deep-background, what is embargoed, and what is paid promotion; and the discipline required to make sure that an embargoed piece of content doesn't slip into a placement that would mislabel it.

## Workflow or implementation guidance

The embargo governance workflow proceeds in six phases.

1. Decide to embargo. The team records the basis for the embargo (the date and time of the official announcement, a concurrent event, a coordination need with partners or analysts, or a third-party reporting consideration), the planned lift moment, and the named approver.
2. Define the embargoed package. The package includes the newsroom materials (press release, fact sheet, FAQ, images, B-roll, technical-spec sheet), the briefing materials, the product demonstration materials, the seeded product where applicable, the marketing creative that pairs with the launch, and the partner or analyst communications plan.
3. Identify recipients. The recipient list names each reporter, outlet, analyst, influencer, blogger, or partner, with the name of the contact, the channel through which the materials were sent, the time the materials were sent, the recipient's written or verbal acknowledgment of the embargo, and the planned lift time.
4. Track acknowledgments. Each recipient's acknowledgment is recorded. A separate log tracks who was sent the materials but did not acknowledge the embargo; those recipients are followed up with before the lift moment.
5. Lift the embargo. At the lift moment, the team confirms that the public channels (newsroom URL, press release distribution, web-published materials) are live and that the official copy matches the materials sent under embargo. Where the materials drift, the drift is corrected before amplification.
6. Watch for breaks. After the lift, the team monitors for early publication by a recipient that should have been embargoed, for partner channels that publish at the lift moment but with embargoed material lifted ahead of time, and for any placement that mislabels the product as paid promotion when it is editorial coverage, or vice versa.

## Controls

The controls in this workflow are designed to make sure that an embargoed launch lands cleanly at the lift moment and that disclosures are consistent across placements.

- A written acknowledgment of the embargo is captured for each recipient. Verbal acknowledgments are recorded in the campaign log with the time and the recipient's representative.
- A single authoritative lift time is set, with a single time zone for everyone. Multiple time zones are documented in the master schedule but reported as one moment, with each recipient's local time recorded for their acknowledgment.
- Marketing creative, partner creative, and editorial coverage are aligned: paid placements of the launch carry the brand as the source, the disclosure (when required) as required, and the substantive description that matches the public materials.
- The newsroom materials are stored under version control so that the version sent under embargo can be matched byte-for-byte against the version publicly released at the lift moment.
- The partner or analyst briefing materials are kept consistent with the public materials. Points in the briefing that the marketing team does not want to see in published coverage are flagged.
- A break of the embargo triggers a documented response: outreach to the recipient who broke it, a decision about the launch (continue, accelerate, delay), and a record of the incident.

## Validation evidence

Evidence is collected at each phase.

- The approved embargo plan: basis, lift time, lift time zone, named approver.
- The embargoed package: version-controlled list of materials.
- The recipient log: each recipient, contact, channel, send time, acknowledgment time.
- The lift record: public materials live at the lift moment, with their public URL or distribution channel.
- The post-lift monitoring log: any pre-lift publications detected and the response.
- Partner or analyst agreement: where partners or analysts commit to the embargo, the agreement reference is recorded.

## Failure modes and correction

Common failures include sending materials without capturing an acknowledgment, setting a single lift time but failing to align all time zones, drifting the public copy from the embargoed copy, briefing recipients with material that isn't safe to publish, treating paid promotion and editorial coverage as if they were interchangeable, having a partner who publishes ahead of the lift, and treating an embargo break as a non-event. Other failures include not having a public version of the materials ready before the lift, and a newsroom URL that does not resolve at the lift moment.

Correction starts with the affected placement. When a partner publishes ahead, the marketing team decides whether to delay the public release, accelerate, or hold. The decision is recorded along with the rationale. When the public copy has drifted from the embargoed copy, the public copy is corrected at the next refresh and the discrepancy is logged. When a recipient broke the embargo, the incident is recorded, the relationship is reviewed, and any future embargoed materials to that recipient are documented with greater care. The corrected baseline is used for any subsequent amplification.

## Limitations

This control does not decide whether a particular placement should be labeled as paid promotion, sponsorship, or editorial; it requires that the labels be applied consistently and in line with relevant disclosures. It does not adjudicate a breach of an embargo as a matter of law; it documents what happened. It does not cover every channel for early publication (for example, financial-news embargoes and research-firm embargoes have their own conventions). It assumes the launch has a clear public lift moment; rolling launches with continuous releases need a different cadence.
