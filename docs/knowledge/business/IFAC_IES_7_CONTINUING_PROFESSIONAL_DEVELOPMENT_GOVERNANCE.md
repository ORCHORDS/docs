# IFAC IES 7 Continuing Professional Development Governance

## Purpose

Govern the application of the IAASB-adjacent IES 7 (Continuing Professional Development, in the International Education Standards issued by IFAC's International Accounting Education Standards Board) so that professional staff in audit, accounting, and assurance roles maintain and develop competence through a structured CPD system: identified development needs, planned learning, and evaluated effectiveness — with tracked evidence, not certificate accumulation.

## Scope

Applies to the studio's professional staff in accounting, audit, and assurance functions bound by IFAC member-body CPD requirements. Covers the CPD cycle (identify, act, evaluate), measurement, and evidence. Does not cover firm-wide training programmes for non-professional roles or jurisdictional licensing requirements beyond the CPD obligation.

## Workflow

1. Operate the CPD cycle per IES 7: identify development needs against current and emerging role requirements, plan and undertake learning activities, and evaluate the learning's effectiveness against the identified need.
2. Anchor identification in competence frameworks: role-level competence expectations (technical, professional, ethical) drive each professional's development plan; generic hour-counting without needs identification is a system failure.
3. Blend learning activity types per the standard's allowance: verifiable CPD (courses, structured learning with evidence) and unverifiable CPD (reading, self-study) within the applicable member-body measurement requirements.
4. Measure per the applicable regime: input-based (hours) or output-based (demonstrated competence) as the member body requires, recording evidence that satisfies the measurement basis.
5. Evaluate effectiveness: for each cycle, confirm learning activities addressed identified needs and produced usable competence — the evaluation is the step certificate-collectors skip.
6. Maintain evidence per claim: course records, assessments, outputs of applied learning; evidence is retained for the member body's monitoring window and audit selection.
7. Escalate non-compliance: professionals falling short of their CPD obligation enter a remediation path with deadlines; persistent non-compliance restricts signing or assurance roles until cured.

## Controls and evidence

- Role competence frameworks driving development plans.
- CPD plans and activity records per professional with input/output measurement basis.
- Effectiveness evaluation records per cycle.
- Evidence retention per member-body monitoring requirements.
- Non-compliance remediation records with role restrictions applied.

## Validation

- Sample five professionals' CPD records and confirm each traces activities to identified needs with an effectiveness evaluation.
- Confirm the measurement basis applied matches the applicable member-body regime.
- Confirm remediation cases enforced role restrictions where required.

## Failure correction

- **CPD records without needs identification** → rebuild the plan from the competence framework for the current cycle; past hours without needs-tracing are re-examined for verifiability.
- **Effectiveness evaluation missing** → complete a retrospective evaluation for the cycle and add the gate to cycle close-out.
- **Evidence gap at member-body audit** → remediate the evidence where possible, disclose where not, and fix the retention configuration.

## Limitations

- IES 7 sets the standard; each IFAC member body translates it into binding CPD regulations — the member-body rule is the compliance target.
- CPD demonstrates development activity and evaluated learning, not guaranteed competence in every engagement.
- Output-based regimes require more evaluation discipline than hour-counting; firms accustomed to input measurement find the transition demanding.
