# OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises 2023 Governance

## Purpose

The OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises (MNE Guidelines) are recommendations from governments to multinational enterprises operating in or from adhering countries. The 2023 update is the seventh revision. Governance ensures that an enterprise subject to the MNE Guidelines observes them across disclosure, human rights, employment, environment, bribery, consumer interests, science and technology, competition, and taxation.

## Current context and source status

The OECD updated the MNE Guidelines in 2023, incorporating responsible business conduct expectations and aligned with the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights. National Contact Points (NCPs) handle cases of alleged non-observance. Verify the current OECD MNE Guidelines before treating any specific recommendation as a current requirement.

## Governance workflow and controls

### 1. Adopt a responsible business conduct policy

Adopt a policy aligned with the MNE Guidelines. Communicate the policy to employees and business partners. Review the policy periodically.

### 2. Apply human rights due diligence

Conduct human rights due diligence per the UN Guiding Principles (incorporated in Chapter IV of the MNE Guidelines). Identify, prevent, and mitigate adverse impacts. Track actions.

### 3. Disclose information

Disclose information on operations, financial performance, and material non-financial information. Disclose climate and sustainability information.

### 4. Address employment practices

Apply the employment recommendations: freedom of association, collective bargaining, non-discrimination, prohibition of child and forced labor, employment promotion.

### 5. Address environmental performance

Apply the environmental recommendations: environmental management system, life cycle approach, climate change, biodiversity, water, circular economy. Disclose environmental performance.

### 6. Combat bribery

Combat bribery in all forms. Apply anti-corruption controls consistent with the OECD Anti-Bribery Convention.

### 7. Apply consumer interests, competition, taxation

Apply the chapters on consumer interests (fair marketing, privacy), competition (comply with applicable antitrust laws), and taxation (comply with applicable tax laws, apply arm's length principle per OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines).

### 8. Engage with NCPs

Engage constructively with NCPs in cases of alleged non-observance. Document the engagement.

## Validation and evidence

- Responsible business conduct policy.
- Due diligence assessments.
- Disclosure documentation.
- NCP engagement records.

## Failure correction

Common defects include policies that don't address all chapters, missing due diligence on human rights, and disengagement from NCPs. Corrective actions include a chapter coverage review, a due diligence program, and an NCP engagement procedure.

## Limitations

- The MNE Guidelines are non-binding recommendations.
- NCPs have varying effectiveness across adhering countries.
- Specific chapter requirements intersect with sectoral regulations.
- Enforcement is through peer review, not judicial sanction.

## Canonical sources
