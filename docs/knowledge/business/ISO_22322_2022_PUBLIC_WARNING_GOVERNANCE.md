# ISO 22322:2022 — Security and Resilience — Emergency Management — Public Warning

## Purpose

Establish governance on the ISO 22322:2022 edition (second edition, December 2022) of the international standard for public warning in the context of emergency management. This article covers the published 2022 international standard. A "ISO 22322:2024" edition could not be verified from primary ISO sources during preparation; any later edition number reported by third parties should be reconfirmed against iso.org before adoption.

## Current status

- Second edition published December 2022 under technical committee ISO/TC 292 (Security and resilience).
- Title as published: "Security and resilience — Emergency management — Guidelines for public warning."
- This edition cancels and replaces the first edition (ISO 22322:2015, "Societal security — Emergency management — Guidelines for public warning").
- Status as of 2026-09-04: ISO 22322:2022 is the most recent ISO international edition verifiable from primary sources.

## Scope note

ISO 22322:2022 sits in the public-policy / emergency-management wing of ISO's security and resilience portfolio and is governed separately from private-sector information-security standards such as ISO/IEC 27001. The 2022 edition's primary shift from the 2015 edition is the change of overarching title prefix from "Societal security" to "Security and resilience," reflecting the wider scope of ISO/TC 292.

Key governance concepts that adopting programs at state, municipal, or organizational levels should understand:

1. Authorities and responsibilities. The standard describes a model where a designated public authority owns public warning decisions, distinct from the technical systems that deliver them. Governance should reflect that separation; technical operators should not have independent authority to issue warnings.
2. Public warning is multi-channel. ISO 22322 expects combinations of channels (sirens, broadcast, cellular, web, door-to-door) deployed in parallel according to coverage gaps and population reach. A governance decision to retire a channel must be backed by a reach-coverage analysis.
3. Message construction principles. The standard calls for messages that name the hazard, the area, the protective action, and the time frame. Templates that omit any of these should not be used operationally.
4. Quality assurance. ISO 22322 expects pre-event exercises and post-event reviews; the standard aligns with ISO 22398 (exercises programme) for rehearsal cadence.
5. Cancellation and stand-down. The standard requires an explicit, communicated closure to each warning; "do nothing / wait" is not acceptable.

Adoption should be distinguished from sector overlays: ISO 22322 is generic; specific country implementations (CAP-IP, EU-Alert, NL-Alert, Wireless Emergency Alerts) are downstream profiles. Any governance record that references ISO 22322 should cite the channel profile(s) used operationally in addition to the base standard.

This article does not cover ISO 22322:2015 (first edition) in detail; for transitional guidance see ISO/TC 292 editorial records.
