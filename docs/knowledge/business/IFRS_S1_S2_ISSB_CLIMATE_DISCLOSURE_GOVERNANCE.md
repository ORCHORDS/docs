# IFRS S1 and S2 Sustainability Disclosure Governance

## Purpose

Govern the application of IFRS S1 (general requirements for disclosure of sustainability-related financial information) and IFRS S2 (climate-related disclosures), issued by the ISSB, so that sustainability-related financial disclosures identify the risks and opportunities reasonably expected to affect the studio's prospects, follow the standards' core content architecture, and connect to the financial statements rather than float free of them.

## Scope

Applies to the studio's sustainability-related financial disclosures prepared under IFRS S1/S2 where required or voluntarily adopted. Covers materiality determination for sustainability-related financial information, the four-pillar core content, climate-specific metrics and targets, and the connectivity to financial reporting. Does not cover GRI impact reporting or jurisdictional regimes that adopt the standards with local modifications.

## Workflow

1. Identify sustainability-related risks and opportunities per S1: those that could reasonably be expected to affect the studio's cash flows, access to finance, or cost of capital over the short, medium, or long term — from the full landscape, not only climate.
2. Determine materiality for disclosure: information is material if omitting, obscuring, or misstating it could influence decisions of primary users of general purpose financial reports; the determination process is documented.
3. Structure disclosures per the core content: governance (who oversees the risks), strategy (how risks/opportunities affect business model and strategy, including scenario analysis for climate), risk management (processes to identify, assess, prioritize, monitor), and metrics and targets (measurement and performance).
4. Apply S2's climate requirements: scope 1, 2, and — if material — scope 3 GHG emissions measured per the GHG Protocol corporate standard, climate scenario analysis, transition and physical risk disclosure, and industry-based metrics from the accompanying industry guidance.
5. Establish connectivity to the financial statements: where sustainability-related risks affect reported amounts or assumptions, the sustainability disclosure references the connection; contradictory siloed narratives are a governance failure.
6. Manage the disclosure process with the same rigor as financial reporting: defined controls over data, review by the audit committee or equivalent, and comparability across periods with restatement policy.
7. Track jurisdictional adoption where the studio reports under regimes that incorporate ISSB standards (with local modifications), preparing one underlying dataset with jurisdiction-specific presentations.

## Controls and evidence

- Risk and opportunity identification records with sources and screening method.
- Materiality determination documentation per S1.
- Core content disclosure drafts with governance, strategy, risk management, and metrics sections.
- GHG emissions measurement records (scope 1/2/3) with GHG Protocol methodology references.
- Scenario analysis methodology and results records.
- Connectivity review between sustainability disclosures and financial statements.

## Validation

- Confirm each disclosed risk traces to an identification record, not to peer-report copying alone.
- Confirm scope 1 and 2 emissions have full measurement records; scope 3 is disclosed or excluded with materiality rationale.
- Confirm a documented review connects sustainability disclosures to financial statement assumptions.

## Failure correction

- **Disclosure lacking identification basis** → document the screening that produced the risk or remove it as immaterial with rationale.
- **Emissions figure without methodology trail** → reconstruct measurement records before publication; un-reconstructable figures are not published.
- **Contradiction with financial statements found** → resolve at the connectivity review, and record which disclosure changes and why.

## Limitations

- S1/S2 adoption timelines and local modifications vary by jurisdiction; track the applicable regime per reporting obligation.
- Scenario analysis outputs depend on scenario choice and method; disclose the basis rather than presenting single-scenario precision.
- ISSB standards target investor-focused financial materiality; impact-oriented frameworks (GRI) run in parallel, not substitution.
