# UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights Governance

## Purpose

The UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights (UNGPs), endorsed by the UN Human Rights Council in 2011, are the global standard for preventing and addressing human rights impacts linked to business activity. The framework rests on three pillars: the state duty to protect human rights; the corporate responsibility to respect human rights; and access to remedy for victims. This article governs the application of the corporate responsibility to respect human rights, including the human rights policy, human rights due diligence, and the grievance or remediation mechanisms businesses are expected to operate.

## Scope

The UNGPs apply to all business enterprises, regardless of size, sector, or location. Within this knowledge base, the article covers the corporate responsibility to respect human rights (the policy commitment, the human rights due diligence process, and the remediation mechanisms), the relationship between the responsibility and the state duty, and the documentation of corporate human rights practice. It does not replace specific human rights laws (modern slavery acts, conflict mineral regulations, sectoral human rights obligations); readers should overlay their sector requirements.

## Workflow

1. Adopt a human rights policy statement at the most senior level, approved by the governing body. The policy should express the organization's commitment to respect human rights, apply to all operations and business relationships, and be communicated internally and externally.
2. Conduct human rights due diligence:
   - Identify and assess actual and potential human rights impacts. Consider impacts across operations, products, services, and business relationships.
   - Integrate the findings into decisions and processes: policy, risk management, operations, and supplier management.
   - Track the effectiveness of the response.
   - Communicate how impacts are addressed, particularly to affected stakeholders.
3. Establish or participate in operational-level grievance mechanisms. The mechanisms should be legitimate, accessible, predictable, equitable, transparent, rights-compatible, and a source of continuous learning.
4. Where the organization has caused or contributed to an impact, provide for or cooperate in remediation.
5. Treat human rights as an ongoing practice, not a one-time project; revisit due diligence as the operating context changes.

## Controls and evidence

UNGPs evidence includes the human rights policy statement, the due diligence records (impact assessments, integration, tracking, communication), the operational grievance mechanism design and case records, the remediation records, and the periodic review of the practice. The evidence should be sufficient to demonstrate the policy is operational, not aspirational.

## Validation

Validation should confirm the policy is approved at the most senior level and communicated widely, the due diligence is performed across the value chain and covers salient human rights issues, the findings are integrated into decisions, tracking produces useful information, communications reach affected stakeholders, and grievance mechanisms are accessible and produce outcomes. External review (such as a UNGP Reporting Framework assessment) provides additional assurance.

## Failure correction

Common failure modes: the policy is approved but not communicated to stakeholders (corrective: communicate the policy externally and document the communication); due diligence is limited to the organization's own operations (corrective: extend the scope to the value chain); grievance mechanisms are nominal (corrective: assess the mechanism against the UNGP effectiveness criteria and remediate gaps); integration is reduced to a single decision at the top (corrective: require human rights inputs in operational decisions); tracking produces no useful information (corrective: define indicators tied to specific impacts and review them at appropriate intervals).

## Limitations

The UNGPs are a normative framework; they do not certify corporate human rights practice. The standard does not prescribe specific human rights impacts; the organization identifies its salient issues through due diligence. Sector regulations may impose specific obligations beyond the UNGPs.

## Scope note

This article summarizes project-neutral business use of the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights. It does not assert any specific organization's UNGP conformance or claim any certification outcome.
