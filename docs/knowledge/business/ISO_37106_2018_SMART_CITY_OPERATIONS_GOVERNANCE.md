# ISO 37106:2018 Smart City Operational Governance

## Purpose

Govern the application of ISO 37106:2018 (sustainable cities and communities — guidance on establishing smart city operational models) so that when the studio participates in smart city or place-based digital initiatives, the engagement is structured around the standard's operational model dimensions: collaborating across organizations, using data and technology deliberately, and delivering outcomes for the place rather than deploying technology for its own sake.

## Scope

Applies to studio engagements in smart city, municipal technology, and place-based digital service programmes. Covers the operational model dimensions, cross-organizational collaboration structures, and outcome management. Does not cover urban data platform engineering or procurement of municipal systems.

## Workflow

1. Anchor the engagement in the place's outcomes: define the city or district outcomes the programme serves (mobility, safety, environment, service access) before technology choices, and keep them as the evaluation frame.
2. Map the operational model per ISO 37106's dimensions: governance and organization, stakeholders and collaboration, assets and infrastructure, data and information, technology, and finance — mapping each dimension surfaces gaps a technology-first approach misses.
3. Establish cross-organizational collaboration deliberately: city departments, utilities, operators, and vendors need defined data-sharing and decision structures; collaboration left informal produces integration failures at deployment.
4. Govern data as a shared asset: identify what data the outcomes require, who holds it, under what terms it flows, and who is accountable for quality — before building platforms around undefined data.
5. Design for inclusion and accessibility: place-based services serve diverse populations; the operational model records how underserved groups access the outcomes, not just the digitally fluent.
6. Measure outcomes on a defined cadence: indicators tied to the place outcomes (not technology deployment metrics like sensor counts) reported to the programme's governance.
7. Manage the technology as replaceable: the operational model decouples outcomes from specific vendors and platforms so technology refresh does not reset the programme.

## Controls and evidence

- Place outcomes definition with evaluation criteria.
- Operational model mapping across the standard's dimensions with gaps identified.
- Collaboration structure records: parties, decision rights, data-sharing terms.
- Data governance records per shared dataset: holder, terms, quality accountability.
- Outcome indicator reports on cadence.
- Technology decoupling review records at refresh points.

## Validation

- Confirm the outcome indicators measure place outcomes, and current reporting shows movement against them.
- Confirm each shared dataset in the programme has recorded holder, terms, and quality accountability.
- Confirm the collaboration structure covers every organization the outcomes depend on.

## Failure correction

- **Technology deployment metrics masquerading as outcomes** → rebuild the indicator set around place outcomes and re-baseline reporting.
- **Dataset without governance terms in active use** → suspend the data flow until terms are recorded and agreed, then resume.
- **Collaboration gap causing integration failure** → escalate to programme governance, formalize the missing structure, and record the failure mode for future mappings.

## Limitations

- ISO 37106 provides the operational model guidance; implementation depends on municipal legal frameworks that vary by jurisdiction.
- Place programmes run on political timescales beyond vendor and studio control; governance structures must survive election and budget cycles.
- Outcome measurement for cities is statistically complex; indicators carry methodological caveats that reporting should preserve.
