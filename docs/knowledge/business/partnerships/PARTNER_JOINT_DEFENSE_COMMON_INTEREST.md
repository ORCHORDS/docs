# Partner Joint-Defense Common-Interest Discipline

When a patent is asserted against a jointly developed offering, the defending partners want to share counsel's work product, prior-art findings, and litigation strategy. The common-interest doctrine and formal joint-defense agreements make that sharing possible without waiving privilege and confidentiality protections, but only when the discipline is observed: a real common legal interest, documented agreement, controlled channels, and clean separation when interests diverge. This article defines that discipline for joint patent defense with partners.

## Scope

Covers common-interest privilege protection and joint-defense or common-interest agreements used when partners defend patent assertions, inter partes reviews, oppositions, or declaratory-judgment actions together. In scope: forming the common interest, the written agreement, information-sharing controls, member changes, and dissolution when interests diverge. Out of scope: litigation strategy itself, merits assessment, and antitrust limits on defense coordination among competitors. The accountable role is the **defense coordination counsel** for legal matters, supported by a **shared-workproduct custodian** for the operational controls.

## Workflow or implementation guidance

Confirm the common legal interest before any sharing occurs. The doctrine protects communications among parties who share a common legal interest and whose communications are made in furtherance of it. A commercial interest in the same outcome is not a legal interest; partners with cross-claims against each other, divergent indemnity positions, or separate accused products may fail the requirement. Assess and record whether the interest is genuinely common in law, not merely convenient.

Execute a written joint-defense agreement at the start. The agreement should identify the parties and their counsel, describe the common legal interest with specificity, state that communications are made in furtherance of that interest, restrict use of shared material to the defense, prohibit disclosure to non-parties without written consent, and address withdrawal. Verbally understood joint defense, or one established by a chain of forwarded emails, invites waiver arguments precisely when protection matters most.

Route all shared material through counsel. Engineers and product managers exchange technical facts under the ordinary confidentiality regime; analysis, strategy, work product, and attorney assessments move only between counsel, with distribution to business personnel controlled and logged. The most common waiver fact pattern is a technical lead forwarding counsel's analysis to a partner's engineer without routing.

Establish the shared-workproduct workspace with structured controls: named authorised users per party, role-based access, immutable access logs, watermarking of key documents, and a manifest of every item placed into the workspace with origin, date, and privilege designation. The manifest is the boundary document: what is in the workspace is within the arrangement, and what is not is outside it.

Handle member changes explicitly. Adding a party requires an accession to the agreement and a fresh common-interest confirmation as to that party. A member settling separately must exit cleanly: access revoked, materials returned or sequestered, and the remaining parties' positions protected against the departing member's new alignment — a settling defendant can become a trial witness against the others, and the agreement should have anticipated that.

Dissolve deliberately when interests diverge. If one partner licenses the asserted patent while others continue to fight, or indemnity positions harden into cross-claims, terminate the arrangement in writing, partition the workspace, and document what each party may retain and for what purpose. Do not let the arrangement decay, because a decayed arrangement leaves shared material in ambiguous status.

## Controls

- Written joint-defense agreement executed before first sharing, with defined parties, interest, purpose, use limits, and withdrawal mechanics.
- Counsel-routing rule for all analytical and strategic material, with a stated exception process for business-personnel access.
- Workspace access control with per-party user lists, role-based permissions, and immutable logging.
- Workspace manifest recording every item's origin, date, and privilege designation.
- Accession instrument and interest confirmation for each added party.
- Exit checklist for departing members: access revocation, sequestration or return of materials, and survivor-position protections.
- Periodic privilege audit sampling the workspace manifest against access logs to detect out-of-scope access.

## Validation evidence

The arrangement produces the executed agreement, accession and withdrawal instruments, the workspace manifest, access logs, counsel-routing records, and privilege audit findings. Validate by sampling ten manifest entries and confirming each trace to a counsel-mediated transfer with a logged date; then sample the access logs for a month and confirm every access corresponds to a currently authorised user, with any anomaly investigated and closed. Test the routing rule with a spot check: recent strategic documents should show counsel distribution points, not direct business-to-business transmission. At dissolution or member exit, validate that the exit checklist was executed and dated before the departing member's role changed; post-exit access is the single most damaging audit finding available to an opponent. Retain the agreement and manifest beyond matter close, because privilege assertions are often tested years later in related proceedings.

## Failure modes and correction

Typical failures: sharing technical analysis directly between engineering teams under a belief that the NDA covers it; admitting a new partner's subsidiary without confirming its common interest; letting a settled defendant retain workspace access; and merging joint-defense material into ordinary project repositories, where it loses both privilege structure and confidentiality marking. Correct routing failures by immediate counsel notification, quarantine of the misdirected material, and documentation of scope — a single misdirected document is survivable when disclosed and addressed; a pattern is not. For repository contamination, extract the material under counsel supervision, log the extraction, and assess whether recipients outside the arrangement obtained access. Where a member's interest has visibly diverged but no exit has occurred, treat dissolution as an urgent legal task, not a housekeeping one.

## Limitations

Common-interest doctrine recognition and scope vary by jurisdiction; some apply it narrowly, others require ongoing rather than merely anticipated litigation, and protection can be lost despite compliance. The discipline here manages risk, it does not guarantee privilege. The arrangement creates no joint liability position, no indemnity, and no coordination right beyond what the agreement states. Antitrust counsel should review any coordination among competitors, even in defense. Nothing in this article is legal advice on a specific matter.
