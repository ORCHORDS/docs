# University-Business Collaboration Governance

## Purpose

ISO/TS 44006:2023 provides guidance for universities, business partners, and other interested parties using the collaborative relationship principles from ISO/TR 44000 to improve university-business collaboration.

University-business relationships can combine research, education, commercialization, talent, facilities, funding, and intellectual property. Those differences make explicit governance especially important.

## Practical controls

1. **Define the collaboration purpose.** State whether the relationship concerns research, sponsored work, knowledge transfer, commercialization, education, talent development, shared infrastructure, or another outcome.
2. **Map contributions.** Record the people, facilities, data, intellectual property, funding, equipment, background knowledge, and decision authority each participant contributes.
3. **Set publication and confidentiality boundaries.** Academic publication goals and commercial confidentiality can conflict if they are not addressed before work begins.
4. **Clarify intellectual-property treatment.** Distinguish background IP, newly created results, licensing rights, publication rights, commercialization options, and improvement ownership.
5. **Define governance and escalation.** Identify relationship owners, technical or academic leads, milestone reviews, dispute paths, and who can approve material changes.
6. **Manage research and operational risk.** Address ethics, security, privacy, export controls, safety, conflicts of interest, and other obligations that apply to the actual collaboration.
7. **Plan for completion and continuation.** Decide how data, materials, equipment, unfinished work, students or staff, licenses, and future use of results will be handled when a project ends.

## Standards status

ISO lists ISO/TS 44006:2023 as published and confirmed in 2024, so the 2023 Edition 1 remains the current published technical specification.

## Scope note

This article summarizes general collaboration governance. Research ethics, intellectual-property law, publication rights, funding conditions, export controls, and university policies remain context-specific.
