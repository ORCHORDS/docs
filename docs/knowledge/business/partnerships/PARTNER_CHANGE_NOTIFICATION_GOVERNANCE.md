# Partner Change Notification Governance

## Purpose

Partner change notification is the process for surfacing material changes in a collaborative or supplier relationship early enough to reassess risk, contracts, access, dependencies, and customer impact before the change creates an unmanaged exposure.

NIST SP 800-161 Rev. 1 integrates cybersecurity supply-chain risk management into organization-wide risk management and emphasizes understanding, assessing, and mitigating risk throughout the supply chain. Change governance is therefore not only a communications task: a material change can alter the assumptions behind an earlier due-diligence, security, resilience, or sourcing decision.

## Changes that may require notification

Define notification triggers that fit the relationship. Depending on scope, they can include:

- ownership, control, merger, acquisition, or material organizational change;
- material changes to products, services, hosting, delivery model, or support arrangements;
- new or replaced critical subcontractors or fourth parties;
- changes to where or how sensitive data is processed, stored, transmitted, or accessed;
- significant security architecture or identity/access changes;
- loss, suspension, or material change of a required license, authorization, certification, or external assurance;
- material vulnerability, compromise, disruption, or resilience issue that changes the relationship risk;
- relocation or replacement of a critical operational dependency;
- significant financial distress or capacity change affecting continued delivery;
- planned end-of-life, deprecation, or withdrawal of a relied-upon product or service; and
- customer-impacting changes to service levels, interfaces, contractual commitments, or continuity arrangements.

Not every change needs the same treatment. Define materiality thresholds so routine operational changes do not overwhelm the review process while genuinely risk-changing events cannot be dismissed as ordinary maintenance.

## Notification expectations

A useful agreement or operating procedure should define:

1. **Who must notify whom.** Identify accountable contacts and an escalation route if the normal contact is unavailable.
2. **When notice is required.** Use advance notice where the change is planned and prompt notice where the event is unplanned.
3. **Minimum content.** Describe the change, expected timing, affected services or data, known customer or security impact, dependencies, rollback or continuity measures, and any decision required from the receiving organization.
4. **Evidence requirements.** Identify which diagrams, risk assessments, test results, assurance reports, migration plans, or other records may be needed for review.
5. **Decision rights.** State whether the receiving organization can approve, reject, condition, defer, or require remediation before the change proceeds where the contract permits that control.
6. **Emergency handling.** Allow urgent protective changes while still requiring retrospective notice and evidence when advance approval would create unacceptable risk.

## Risk reassessment workflow

After a material notification:

1. confirm the affected relationship, services, assets, data, and dependencies;
2. compare the new state with the assumptions recorded during onboarding or the previous review;
3. reassess cybersecurity, privacy, resilience, compliance, concentration, operational, and commercial risk as applicable;
4. determine whether contracts, data-processing terms, access rights, business-continuity plans, or technical controls must change;
5. identify customer or downstream-partner notifications that may be required;
6. record conditions, remediation actions, owners, and deadlines;
7. decide whether the relationship can continue unchanged, continue with conditions, reduce scope, pause, or exit; and
8. update the partner risk record and the date of the next review.

## Evidence record

A change record can capture:

- date and source of notification;
- affected partner and relationship scope;
- description and effective date of the change;
- systems, services, data, subcontractors, or locations affected;
- prior assumptions invalidated by the change;
- risk reassessment result;
- contractual or technical actions required;
- customer or stakeholder impact;
- approvals, conditions, and unresolved issues;
- completion evidence; and
- follow-up review date.

## NIST publication status

Use the current publication when citing NIST supply-chain guidance. The May 2022 edition of NIST SP 800-161 Rev. 1 was withdrawn on November 1, 2024 and superseded by the updated SP 800-161 Rev. 1 publication that incorporates updates through November 1, 2024. Do not cite the withdrawn edition as though it were the current version.

## Scope note

This article describes reusable change-governance practice. Contractual notice periods, approval rights, regulated-event reporting, and customer-notification duties depend on the agreement, jurisdiction, sector, and facts. It does not assert that any particular partner, supplier, or relationship has changed or been approved.
