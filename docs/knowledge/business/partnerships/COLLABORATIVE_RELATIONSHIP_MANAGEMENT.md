# Collaborative Relationship Management

## Purpose

Collaborative relationships should be managed as explicit business relationships with defined objectives, governance, responsibilities, information-sharing boundaries, and review points rather than as informal goodwill arrangements.

## Current standards context

ISO 44001:2017 remains the published international standard for collaborative business relationship management. ISO states that it applies to organizations of all sizes and can be used for individual relationships, alliances, partnerships, joint ventures, networks, and broader supply-chain relationships. ISO also shows a second edition, ISO/DIS 44001, under development; until a replacement is published, the 2017 edition remains the current published standard.

## Practical controls

A reusable partnership-management process should:

1. define the business purpose and expected value of the relationship;
2. identify decision rights, accountable owners, and escalation routes;
3. document what information may be shared and under what safeguards;
4. identify dependencies, commitments, and measurable outcomes;
5. review relationship health and emerging risks at agreed intervals;
6. maintain an agreed change-management process; and
7. preserve an orderly exit path if collaboration no longer creates sufficient value.

## Governance principle

Relationship governance should be proportional to risk and importance. A low-risk referral arrangement does not require the same controls as a joint venture or a partner with access to sensitive information, but both benefit from clear ownership and documented expectations.
