# Partner Performance Review

## Purpose

A partner-performance review should test whether a collaborative relationship is still delivering its intended outcomes, whether risk has changed, and whether renewal, remediation, expansion, or exit decisions are supported by evidence rather than relationship history alone.

ISO 44001:2017 remains the published international standard for collaborative business relationship management. ISO describes it as a framework for the effective identification, development, and management of collaborative relationships across organizations of all sizes. ISO 44002:2019 remains the published implementation-guidance standard, although ISO currently marks it for revision.

## Review dimensions

Use measures that fit the relationship rather than forcing every partnership into the same scorecard. Typical dimensions include:

- delivery against agreed commitments and milestones;
- service quality and defect or rework trends;
- customer or stakeholder outcomes where relevant;
- financial performance and material commercial exceptions;
- security, privacy, compliance, and operational risk signals;
- change-management effectiveness;
- responsiveness to incidents and corrective actions;
- dependency or concentration risk;
- innovation, improvement, or other value expected from the collaboration; and
- relationship-health indicators such as escalation volume, unresolved disputes, and decision latency.

## Review method

1. **Confirm the review scope.** Identify the agreement, workstreams, period, measures, and material changes since the previous review.
2. **Use agreed evidence.** Prefer records, service measurements, customer outcomes, incident data, audit findings, and documented commitments over anecdotal impressions.
3. **Separate isolated events from trends.** A single incident may require action without proving systemic underperformance; recurring failures can indicate a structural problem even when individual incidents are closed.
4. **Record causes as well as symptoms.** Distinguish partner-controlled issues from shared dependencies, customer-driven changes, external events, and unclear requirements.
5. **Agree corrective actions.** Give each action an accountable owner, due date, expected result, and verification method.
6. **Reassess risk and dependency.** Expansion, new access, additional data sharing, or increased operational reliance can change the relationship's risk profile even when service levels remain acceptable.
7. **Tie decisions to evidence.** Renewal, scope growth, commercial changes, remediation, or exit should reference the documented review rather than informal expectations.
8. **Close the loop.** Verify whether previous actions were completed and whether they produced the intended improvement.

## Evidence record

A reusable review record can capture:

- review period and scope;
- agreed measures and actual results;
- material incidents, exceptions, and trends;
- changed risks or dependencies;
- unresolved disputes or assumptions;
- corrective actions, owners, and due dates;
- decision on continue, remediate, expand, reduce, or exit; and
- date and trigger for the next review.

## Standards status

- **ISO 44001:2017** is still published and current, with Amendment 1:2024. ISO also shows a replacement edition under development. Do not present the draft replacement as the published standard.
- **ISO 44002:2019** remains published implementation guidance, but ISO marks it as an international standard to be revised. A second edition is under development.
- **ISO 44003:2021**, which gives collaborative-relationship guidance for micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises, is published and was under systematic review in 2026.

These status notes matter when a review record names a standard or edition: cite the edition actually used and avoid implying that a draft has already replaced the published document.

## Scope note

This article describes reusable performance-review practice. It does not assert certification to ISO 44001, compliance with a specific contract, or that any particular partner has been reviewed or approved.
