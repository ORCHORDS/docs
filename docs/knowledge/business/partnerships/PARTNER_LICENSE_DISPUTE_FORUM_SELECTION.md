# Partner License Dispute Forum Selection

Where a partner licence dispute gets decided shapes everything else: who hears interim relief applications, what disclosure the parties must endure, whether the underlying licence terms stay confidential, and how fast and enforceable the outcome is. Forum selection is negotiated when the licence is signed, not when the dispute arises, so it must be chosen with the disputes that licence could plausibly generate in mind. This article covers arbitration versus court for licence disputes, seat, interim relief, and confidentiality.

## Scope

Covers forum-selection analysis for cross-partner licence disputes: choosing between institutional arbitration and national courts, selecting the seat and governing law separately, preserving interim and injunctive relief, and protecting confidential information in the chosen forum. In scope: clause drafting considerations, institution and rule selection, multi-party and multi-contract complexity, and post-award enforcement. Out of scope: the merits of any dispute type and the cure-window process that precedes termination. The accountable role is the **forum clause owner**, counsel-led, with input from the commercial teams who will live with the choice.

## Workflow or implementation guidance

Match the forum to the dispute profile the licence actually creates. Disputes over royalty calculation, audit rights, and scope interpretation suit arbitration: they are contractual, often technical, and benefit from a tribunal that can be given relevant industry expertise. Disputes requiring injunctions against ongoing unauthorised use, validity challenges to registered rights, or binding non-parties favour courts. A licence that sits inside a stack of related agreements — partnership, escrow, supply — generates multi-contract disputes that arbitrators can only hear with care; check whether the clauses across the family are aligned, because mismatched clauses split one commercial dispute across two forums.

Select the seat as a distinct decision from the institution and the governing law. The seat's law governs the arbitration's supervision — challenge grounds, tribunal powers, court assistance — while the institution supplies procedure and the governing law supplies the substantive rules. A neutral seat with a modern arbitration statute and a track record of enforcing arbitration agreements is worth more than institutional familiarity. Confirm the seat's courts are competent for the support role: appointing tribunals on default, granting evidence assistance, and staying parallel proceedings.

Preserve interim relief explicitly. The historical rule that arbitrators could not grant interim measures has given way in most seats, but the clause should still state that parties may seek urgent interim or conservatory measures from a competent court without waiving arbitration. For partner disputes, the urgent scenario is usually continued use of licensed technology pending resolution or preservation of audit records; decide in advance which court would hear such applications and put that in the clause.

Engineer confidentiality deliberately. Arbitration is privately run but not automatically confidential — institutional rules vary on the confidentiality of submissions, evidence, and awards, and the seat's default law may or may not impose confidentiality. Where the licence terms, royalties, or technical disclosures are sensitive, add an express confidentiality agreement within the arbitration clause covering hearings, documents, and the award, with defined exceptions for legal advice, enforcement, and regulatory duties. Court proceedings are presumptively public; protective orders exist but are discretionary, which is often the decisive factor for parties to a licence whose commercial terms could not survive publication.

Plan enforcement before choosing. An arbitral award travels under the New York Convention's framework across a large set of states, with narrow refusal grounds; a court judgment's recognition depends on bilateral or regional arrangements that vary widely. Map where the counterparty's enforceable assets and operations sit, and prefer the award route where those locations recognise awards reliably. For a counterparty concentrated in one state with reliable courts, that state's courts may be the simpler answer.

Draft the clause tightly: institutional arbitration under named rules, seat and language stated, number and method of appointing arbitrators, and — for technical licence disputes — an express power for the tribunal to appoint an independent expert on royalty or technical questions. Vague clauses combining arbitration and courts in the alternative generate jurisdictional litigation before the merits begin.

## Controls

- Forum decision record per licence family: dispute profile, options considered, seat, institution, governing law, and the reasoning retained.
- Clause-consistency check across the related agreement stack, ensuring one dispute lands in one forum.
- Interim-relief carve-out wording in every arbitration clause, naming the competent court class.
- Express confidentiality annex covering submissions, evidence, hearings, and awards, with enumerated exceptions.
- Enforcement map per significant counterparty: asset locations and recognition posture for the chosen route.
- Expert-appointment power stated in the clause for calculation-heavy disputes.
- Periodic review of the clause library against institutional rule changes and seat-law developments.

## Validation evidence

Forum selection produces the executed clause, the decision record behind it, the clause-consistency review, and the enforcement map. Validate by extracting the clause from each executed agreement in a partner stack and running the consistency check mechanically: seat, institution, governing law, and interim-relief wording should either match or diverge deliberately with a recorded reason. Test the decision record against the dispute profile: if the licence now generates audit disputes it was not expected to, the record shows the assumption and the review date. For confidentiality, confirm the annex's exceptions cover enforcement and regulatory disclosure, since an annex without exceptions fails at the first enforcement filing. Where institutions have revised rules since execution, record the version that governs each agreement — institutional rules are typically fixed at commencement, and the version matters for emergency arbitrator availability and document-production defaults.

## Failure modes and correction

Typical failures: pathological clauses that neither submit to arbitration nor clearly litigate; a partnership agreement choosing courts while the licence chooses arbitration, splitting the same facts; seats chosen for neutrality whose supervisory courts are slow or interventionist; confidentiality assumed from arbitration's privacy and discovered absent at the evidentiary stage; governing law unstated, leaving the tribunal to choose it; and interim-relief availability assumed in arbitration without a carve-out, forcing a waiver argument at the worst moment. Correct pathological clauses by amendment at renewal, or by a submission agreement when a dispute is imminent but not yet commenced. Resolve split-forum stacks through a joinder or consolidation mechanism the chosen rules provide, agreed before either proceeding advances. Where confidentiality protection proved weaker than assumed, apply for the forum's available protective measures immediately and adjust the clause library so the next agreement carries the express annex.

## Limitations

Arbitrability of registered-rights validity questions varies by jurisdiction; some matters must reach courts regardless of the clause. Interim-measure availability and enforcement differ by seat and institution, and rules change. This article supports clause strategy and operational review, not advice on any pending dispute; counsel must assess specific enforcement and jurisdictional questions case by case.
