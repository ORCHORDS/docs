# Partner Standard-Essential Patent Disclosure Governance

When partners participate together in collaborative standards work, each participant carries a duty to disclose patents it holds that are or may become essential to the specification under development. Disclosure governance is the internal control set that decides what gets disclosed, verifies completeness, records the disclosure, and keeps it synchronized with the patent portfolio as claims change. Failures here are not drafting errors: undisclosed essential patents are a leading source of standards litigation and regulatory enforcement, and partners who relied on the specification are the ones injured.

## Scope

Covers the internal governance for standard-essential patent disclosure when personnel participate in standards-development organisations, industry forums, and consortium specification work, including participation seconded through or coordinated with partners. In scope: disclosure-duty mapping across forums, portfolio review before and during participation, disclosure execution, update obligations as claims amend, and records. Out of scope: the content of FRAND commitments and their scope, treated in a separate article, and general consortium IP allocation. The accountable role is the **SEP disclosure governor**, typically IP counsel supported by the standards participation lead.

## Workflow or implementation guidance

Map each forum's disclosure duty precisely before participation begins. Duty sources differ: some organisations impose disclosure of patents that are or may become essential, on a continuing basis; some require disclosure of patent applications; some tie the duty to specific technical contributions or working-group sessions rather than to the specification as a whole. Record, per forum, the trigger event, the subject matter to be disclosed, the form of disclosure, the recipient, and the continuing-update obligation. A single organisation-wide disclosure policy applied uniformly across forums will both over-disclose in some and under-disclose in others.

Run a portfolio-to-draft mapping at defined checkpoints: at first participation, when the draft specification enters feature freeze or balloting milestones, and before each publication. The mapping compares the organisation's granted claims and pending applications against the draft's mandatory technical requirements. Pending applications need a good-faith judgment of potential essentiality; document the basis for each may-be-essential call, including who made it and against which draft section, because that judgment is what later enforcement will test.

Route disclosures through the forum's required channel and capture receipts. Disclosures made verbally in working sessions frequently do not satisfy written duties; where oral disclosure is permitted, follow the forum's confirmation procedure immediately. File the disclosure text, the draft version it references, the date, and the forum's acknowledgment together — the bundle that proves both the fact and the content of disclosure.

Maintain disclosure currency. Patent claims change during prosecution; a disclosure that matched the published application can cease to match the granted claims, and new claims can become essential after grant. Schedule re-mapping at grant of each application in technologies relevant to active specifications, and at amendment of claims in ways that alter coverage of the specification's mandatory elements.

Coordinate partner-side participation. Where the organisation's engineers attend through a partner delegation or contribute technology that the partner then proposes, establish in writing who bears the disclosure duty for jointly developed material. Ambiguity here leaves both parties exposed: the standard-setter sees undisclosed essentiality, and each participant assumed the other disclosed.

Train contributing engineers on the disclosure trigger as it affects their own conduct: describing a technical solution in a working session can create patentability bars and essentiality facts simultaneously. Engineers do not perform essentiality analysis, but they must route their contributions through the mapping process before proposal.

## Controls

- Forum duty register: one entry per forum with trigger, subject, form, recipient, and update cadence.
- Checkpoint mapping reports: portfolio versus draft specification, dated, with per-claim essentiality judgments and their authors.
- Disclosure file per item: disclosure text, draft version referenced, submission receipt, and forum acknowledgment.
- Grant-trigger re-mapping hook in the docketing system for applications in specification-relevant technologies.
- Partner-participation protocol allocating disclosure responsibility for joint material.
- Engineer contribution gate: no technical proposal leaves the organisation without a mapping pass recorded against it.
- Annual completeness audit sampling recent grants and amendments against active specifications to find disclosures that should have been made and were not.

## Validation evidence

Governance produces the forum duty register, checkpoint mapping reports with judgments and authors, disclosure submissions with receipts, grant-trigger re-mapping records, partner protocols, and audit findings. Validate by selecting the current published specification version and re-deriving the organisation's essentiality position from the mapping report: every patent or application judged essential or potentially essential should have a corresponding disclosure on file, and every non-essential judgment should record its reasoning. Sample in reverse: pick three recent grants in relevant technology classes and confirm each triggered a re-mapping decision, including a reasoned decision that no disclosure was required. Trace one engineer proposal from working-session record to mapping pass. Where disclosures were made, confirm the text identifies the patent or application with enough specificity for the forum's purposes; a disclosure that names a patent family without identifying claims may not satisfy strict duties.

## Failure modes and correction

Recurring failures: mapping performed once at joining and never refreshed as drafts evolved; may-be-essential judgments made by a single engineer without documentation; disclosures submitted to a working-group chair rather than the forum's official register; pending applications ignored on the theory that only grants matter, contrary to many forum rules; and joint material with partners left unassigned. Correct missed disclosures immediately on discovery — most forums permit late disclosure, and prompt action materially improves the position compared to disclosure first surfacing in litigation. For undocumented judgments, reconstruct the analysis with counsel from contemporaneous records rather than inventing it, and record the reconstruction as such. Where a partner failed to disclose joint material, invoke the participation protocol's allocation clause and assess exposure jointly before any enforcement step.

## Limitations

Essentiality judgment for pending applications is inherently probabilistic; governance improves process quality, not certainty. Forum rules differ materially in scope, timing, and sanctions, and the register must track the specific rules rather than a general standard. Disclosure duties interact with antitrust and competition-law exposure, which varies by jurisdiction and requires counsel. This article describes control discipline and does not opine on any organisation's compliance posture.
