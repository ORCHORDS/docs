# Partner Licensee Cure-Window Analysis

Terminating a licence for breach is only clean when the breach was real, noticed properly, and left uncured through a fairly run cure window. When the licensee is a partner — simultaneously a collaborator on other workstreams — the termination decision carries relationship consequences that make disciplined cure-window management more, not less, important. This article defines good-faith compliance scoring for inbound licensees: measuring breach, running the notice and cure mechanics, and deciding termination on evidence.

## Scope

Covers the licensor-side process for managing compliance by partner licensees under technology and software licences: detecting breaches, categorising them by cure type, issuing compliant notices, scoring cure efforts in good faith, and executing or withholding termination. In scope: payment defaults, under-reporting and audit-triggered shortfalls, scope and field violations, sublicensing beyond rights, and notice/cooperation failures. Out of scope: the licensee's own defensive strategy and the choice of forum for resulting disputes. The accountable role is the **licence compliance manager**, with termination authority held above the relationship owner.

## Workflow or implementation guidance

Detect breaches through designed channels, not accident. Royalty reporting arrives on a calendar; audit rights surface under-reporting when exercised; field and scope violations surface through market intelligence, channel conflict reports, or the partner's own marketing; sublicensing violations surface in sublicensee announcements. Register each suspected breach on discovery with date, source, and preliminary categorisation, and freeze the record before contacting the partner.

Categorise by cure type, because the agreement distinguishes them. Curable breaches — payment shortfalls, reporting errors, late reports, unauthorised-but-innocent sublicensing — carry a defined cure window from notice. Non-curable or notice-and-termination breaches — challenge to licence validity, assignment without consent, insolvency events — often bypass cure entirely or carry different mechanics. Read the actual clause: cure windows range from ten to sixty days, some run from receipt of notice, others from dispatch, and some agreements require a second notice before termination rights mature for payment defaults.

Draft the breach notice to terminate-proof standard: identify the agreement and clause breached, state the facts with dates and amounts where quantifiable, specify the cure required, state the window and its start event, and reserve rights without threatening termination the agreement does not yet permit. Send through the notice channel the agreement requires — post to a registered address, email to named contacts, or both — and retain proof of dispatch and receipt. A defective notice restarts the window and signals weakness.

Score the cure effort in good faith while the window runs. Good faith does not mean accepting promises; it means evaluating the cure actually delivered against the cure required. For payment: funds received in cleared form within the window, or a disputed portion escrowed with a documented dispute basis. For under-reporting: corrected reports delivered, with a reconciliation method the licensor can verify, not a new self-serving total. For scope violations: cessation documented, plus disposition of infringing activity's proceeds where the agreement requires accounting. Partial cures — payment of half, corrected reports for some periods — are not cures; record them and assess whether the agreement treats partial cure as no cure.

Decide termination on the scored record. Where cure failed, confirm no waiver has accumulated: repeated earlier breaches tolerated without notice can undermine a termination that arrives without warning on the third occurrence. Where waiver risk exists, issue a fresh notice reasserting strict compliance going forward and allow a reasonable period before acting on the next breach. Where the breach is cured, close the record with what was cured, when, and any residual follow-up, because a cured breach that recurs changes the good-faith calculus on the next window.

Coordinate across workstreams before acting: termination of the licence may trigger cross-termination or wind-down rights in the partnership agreement, escrow release triggers, or transition obligations to shared customers. Sequence these deliberately; the compliance decision is legal, the sequencing is a joint legal-commercial decision.

## Controls

- Breach register: every suspected breach logged on discovery with source, categorisation, and evidence retained before partner contact.
- Cure-type matrix derived from the executed agreement: breach class, notice requirement, window length, window start event, repeat-breach rule.
- Notice template and channel checklist enforcing the agreement's notice clause exactly.
- Cure scoring sheet with per-element pass criteria: amount, form, timing, completeness.
- Waiver ledger recording every historical tolerance of late or defective performance, reviewed before any termination decision.
- Termination authority gate: no termination issues without legal review and sign-off above the relationship owner.
- Cross-workstream impact checklist covering partnership cross-termination, escrow, and customer-transition effects, completed before termination is effective.

## Validation evidence

The process produces the breach register, notice drafts with dispatch and receipt proof, cure scoring sheets, correspondence, payment records, corrected reports and their reconciliations, waiver-ledger extracts supporting the decision, and the termination or closure instrument. Validate by selecting a terminated licence and reconstructing the chain without gaps: discovery date, notice date, receipt evidence, window start, cure deadline, scoring date, decision date — each link dated and sourced. Confirm the cure window length used matches the executed agreement, not a template. For a cured breach, confirm the scoring sheet records what was delivered against each required element and the closure decision names any residual items. Reverse-test the waiver ledger: sample tolerated historical breaches and confirm each is recorded, because an unrecorded tolerance is what opposing counsel will produce. Where the agreement required two notices for repeat defaults, confirm both were sent and received.

## Failure modes and correction

Frequent failures: notices sent by the wrong channel or to superseded contacts, restarting or voiding the window; termination threatened before the window closes, converting a clean position into a repudiation argument; partial cure accepted informally with no record, creating a waiver argument for the next round; the relationship owner quietly extending windows out of commercial sympathy without authority, destroying the record's integrity; and termination executed while cure funds were in transit. Correct channel errors immediately by re-sending compliantly and running the window from proper receipt, recording the restart honestly. Where informal extensions occurred, document the extension, its scope, and the reassertion of strict compliance. Where termination was wrongful, prompt reinstatement on documented terms is far cheaper than the dispute. A cure that arrived late but was refused without scoring deserves re-examination before positions harden.

## Limitations

Cure-window interpretation, waiver, and good-faith standards are jurisdiction- and clause-specific; this process discipline does not predict legal outcomes. Audit rights underlying under-reporting detection depend on the agreement's scope and are often narrower than licensors assume. Relationship consequences of termination can outweigh the breach's value, which is a commercial judgment outside this article. Counsel must review any termination decision on a live agreement.
