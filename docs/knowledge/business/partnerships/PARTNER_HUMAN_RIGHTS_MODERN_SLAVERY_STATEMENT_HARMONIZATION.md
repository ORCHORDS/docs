# Partner Human-Rights Modern Slavery Statement Harmonization

## Scope

This article governs how an organization coordinates its modern-slavery and human-rights statement with the comparable statements its partners publish. The scope is the harmonization of content, period, approval, and disclosure across the partnership, with reference to the transparency framing of the UK Modern Slavery Act 2015 and the statement requirements under section 54.

Section 54 of the Act requires commercial organizations that carry on a business or part of a business in the UK, supply goods or services, and meet a size threshold (turnover or, in some interpretations, other criteria) to prepare a slavery and human trafficking statement for each financial year, publish it on the organization's website (with a prominent link from the homepage), and (where the organization is in scope of the post-2020 amendments) have it approved by the board and signed by a director. The Act applies to the organization's own statement; harmonization with a partner's statement is a governance discipline, not a substitution for compliance.

The scope of this article is the harmonization of the disclosure content and cadence between two organizations' statements where they relate to the same partnership, and the governance discipline that makes the harmonization possible. It does not constitute legal advice, does not establish that any particular statement satisfies section 54, and does not address the wider universe of human-rights due diligence under the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights or equivalent instruments.

## Workflow or implementation guidance

1. Maintain a single point of accountability for the statement on each side of the partnership. The accountable owner coordinates the content of this organization's statement, the disclosure cadence, and the harmonization with the partner.
2. Map the partnership against the six disclosure areas commonly required by section 54 (as amended): the organization's structure and supply chains, policies in relation to slavery and human trafficking, due-diligence processes, the parts of the business and supply chains where there is a risk, the organization's effectiveness in ensuring that slavery and human trafficking is not taking place, and (for in-scope organizations) the training available to staff. Identify which of these areas the partnership affects.
3. Identify the partnership-specific facts that should appear in each party's statement: the partnership's purpose, the categories of workers involved, the geographies in scope, the due-diligence findings, the remediation actions where relevant, and the training delivered. Generic assertions about anti-slavery policy are not a substitute for partnership-specific facts.
4. Coordinate the reporting period. Statements are annual; the parties should agree on the financial year used for the partnership narrative, so that the two statements describe the same period. A period mismatch makes the disclosures harder to compare and dilutes their value.
5. Agree on the language used for the partnership. The two organizations may use different vocabulary (modern slavery, human trafficking, forced labour, child labour, human rights); harmonization does not require identical wording, but it does require that the words are not used in ways that contradict each other.
6. Synchronize approval timelines where the parties' statements are mutually referenced. A statement that refers to a partner's prior-period statement should not be approved after the partner has published a new statement that contradicts it.
7. Treat the statement as a public commitment, not as an internal document. Once published, the partnership narrative can be quoted back by stakeholders, regulators, investors, and customers; it should be written with that audience in mind.
8. Reconcile the statement against the partnership's actual practice. The statement should describe what is done, not what is intended. Where the practice is weaker than the statement, the practice is the priority for correction.
9. Update the statement when the partnership changes materially — new geographies, new suppliers in scope, new categories of workers, new findings from due diligence, new remediation actions, new training delivered.
10. Retain evidence supporting the statement. Section 54 requires the statement to be a true and fair description, and the underlying records (due-diligence assessments, supplier audits, training records, remediation outcomes) are the basis for that description.

## Controls

The harmonization discipline requires controls on both the content and the process. A content control ensures that partnership-specific facts are consistent between the two statements. A period control ensures the reporting period is synchronized. An approval control ensures that statements are approved by the bodies each organization requires (board, director, governance committee) before publication. A retention control ensures that supporting evidence is retained for the period required by each organization's record-retention policy and by the partnership's own commitments. A change-management control ensures that material changes to the partnership trigger a statement update. A consistency control ensures that the language used in the statement is consistent with the language used in other public disclosures (privacy notice, supplier code of conduct, anti-bribery statement) on the same topics.

## Validation evidence

Validation evidence includes the published statement for each financial year, the partnership-specific narrative and the underlying facts, the board or director approval record, the retained due-diligence assessments and remediation records, the training records, the change-management records, the synchronization records between the parties, and any reconciliation between the statement and the actual partnership practice. Evidence should be sufficient for a reader to trace any specific claim in the statement back to the underlying record.

## Failure modes and correction

Common failure modes include the partnership narrative being generic rather than specific, the reporting period being out of sync between the two parties, the statement being approved without review of the partnership facts, the statement being inconsistent with the partner's statement on the same period, and the underlying practice diverging from the statement over time. Another failure is the statement being treated as a one-off publication rather than as a continuing disclosure that should be updated as the partnership changes.

Correction requires reconciling the statement with the underlying practice, updating the affected statement (and the partner's statement where the discrepancy is material), approving and publishing the corrected version, and where the failure has caused a misleading disclosure, considering whether further notification or stakeholder communication is required. Repeated failure indicates that the harmonization discipline has been bypassed and should be escalated to the governance function responsible for the organization's public disclosures.

## Limitations

This article covers the harmonization of modern-slavery and human-rights statements between partners. It does not constitute legal advice on the application of section 54 to a specific organization, does not establish that any particular statement satisfies the Act, and does not address the broader human-rights due-diligence framework. The Act has been amended since its enactment, and other jurisdictions have enacted or proposed comparable legislation; the article does not address those regimes.
