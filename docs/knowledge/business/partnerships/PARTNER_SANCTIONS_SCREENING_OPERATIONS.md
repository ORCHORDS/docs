# Partner Sanctions-Screening Operations

## Purpose and scope

This control governs onboarding, ownership changes, payment changes, transaction release, and periodic refresh. Its purpose is to turn an informal cross-organization activity into a bounded, reviewable process. It does not establish that a party is compliant, insured, licensed, safe, or approved. Owners must adapt the process to the contract, jurisdictions, sector, facts, and the authority of each participating entity for partner sanctions-screening operations.

Use the control for the legal entities and activities named in the relationship record. Identify exclusions explicitly so that adjacent services are not assumed to be covered. Risk determines depth: a low-impact case may justify a documented check, while ambiguity, high impact, unusual geography, or vulnerable people may require specialist and independent review for partner sanctions-screening operations.

## Controls and responsibilities

The relationship owner coordinates but should not be the sole judge when the outcome materially benefits that owner. Evidence providers must not approve their own exceptions. Define incompatible duties, escalation routes, substitute approvers, and emergency authority. Access to sensitive case material follows least privilege, and onward disclosure requires a stated purpose and lawful basis for partner sanctions-screening operations.

The governing agreement or operating charter should cover cooperation, confidentiality, response expectations, record access, correction duties, and consequences of material non-cooperation. Every exception needs a reason, owner, compensating safeguard, expiry, and approval. Automation must report feed failures and stale inputs: no result from a broken check is not a successful result for partner sanctions-screening operations.

## Evidence and validation

Retain search date, official-list version, identifiers, candidate matches, ownership analysis, reasoning, and disposition. Strong evidence is attributable, dated, scoped, reproducible, and linked to the decision it supports. Record assumptions, contradictory information, unavailable records, and how those limitations affected the outcome. A screenshot, certificate, or self-attestation may support a narrow fact but must not be described as assurance beyond its actual scope.

Validation should trace a sample from initiation to source evidence, decision, system enforcement, and closure. Include rejected cases, exceptions, and failed automated checks, not only successful transactions. Reconcile registers against source systems and investigate missing identifiers, expired approvals, unexplained overrides, or records that cannot be reproduced. Trend recurring exceptions because repetition may show that the designed process does not match operations for partner sanctions-screening operations.

## Failure handling

When the control fails, place a reversible hold, prevent prohibited disclosure, seek specialist advice, and release only with authority. The case owner records containment, affected scope, notification decisions, remediation owners, deadlines, and restart criteria. Preserve relevant records and metadata; do not rewrite history by replacing an original request or decision with a corrected version.

Repeated failure, obstruction, falsified evidence, or missed remediation triggers broader relationship-risk review. Available responses may include enhanced monitoring, reduced scope, a temporary hold, independent investigation, contractual remedies, responsible exit, or external notification where qualified advice establishes a duty. Commercial urgency does not itself justify bypassing a safety, legality, or integrity hold for partner sanctions-screening operations.

## Review questions

- Are the responsible legal entity and covered activities unambiguous?
- Is each material conclusion traceable to current evidence?
- Are authority and independence adequate for the decision?
- Do exceptions expire and receive tested follow-up?
- Would stale data or a failed integration generate a visible alert?
- Are containment, escalation, restart, and closure criteria defined?
- Are protected records shared, retained, and disposed of appropriately?
