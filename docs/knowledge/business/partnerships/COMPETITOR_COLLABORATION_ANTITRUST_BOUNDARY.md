# Competitor Collaboration Antitrust Boundary

## Purpose

Businesses can have legitimate reasons to collaborate with competitors through joint ventures, alliances, trade groups, standards work, research, purchasing, distribution, or other arrangements. In the United States, those collaborations can also create antitrust risk when they reduce independent competition or enable prohibited coordination.

## Core boundary

The Federal Trade Commission explains that collaboration among competitors can be procompetitive, but risk increases when firms stop acting independently or gain the ability to exercise market power together. Agreements such as price fixing, bid rigging, and market division are especially serious antitrust concerns.

A partnership label does not remove the need to assess the competitive relationship between the parties.

## Practical safeguards

1. Define the legitimate business purpose and scope of the collaboration before exchanging competitively sensitive information.
2. Limit shared information to what is reasonably necessary for that purpose.
3. Keep independent decisions on pricing, bidding, customers, markets, output, hiring, and strategy outside the collaboration unless qualified antitrust counsel has assessed the arrangement.
4. Use access controls, clean teams, aggregation, delayed data, or other safeguards where detailed competitor information is genuinely needed.
5. Document governance for meetings and working groups so the collaboration does not become a channel for unrelated competitive coordination.
6. Escalate unexpected requests for current or future pricing, costs, bids, customer allocation, market plans, wages, or similarly sensitive competitive information.
7. Review material scope changes because a collaboration that was reasonable for one activity may present different risks when expanded.
8. Obtain qualified legal advice for competitor collaborations that could materially affect competition; fact-specific antitrust analysis cannot be replaced by a generic checklist.

## Current U.S. guidance status

The FTC and U.S. Department of Justice withdrew the 2000 *Antitrust Guidelines for Collaborations Among Competitors* in December 2024 and stated that those guidelines no longer provided reliable guidance on how the agencies assess competitor collaborations.

In February 2026 the agencies opened a public inquiry to consider updated guidance, including questions about joint licensing, information and data sharing, algorithmic pricing, labor collaborations, joint ventures, and alliances. The comment period was later extended to May 21, 2026.

Accordingly, the withdrawn 2000 guidelines should not be presented as current agency guidance. Current statutes, case law, agency materials, and qualified legal advice should be used for live decisions.

## Scope note

This article is U.S.-focused general compliance guidance, not legal advice and not a statement that a particular collaboration is lawful or unlawful. Competition-law rules vary by jurisdiction and depend heavily on the facts.
