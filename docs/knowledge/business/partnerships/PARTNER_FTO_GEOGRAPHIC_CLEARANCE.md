# Partner Freedom-to-Operate Clearance

A jointly built offering inherits patent exposure in every territory where either partner makes, sells, offers for sale, uses, or imports it. Freedom-to-operate clearance run only in the partners' home markets misses the exposure: patents are territorial, so the product that is clean at home can infringe in the market it was actually built to enter. This article defines territory-by-territory FTO clearance for jointly developed offerings — scoping, search, opinion formation, risk treatment per territory, and maintenance as the portfolio and the product both change.

## Scope

Covers FTO analysis for jointly developed products, services, and processes commercialised with a partner across multiple jurisdictions. In scope: territory scoping driven by commercial plans, per-territory search and analysis, opinion documentation, risk treatment decisions, and clearance maintenance. Out of scope: validity challenges strategy, SEP and FRAND questions, and clearance for standards-essential technology, which follow their own governance articles. The accountable role is the **FTO programme owner** in IP, coordinating with both partners' patent counsel per territory and the commercial owner of the launch plan.

## Workflow or implementation guidance

Scope territories from the commercial plan, not from geography. Clearance is needed where the legally significant acts occur: manufacture locations, import routes, offer and sale markets, and use locations for services. Rank territories by expected revenue and by litigation climate; a phased plan can clear launch territories first and follow-on markets later, provided the sequencing decision is recorded and the follow-on work is scheduled rather than aspirational.

Define the product claims precisely before searching. The search target is the set of technical features as they will be practiced, including alternatives — where the design offers two implementations, both need clearance, because switching implementations mid-lifecycle without re-clearing is a common failure. Freeze a feature description document per product version and search against that.

Search per territory with local methodology. Patent databases and classification systems are global, but relevant claims, application publication timing, divisional practice, and the density of local filings differ by market; searches should be run or reviewed by counsel admitted or practised in each territory, focusing on granted claims plus pending applications that could grant before or during the commercial window. Record search parameters — databases, classifications, date ranges, keyword strategies — so the search is reproducible and its coverage can be defended.

Analyse hits against the feature description. For each potentially relevant claim, map claim elements to product features and record the conclusion: not relevant, relevant with design-around available, or blocking with exposure. Where claim construction turns on ambiguous terms, record both plausible constructions and their consequences rather than choosing the comfortable one. Number each analysis and tie it to the exact patent document version reviewed.

Form the opinion per territory in writing. An FTO conclusion is a dated, reasoned document — claim-by-claim analysis, assumptions, and a confidence statement — not a summary email. Both partners should hold copies, and the opinion should state whose product configuration it covers, because partner-specific variations break the opinion's coverage silently.

Treat residual risk per territory with an explicit decision: proceed as cleared; redesign around the blocking claim; obtain a licence; reassign which partner bears the territory; or exclude the territory from launch scope. Each treatment needs an owner, a cost, and a date. Where one partner takes a territory the other declined, record the indemnity position between them, because cross-partner exposure allocation is the part of FTO that partnership agreements routinely leave vague.

Maintain clearance as conditions change: new relevant applications publishing in cleared territories, competitor grants issuing, product versions adding features, and territory expansion. Set monitoring per cleared territory for new publications in the relevant classifications, and require a re-clearance trigger on any feature change or new market entry.

## Controls

- Territory register ranked by commercial priority, with clearance status, opinion date, and re-clearance due date per product version.
- Frozen feature description per version, version-controlled, with alternatives enumerated.
- Search protocol record per territory: sources, classifications, keywords, date ranges, reviewer.
- Claim-analysis log mapping each reviewed claim to product features with conclusion and construction assumptions.
- Written opinion per territory per version, retained by both partners.
- Risk treatment decision register: treatment, owner, cost, date, and partner indemnity position.
- Monitoring feed per cleared territory with triage rules and re-clearance triggers tied to feature change and market expansion.

## Validation evidence

Clearance produces the territory register, feature descriptions, search protocols, claim-analysis logs, written opinions, treatment decisions, and monitoring records. Validate by selecting one cleared territory and tracing the chain: the register entry points to an opinion, the opinion cites the feature description version actually shipped, and the search protocol covers the classes where the shipped features would classify. Reverse-test the monitoring feed: sample recent publications in relevant classes and confirm each was triaged with a dated disposition, including "no action" entries. Check version discipline — a shipped release whose features differ from the frozen description without a re-clearance trigger firing is the central audit failure of FTO programmes. Confirm opinions held by both partners are identical versions, since divergent copies indicate one partner updated and the other did not.

## Failure modes and correction

Recurring failures: clearance confined to headquarters jurisdictions while the launch plan quietly added markets through channel partners; alternatives implemented without clearance because the primary design was cleared and the option was never analysed; opinions written against draft product specifications that changed before launch; divisional applications publishing after clearance and ignored because monitoring was never funded; and territory risk accepted by one partner without the indemnity position being recorded, surfacing as a partnership dispute when an assertion arrives. Correct scope gaps by emergency clearance before further sales in the uncovered territory, with sales-pause decisions taken deliberately rather than by default. For un-analysed alternatives, freeze the option until analysed or redesign to the cleared implementation. Where monitoring lapsed, commission a catch-up search over the lapse period and record the exposure window honestly — the window's existence is a fact; its duration is what counsel can work with.

## Limitations

FTO analysis is opinion-level certainty, not a guarantee: unsearched pending applications, future grants, and claim-construction differences can produce assertions despite diligent clearance. Search coverage is uneven across jurisdictions, with some offices' data less accessible than others. Opinions date quickly in fast-moving fields. Cross-partner indemnities and allocation of defense costs depend on the partnership agreement, which must be read alongside this process. Patent counsel must supervise territory-specific conclusions.
