# Leadership for Collaborative Working

## Purpose

Collaborative arrangements often depend on leaders from different organizations aligning expectations, decision-making, behaviours, and relationship governance without relying on a single organizational hierarchy.

ISO/TS 44005:2026 provides guidance for leaders operating in collaborative arrangements and aligns that guidance with the structure of ISO 44001. ISO lists the Technical Specification as published, Edition 1, with a June 2026 publication date.

## Leadership considerations

A practical leadership approach for collaborative working should make the relationship understandable and governable across organizational boundaries. Leaders should consider:

- why the collaboration exists and what outcomes the parties are trying to achieve;
- how responsibilities, authority, escalation, and decision rights are understood across participating organizations;
- how leaders model behaviours that support trust, transparency, constructive challenge, and accountable delivery;
- how competing organizational priorities and incentives will be surfaced and managed;
- how information needed for joint decisions will be shared while respecting confidentiality, security, legal, and contractual constraints;
- how the parties will identify and address relationship risks, opportunities, dependencies, and changes; and
- how lessons from the collaboration will be captured and used to improve future joint work.

## Operating pattern

1. **Establish shared intent.** Describe the collaboration's purpose, expected outcomes, boundaries, and important stakeholder expectations.
2. **Clarify governance.** Define how joint decisions, escalation, accountability, and relationship oversight work across the participating organizations.
3. **Set behavioural expectations.** Make the leadership behaviours needed for constructive collaboration explicit rather than assuming each organization interprets collaboration in the same way.
4. **Maintain dialogue.** Create regular opportunities to surface changing priorities, dependencies, concerns, and opportunities before they become avoidable disputes.
5. **Review relationship health.** Evaluate whether governance and leadership practices continue to support the collaboration's purpose as circumstances change.
6. **Learn and adapt.** Preserve useful lessons and adjust the relationship approach when evidence shows that current practices are not producing the intended outcomes.

## Relationship to ISO 44001

ISO describes ISO/TS 44005:2026 as guidance for leaders operating in collaborative arrangements in alignment with ISO 44001. The Technical Specification is guidance rather than a replacement for ISO 44001 requirements. Organizations using either document should verify the edition and status that applies to their work.
