# Partner Environmental-Claims Evidence

## Purpose and scope

This control governs joint claims about environmental attributes, comparisons, reductions, recyclability, or lifecycle performance. Its purpose is to turn an informal cross-organization activity into a bounded, reviewable process. It does not establish that a party is compliant, insured, licensed, safe, or approved. Owners must adapt the process to the contract, jurisdictions, sector, facts, and the authority of each participating entity for partner environmental-claims evidence.

Use the control for the legal entities and activities named in the relationship record. Identify exclusions explicitly so that adjacent services are not assumed to be covered. Risk determines depth: a low-impact case may justify a documented check, while ambiguity, high impact, unusual geography, or vulnerable people may require specialist and independent review for partner environmental-claims evidence.

1. Open a uniquely identified case and name one accountable owner in each organization for partner environmental-claims evidence.

6. Record approval, rejection, conditions, unresolved limitations, review date, and any event that forces reassessment for partner environmental-claims evidence.
7. Translate conditions into system access, payment, release, communication, or monitoring controls rather than leaving them only in meeting notes for partner environmental-claims evidence.

A change in ownership, personnel, geography, technology, service, transaction pattern, evidence, or law should trigger a scoped reassessment. Prior approval is evidence of a past decision, not permission to ignore changed facts for partner environmental-claims evidence.

## Controls and responsibilities

The relationship owner coordinates but should not be the sole judge when the outcome materially benefits that owner. Evidence providers must not approve their own exceptions. Define incompatible duties, escalation routes, substitute approvers, and emergency authority. Access to sensitive case material follows least privilege, and onward disclosure requires a stated purpose and lawful basis for partner environmental-claims evidence.

The governing agreement or operating charter should cover cooperation, confidentiality, response expectations, record access, correction duties, and consequences of material non-cooperation. Every exception needs a reason, owner, compensating safeguard, expiry, and approval. Automation must report feed failures and stale inputs: no result from a broken check is not a successful result for partner environmental-claims evidence.

## Evidence and validation

Retain datasets, source records, factors, calculations, assurance, approvals, artwork, publication dates, and superseded versions. Strong evidence is attributable, dated, scoped, reproducible, and linked to the decision it supports. Record assumptions, contradictory information, unavailable records, and how those limitations affected the outcome. A screenshot, certificate, or self-attestation may support a narrow fact but must not be described as assurance beyond its actual scope.

Validation should trace a sample from initiation to source evidence, decision, system enforcement, and closure. Include rejected cases, exceptions, and failed automated checks, not only successful transactions. Reconcile registers against source systems and investigate missing identifiers, expired approvals, unexplained overrides, or records that cannot be reproduced. Trend recurring exceptions because repetition may show that the designed process does not match operations for partner environmental-claims evidence.

## Failure handling

When the control fails, pause publication, correct or withdraw claims, notify downstream users, and investigate the substantiation failure. The case owner records containment, affected scope, notification decisions, remediation owners, deadlines, and restart criteria. Preserve relevant records and metadata; do not rewrite history by replacing an original request or decision with a corrected version.

Repeated failure, obstruction, falsified evidence, or missed remediation triggers broader relationship-risk review. Available responses may include enhanced monitoring, reduced scope, a temporary hold, independent investigation, contractual remedies, responsible exit, or external notification where qualified advice establishes a duty. Commercial urgency does not itself justify bypassing a safety, legality, or integrity hold for partner environmental-claims evidence.

## Review questions

- Are the responsible legal entity and covered activities unambiguous?
- Is each material conclusion traceable to current evidence?
- Are authority and independence adequate for the decision?
- Do exceptions expire and receive tested follow-up?
- Would stale data or a failed integration generate a visible alert?
- Are containment, escalation, restart, and closure criteria defined?
- Are protected records shared, retained, and disposed of appropriately?
