# Partnership Lifecycle and Exit Planning

## Why lifecycle planning matters

Partnerships change over time. A sound relationship-management process should cover not only formation and operation but also review, renewal, transition, and exit.

ISO material on collaborative business relationships describes a disciplined relationship lifecycle and explicitly includes partner selection, working together, staying together, and exit strategies. That makes exit planning part of relationship design rather than something to invent only after a relationship deteriorates.

## Lifecycle checkpoints

Useful checkpoints include:

- **qualification** — why collaboration is needed and whether the prospective partner is suitable;
- **formation** — objectives, governance, responsibilities, commercial terms, confidentiality, and decision rights;
- **operation** — delivery, communication, issue handling, measurement, and change control;
- **review** — whether the relationship still creates value and whether risk has materially changed;
- **renewal or expansion** — explicit approval before increasing scope, access, dependency, or commitment; and
- **exit** — transition of work, data, assets, access rights, obligations, and communications.

## Exit design

An exit plan should identify what needs to happen if the relationship ends, including:

1. notice and decision authority;
2. completion or transfer of open commitments;
3. return, deletion, or continued retention of shared information as applicable;
4. revocation of system and facility access;
5. transfer or disposal of jointly managed assets;
6. treatment of intellectual property and confidential material;
7. customer, supplier, or stakeholder communications where required; and
8. post-exit obligations and lessons learned.

Exact contractual exit rights depend on the agreement and applicable law. This article describes reusable governance considerations, not legal advice.
