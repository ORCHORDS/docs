# SOX Sarbanes-Oxley Section 404 Governance

## Purpose

The Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002 (SOX) Section 404 requires public companies to establish and maintain internal control over financial reporting (ICFR). Governance ensures that the enterprise designs, implements, and tests controls over financial reporting, that material weaknesses are remediated, and that management's annual assessment and external auditor attestation (where applicable) are supported by evidence.

## Current context and source status

SOX was enacted in 2002 in response to major corporate accounting scandals. Section 404 (as amended by the Dodd-Frank Act of 2010) requires management's annual assessment of ICFR for all public companies; external auditor attestation applies to accelerated and large filers. The Public Company Accounting Oversight Board (PCAOB) Auditing Standard 2201 governs the external attestation. Verify the current PCAOB standards and SEC guidance before treating any specific requirement as a current requirement.

## Governance workflow and controls

### 1. Apply scoping

Identify in-scope entities, locations, business units, and IT systems. Apply a top-down, risk-based approach. Document scoping rationale.

### 2. Identify significant accounts and disclosures

Identify significant accounts and disclosures based on qualitative and quantitative factors. Consider accounts that contain fraud risk, complexity, judgment, or change.

### 3. Identify significant processes

Identify the processes that produce significant accounts and disclosures. Map process flows. Identify the IT systems and automated controls.

### 4. Identify key controls

Identify key controls, including:

- entity-level controls (tone at the top, risk assessment, monitoring);
- process-level controls (transaction processing, reconciliation, review);
- IT general controls (program development, program changes, computer operations, access).

### 5. Design and implement controls

Design and implement controls to address identified risks. Document control descriptions. Test controls during design and implementation.

### 6. Operate and monitor controls

Operate controls consistently. Monitor control performance. Investigate control failures.

### 7. Test controls

Test operating effectiveness of key controls. Apply a risk-based testing approach. Document test procedures and results.

### 8. Assess deficiencies

Assess control deficiencies. Classify as control deficiency, significant deficiency, or material weakness. Communicate material weaknesses to the audit committee and external auditor.

### 9. Report

Issue management's annual assessment of ICFR. For accelerated filers, obtain external auditor attestation per PCAOB AS 2201.

## Validation and evidence

- Scoping documentation.
- Significant accounts and processes mapping.
- Control descriptions.
- Test procedures and results.
- Deficiency log with classification.
- Management's annual assessment.

## Failure correction

Common defects include incomplete scoping, control descriptions that don't reflect actual practice, and material weaknesses not communicated. Corrective actions include a scoping review, a documentation-actual practice alignment check, and a material weakness communication procedure.

## Limitations

- SOX Section 404 applies to U.S. public companies and to non-U.S. companies that are SEC registrants.
- The standard is evolving; track PCAOB standard amendments.
- ICFR is a subset of overall internal control; consider COSO 2013 for broader coverage.
- Material weakness remediation can be slow; plan remediation timelines carefully.
