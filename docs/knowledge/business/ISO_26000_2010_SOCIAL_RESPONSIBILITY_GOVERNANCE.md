# ISO 26000:2010 Social Responsibility Governance

## Purpose

ISO 26000:2010, "Guidance on social responsibility," provides guidance on the underlying principles of social responsibility, recognizing social responsibility and engaging stakeholders, and on the seven core subjects (organizational governance, human rights, labor practices, the environment, fair operating practices, consumer issues, community involvement and development). The standard is a guidance document, not a management system standard. This article governs the application of ISO 26000 so an organization can integrate social responsibility into its strategy, culture, and operations in a way that is documented and reviewable.

## Scope

The guidance applies to any organization. Within this knowledge base, the article covers the principles of social responsibility (accountability, transparency, ethical behavior, respect for stakeholder interests, respect for the rule of law, respect for international norms of behavior, respect for human rights), the seven core subjects, the process for recognizing social responsibility, and the integration of social responsibility into the organization. It does not certify social responsibility.

## Workflow

1. Recognize the organization's social responsibility context: review the principles, consider how each applies to the organization, and identify the most material subjects for the organization.
2. Identify the relevant stakeholders and engage them. Stakeholder engagement is a continuous activity; the organization should understand the concerns and expectations of those affected by its decisions.
3. Review each of the seven core subjects: organizational governance, human rights, labor practices, the environment, fair operating practices, consumer issues, community involvement and development. For each subject, identify the issues and the actions relevant to the organization.
4. Integrate social responsibility into the organization's strategy, policies, and operations. Social responsibility is not a standalone program; it should inform decisions across the organization.
5. Communicate the organization's social responsibility commitments and performance to stakeholders. Transparency is one of the principles.
6. Review and improve: track performance against the principles, address findings, and update commitments as the organization's context evolves.

## Controls and evidence

Social responsibility evidence includes the documented social responsibility commitments, the stakeholder engagement records, the subject-level action plans, the integration into policies, the communication outputs, and the review records. Each core subject relevant to the organization should have documented actions and outcomes.

## Validation

Validation should confirm the principles have been considered, stakeholders have been identified and engaged, the relevant core subjects have been reviewed, social responsibility is integrated into policies and decisions, and the organization communicates its performance. Periodic review confirms alignment with the principles and with the organization's strategy.

## Failure correction

Common failure modes: social responsibility is a communication exercise rather than an integrated practice (corrective: embed the principles in decisions and operations, not just in communications); stakeholders are listed but not engaged (corrective: implement the engagement process and document the results); action plans are produced but not executed (corrective: assign owners, schedules, and measures); the organization addresses easy subjects and ignores hard ones (corrective: prioritize subjects by materiality and review the prioritization periodically).

## Limitations

ISO 26000 is guidance, not a management system standard; it does not offer certification. The standard does not prescribe specific social responsibility targets or metrics; the organization determines its own level of ambition. The standard is not a substitute for legal compliance; the organization remains subject to applicable law.

## Scope note

This article summarizes project-neutral business use of ISO 26000:2010. It does not assert any specific organization's social responsibility conformance or claim any certification outcome.
