# Complaint Response Review and Feedback Workflow

## Purpose

A complaint process does not end when an organization sends a response. A mature customer-success workflow should distinguish an initial response, a final response, the customer's opportunity to review it, and any feedback that reveals unresolved issues.

The U.S. Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's current complaint process provides one concrete model: companies generally provide a response within 15 calendar days, may identify the response as still in progress when appropriate, and then have up to 60 calendar days to provide a final response. Consumers are invited to review the response and have 60 days to provide feedback.

## Response workflow

For complaints handled under a timed response model:

1. Record the date the complaint entered the response queue and the applicable response deadline.
2. Review the complaint, supporting documents, prior communications, and account or service evidence needed to understand the issue.
3. Communicate with the customer where clarification is necessary rather than filling gaps with assumptions.
4. State the actions already taken, relevant communications, and any follow-up actions that remain planned.
5. Distinguish an interim or in-progress response from a final response so the customer is not led to believe unfinished work is complete.
6. Preserve the response category and evidence used to support the outcome.
7. Notify the customer when the final response is available and provide a practical way to review it.

## Customer feedback after the response

Post-response feedback is useful evidence about whether the resolution was understandable and whether the underlying issue remains unresolved. A reusable process should:

- preserve the customer's feedback with the complaint record;
- route new factual information or evidence to the appropriate owner;
- distinguish disagreement with the outcome from evidence that the response was inaccurate or incomplete;
- avoid silently rewriting the original response after feedback is received; and
- use recurring feedback themes to improve policy, communications, and root-cause analysis.

## Quality review

CFPB guidance says it analyzes company responses for accuracy, completeness, and timeliness. Those dimensions are useful beyond the CFPB process:

- **Accuracy** — does the response match the available evidence?
- **Completeness** — does it address the material issues raised by the customer?
- **Timeliness** — was it delivered within the promised or required period?

A fast response that does not address the complaint is not a high-quality response, and a complete response that routinely misses required deadlines is also a process failure.

## Scope note

The 15-day and 60-day periods described above are specific to the CFPB complaint process and must not be presented as universal complaint-handling deadlines. Other sectors and jurisdictions may use different requirements.
