# Customer Success Stakeholder Influence Map Refresh

## Scope

This practice structures how the customer success function refreshes the stakeholder influence map for each account so that the map reflects the customer's current decision-making structure, the people who actually exercise influence, and the people the supplier has formal access to. The framing is adapted from the stakeholder register practice in the PMI PMBOK Guide, seventh edition, and is referenced here as a documented structure rather than as a binding rule; adopting it does not by itself establish conformance with PMBOK and does not replace the customer's contract, the supplier's anti-bribery policy, or any specialist legal, ethics, or accessibility advice. The practice applies whenever the supplier needs to understand who within the customer organisation can move an adoption, expansion, renewal, or remediation decision, and applies whether the customer is a single individual buyer, a small team, a department, or a multi-stakeholder strategic account.

It does not apply to the map of individuals inside the supplier's own organisation, which is a different artefact and should be governed by the supplier's internal resourcing practice rather than by this practice.

## Workflow or implementation guidance

Open the refresh by capturing the customer's stated decision-making structure, the customer's stated objectives, the supplier's access points, and the most recent executive sponsor. Pull the latest version of any third-party attestation, regulatory change, or organisational change at the customer that materially affects who can exercise influence. Reconcile the prior map against the new information before any map is published, and confirm with the customer that the customer's stated structure is the version the supplier should rely on, rather than an internally inferred version.

Sequence the refresh so that the customer's stated structure leads, not the supplier's inference. A workable order is: customer's stated decision-making structure; the people who actually exercise influence against each of the customer's stated objectives; the people the supplier has formal access to and the people the supplier does not; the gaps in access and the gaps in knowledge; the planned action to close the most material gaps; and the route for revising the map when the customer's structure changes. Close by stating explicitly which items remain open, which decisions are recorded, and what evidence will be re-presented at the next governance forum.

Authoring notes: separate what the customer has stated, what the supplier has inferred from observation, what a third party has reported, and what the supplier is estimating. Where the relationship involves any benefit to an individual that is material to a commercial decision, ensure the benefit is disclosed to the customer's ethics contact and recorded in the supplier's anti-bribery register. Never present inferred or estimated influence as if it were endorsed by the customer.

## Controls

The controlled map identifies each stakeholder, their role, their stated objectives, their influence against those objectives, the supplier's access, the disclosed benefit, the customer's ethics contact, the reviewer, and the route for revision. Give it one accountable owner and a named delegate. State the frequency of refresh, the approval authority for any change that affects commercial exposure, the data classification, the retention period, and the route for contesting an entry by the customer.

Material changes to the map require review by someone other than the author before they are reused, particularly where the change affects the disclosed benefit to an individual. Restrict distribution to the audience agreed with the customer and to the supplier's anti-bribery register, follow the approved retention schedule, and do not let informal observations become the de facto basis for a map change without being recorded. Where the map is automated, the inputs, thresholds, and human override must remain understandable.

## Validation evidence

Direct validation must reconcile the map against the customer's stated decision-making structure, the supplier's access list, and the supplier's anti-bribery register. Record the sample, the source, the expected result, the observed result, the reviewer, and the correction ticket. Reconcile the disclosed benefit against the anti-bribery register and include rejected, overdue, and overridden cases so testing cannot pass by examining only clean examples.

To validate the practice itself, trace a sample of stakeholder changes and verify that the supplier's actions were anchored to the customer's stated structure rather than to an inferred view. Useful indicators of effectiveness include stakeholder transitions that are planned rather than reactive, gaps in access that are closed without overreach, and disclosed benefits that the customer has accepted in writing. Internal targets are management choices, not public guarantees unless formally authorised.

## Failure modes and correction

When the practice fails its own controls, protect the customer's position and the integrity of the supplier's relationship first. If a benefit to an individual has not been disclosed, surface the disclosure immediately and record it. If a stakeholder has changed role without the supplier being informed, treat the change as an incident: name the customer impact, the containment owner, the next update, the recovery deadline, and the closure test. Do not close because the supplier's internal record moved. If the customer disputes the map, preserve the disputed record and do not rewrite history.

Search for similarly exposed accounts in the same portfolio, since a single unrecorded benefit usually signals a wider problem in the supplier's anti-bribery discipline. Where the failure relates to data classification or distribution, treat the disclosure channel as the affected control. Reassess the practice after a material incident, a change in the customer's structure, or evidence that the current shape creates unintended outcomes.

## Limitations

This practice cannot prove causation, eliminate professional judgement, or repair unreliable source data. The PMBOK framing referenced here is a documented structure, not a binding rule; adopting it does not by itself establish certification, conformance, or legal compliance. Stakeholder influence can shift quickly and is subject to organisational changes the supplier cannot anticipate. Sampling can miss rare harms, lagging measures can reveal defects late, and the customer's own objectives can drift between the map and the next QBR.

A documented map can still fail in use. The map is a structure, not a substitute for ethical discipline, judgement, or a strong account team. Treat it as a starting point, and reassess after a material incident, a change in the customer's environment, or evidence that the current shape creates unintended outcomes.
