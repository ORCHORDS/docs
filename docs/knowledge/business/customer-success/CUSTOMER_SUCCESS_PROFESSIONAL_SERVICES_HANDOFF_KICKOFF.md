# Customer Success Professional Services Handoff Kickoff

## Scope

This practice structures the handoff and kickoff between professional services delivery and customer success so that the moment of transfer does not become a moment of customer-visible discontinuity. The framing is adapted from the PMI PMBOK Guide, seventh edition, on handoff as a project artefact, and is referenced here as a documented handoff structure rather than as a binding rule; adopting it does not by itself establish conformance with PMBOK and does not replace the customer's contract, the supplier's delivery methodology, or any specialist legal, security, or accessibility advice. The practice applies whenever a contracted implementation, configuration, or migration engagement is approaching the point at which operational ownership transfers to the customer success team, and it applies whether the engagement is a fixed-scope project, a time-and-materials engagement, or an internal pilot.

It does not apply where the supplier never had a delivery engagement, in which case the relationship opens directly with customer success and the kickoff governs the first ninety days rather than a handoff.

## Workflow or implementation guidance

Open the handoff by fixing the transfer date, the customer's success owner, the supplier's delivery owner, the customer's executive sponsor, the open commitments, and the assets that will move with the engagement. Pull the latest status of in-flight work, the latest status of any unresolved incident, the customer's adoption baseline, the customer's stated business outcomes, and any third-party attestation that is required for the customer to accept the engagement. Reconcile these against the engagement's exit criteria before any handoff is announced, and confirm that the customer accepts the criteria, the reconciliation result, and the assets that will transfer.

Sequence the kickoff so that the customer's voice leads, not the supplier's process. A workable order is: customer's stated outcomes and the period since contract signature; verified state of the engagement against exit criteria; open items, dependencies, and escalations; the customer success team's first ninety-day plan, anchored to the customer's stated outcomes; the agreed communication cadence; and the criteria by which the customer success team will be measured. Close by stating explicitly which items remain open, which decisions are recorded, and what evidence will be re-presented at the next governance forum.

Authoring notes: keep slides short and load-bearing; record the source, the verification method, the reviewer, and the date next to every claim that could be challenged. Where a figure is estimated, label it as such and separate it from verified facts. Where a customer statement is paraphrased, prefer the customer's own words for the part they authored.

## Controls

The controlled handoff record identifies the engagement, the transfer date, the customer's success owner, the supplier's delivery owner, the customer's executive sponsor, the open items, the dependencies, the assets that transfer, the reconciliation result, the reviewer, the customer's acceptance, and the route for revision. Give it one accountable owner and a named delegate. State the frequency of review, the approval authority for any commitment that exceeds operational latitude, the data classification of every attached exhibit, the retention period, and the escalation route when the customer contests the handoff.

Material changes to the handoff require review by someone other than the author before they are reused, particularly where the change affects renewal terms or commercial exposure. Restrict distribution to the audience agreed with the customer, follow the approved retention schedule, and do not let informal conversations become the de facto basis for a handoff change without being recorded. Where the handoff is automated, the inputs, thresholds, and human override must remain understandable.

## Validation evidence

Direct validation must reconcile the handoff record against the engagement's exit criteria, the customer's acceptance, and the assets that transfer. Record the sample, the environment, the expected result, the observed result, the reviewer, and the correction ticket. Reconcile open-item counts to the action register and include rejected, overdue, and overridden cases so testing cannot pass by examining only clean examples.

To validate the handoff itself, trace a sample of accepted handoffs and verify that the customer success team's first ninety days were anchored to the customer's stated outcomes and to the open items the engagement left unresolved. Useful indicators of effectiveness include open items that age without closure, escalations that recur after the handoff, customer-stated outcomes that drift between the engagement and the first QBR, and lag between a handoff decision and the action it implied. Internal targets are management choices, not public guarantees unless formally authorised.

## Failure modes and correction

When the handoff fails its own controls, protect the customer's position first. If exit criteria were waved through before reconciliation, pause the handoff, complete the reconciliation, and re-issue the record. If the customer success team's first ninety-day plan does not match the customer's stated outcomes, treat the plan as an interim plan, surface the gap, and rebuild it with the customer. If the customer contests the handoff, preserve the disputed record and do not rewrite history.

When a handoff has gone materially wrong after the fact, name the customer impact, the containment owner, the next update, the recovery deadline, and the closure test. Do not close because an internal ticket moved state. Search for similarly exposed handoffs in the same portfolio, since a single source of error usually signals a wider problem in the underlying extract. Where the failure relates to data classification or distribution, treat the disclosure channel as the affected control.

## Limitations

This practice cannot prove causation, eliminate professional judgement, or repair unreliable source data. The PMBOK framing referenced here is a documented handoff structure, not a binding rule; adopting it does not by itself establish certification, conformance, or legal compliance. Sampling can miss rare harms, lagging measures can reveal defects late, and the customer's own objectives can drift between the engagement and the first QBR.

A documented handoff can still fail in use. The handoff is a structure, not a substitute for preparation, judgement, or a strong account team. Treat it as a starting point, and reassess after a material incident, a change in the customer's environment, or evidence that the current shape creates unintended outcomes.
