# Customer Success Champion Development Plan

## Scope

This practice structures how customer success teams develop and sustain a champion within a customer organisation so that the relationship survives individual turnover and produces a documented account of how the customer is prepared to lead its own adoption, expansion, and renewal. The framing is adapted from the champion development perspective articulated in MIT Sloan Management Review, and is referenced here as a practitioner framing rather than as an authoritative standard; adopting it does not by itself establish certification, conformance, or legal compliance, and it does not replace the customer's contract or any specialist advice on ethics, anti-bribery, or organisational change. The practice applies whenever the customer success team is investing in a relationship with an individual who is expected to advocate for the supplier's product, sponsor internal change, or participate in commercial decisions, and it applies whether the champion is at the operational, managerial, or executive level.

It does not apply where the supplier's relationship with the individual would create an anti-bribery or conflict-of-interest exposure, in which case the relationship must be governed by the recipient organisation's ethics policy and the supplier's anti-bribery policy, not by this practice.

## Workflow or implementation guidance

Open the plan by naming the candidate champion, the role the champion currently occupies, the role the champion is being prepared for, and the customer-stated outcomes the champion is expected to support. Establish the candidate's influence over the relevant business outcome, the candidate's tenure within the customer organisation, and any prior relationship between the supplier and the candidate that could be perceived as a conflict. Confirm with the customer that the candidate is the right person to develop for this purpose; do not assume that visibility in the supplier's data is the same as influence in the customer's decision.

Sequence development so that the champion's growth in the customer organisation is the primary trajectory, not the supplier's commercial goals. A workable sequence is: shared understanding of the customer's stated outcomes; co-authoring of the success plan that the champion will own inside the customer organisation; preparation of the champion for the next governance forum in the customer's structure; preparation of the champion for the next expansion or renewal conversation; and explicit succession planning so that a change in the champion's role does not collapse the account. Where the champion changes role, treat the change as a planned event rather than as an exception.

Authoring notes: distinguish what the champion has stated, what the customer has stated about the champion, what the supplier has inferred from observation, and what the supplier is estimating. Never present inferred or estimated influence as if it were endorsed by the customer. Where the relationship involves any benefit to the champion that is material to a commercial decision, ensure the benefit is disclosed to the customer's ethics contact and recorded in the supplier's anti-bribery register.

## Controls

The controlled champion development plan identifies the candidate champion, the role being prepared for, the customer's stated outcomes, the influence map, the succession plan, the disclosure of any benefit to the champion, the reviewer, the customer's ethics contact, and the route for revision. Give it one accountable owner and a named delegate. State the frequency of reassessment, the approval authority for any change that affects commercial exposure, the data classification, the retention period, and the route for contesting an entry by the customer.

Material changes to the plan require review by someone other than the author before they are reused, particularly where the change affects the disclosed benefit to the champion. Restrict distribution to the audience agreed with the customer and to the supplier's anti-bribery register, follow the approved retention schedule, and do not let informal mentoring become the de facto benefit without being recorded. Where the plan is automated, the inputs, thresholds, and human override must remain understandable.

## Validation evidence

Direct validation must reconcile the champion development plan against the customer's stated outcomes, the customer's governance structure, and the supplier's anti-bribery policy. Record the sample, the source, the expected result, the observed result, the reviewer, and the correction ticket. Reconcile the disclosed benefit against the supplier's anti-bribery register and include rejected, overdue, and overridden cases so testing cannot pass by examining only clean examples.

To validate the plan itself, trace a sample of champion transitions and verify that the plan anticipated the change, that the customer was informed where required, and that the account survived the transition without an unresolved customer impact. Useful indicators of effectiveness include champion transitions that are planned rather than reactive, succession depth that is independent of a single individual, and disclosed benefits that the customer has accepted in writing. Internal targets are management choices, not public guarantees unless formally authorised.

## Failure modes and correction

When the plan fails its own controls, protect the customer and the integrity of the supplier's relationship first. If a benefit to the champion has not been disclosed, surface the disclosure immediately and record it. If the champion has changed role without succession planning, treat the change as an incident: name the customer impact, the containment owner, the next update, the recovery deadline, and the closure test. Do not close because the supplier's internal record moved. If the customer disputes the plan, preserve the disputed record and do not rewrite history.

Search for similarly exposed accounts in the same portfolio, since a single unrecorded benefit usually signals a wider problem in the supplier's anti-bribery discipline. Where the failure relates to data classification or distribution, treat the disclosure channel as the affected control. Reassess the plan after a material incident, a change in the customer's ethics policy, or evidence that the current shape creates unintended outcomes.

## Limitations

This practice cannot prove causation, eliminate professional judgement, or repair unreliable source data. The MIT Sloan framing referenced here is a practitioner perspective, not an authoritative standard; adopting it does not by itself establish certification, conformance, or legal compliance. Champion relationships can be brittle and are subject to organisational changes that the supplier cannot anticipate. Sampling can miss rare harms, lagging measures can reveal defects late, and the customer's own objectives can drift.

A documented champion development plan can still fail in use. The plan is a structure, not a substitute for ethical discipline, judgement, or a strong account team. Treat it as a starting point, and reassess after a material incident, a change in the customer's environment, or evidence that the current shape creates unintended outcomes.
