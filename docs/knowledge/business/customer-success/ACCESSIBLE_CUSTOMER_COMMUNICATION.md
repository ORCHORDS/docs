# Accessible Customer Communication

## Purpose

Customer communication should remain effective for people with vision, hearing, and speech disabilities. The communication method should match the nature, length, complexity, and context of the interaction.

## Service pattern

- Ask what communication method is effective rather than assuming.
- Provide appropriate auxiliary aids or services when needed.
- Make important written information available in accessible electronic or alternative formats where appropriate.
- Use qualified interpreters, captioning, readers, or other aids when the interaction requires them.
- Do not force a customer to supply their own interpreter as the default solution.
- Train frontline staff to escalate accessibility requests without treating them as exceptional customer behavior.
- Record only the information necessary to fulfil the accommodation; avoid unnecessary disability details.

## Practical principle

Simple interactions may need simple solutions, while complex or high-stakes conversations may require more capable aids or services.

## Boundary

For businesses covered by ADA Title III, effective communication is a legal requirement. Exact obligations depend on the circumstances and applicable exceptions.
