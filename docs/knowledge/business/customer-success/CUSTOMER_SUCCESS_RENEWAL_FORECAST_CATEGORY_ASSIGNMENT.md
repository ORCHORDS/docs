# Customer Success Renewal Forecast Category Assignment

## Scope

This practice defines how customer success teams assign a renewal forecast category to each account so that the assignment can be reviewed, replayed, and audited consistently across the portfolio. It adapts the practitioner convention articulated by Gainsight in its published material on renewal categories into a documented evidence framework rather than a free-form opinion; it does so as a documentation aid, not as an authoritative standard, and adopting it does not by itself establish certification, conformance, or legal compliance. The category assignment does not replace the customer's contract, the financial close process, or the revenue recognition policy; it informs the inputs that those functions rely on, but does not determine them.

The practice applies whenever a renewal is being forecast, regardless of whether the renewal horizon is months or years out, and applies equally to renewals at risk, renewals on track, renewals expected to expand, and renewals expected to contract. It does not apply to forecast inputs that originate entirely outside customer success, such as revenue from a new logo, where the customer success team is not the originating function.

## Workflow or implementation guidance

Open the renewal forecast cycle by capturing the customer's renewal date, the most recent executive sponsor, the open commitments in any current contract or order form, and the most recent customer-stated business outcomes. Pull the leading and lagging indicator movement, the open support incidents at severity-2 or above, the dependency register, the adoption baseline, the most recent QBR decisions, and any external evidence such as a third-party attestation, regulatory action, or change in the customer's industry that materially affects the forecast.

Assign the renewal category only after the evidence is in place, and only against the criteria version that is current at the time of assignment. Do not backfill categories with new criteria without flagging the change. The assignment must record the criteria version, the source of each criterion, the reviewer, the snapshot date, the customer's last verified reaction, and any unresolved disagreement between the team and the customer. A category is not a commitment; it is a documented assessment at a point in time, and it can be reassessed.

Authoring notes: separate what the customer has stated, what the team has inferred, what a third party has reported, and what the team is estimating. Where the team and the customer disagree, record both positions and the route to reconcile. Where a forecast category is influenced by commercial opportunity that the customer has not yet decided, label that influence as commercial, not as realised renewal intent.

## Controls

The controlled category assignment identifies the customer, the renewal date, the category, the criteria version, the indicators used, the reviewer, the snapshot date, the customer's last verified reaction, and the route for revision. Give it one accountable owner and a named delegate. State the frequency of reassessment, the approval authority for any assignment that drives revenue recognition or executive communication, the data classification, the retention period, and the escalation route when the customer contests the category.

Material changes to the criteria used for assignment require review by someone other than the author before they are reused. Restrict distribution to the audience agreed with the customer, follow the approved retention schedule, and do not let a single informal conversation become the de facto basis for a category change without being recorded. Where the assignment is automated, the inputs, thresholds, and human override must remain understandable.

## Validation evidence

Direct validation must reconcile a sample of assigned categories against the underlying indicators and the customer's stated position. Record the sample, the source, the expected result, the observed result, the reviewer, and the correction ticket. Reconcile the assignment count to the underlying portfolio and include rejected, overdue, and overridden cases so testing cannot pass by examining only clean examples.

To validate the framework itself, trace a sample of renewals back to the categories they were assigned at the points when each renewal-relevant event occurred, and verify that the category reflected the evidence available at that moment. Useful indicators of effectiveness include forecast accuracy against actual renewal outcome, the lag between indicator movement and category reassessment, the proportion of categories the customer disputes, and the proportion of categories revised without new evidence. Internal targets are management choices, not public guarantees unless formally authorised.

## Failure modes and correction

When the assignment practice fails its own controls, protect the customer's position first. If a category has been inflated by a single informal conversation, revert to the prior assignment and document the basis for the change. If a category was based on indicators that were quietly redefined, disclose the change to anyone who previously relied on the prior definition. If the customer disputes a category, preserve the disputed record and do not rewrite history.

When a forecast category proves to be materially wrong on renewal, name the customer impact, the reconciliation owner, the next update, the recovery deadline, and the closure test. Do not close because an internal dashboard moved state. Search for similarly exposed accounts in the same portfolio, since a single misclassified renewal usually signals a wider problem in the underlying extract. Where the failure relates to data classification or distribution, treat the disclosure channel as the affected control.

## Limitations

This practice cannot prove causation, eliminate professional judgement, or repair unreliable source data. The practitioner convention adapted here is a vocabulary, not an authoritative standard; using it does not by itself establish certification, conformance, or legal compliance. Sampling can miss rare harms, lagging measures can reveal defects late, and customer-stated outcomes can drift after the category is assigned.

A documented category assignment can still fail in use. The framework is a checklist, not a substitute for preparation, judgement, or a strong account team. Treat it as a starting point, and reassess after a material incident, a change in the customer's environment, or evidence that the current shape creates unintended outcomes.
