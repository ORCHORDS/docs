# Customer Promise Codes of Conduct

## Purpose

ISO 10001:2018 gives guidance for planning, designing, developing, implementing, maintaining, and improving customer-satisfaction codes of conduct. ISO lists the 2018 second edition as published and confirmed, making it the current edition in the ISO catalogue.

The standard addresses product- and service-related promises an organization makes to customers about its behaviour. These customer-facing promises can help set expectations and support satisfaction when they are specific, understandable, realistic, and backed by workable processes.

## Designing customer promises

A useful customer code should be more than marketing language. Consider:

1. **Purpose and scope** — state what products, services, transactions, or customer situations the promise covers.
2. **Specific commitments** — make promises clear enough that customers and staff can understand what behaviour is expected.
3. **Feasibility** — confirm operational capability before publishing a promise; do not commit publicly to service levels or remedies the organization cannot reliably support.
4. **Customer relevance** — base promises on material customer needs and recurring friction rather than internal preferences alone.
5. **Accessibility and communication** — make the code easy to find, understand, and use by the audiences it is intended to serve.
6. **Ownership and implementation** — assign responsibility for delivering, monitoring, and updating each material commitment.
7. **Remedies and complaints** — connect failures against the promise to practical resolution and complaint-handling paths.
8. **Monitoring and review** — use complaints, customer feedback, fulfilment data, and changes in products or regulation to determine when promises need revision.

## Avoid unsupported assurance

A customer code should not be written as evidence that every promise is always achieved. Public commitments and operational performance are different things. Where evidence is incomplete, describe the commitment accurately and measure performance rather than presenting aspirational language as verified fact.

## Relationship to other customer-satisfaction guidance

ISO 10001 focuses on customer-facing codes and promises. It complements other ISO 10000-series guidance, including ISO 10002 for complaint handling, ISO 10003 for external dispute resolution, and ISO 10004 for monitoring and measuring customer satisfaction. Each document has a different scope and should not be treated as interchangeable.
