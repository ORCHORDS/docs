# Authorized Representative Complaint Handling

## Purpose

Customers sometimes need another person or organization to submit or manage a complaint on their behalf. A complaint process should support legitimate representation without exposing customer information or accepting instructions from someone who lacks authority.

The U.S. Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's complaint process recognizes authorized representatives and notes that companies may require signed, written proof that the consumer has authorized the representative.

## Intake controls

When a complaint is submitted by someone other than the customer:

1. Identify the representative and the claimed relationship to the customer.
2. Confirm that the customer knows about and authorizes the representation before relying on the representative's instructions.
3. Obtain the evidence of authority required for the type of complaint and relationship, such as written consent or another recognized authorization document.
4. Limit the representative's access and decision authority to the scope actually granted by the customer.
5. Record the authorization evidence, its scope, and any relevant expiry or revocation information.
6. Keep the customer's own contact details distinct from the representative's so privacy-sensitive responses are not automatically sent to the wrong party.

## Privacy and response handling

Authorization to submit a complaint does not automatically authorize unrestricted disclosure of account or personal information. Before sharing a response, supporting documents, or sensitive details, verify that the authorization covers that disclosure.

Where a process requires the customer to receive the final response directly, communicate that boundary clearly to the representative rather than creating an expectation that all information will flow through them.

## Changes and revocation

A customer should be able to withdraw or replace a representative. When authority changes:

- stop relying on superseded instructions;
- update access and contact routing;
- preserve the audit trail of decisions already taken; and
- tell operational teams which representative, if any, remains authorized.

## Fraud and coercion indicators

Escalate cases where the representative appears to be acting without the customer's knowledge, pressures the customer, provides inconsistent authority documents, attempts to redirect payments or account control, or resists reasonable confirmation of the customer's intent.

## Scope note

This article describes general complaint-representation controls. Legal authority for guardians, attorneys, trustees, powers of attorney, minors, or other representatives varies by jurisdiction and context.
