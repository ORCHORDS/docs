# Customer Complaint Handling

## Purpose

A complaint-handling process gives customers a clear route to raise problems and gives an organization structured evidence for correcting recurring service or product issues.

ISO 10002:2018 provides guidelines for complaints handling related to products and services and is intended for organizations of any type or size. ISO lists the standard as current and confirmed.

## Core process characteristics

A reusable complaint process should be:

- easy for customers to find and use;
- open to feedback rather than designed to discourage complaints;
- clear about how complaints are received, acknowledged, assessed, investigated, resolved, and closed;
- supported by people with appropriate authority and training;
- able to recognize the needs and expectations of complainants;
- capable of recording enough information to identify patterns without collecting unnecessary personal data; and
- reviewed for effectiveness and opportunities to improve products, services, and customer service.

## Operational workflow

1. **Receive and record** the complaint, channel, date, affected product or service, and the minimum information required to investigate it.
2. **Acknowledge** receipt and explain the expected process or next step.
3. **Assess** urgency, safety, legal, privacy, financial, and customer-impact considerations.
4. **Investigate** using relevant records and responsible teams while preserving confidentiality.
5. **Respond and resolve** with a clear outcome, corrective action where appropriate, and an explanation that matches the available evidence.
6. **Close and retain** only the records required by policy or law.
7. **Analyse trends** to identify recurring causes and opportunities for prevention or improvement.

## Learning from complaints

Individual resolution is only one purpose of the process. Aggregate complaint information can reveal recurring defects, confusing communications, service gaps, training needs, or process failures. Trend analysis should distinguish frequency from severity and avoid treating a small sample as representative without supporting evidence.

## Process review

Periodically review whether the process is accessible, timely, consistently applied, properly resourced, and producing useful improvement signals. A complaint count by itself is not a sufficient measure of process quality; changes in volume can reflect changes in customer numbers, channel visibility, reporting friction, or underlying quality.

## Scope note

This article describes general complaint-handling principles. It does not cover external dispute resolution, employment disputes, or jurisdiction-specific consumer-law obligations.
