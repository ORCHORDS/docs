# Customer Success Value Achievement Evidence Archive

## Scope

This practice structures the archive of evidence that documents the value a customer has achieved during the supplier's relationship, so that the archive can be replayed, defended to the customer, and used as input to commercial, financial, and customer success decisions years after the events. The framing is the evidence-retention perspective articulated in AICPA SSAE 18, which codifies the standard for attestation engagements, and is referenced here as a documented retention structure rather than as a binding rule; adopting it does not by itself establish a SSAE 18 attestation and does not replace the customer's contract, the supplier's revenue recognition policy, or any specialist legal, accounting, or audit advice. The practice applies whenever the supplier prepares an artefact that will outlive the people who prepared it, and applies whether the artefact is a single evidence packet, a recurring record, or a periodic attestation.

It does not apply to evidence that has no commercial, financial, or customer-success significance, where a lighter retention rule is appropriate and the absence of a significant-evidence classification should itself be documented.

## Workflow or implementation guidance

Open the archive by capturing the customer's identifier, the artefact identifier, the period covered, the source of each piece of evidence, the verification method, the reviewer, the snapshot date, and the data classification. Pull the latest version of the artefact, the latest version of the evidence, and any third-party attestation that the artefact relies on. Reconcile the artefact against the evidence before the archive is published, and confirm with the supplier's records function that the retention schedule is the version the customer should rely on.

Sequence the archive so that the customer's stated outcome is the primary reference, not the supplier's internal definition. A workable order is: customer's stated outcome for the period; the verified evidence against that outcome, anchored to the source data and the snapshot date; reconciliation of the evidence against the artefact; any third-party attestation required by the artefact; the route for revising the artefact if the customer disputes it; and the retention schedule that governs the artefact. Close by stating explicitly which items remain open, which decisions are recorded, and what evidence will be re-presented if the artefact is contested.

Authoring notes: separate what the customer has stated, what the supplier has observed, what a third party has reported, and what the supplier is estimating. Where a figure is estimated, label it as such and separate it from verified facts. Where a customer statement is paraphrased, prefer the customer's own words for the part they authored.

## Controls

The controlled archive record identifies the customer's identifier, the artefact, the period, the source of each piece of evidence, the verification method, the reviewer, the snapshot date, the reconciliation result, the customer's acceptance, the retention schedule, and the route for revision. Give it one accountable owner and a named delegate. State the frequency of archive refresh, the approval authority for any change that affects commercial exposure, the data classification of every attached exhibit, the retention period, and the escalation route when the customer contests the artefact.

Material changes to the artefact require review by someone other than the author before they are reused, particularly where the change affects the supplier's revenue recognition or the customer's audit trail. Restrict distribution to the audience agreed with the customer and to the supplier's records function, follow the approved retention schedule, and do not let informal conversations become the de facto basis for an artefact revision without being recorded. Where the archive is automated, the inputs, thresholds, and human override must remain understandable.

## Validation evidence

Direct validation must reconcile a sample of archived artefacts against the underlying evidence, the verification method, and the customer's acceptance. Record the sample, the environment, the expected result, the observed result, the reviewer, and the correction ticket. Reconcile the artefact count against the underlying extract and include rejected, overdue, and overridden cases so testing cannot pass by examining only clean examples.

To validate the practice itself, trace a sample of archived artefacts forward through the retention schedule and verify that each artefact can still be replayed against the evidence that was originally archived, that the data classification is still correct, and that the customer's acceptance is still on file. Useful indicators of effectiveness include the proportion of artefacts that can be replayed without manual reconstruction, the proportion of artefacts whose data classification is still correct after the retention horizon, and the proportion of artefacts that the customer disputes after the fact. Internal targets are management choices, not public guarantees unless formally authorised.

## Failure modes and correction

When the practice fails its own controls, protect the customer's position and the integrity of the supplier's records first. If an artefact has been archived without adequate evidence, withdraw the artefact, notify the customer's executive sponsor, and identify the correction route. If an evidence source has been quietly redefined, disclose the change to anyone who previously relied on the prior definition. If the customer disputes an artefact, preserve the disputed record and do not rewrite history.

When an archived artefact proves materially wrong after the fact, name the customer impact, the records impact, the reconciliation owner, the next update, the recovery deadline, and the closure test. Do not close because an internal ticket moved state. Search for similarly exposed artefacts in the same portfolio, since a single source of error usually signals a wider problem in the underlying extract. Where the failure relates to data classification or distribution, treat the disclosure channel as the affected control.

## Limitations

This practice cannot prove causation, eliminate professional judgement, or repair unreliable source data. The AICPA SSAE 18 framing referenced here is an evidence-retention framing, not a binding rule; adopting it does not by itself establish a SSAE 18 attestation, certification, conformance, or legal compliance. Sampling can miss rare harms, lagging measures can reveal defects late, and the customer's own outcomes can drift after the artefact is archived.

A documented archive record can still fail in use. The record is a structure, not a substitute for preparation, judgement, or a strong records function. Treat it as a starting point, and reassess after a material incident, a change in the customer's environment, or evidence that the current shape creates unintended outcomes.
