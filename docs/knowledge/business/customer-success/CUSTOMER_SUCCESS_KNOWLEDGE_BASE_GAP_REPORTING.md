# Customer Success Knowledge Base Gap Reporting

## Scope

This practice structures how the customer success function reports content gaps in the supplier's customer-facing knowledge base so that the gaps can be triaged, prioritised, and resolved against an evidence trail that survives the people who reported them. The framing is adapted from the content-development perspective articulated in ISO 26514 on the design and development of information for users, and is referenced here as a documented structure rather than as a binding rule; adopting it does not by itself establish conformance with ISO 26514 and does not replace the customer's contract, the supplier's content lifecycle policy, or any specialist advice on technical communication, accessibility, or translation. The practice applies whenever a customer, a customer success manager, a support engineer, or a field consultant identifies that an article, a procedure, a reference architecture, or a release note does not match what the customer needs, and applies whether the gap is in a published article, a draft article, or a planned article that has not yet been written.

It does not apply to one-off product defects or feature requests, which belong in the engineering backlog rather than in the knowledge base; the practice is for the documentation surface that sits alongside the product, not the product itself.

## Workflow or implementation guidance

Open a gap report by capturing the customer's stated need, the role of the person reporting, the moment at which the gap was encountered, the customer's environment at that moment, and the article, procedure, or release note that was consulted. Capture the customer's verbatim reaction where the reaction is material to the report, and capture the supplier's verification that the consulted material is the version that the customer would have seen. Reconcile the gap against the existing article inventory before the report is filed, since a gap can sometimes be closed by an existing article that the reporting path did not surface.

Sequence the report so that the customer's stated need leads, not the supplier's editorial view. A workable order is: the customer's stated need; the moment at which the gap was encountered; the consulted material and its version; the gap between the consulted material and the customer's stated need; the impact on the customer's outcome; and the recommended action, drawn from a small set of pre-defined actions such as update, expand, translate, retire, or escalate. Close by stating the owner of the recommended action, the deadline, and the route by which the customer will be told what action was taken.

Authoring notes: separate what the customer has stated, what the reporting person has observed, what the supplier's editorial team has stated, and what the supplier is estimating. Never present inferred or estimated impact as if it were customer-stated. Where the gap is in a regulated or accessibility-sensitive article, route the report through the appropriate specialist before the recommended action is taken.

## Controls

The controlled gap report identifies the customer's stated need, the reporting person, the consulted material, the gap, the impact, the recommended action, the action owner, the deadline, and the route for telling the customer what action was taken. Give it one accountable owner and a named delegate. State the frequency of triage, the approval authority for any action that retires or rewrites a customer-visible article, the data classification, the retention period, and the route for contesting an action by the customer or by another internal function.

Material changes to the recommended action require review by someone other than the author before they are reused, particularly where the change retires an article that customers still consult. Restrict distribution to the audience agreed with the customer and to the supplier's editorial team, follow the approved retention schedule, and do not let informal edits become the de facto resolution without being recorded. Where the report is automated, the inputs, thresholds, and human override must remain understandable.

## Validation evidence

Direct validation must reconcile a sample of gap reports against the consulted material, the customer's stated need, and the action that was ultimately taken. Record the sample, the source, the expected result, the observed result, the reviewer, and the correction ticket. Reconcile the report count against the editorial pipeline and include rejected, overdue, and overridden cases so testing cannot pass by examining only clean examples.

To validate the practice itself, trace a sample of reported gaps back to the editorial action they triggered and verify that the customer's stated need was actually addressed in the published material. Useful indicators of effectiveness include the lag between a reported gap and the editorial action, the proportion of gaps closed by an existing article rather than by a new article, the proportion of reported gaps that recur after the action, and the accessibility and translation impact of each action. Internal targets are management choices, not public guarantees unless formally authorised.

## Failure modes and correction

When the practice fails its own controls, protect the customer's position first. If a gap has been reported but not yet triaged and the customer is being asked to operate against the wrong material, surface the gap to the customer immediately and provide the closest current guidance. If an article has been retired without an alternative, treat the retirement as an incident: name the customer impact, the containment owner, the next update, the recovery deadline, and the closure test. Do not close because the editorial pipeline moved state.

If the customer disputes the recommended action, preserve the disputed record and do not rewrite history. Search for similarly exposed articles in the supplier's catalogue, since a single source of error usually signals a wider problem in the underlying inventory. Where the failure relates to data classification, accessibility, or translation, treat the disclosure channel as the affected control. Reassess the practice after a material incident, a change in the customer's environment, or evidence that the current shape creates unintended outcomes.

## Limitations

This practice cannot prove causation, eliminate professional judgement, or repair unreliable source data. The ISO 26514 framing referenced here is a documented structure, not a binding rule; adopting it does not by itself establish certification, conformance, or legal compliance. Sampling can miss rare harms, lagging measures can reveal defects late, and the customer's own needs can drift between the report and the editorial action.

A documented gap report can still fail in use. The report is a structure, not a substitute for editorial discipline, judgement, or a strong content team. Treat it as a starting point, and reassess after a material incident, a change in the supplier's product surface, or evidence that the current shape creates unintended outcomes.
