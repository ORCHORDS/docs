# Customer Success Expanded Team Training Schedule

## Scope

This practice structures the training schedule that a customer success team prepares for the customer's expanded audience, so that the schedule can be reviewed, replayed, and adapted as the customer's environment evolves. The framing is the learning-services perspective articulated in ISO 29995 on learning services outside formal education, and is referenced here as a documented structure rather than as a binding rule; adopting it does not by itself establish conformance with ISO 29995 and does not replace the customer's contract, accessibility obligations, or any specialist advice on pedagogy, learning design, or occupational competence. The practice applies whenever the supplier offers training as part of a customer success plan, whether the training is delivered by the supplier, by a partner, by the customer itself, or through a recorded channel, and applies whether the audience is operators, managers, or executives.

It does not apply to formal education programmes that lead to a regulated qualification, where the customer's education authority sets the applicable rules; this practice is for the supplementary learning that supports adoption, expansion, and renewal.

## Workflow or implementation guidance

Open the schedule by capturing the customer's expanded audience, their roles, their prior experience with the supplier's product, their stated outcomes, and any accessibility or language requirement the customer has signalled. Pull the latest version of the supplier's curriculum, the latest version of the customer's reference architecture, and any third-party attestation that the training material should reference. Reconcile the curriculum against the customer's reference architecture so that training is anchored to what the customer actually uses, not to a generic catalogue.

Sequence the schedule so that the customer's workflow leads, not the supplier's product features. A workable order is: orientation to the customer's stated outcomes and to the supplier's role in them; the workflow the customer actually runs; the product features the workflow depends on; the failure modes the customer is most likely to encounter; the data classification, security, and accessibility obligations the customer must observe; and the escalation routes the customer is expected to use. Close each module with an observable completion condition that the customer can demonstrate back to the supplier, rather than an attendance certificate alone.

Authoring notes: separate what the customer has stated it needs, what the supplier has inferred from prior customers, what the supplier's reference architecture requires, and what the supplier is estimating. Never present inferred or estimated needs as if they were customer-stated. Where the training involves any regulated content, ensure the regulated content is reviewed by the appropriate specialist before it is delivered.

## Controls

The controlled schedule identifies the audience, the modules, the delivery channel, the duration, the completion condition, the reviewer, the source of the curriculum, the source of the reference architecture, the accessibility provisions, the data classification, and the route for revision. Give it one accountable owner and a named delegate. State the frequency of reassessment, the approval authority for any change that affects commercial exposure, the retention period, and the escalation route when the customer contests the schedule.

Material changes to the schedule require review by someone other than the author before they are reused, particularly where the change affects an accessibility commitment or a regulated content review. Restrict distribution to the audience agreed with the customer, follow the approved retention schedule, and do not let informal coaching become the de facto delivery channel without being recorded. Where the schedule is automated, the inputs, thresholds, and human override must remain understandable.

## Validation evidence

Direct validation must reconcile a sample of delivered modules against the curriculum, the customer's reference architecture, the completion condition, and the customer's reaction. Record the sample, the source, the expected result, the observed result, the reviewer, and the correction ticket. Reconcile the audience count against the customer's roster and include rejected, overdue, and overridden cases so testing cannot pass by examining only clean examples.

To validate the schedule itself, trace a sample of training outcomes back to the customers' stated outcomes and verify that the modules contributed to the intended adoption or renewal signal. Useful indicators of effectiveness include completion conditions that the customer can actually demonstrate, modules that the customer repeats by request, accessibility provisions that survive a change in audience, and escalations that decrease after the relevant module. Internal targets are management choices, not public guarantees unless formally authorised.

## Failure modes and correction

When the schedule fails its own controls, protect the customer and the integrity of the learning first. If a module is delivered without the completion condition, treat it as a partial delivery and surface the gap before claiming completion. If the curriculum has drifted from the customer's reference architecture, reconcile the two and re-issue the affected modules. If the customer contests the schedule, preserve the disputed record and do not rewrite history.

When a schedule proves materially wrong after the fact, name the customer impact, the containment owner, the next update, the recovery deadline, and the closure test. Do not close because an internal record moved state. Search for similarly exposed schedules in the same portfolio, since a single source of error usually signals a wider problem in the underlying curriculum or in the reference architecture. Where the failure relates to accessibility or data classification, treat the disclosure channel as the affected control.

## Limitations

This practice cannot prove causation, eliminate professional judgement, or repair unreliable source data. The ISO 29995 framing referenced here is a documented structure, not a binding rule; adopting it does not by itself establish certification, conformance, or legal compliance. Sampling can miss rare harms, lagging measures can reveal defects late, and the customer's own objectives can drift between the schedule and the next QBR.

A documented schedule can still fail in use. The schedule is a structure, not a substitute for preparation, judgement, or a strong account team. Treat it as a starting point, and reassess after a material incident, a change in the customer's reference architecture, or evidence that the current shape creates unintended outcomes.
