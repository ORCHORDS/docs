# Customer Success Quarterly Business Review Agenda Template

## Scope

This practice gives customer success managers a repeatable agenda template for the quarterly business review (QBR) with each enterprise customer. It is the customer-facing analogue of the management-review activity described in ISO 9001:2015 clause 9.3, in which top management periodically review the quality management system and decide what to keep, change, and resource. Adopting the agenda does not by itself establish conformance with ISO 9001, does not replace the customer's contract, and does not substitute for legal, security, or accessibility advice; it is a documentation aid so that a recurring high-stakes meeting can be prepared, replayed, and audited in a consistent shape across many accounts. The template applies whenever the meeting covers a renewal horizon, a planned expansion, a remediation action, or a quarterly statement of value, and it applies whether the customer is on a multi-year strategic agreement or a smaller renewable term.

The template should not be used to manufacture favorable interpretations. Where the data is unfavourable, the meeting is the place to surface it, not to soften it. The agenda must accept both outcomes and must record the customer's verbatim reactions where those reactions are material to scope, timeline, or resourcing decisions.

## Workflow or implementation guidance

Open the QBR cycle by fixing the meeting window against the customer's fiscal calendar, the renewal date, and the most recent executive sponsor checkpoint. Pull the value-realisation evidence, the open support incidents at severity-2 or above, the adoption baseline, the dependency register, and any procurement, security, or accessibility change that the customer has signalled since the previous QBR. Do not reuse a prior deck without re-checking each data point: evidence ages, definitions drift, and reuse is a frequent cause of stale assertions reaching an executive audience.

Sequence the agenda so that the customer's own performance against its goals leads, not the supplier's product metrics. A workable order is: customer-stated objectives and the period under review; verified outcomes against those objectives; adoption and breadth-of-use evidence; open risk, dependencies, and escalations; remediation status from the previous QBR; a small number of decisions sought in this meeting; and the agreed next steps with owners and deadlines. Close by stating explicitly which items remain open, which decisions are recorded, and what evidence will be re-presented next quarter.

Authoring notes: keep slides short and load-bearing; record the source, the verification method, the reviewer, and the date next to every claim that could be challenged. Where a figure is estimated, label it as such and separate it from verified facts. Where a customer statement is paraphrased, prefer the customer's own words for the part they authored.

## Controls

The controlled QBR record identifies the customer, the meeting date, the participants and their roles, the period under review, the verified objectives, the data sources, the data reviewers, the open items, the decisions taken, the assigned owners, the deadlines, and the next meeting. Give it one accountable owner and a named delegate. State the review frequency, the approval authority for any commitment that exceeds operational latitude, the data classification of every attached exhibit, and the escalation route when the customer contests a figure.

Materials that depend on telemetry or third-party data must identify the source, the snapshot date, and the reconciliation method, and must separate verified facts from estimates and from customer statements. Material changes to a commitment made in a QBR require review by someone other than the author before they are reissued, particularly where the change affects renewal terms or commercial exposure. Restrict distribution to the audience agreed with the customer, retain for the period required by the contract, and follow the approved destruction schedule.

## Validation evidence

Direct validation must reconcile the published outcomes against the customer's underlying objectives, the support records, the adoption telemetry snapshot, and any third-party data source cited in the deck. Record the sample, the environment, the expected result, the observed result, the reviewer, and the correction ticket. Reconcile open-item counts to the action register and include rejected, overdue, and overridden cases so the validation cannot pass by examining only clean examples.

To validate the meeting itself, capture meeting minutes that record attendees, decisions, owners, and deadlines, and reconcile them against the action register within the agreed interval. Retain the criteria version, dated source extracts, approvals, change history, the test method and result, sampled communications, exceptions, and corrective actions. Review effectiveness as well as procedural completion: useful indicators include repeat decisions that never close, escalations that recur across quarters, value claims that the customer later disputes, and lag between a decision and the action it implied. Internal targets are management choices, not public guarantees unless formally authorised.

## Failure modes and correction

When the QBR fails its own controls, protect the customer's position first. If the deck overstated value, surface the correction in the next meeting and notify the customer's executive sponsor; do not silently retcon prior decks. If an open item has aged past its deadline without closure, treat it as an incident: name the customer impact, the containment owner, the next update, the recovery deadline, and the closure test. Do not close because an internal ticket moved state.

If the customer contests a figure, preserve the disputed evidence with its original timestamps and rationale; never rewrite history. Search for similarly exposed QBRs in the same portfolio, since a single-source error usually signals a wider error in the underlying extract. Where the failure relates to data classification or distribution, treat the disclosure channel as the affected control and report to the customer.

## Limitations

This practice cannot prove causation, eliminate professional judgement, or repair unreliable source data. Sampling can miss rare harms, lagging measures can reveal defects late, and the customer's own objectives can change after the meeting, which makes a delivered outcome unrecognisable against the original commitment. Standards and local obligations change; the agenda should be reassessed after a material incident, a significant change in the customer's environment, or evidence that the current shape creates unintended outcomes.

A documented QBR can still fail in use. The agenda is a checklist, not a substitute for preparation, judgement, or a strong account team. Treat it as a starting point, not an endpoint.
