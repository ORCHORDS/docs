# Customer Success Renewal SMART Goal Discipline

Renewal conversations drift when the desired outcome is phrased as "keep the customer happy and growing." A renewal goal stated without a measurable baseline and a deadline cannot be tracked, coached, or honestly declared achieved. This article applies the SMART structure — specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound — to renewal outcomes, and defines how baselines are captured so that progress claims rest on measurement rather than narrative.

## Scope

Applies to goal-setting for renewal accounts of all sizes: the renewal objective the account owner is accountable for, the supporting adoption goals that feed it, and the quarterly goals assigned across a renewal portfolio. Covers goal formulation, baseline capture, progress review, and the honest declaration of achievement or miss. Does not cover quota-setting and compensation design (commercial policy), the legal terms of renewal, or product roadmap commitments made to secure a renewal, each of which has its own governance. Where a goal's measure depends on usage data, the authoritative telemetry source governs the measurement.

## Workflow or implementation guidance

1. **Write the outcome sentence first, then test each letter.** A renewal goal takes the form: by [date], [named account] renews [scope] because [evidence-backed value condition] is met, measured by [metric against baseline]. Each element is then challenged — is the scope specific, does a measurement exist, is the target achievable given remaining runway, does it relate to what the customer values, and is the date real?
2. **Capture the baseline before setting the target.** For every measurable element, record the current value, the measurement source and definition, and the extraction date. A target of "increase weekly active administrators by thirty percent" is meaningless until the denominator is pinned. Where no baseline exists, the first goal period may be baseline establishment, and that is stated as the goal.
3. **Prefer customer-outcome measures over vendor-activity measures.** "Complete twelve check-ins" is activity; "three of four contracted workflows in weekly use by two named teams" is an outcome. Activity counts may support an outcome goal but cannot substitute for one.
4. **State the achievability argument.** The goal record includes a short rationale for why the target is reachable in the time available — historical pace for comparable accounts, dependencies already in place, known blockers — so an unrealistic goal is visible at setting time, not at miss time.
5. **Limit each renewal to a small number of goals.** Two to four outcome goals per account keeps attention coherent; a list of ten dilutes every one of them. Supporting activities live beneath goals as tactics, not as peer goals.
6. **Review on a fixed cadence with evidence attached.** Progress reviews show each goal's current measured value against baseline and target with extraction dates. A goal showing no movement across two reviews triggers either a tactic change or an explicit re-plan — silence is not a status.
7. **Close every goal with a verdict.** At renewal decision or deadline, each goal is marked achieved, partially achieved with the measured shortfall, missed with explanation, or obsolete with the reason the goal ceased to matter. Unverdicted goals are closed as "no evidence" which counts as a miss for process-quality purposes.
8. **Feed verdicts into portfolio learning.** Aggregated goal outcomes by type reveal which goal patterns actually predict renewal, and the template library is pruned of goals that correlate with nothing.

## Controls

- Baselines must cite the authoritative source and definition version; a goal whose measure cannot be re-extracted is returned for rework.
- Goals and their verdicts are visible to the account team and management, not private commitments, preventing quiet retro-fitting of targets.
- Changing a goal mid-period requires a dated amendment with reason; the original is retained so the record shows what was first promised.
- Compensation-sensitive goals receive a second-reader check for gaming risk — measures that can be satisfied without customer benefit are reworded or paired with a counterbalancing quality measure.
- Customer-identifiable data inside goal records follows access control by account team, not blanket portfolio visibility.

## Validation evidence

A disciplined renewal goal set demonstrates: goal records with all five SMART elements explicitly present; baselines with source, definition version, and extraction date; at least one mid-period review showing measured values against baseline and target; dated amendments where goals changed; and final verdicts with measured values at close. Portfolio-level evidence includes the distribution of verdicts by goal type across at least one full cycle and the disposition of goals that failed to predict anything. Spot-check that three randomly selected "achieved" verdicts reproduce from their cited measurement sources.

## Failure modes and correction

- **Baseline retrofitting** (a target set after seeing the trend): compare baseline extraction dates against goal creation dates; a goal created after its baseline was already achieved is voided and rewritten.
- **Measure substitution at close** (quietly swapping to an easier metric): verdicts must reference the goal's original measure identifier; substitutions are treated as misses under the original measure.
- **Activity theater** (all goals are completed-actions, none are outcomes): the second-reader check rejects goal sets with no customer-outcome measure.
- **Evergreen goals** (rolled forward period after period without verdict): the closure rule forces a verdict at each period end; renewal of a goal without a verdict of the prior instance is blocked.

## Limitations

SMART discipline improves the honesty of goal management; it cannot guarantee that goals capture everything that decides a renewal. Customer-side changes — acquisitions, budget shocks, leadership turnover — can obsolete a well-formed goal overnight, and the "obsolete" verdict exists precisely for that. Small accounts may not justify heavy measurement, and the structure should scale down rather than be applied ritualistically.

## Canonical sources

- [ISO 9001 Quality management](https://www.iso.org/iso-9001-quality-management.html) — measurable objectives, evidence-based evaluation, and review discipline.
- [NIST Baldrige Performance Excellence Program](https://www.nist.gov/baldrige) — performance measurement, baseline, and results-oriented review practices.
