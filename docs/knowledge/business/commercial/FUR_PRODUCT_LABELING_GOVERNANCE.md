# Fur Product Labeling Governance

## Purpose

The U.S. Federal Trade Commission's Fur Products Labeling Act and Fur Rules require covered fur garments and other fur products to carry material product information in a conspicuous, durable form through delivery to the consumer.

## Required product facts

For covered fur products, a labeling process should determine and preserve the facts needed for the applicable label, including:

- the animal name recognized by the Fur Products Name Guide;
- the country of origin for imported fur;
- whether the fur is pointed, dyed, bleached, or otherwise artificially colored, or is natural where appropriate;
- whether the product contains more than the applicable amount of paws, tails, bellies, scraps, or other pieces or waste fur;
- whether the fur is used or damaged; and
- textile or wool content and the responsible manufacturer or dealer identity where those rules also apply.

A name that contains a geographic term should not be used in a way that creates a false origin impression.

## Label mechanics

FTC guidance requires required information to be legible, conspicuous, readily accessible to the prospective buyer, and presented with equal conspicuousness on the same side of the label. The label must be durable enough to remain on the product until delivery to the consumer.

Pairs or ensembles containing the same fur and country of origin can qualify for a single label in the circumstances described by the FTC guidance.

## Supply-chain controls

A reusable process should:

1. obtain species, origin, treatment, and composition data from controlled supplier records;
2. validate that prohibited fur is not introduced into the product or supply chain;
3. generate the physical label, invoice data, and ecommerce product facts from the same controlled source;
4. preserve the evidence used to support the label; and
5. reissue customer-facing information when species, source, treatment, composition, or responsible-party information changes.

## Advertising relationship

Fur labeling and fur advertising are related but distinct controls. The required information can also apply to invoices and advertising, but a compliant physical label should not be treated as automatically curing a misleading remote-sale listing or advertisement.

## Scope note

This article summarizes general U.S. FTC labeling guidance. Wildlife law, the Dog and Cat Protection Act, customs requirements, sanctions, textile and wool rules, state law, and other restrictions may apply separately.
