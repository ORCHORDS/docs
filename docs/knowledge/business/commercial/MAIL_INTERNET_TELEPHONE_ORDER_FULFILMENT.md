# Mail, Internet, and Telephone Order Fulfilment

## Purpose

The U.S. Federal Trade Commission's Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule applies to covered merchandise ordered by mail, online, or by telephone. Sellers must have a reasonable basis for the shipment timing they advertise. If no shipment time is stated, the FTC rule uses a 30-day default expectation.

## Operational controls

A reusable fulfilment process should:

1. make shipment promises only when there is a reasonable operational basis for them;
2. track whether an order can ship within the promised period;
3. notify the customer when the promised shipment timing cannot be met;
4. obtain the customer's consent to a qualifying delay when the rule permits continued fulfilment;
5. cancel and issue the required refund when consent is not obtained or the customer cancels; and
6. retain enough order and communication evidence to reconstruct how shipment timing, delay notices, cancellation, and refunds were handled.

## Refund handling

When a Rule-required refund is due, do not substitute store credit or future-purchase credit for the required refund. The FTC's business guidance also distinguishes refund timing based on the payment method and explains how shipping and handling amounts are treated for partial shipments.

## Scope boundaries

The Rule has defined coverage and exceptions. A generic order-management system should therefore separate operational controls from legal-scope decisions and route uncertain cases for jurisdiction-specific review.
