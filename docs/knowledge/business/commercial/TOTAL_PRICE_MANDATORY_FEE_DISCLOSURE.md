# Total Price and Mandatory Fee Disclosure

## Purpose

Pricing should be presented so customers can understand the amount they are expected to pay without discovering unavoidable charges only at the end of a transaction.

## Current U.S. rule for covered sectors

The U.S. Federal Trade Commission's Rule on Unfair or Deceptive Fees, 16 C.F.R. Part 464, took effect on May 12, 2025. The FTC states that the rule applies to businesses offering, displaying, or advertising live-event tickets and short-term lodging, including relevant intermediaries and business-to-business transactions in those covered sectors.

For covered offers that display a price, the FTC's current small-entity guidance says the displayed total price generally must include mandatory fees the business knows about and can calculate upfront. Government charges, shipping charges, and genuinely optional ancillary goods or services may be excluded from that initial total, but excluded charges must be disclosed before payment and included in the final amount of payment.

## Operational checklist

For a covered transaction:

1. identify every mandatory charge that is known and calculable before display;
2. include those mandatory charges in the total price shown upfront;
3. keep the total price more prominent than component-price or fee itemization;
4. describe fees truthfully and specifically rather than using misleading labels;
5. disclose permitted excluded charges clearly before asking for payment;
6. show the final amount of payment before the customer commits;
7. ensure marketplaces and intermediaries exchange the pricing data needed to calculate the correct total; and
8. review state and local requirements that may provide additional protections.

## Scope boundary

The FTC rule discussed here is sector-specific. It does not create a universal U.S. all-in-pricing rule for every product or service. Businesses outside live-event ticketing and short-term lodging should evaluate the laws and rules that apply to their own sector and jurisdiction and should still avoid deceptive price or fee representations.
