# Export License Determination Release Control

## Purpose

An export license determination is a gated decision connecting item classification, destination, parties, end use, end user, and transaction facts before export, reexport, transfer, or controlled technology access. The control prevents a shipment or release from proceeding on the strength of an item code alone.

## Scope and ownership

Apply the workflow to goods, software, technology, demonstrations, technical support, returns, replacements, hand-carry movements, and remote access within the organization’s export-control scope. Export compliance owns the determination method and final release authority. Product specialists substantiate technical characteristics; commercial teams identify parties and intended use; logistics and IT enforce the resulting hold or authorization.

## Workflow

### Define the contemplated activity

Record what will move or be accessed, from and to where, by whom, through which intermediaries, for whose benefit, and for what stated use. Include nationality or location facts relevant to technology access where applicable rules require them. A vague project or customer label is insufficient.

### Establish classification

Use a documented Export Control Classification Number determination or other applicable status, including technical facts and decision source. Supplier classifications can be useful evidence but should be checked against the item and transaction actually supplied. Version-control classifications when product capabilities change.

### Evaluate transaction controls

Assess destination-based requirements and end-use and end-user restrictions under applicable regulations. Complete restricted-party review and investigate red flags. Determine whether an authorization, license exception, or no-license-required conclusion is supportable. Record each regulatory input and do not infer authorization from a previous sale with different facts.

### Approve conditions

Where a license or exception is used, translate its scope and conditions into executable controls: authorized items, quantities, values, destinations, parties, validity period, reporting, document legends, and technology-access limits. Assign owners for decrementing and condition monitoring.

### Enforce release

Shipping, download, repository, support, and access systems should require a valid determination tied to current facts. Recheck when the product, party, destination, route, use, quantity, value, or authorization status changes.

## Controls

Separate commercial approval from export release. Restrict determination and override permissions. Expire decisions when supporting facts or authorization periods lapse. Reconcile physical exports and controlled access events to approvals. Monitor manual shipments, free samples, employee travel, returns, cloud access, and post-sale support because they may bypass order controls.

Use controlled regulatory content and log update reviews. An Export Compliance Program should include management commitment, risk assessment, authorization procedures, recordkeeping, training, audits, and corrective action; the release gate is one operating component rather than the entire program.

## Validation evidence

Retain the technical classification record, transaction facts, party-screening result, end-use statement where obtained, red-flag resolution, regulatory analysis, authorization or exception support, conditions checklist, approvals, system release record, shipping or access evidence, and post-release reports. Sampling should trace released events backward and confirm the decision was valid at event time.

Test negative scenarios in nonproduction: expired authorization, changed destination, added consignee, quantity beyond authorization, and missing end-use information. Each should create a durable hold and auditable escalation.

## Failure handling

When facts are missing, inconsistent, or raise a red flag, retain the hold and request reliable clarification. Commercial pressure is not evidence. If regulatory data or enforcement interfaces are unavailable, stop affected activity or invoke a documented manual contingency controlled by export compliance.

For a suspected unlicensed release, preserve records, stop related activity, secure remaining goods or access, and escalate promptly to qualified export-control specialists. Do not backdate a determination or alter logs. Define the affected population, investigate cause, evaluate correction or disclosure options with qualified advisers, remediate the control, and retest it.

## Scope note

Other U.S. regimes and foreign jurisdictions may control the same activity. A determination under one regime does not establish compliance with all others.
