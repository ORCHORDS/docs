# Automotive Fuel Rating Governance

## Purpose

The U.S. Federal Trade Commission's Automotive Fuel Ratings, Certification and Posting Rule requires covered participants in the liquid automotive fuel supply chain to determine, certify, post, and retain evidence of fuel ratings so consumers receive consistent information at the point of sale.

## Covered workflow

A reusable compliance process should distinguish the responsibilities of refiners, importers, producers, distributors, common carriers, and retailers. Covered parties should determine which fuels and transactions fall within the Rule before relying on a simplified retail-only checklist.

## Rating determination and certification

For covered liquid automotive fuels:

1. Establish the automotive fuel rating using an allowed determination method.
2. Carry the rating through transfers in the supply chain using the required certification mechanism.
3. Preserve delivery tickets, certification letters, or other records used to support the rating.
4. Prevent downstream systems from changing or dropping the certified rating without an evidence-based reason.
5. Escalate discrepancies between received certification, product identity, and the rating presented to consumers.

The FTC's current business guide states that covered businesses must retain specified fuel-rating determination and certification records for one year and make them available for authorized inspection.

## Retail posting

Retailers of covered automotive fuel should ensure that:

- at least one fuel-rating label appears on each face of each dispenser through which the fuel is sold;
- separate ratings are posted when a dispenser sells multiple fuels with different ratings;
- labels correspond to the fuel actually dispensed; and
- damaged, missing, or outdated labels are corrected promptly.

## Change and incident control

A rating-governance process should trigger review when fuel composition, supplier certification, dispenser configuration, branding, or product mapping changes. Suspected mislabeling should be treated as both a consumer-information and supply-chain evidence issue: preserve the relevant delivery and rating records, stop propagating unsupported values, and correct affected postings.

## Scope note

This article summarizes general U.S. FTC fuel-rating governance. It does not determine whether a particular fuel, blend, business, or transaction is covered and does not replace the rule text or other federal, state, environmental, or weights-and-measures requirements.
