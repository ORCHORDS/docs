# APHIS PPQ-505 Import Permit Operations

## Regulatory boundary for plant imports

The U.S. Department of Agriculture's Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service (APHIS) regulates the importation of regulated plants, plant products, biological control organisms, and soil under 7 CFR part 319. Domestic importers of regulated material must obtain a Plant Protection and Quarantine (PPQ) permit using form PPQ-505 ("Application for Permit to Import Plants and Plant Products"). The PPQ-585 ("Plants and Plant Products Permit") is then issued, governing the conditions under which the material may enter. This article covers the operational workflow for applying for a PPQ-585 permit, transmitting conditions into the import process, and capturing evidence of compliance. It does not cover PPQ-526 permits for biological organisms, nor does it cover CITES permits, which are administered by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.

## Permit-to-shipment control sequence

1. **Identify the regulated material.** The importer determines that the plants or plant products being imported fall under 7 CFR part 319. Common regulated articles include bulbs, corms, cuttings, nursery stock, fruits and vegetables, rice, cotton, sugarcane, and certain timber and wood products (the latter are also subject to foreign-origin plant pest regulations).
2. **Submit PPQ-505 in APHIS ePermits.** The importer creates an APHIS ePermits account, completes the PPQ-505 application, and supplies the botanical name, country of origin, intended port of entry, intended use, and proposed treatment or processing.
3. **Receive the PPQ-585 permit.** APHIS issues the PPQ-585 electronically with permit conditions that include the regulated article, the authorized countries of origin, the authorized ports of entry, the inspection requirements, any required treatments (e.g., cold treatment, methyl bromide fumigation), and the distribution limits.
4. **Transmit the conditions to the supply chain.** The importer transmits the permit number, the regulated article, the authorized origin and port, and the treatment requirements to the supplier and to the customs broker. The customs broker needs the permit number to file the entry.
5. **Pre-arrival notification.** For certain commodities the importer must provide a pre-arrival notice to APHIS PPQ at the port of entry with the date of arrival, the means of conveyance, and the container or lot identifier.
6. **Inspection at port of entry.** APHIS PPQ inspects the shipment at the port of entry. If the conditions are met, the shipment is released. If not, the shipment is refused entry, held for treatment, or re-exported.
7. **Maintain the permit and the records.** The importer retains the PPQ-585 and the underlying records (entry summary, treatment certificate, inspection results) for the period required by APHIS regulations.

## Botanical, origin, and routing data

The permit record must carry the PPQ-585 permit number, the regulated article (genus and species), the country of origin, the authorized port of entry, the treatment requirement (chemical, temperature, or irradiation), the inspection point of contact, the expiration date, and any distribution restriction (e.g., "for processing only," "limited distribution state"). The shipment record must carry the import permit number, the entry summary number, the date of arrival, the container identifier, the inspection result, the treatment applied, and the release decision.

## Evidence from permit through release

Validation evidence includes the PPQ-585 permit document with conditions, the pre-arrival notification acknowledgment, the port-of-entry inspection record, the treatment certificate when applicable, the entry summary filed by the customs broker, and the post-entry reconciliation showing that the material was processed in accordance with the permit conditions. Periodic audits reconcile the actual shipments against the permit conditions and confirm any required state notification was performed.

## Plant quarantine exceptions and remediation

- **Permit expired.** A shipment arrived after the PPQ-585 expiration date. The shipment is refused entry; the importer applies for a new permit, updates the supply chain to plan around the renewal window, and ensures that future shipments arrive before expiration.
- **Wrong port of entry.** A shipment arrived at a port not authorized by the permit. APHIS PPQ may refuse entry or, in some cases, allow an in-transit shipment to be routed to the authorized port; the importer corrects the routing and updates the customs broker instructions.
- **Treatment not performed.** The shipment arrived without the treatment required by the permit. APHIS PPQ may hold the shipment for treatment at the importer's expense, refuse entry, or order re-export. The importer updates the supplier instructions and adds a pre-shipment treatment check.
- **Restricted distribution not enforced.** A permit limited distribution to certain states, and the product was distributed more broadly. The product is recalled to the authorized states, the distribution records are reviewed, and the control is updated to require a destination check at order entry.
- **Pre-arrival notification missing.** The notification was not filed within the required window. The shipment is held at port until the notification is provided; the importer updates the process to ensure the notification is filed in time.

## Boundaries of a PPQ permit

Permit coverage and conditions are commodity-, origin-, destination-, and use-specific. A PPQ permit does not replace customs entry, state quarantines, CITES documentation, food rules, or other agency approvals. The issued permit and current APHIS manuals control over this workflow.
