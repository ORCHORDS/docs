# Consumer Lease Advertising Disclosures

## Purpose

U.S. consumer-lease advertisements can trigger specific disclosure duties under the Consumer Leasing Act and Regulation M. Commercial teams should identify those duties before publishing lease-payment, amount-due, or similar promotional claims.

## Current regulatory basis

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's current Regulation M, 12 CFR Part 1013, was most recently amended effective January 1, 2026. Section 1013.7 governs consumer-lease advertising.

The rule requires advertised lease terms to be usually and customarily available and requires mandated disclosures to be clear and conspicuous. Certain payment or amount-due statements trigger additional lease disclosures.

The coverage threshold in the definition of a consumer lease is inflation-adjusted. For January 1 through December 31, 2026, the CFPB states the threshold is **$73,400**. Do not hard-code that number into evergreen systems or policies without also tracking the effective year because the threshold is adjusted periodically.

## Advertising workflow

1. Determine whether the promoted transaction is a consumer lease within the current Regulation M scope.
2. Confirm the applicable inflation-adjusted threshold for the date of the advertisement.
3. Identify whether the advertisement contains a triggering term, such as an amount required before or at consummation or the amount of a payment.
4. If triggered, include the disclosures required by current § 1013.7, including that the transaction is a lease and the other applicable payment, timing, security-deposit, and end-of-term information.
5. Make required disclosures clear and conspicuous for the actual medium; do not rely on unreadable fine print or rapidly delivered audio.
6. Ensure advertised terms are genuinely available on the stated basis and do not advertise a lease configuration that is not usually and customarily offered.
7. Review special rules before advertising lease rates or using radio or television disclosure alternatives.
8. Keep the underlying offer, approval, and disclosure evidence needed to show what terms were actually available when the advertisement ran.

## Current-source caution

The FTC's plain-language *Advertising Consumer Leases* guide remains useful for explaining triggering terms and disclosure concepts, but its page currently displays an older contractual-obligation threshold. For the current threshold and operative regulatory text, use the CFPB's current Regulation M pages or the official Code of Federal Regulations.

This article is U.S.-focused general compliance guidance, not legal advice. It does not determine whether a particular transaction, advertiser, medium, or lease is covered, exempt, or compliant, and state-law requirements may also apply.
