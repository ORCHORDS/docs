# Clothing Care Label Governance

## Purpose

The U.S. Federal Trade Commission's Care Labeling Rule requires covered manufacturers and importers to provide care instructions on clothing and certain piece goods. Care information is a product-use control: inaccurate or unsupported instructions can cause avoidable damage after sale.

## Coverage and ownership

A compliance process should first determine whether the product and business role are covered. FTC guidance identifies manufacturers and importers of textile wearing apparel, manufacturers and importers of certain piece goods, and parties that direct or control manufacturing or importing as covered roles, subject to specified exclusions and exemptions.

## Instruction controls

For covered products:

1. Provide complete instructions for regular care or an appropriate warning when the product cannot be cleaned without harm.
2. Maintain a reasonable basis for each care method represented on the label.
3. Ensure that following the stated instructions will not cause substantial harm to the product.
4. Warn about commonly assumed procedures that would damage the item, such as ironing where washing instructions alone could otherwise imply that ironing is safe.
5. Use restrictive claims such as `Dryclean Only` only when supported by evidence that an alternative method would damage the garment.
6. Keep care information permanent and legible for the product's useful life as required by the Rule.

## Multi-piece and manufacturing changes

Where a product contains multiple pieces, determine whether one care label is sufficient or whether pieces require different instructions. Revalidate care instructions after material, dye, finish, construction, trim, coating, or supplier changes that could affect how the product reacts to laundering, drying, ironing, bleaching, or professional cleaning.

## Evidence and change control

Retain the testing, supplier data, technical evaluation, or other evidence that supports the care method and any warning. Product-development and merchandising systems should prevent a stale care instruction from surviving a material product change.

## Scope note

This article summarizes general U.S. FTC care-label governance. Textile fiber, wool, country-of-origin, customs, safety, chemical, and state requirements may apply separately.
