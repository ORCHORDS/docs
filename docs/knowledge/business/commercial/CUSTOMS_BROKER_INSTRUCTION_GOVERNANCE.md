# Customs Broker Instruction Governance

## Purpose

A customs broker can transmit declarations and interact with customs authorities, but outsourcing transmission does not make inaccurate commercial data reliable. Broker instruction governance ensures each filing is based on approved facts, changes are authorized, and filed results are reconciled to company records.

## Scope and roles

Cover imports, exports where a filing agent is used, special procedures, returns, corrections, and post-entry activity. Trade compliance owns filing policy and classification governance. Logistics provides movement facts, procurement or sales provides transaction terms, finance provides value support, and the broker executes within written authority. Power-of-attorney administration is restricted to authorized corporate roles.

## Workflow

### Onboard the broker

Perform competence and service review for the relevant countries, commodities, and procedures. Execute required authority and contract terms, define approved contacts, escalation paths, confidentiality protections, record access, service levels, and termination steps. Document which decisions the broker may make and which require written company approval.

### Create an instruction package

Send a transaction identifier, parties, goods descriptions, approved classification, origin, value and currency, quantity, Incoterms information, transport references, license or program data where applicable, and supporting documents. Draw fields from controlled systems. Distinguish facts from requests for broker advice.

### Validate exceptions

A broker query, proposed classification change, value adjustment, or claim for preferential treatment must route to the designated owner. Operational urgency does not authorize the broker or warehouse to guess. Record the question, evidence, decision, approver, and transactions to which the decision applies.

### Review before filing when feasible

For high-risk entries, new products, new procedures, or material exceptions, require a draft declaration or structured pre-filing confirmation. Confirm importer or exporter identity, line details, procedure, value, classification, origin, and supporting references.

### Reconcile filed results

Obtain filing and release records and compare them with instructions. Investigate differences in line count, classification, origin, value, quantities, duties, fees, and procedure codes. Feed accepted corrections into source governance rather than allowing recurring broker-side workarounds.

## Controls

Maintain an approved-broker register and current authority records. Use secure transmission and prohibit instructions from unverified personal addresses. Apply dual approval to new powers of attorney, new special programs, and material master-data overrides. Review broker user access and company contact lists periodically.

Measure late instruction packages, broker queries, unapproved changes, filing-to-instruction differences, customs holds, correction frequency, and duty variances. Service reviews should use transaction evidence, not only aggregate on-time statistics. Contracts should preserve access to records needed for company oversight and regulatory response.

## Validation evidence

Retain due diligence, contract and authority records, contact approvals, each instruction package, source documents, broker questions, company decisions, draft reviews, filed declarations, release messages, duty statements, reconciliations, and corrections. A sample should trace filed line data back to an approved company source and verify that any variance has a documented disposition.

Independently test terminated brokers and departed contacts to confirm they cannot submit new instructions through company channels. Test new-product and manual-entry populations because these are more likely to bypass governed master data.

## Failure handling

If instructions are incomplete or contradictory, hold the transaction and identify the accountable decision owner. Do not direct the broker to “use best judgment” for a material fact. If an unauthorized filing or discrepancy is discovered, preserve instruction and filing records, stop repeat transactions where appropriate, and consult the broker and qualified compliance personnel on available correction processes and deadlines.

When a broker system or secure channel fails, use only an approved contingency route with identity verification and later reconciliation. Following a systemic error, identify all filings using the same product, rule, contact, or upload; conduct a lookback; correct source data; and verify the broker implemented the change.

## Scope note

Broker licensing, representation, recordkeeping, and correction processes differ by jurisdiction. Local requirements and the applicable customs procedure control.
