# Used Car Buyers Guide Governance

## Purpose

The U.S. Federal Trade Commission's Used Car Rule requires covered dealers to use a standardized Buyers Guide when offering used vehicles for sale. The Guide is a consumer-facing disclosure control: it communicates warranty status and other required information before sale and must remain consistent with the final transaction.

This article summarizes reusable governance practices for organizations subject to the Rule. It is U.S.-specific and should be applied with current federal and state requirements.

## Applicability

The FTC's Dealer's Guide explains that most dealers that sell, or offer for sale, more than five used vehicles in a twelve-month period are covered. The federal Rule contains exemptions and does not apply identically in every state; Maine and Wisconsin use qualifying state regimes instead of the federal Buyers Guide.

Before using this workflow, determine whether the seller, vehicle, location, and transaction are within scope. Do not assume every resale, auction, lease disposition, motorcycle, or salvage transaction is covered in the same way.

## Pre-sale display control

For a covered vehicle, the Buyers Guide must be displayed before the vehicle is offered for sale or shown to a consumer for the purpose of buying it. Operationally:

- generate the Guide before the vehicle becomes customer-visible inventory;
- use the FTC-prescribed form and required formatting;
- ensure the Guide is prominent and both sides can be viewed;
- restore the Guide promptly if it is temporarily removed for a test drive; and
- prevent listing or lot-status changes from bypassing the disclosure step.

A workflow should treat a missing Guide as a release blocker rather than as a paperwork defect to be repaired after sale.

## Required vehicle and dealer information

The Guide should be populated from controlled source data so that make, model, model year, vehicle identification number, dealer information, warranty status, and complaint contact information are consistent with the transaction record.

Where data is copied manually, use a review step for high-impact fields such as VIN and warranty coverage. Avoid reusing a prior vehicle's Guide as a template without clearing vehicle-specific information.

## Warranty status

The FTC form distinguishes the applicable warranty posture, including "as is" or warranty disclosures, subject to state law. If the dealer offers a written warranty, the Buyers Guide is not itself a complete written warranty document and separate federal warranty requirements may apply.

Controls should therefore separate:

- the Buyers Guide disclosure;
- the written warranty document, when one exists;
- service contracts or third-party warranty information; and
- state-specific implied-warranty requirements.

Do not treat a checked box on the Guide as a substitute for a legally sufficient warranty document.

## Negotiated changes

The Buyers Guide given to the buyer must reflect the final negotiated warranty coverage. If terms change during negotiation:

1. update the Buyers Guide before closing;
2. update any separate warranty documentation as required;
3. reconcile the sales contract to the final Guide; and
4. prevent stale versions from being handed to the buyer.

FTC guidance states that the window-form information becomes part of the sales contract and can override contrary contract provisions. This makes version control operationally important: the final Guide cannot be treated as an informational flyer detached from the contract process.

## Spanish-language transactions

If the used-car transaction is conducted in Spanish, FTC guidance requires use of a Spanish-language Buyers Guide. Sales-channel design should therefore identify the language of the transaction early enough to produce the correct form rather than relying on an ad hoc translation at closing.

The prescribed FTC form and its wording should be used; unofficial translations should not replace the required form.

## Delivery at sale

At the sale, provide the buyer the original or a copy of the final Buyers Guide. The copy should match the final warranty terms and transaction state.

A defensible closing workflow records that:

- the correct Guide was displayed before sale;
- any negotiated changes were incorporated;
- the final Guide was provided to the buyer; and
- related warranty documents were provided or made available when required.

If a signature line is used, follow the FTC's required acknowledgement wording rather than inventing alternative language.

## Online and remote sales

The FTC's consumer guidance states that buyers are entitled to a Buyers Guide when purchasing a used car from a dealer online. A digital sales path should therefore preserve the same core disclosure objectives as an in-person path and should not hide the Guide behind optional navigation or provide it only after payment.

Where a business combines online shopping with physical delivery, define which system is authoritative for the final Guide and how last-minute warranty changes propagate to the customer-facing copy.

## Quality-control checklist

Periodically sample transactions and verify:

- every covered vehicle had a Guide before being offered for sale;
- the correct federal or applicable state form was used;
- vehicle identifiers matched the vehicle sold;
- warranty status was accurate;
- Spanish-language transactions used the required Spanish form;
- final negotiated changes appeared on the Guide;
- the buyer received the final version; and
- the sales contract did not contradict the Guide.

Investigate repeated defects as process failures rather than correcting individual forms only.

## Records and change management

Retain evidence appropriate to the organization's legal and records policy, such as the final Buyers Guide, related warranty documents, transaction date, and version or generation record. Avoid retaining unnecessary personal information merely to prove form compliance.

When the FTC updates the form, rule, or staff guidance, update templates centrally and retire obsolete versions. State-law overlays should be tracked separately so a federal template update does not overwrite stricter local requirements.
