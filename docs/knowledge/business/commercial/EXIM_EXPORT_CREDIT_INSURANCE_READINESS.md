# EXIM Export-Credit Insurance Readiness

## Purpose

Export credit insurance can protect an exporter against the risk that a foreign buyer does not pay an insured receivable. The Export-Import Bank of the United States (EXIM) offers several export-credit insurance products, including small-business multi-buyer coverage.

Insurance can support more competitive open-account terms and can also improve financing options because insured foreign receivables may be more acceptable to a lender as collateral. It does not eliminate every trade risk, and policy conditions, buyer limits, reporting, and claim procedures remain important.

## Current EXIM small-business product example

EXIM's current **Multi-Buyer Small Business Insurance** page states that the policy is designed for eligible small businesses that want to insure their international customer portfolio.

Current published features include:

- coverage for commercial risks such as buyer bankruptcy;
- coverage for political risks such as war or currency inconvertibility;
- ability to support credit terms to multiple foreign customers;
- potential use of insured receivables as additional collateral for lender financing;
- **95% coverage** for commercial and political risks under the current product summary;
- **100% coverage** for sovereign buyers under the current product summary;
- no application fee or annual minimum premium; and
- a current published refundable advance deposit requirement for policy issuance.

Product terms can change. Verify the live EXIM policy page and policy documents before quoting coverage or pricing to a customer, lender, or board.

## Insurance does not replace buyer underwriting

A business should still make a credit decision about each foreign buyer.

Evaluate:

- buyer legal identity;
- ownership;
- financial condition;
- payment history;
- trade references;
- requested credit limit;
- payment terms;
- country risk; and
- concentration.

Insurance is part of the risk structure, not a substitute for knowing who owes the money.

## Policy selection

EXIM offers different insurance structures, including multi-buyer and single-buyer products and other specialized forms.

Before applying, determine:

1. number of foreign buyers;
2. annual insured export volume;
3. typical payment terms;
4. buyer concentration;
5. countries served;
6. expected growth;
7. services versus goods exports;
8. need for lender assignment; and
9. administrative capacity to report shipments and maintain compliance.

Choose a policy structure that matches the portfolio rather than trying to force every transaction into one product.

## Buyer credit limits

Insurance coverage is normally tied to approved or permitted buyer credit exposure.

Maintain a buyer-limit register showing:

- buyer;
- country;
- approved/discretionary limit;
- current outstanding insured receivables;
- oldest invoice;
- payment terms;
- overdue amount; and
- current coverage status.

Do not continue shipping on insured terms solely because prior invoices were covered if the buyer exceeds its permitted credit limit or violates policy conditions.

## Open-account sales

Export credit insurance is particularly relevant when the exporter offers open-account terms.

A practical workflow is:

1. approve buyer credit;
2. confirm insurance eligibility/limit;
3. agree enforceable sales terms;
4. ship eligible goods/services;
5. issue invoice;
6. report the insured shipment as required;
7. monitor payment; and
8. escalate overdue receivables before policy deadlines are missed.

The policy should be integrated with ordinary receivables operations.

## Commercial and political risk

Distinguish the reason a buyer did not pay.

Commercial risk can include matters such as buyer insolvency or protracted default. Political risk can include specified events such as war or currency-transfer/inconvertibility restrictions under the policy.

Not every delay or dispute is necessarily an insured event. Review the actual policy wording and exclusions.

## Disputes and performance risk

Credit insurance generally addresses nonpayment risk, not poor exporter performance.

Maintain evidence that the exporter:

- delivered what the contract required;
- met shipment/service obligations;
- issued accurate invoices;
- complied with material documentation requirements; and
- addressed legitimate buyer disputes.

A commercial dispute can complicate or delay a claim if the buyer asserts that payment is withheld because the exporter failed to perform.

## Shipment reporting

If the policy requires shipment or premium reporting, build it into the billing workflow.

Track:

- shipment/service completion date;
- invoice amount;
- buyer;
- destination;
- payment terms;
- report date;
- premium; and
- policy/buyer limit used.

Late or incomplete reporting can create avoidable coverage questions.

## Overdue receivables

Define escalation thresholds before invoices become severely delinquent.

A useful process can include:

- routine reminders;
- internal credit hold;
- stop-ship decision;
- insurer notification where required;
- collections escalation;
- legal review where appropriate; and
- claim preparation.

Do not keep extending credit to a deteriorating buyer because insurance exists.

## Claims readiness

A claim file can require evidence such as:

- policy and buyer approval/limit;
- purchase order or contract;
- invoice;
- shipping/delivery evidence;
- payment terms;
- collection communications;
- buyer dispute information;
- account statement; and
- required insurer notices.

Preserve records from the start of the transaction rather than reconstructing them after default.

## Lender use of insured receivables

EXIM's current small-business policy page notes that insured receivables can support financing by being used as additional collateral with a lender.

If receivables are assigned or pledged:

- identify lender rights to policy proceeds;
- coordinate claims/payment instructions;
- prevent duplicate pledges;
- reconcile insured balances with the borrowing base; and
- notify insurer/lender of material policy changes where required.

## Country and buyer restrictions

Before shipping, confirm current EXIM eligibility and country restrictions, including the current **Country Limitation Schedule** where applicable.

A market that was eligible when the customer relationship began may have different terms later.

Do not assume an existing policy automatically covers every destination, buyer type, term, product, or transaction.

## Export-content and program requirements

EXIM support can include U.S.-content, shipment, eligibility, and other requirements depending on the policy and transaction.

Maintain enough product/service sourcing information to support required certifications. Verify current EXIM rules before representing a transaction as insurable.

## Portfolio concentration

Insurance reduces loss severity but does not remove concentration risk.

Track exposure by:

- buyer;
- country;
- industry;
- currency;
- payment term; and
- insurer credit limit.

A portfolio heavily dependent on one foreign buyer can still create severe liquidity disruption before a claim is paid.

## Pricing and customer terms

When offering longer credit terms, model:

- insurance premium;
- financing cost;
- currency risk;
- expected loss/claim timing;
- collection cost; and
- working-capital duration.

Do not offer open-account terms purely to win a sale without including the cost and risk in margin analysis.

## Evidence to retain

For insured export receivables, retain where appropriate:

- policy and endorsements;
- buyer limits/approvals;
- shipment reports;
- premium records;
- contracts/orders;
- invoices;
- delivery/shipping evidence;
- overdue notices;
- insurer communications;
- claims and recoveries; and
- lender assignments where applicable.

## Scope note

This article provides general export-credit-insurance readiness guidance using current EXIM and U.S. government trade-finance information. It does not guarantee policy eligibility, coverage, claim payment, a 95% recovery, buyer approval, or lender financing. The issued EXIM policy, current program rules, and transaction facts control coverage.
