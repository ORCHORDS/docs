# Preferential Origin Claim Governance

## Purpose

A preferential origin claim can reduce duty only when goods satisfy the applicable trade agreement’s origin rule and procedural requirements. Governance prevents sales preference, supplier assurances, or country-of-shipment data from being treated as proof of originating status.

## Scope and ownership

Apply the workflow to import preference claims, exporter or producer certifications, customer origin statements, and free-trade-agreement pricing assumptions. Trade compliance owns rule interpretation and claim approval. Product engineering and sourcing maintain bill-of-materials facts; suppliers substantiate inputs; finance validates value calculations; customs brokers transmit only approved claims.

## Workflow

### Identify the exact rule

Start with the specific agreement, importing country, product classification, and claim period. Locate the product-specific rule and relevant definitions, de minimis provisions, accumulation rules, value-content methods, and processing requirements. Do not rely on a rule copied from another agreement or tariff classification.

### Build the origin record

Create a controlled bill of materials identifying materials, classifications where needed, supplier, origin status, values, and manufacturing locations. Document the production process. Separate “purchased domestically” from “originating”; location of purchase alone does not prove status.

### Obtain supplier support

Request certifications or factual declarations containing the required scope and period. Validate identity, product coverage, authorized signer, dates, and agreement. Track expiration and changes. A supplier statement should feed the analysis but does not transfer accountability for an unsupported claim.

### Perform and approve the analysis

Apply the relevant tariff-shift, wholly obtained, regional-value-content, or processing rule. Show calculations, currency and period assumptions, and treatment of materials. Have a qualified reviewer approve the result and define the products, facilities, destinations, and validity period covered.

### Issue and monitor claims

Provide the approved claim or certification using applicable format and data elements. Connect broker instructions and customer documents to the approved origin record. Reassess after classification changes, supplier or material substitutions, production relocation, cost changes affecting value calculations, or agreement updates.

## Controls

Block preference claims without an active approved determination. Restrict origin overrides and certification signing. Use expiration alerts and change triggers from product lifecycle and procurement systems. Reconcile broker claims to the approved-product population and sample invoices for unauthorized preference indicators.

Monitor missing supplier support, expired certifications, calculations near thresholds, claim changes, denied claims, and duty savings by agreement. Duty savings help define validation priority but do not establish eligibility.

## Validation evidence

Retain the applicable rule text or stable reference, classification support, bill of materials, manufacturing narrative, supplier certifications, invoices and values, calculations, reviewer approval, issued certification, broker instruction, entry record, and change history. Evidence should show facts existing when the claim was made, not a later reconstruction alone.

Validation should recalculate selected determinations, verify supplier documents directly against covered parts and dates, and trace customs entries to active approvals. Test change events to confirm they suspend or reassess claims as designed.

## Failure handling

If support is incomplete before entry, do not claim preference merely to preserve expected landed cost. Use applicable nonpreferential treatment and evaluate whether the governing procedure permits a later claim after substantiation.

If an unsupported or incorrect claim is discovered, suspend new claims for the affected scope, preserve records, identify entries and certifications, and notify trade compliance and the broker. Qualified customs specialists should determine correction, notification, repayment, and disclosure steps under the relevant jurisdiction and agreement. Investigate whether the cause was a stale supplier statement, classification error, bill-of-materials change, calculation defect, or unauthorized broker action; then correct and retest the source control.

## Scope note

Each agreement has distinct substantive and procedural requirements. The USMCA sources illustrate an authoritative framework; they must not be generalized to a different agreement.
