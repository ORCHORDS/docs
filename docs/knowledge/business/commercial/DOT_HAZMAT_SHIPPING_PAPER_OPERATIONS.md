# DOT Hazmat Shipping Paper Operations (49 CFR Part 172 Subpart C)

## Hazardous-material shipment boundary

The U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT) Pipeline and Hazardous Materials Safety Administration (PHMSA) regulates the preparation of shipping papers for hazardous materials transported in commerce. The technical requirements are in 49 CFR part 172 subpart C (sections 172.200 through 172.205). A shipping paper is required for every shipment of hazardous material offered for transportation, including those transported by motor vehicle, rail, air, and vessel under each mode's separate applicability rules. This article covers the data fields and workflow required to produce a compliant shipping paper for ground transport. Air transport must additionally follow the ICAO Technical Instructions and the IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations; vessel transport follows 46 CFR parts 146 to 149 and the IMDG Code. Those are separate and not within the 49 CFR part 172 subpart C boundary.

## Shipping-description assembly and handoff

1. **Identify the hazardous material.** The shipper determines that the material being offered for transport is a hazardous material under the HMR (49 CFR part 171). The determination is based on the hazard class, the packing group, and any special provisions in column 7 of the 49 CFR 172.101 Hazardous Materials Table.
2. **Assign the proper shipping name and identification number.** The proper shipping name, the UN/NA identification number, the hazard class, and the packing group are copied from the 49 CFR 172.101 Table for the specific material. For mixtures or solutions not listed by name, the shipper follows 49 CFR 172.101(c)(12) to select the generic "n.o.s." entry with the technical name of the hazardous ingredient.
3. **Prepare the basic description.** The basic description is the contiguous sequence of "UN/NA" identification number, proper shipping name, hazard class, and packing group; this sequence must appear together on the shipping paper without intervening text (49 CFR 172.202(a)).
4. **Add required additional information.** The shipping paper includes the total quantity of hazardous material in the package (with the unit of measure), the number and type of packages, the emergency response telephone number monitored by a person knowledgeable in emergency response, and any special permits or approvals cited.
5. **Sequence and order.** The order of the basic description is fixed: identification number first, proper shipping name second, primary hazard class third, packing group fourth. Subsections 172.202(a)(1)-(5) prescribe this ordering.
6. **Retain the shipping paper.** The shipper retains a copy of the shipping paper for two years after the material is accepted by the initial carrier (49 CFR 172.201(e)). A retention period of three years applies when the shipping paper describes hazardous waste; that retention is governed separately under RCRA.
7. **Issue the shipping paper.** The shipping paper is handed to the carrier with the package at the time of tender; an "order-bill" paper may be issued after the tender if the original was not yet completed.

## Basic description and emergency data

A compliant shipping paper carries the basic description (UN/NA, proper shipping name, hazard class, packing group), the total quantity with unit of measure, the number and type of packages (e.g., "4 fiberboard boxes"), the consignor and consignee identification with address, the emergency response telephone number, the date of acceptance by the carrier, and any special instructions or special permit number. For Class 1 (explosives) materials, additional information about the compatibility group is required; for Class 7 (radioactive) materials, additional information about the radionuclide, activity, and the transport index is required.

## Tender-to-delivery evidence

Validation evidence consists of five artifacts. First, the operator's draft shipping paper is reviewed by a second qualified hazmat employee (49 CFR 172.800(d)) before the tender. Second, the emergency response telephone number is dialed during the review to confirm that a person knowledgeable in emergency response picks up. Third, the basic description is checked for the required contiguity and the order; missing commas, transposition of the UN/NA identifier, or an inserted character between elements is a frequent citation. Fourth, the 49 CFR 172.101 special provisions are checked against the material to confirm that all relevant special provisions are addressed. Fifth, the shipper retains a scanned copy of the shipping paper in a documented archive for two years from the acceptance date.

## Description and retention corrections

- **Order inversed in the basic description.** The shipping paper placed the proper shipping name before the UN/NA identifier; this is rejected at the basic-description contiguity check. The operator corrects the order and reissues; the underlying shipment is held until the corrected paper is in the carrier's possession.
- **Missing packing group.** A material assigned packing group III in the 172.101 Table was shipped without the PG III suffix. The shipment is corrected by issuing a replacement shipping paper; the underlying transportation can continue, but the omission is logged as a control failure.
- **Emergency response phone unanswered.** The validation phone call reached voicemail. The shipment is held at the dock, the phone number is updated to one staffed continuously during the shipment's transit, and the new paper is reissued.
- **Improper shipping name selected.** A mixture not listed by name was shipped under a generic name without the technical name of the hazardous constituent in parentheses. The correction requires re-issuing the shipping paper with the parenthetical technical name; the package itself may be re-labeled under 49 CFR 172.301.
- **Expired two-year retention window.** A retrieval request finds that the shipping paper was destroyed after one year. The control gap is documented; subsequent retrievals are answered from the carrier's own retention, and the internal control is updated to enforce the two-year minimum.

## Modal and material-specific limits

A correct shipping paper does not establish compliant classification, packaging, marking, labeling, placarding, training, or security planning. Modal and international rules can add or alter requirements, so the shipper must check the current rules for the material, package, route, and mode.
