# Cooling-Off Rule Sales Cancellation

## Purpose

Some sales made at a consumer's home or at certain temporary locations carry a federal right to cancel. Commercial processes should identify covered transactions before the sale is completed and preserve the required cancellation path.

## Operational controls

- Determine whether the transaction is within the FTC Cooling-Off Rule's scope.
- Treat in-home sales and covered temporary-location sales separately because the monetary thresholds differ.
- Provide the required cancellation disclosures and forms at the time of sale.
- Do not obstruct, delay, or misrepresent a consumer's cancellation right.
- Record when the agreement and cancellation materials were delivered.
- Route timely cancellation requests to a workflow that stops fulfilment or begins reversal as appropriate.
- Preserve evidence of the request and resulting refund or cancellation action.

## Known federal thresholds

FTC guidance states that the rule generally covers qualifying in-home sales of $25 or more and qualifying sales at certain temporary locations of $130 or more. Covered consumers generally have three business days to cancel.

## Boundary

The rule has exclusions and does not apply to every off-premises or online transaction. State law may provide additional cancellation rights.
