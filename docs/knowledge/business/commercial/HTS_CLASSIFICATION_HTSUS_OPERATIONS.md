# HTS Classification (HTSUS) Operations

## Classification decision boundary

The Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States (HTSUS) is the legal basis for classifying imported goods and assigning duty rates. The HTSUS is administered by U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) under the Tariff Act of 1930, as amended, and is published in title 19 of the Code of Federal Regulations. The underlying nomenclature is the Harmonized System maintained by the World Customs Organization; the HTSUS adds U.S.-specific statistical suffixes and rate lines. This article covers the operational workflow an importer follows to assign an HTSUS classification, validate it, and document the rationale. It does not cover antidumping/countervailing duty determinations or Section 301 special tariffs, which are separate entries on top of the HTSUS duty column.

## GRI-based classification sequence

1. **Determine the tariff heading.** The importer reads the Harmonized System rules of interpretation, beginning with rule 1, and consults the General Rules of Interpretation (GRIs), section and chapter notes, and any relevant section or chapter ruling before assigning a tariff number. Headings are listed in numeric order.
2. **Move through the subheadings.** Once the four-digit heading is identified, the importer moves to the one-digit subheading, then to the two-digit national suffix, and finally to the U.S. statistical suffix, applying the GRIs at each level.
3. **Check CBP rulings.** The importer searches the Customs Rulings Online Search System (CROSS) for prior CBP rulings on the same product or a comparable product. Rulings that match the importer's facts are binding under 19 CFR part 177. Rulings that address a different product are useful background but not binding.
4. **Assign the duty rate.** The rate is taken from the column 1 (normal trade relations) or column 2 (non-MFN) rates in the HTSUS. Rates of duty are subject to change; the importer confirms that the rate at the time of entry is correct.
5. **Issue the classification rationale.** The importer prepares a written rationale that lists the HTSUS number, the GRIs applied, the chapter and section notes, the CROSS rulings consulted, and the rationale for not selecting alternative headings. The rationale is retained for the life of the entry under CBP recordkeeping rules (19 CFR part 163).
6. **Maintain on material change.** A change in composition, function, or essential character triggers a reclassification. The importer updates the rationale and re-issues the entry; CBP may require a binding ruling request under 19 CFR part 177 if the change is significant.

## Product facts required for tariff analysis

The classification record must include the ten-digit HTSUS number (heading + subheading + suffix), the column 1 or column 2 rate that applies, the description of the article being classified, the GRIs applied, the chapter and section notes considered, the CROSS rulings reviewed (with their reference numbers), the rationale for selecting the heading over alternative headings, the date of the entry, the entry number, and the importer of record.

## Ruling and entry evidence

Validation evidence consists of three artifacts. First, the CROSS ruling search log records the rulings reviewed and their disposition. Second, the entry summary (CBP Form 7501) carries the HTSUS number and the duty paid, which can be reconciled against the importer's classification record. Third, the importer's internal classification register shows the HTSUS number, the rationale, the date of classification, the entry number, and the operator who approved. Periodic audits reconcile the register against the entry summaries and the duty paid.

## Classification dispute correction

- **Wrong heading.** The importer selected heading 8517 (telephone sets and apparatus) when the article was a wireless audio receiver more properly classified under 8518 (microphones, headphones). The CROSS ruling search confirms 8518 is binding, the classification is corrected, and a refund claim is filed under 19 U.S.C. 1520(d) within one year of liquidation if duty was overpaid.
- **Missing chapter note application.** The importer classified a product under chapter 39 (plastics) without applying the chapter note that excludes certain rubber products from chapter 39. The classification is re-evaluated under the correct heading and corrected.
- **Statistical suffix mismatch.** The importer used a U.S. statistical suffix for one variant while the entry summary shows a different variant. The correction is to determine the correct suffix for the variant shipped, reissue the entry summary, and update the rationale.
- **Ruling no longer current.** The CROSS ruling that supported the classification has been modified or revoked. The importer re-runs the ruling search, identifies the new controlling ruling, and updates the classification rationale. CBP may assess a different rate from the date the prior ruling ceased to be controlling.
- **Essential character question.** A multi-component good presents an essential character question under GRI 3(b). The importer documents the basis for selecting one component as imparting the essential character, retains engineering and marketing evidence, and reconciles with the ruling history; an incorrect essential character finding is a common source of post-entry adjustments.

## HTS scope and update limits

Classification alone does not resolve valuation, origin, admissibility, quota, trade-remedy, or partner-agency requirements. HTSUS editions and rulings change; only CBP can issue a binding U.S. ruling on properly presented facts, and this operational rationale is not such a ruling.
