# Wool Product Labeling Governance

## Purpose

The U.S. Wool Products Labeling Act and FTC Wool Rules require most products represented to contain wool or recycled wool to carry accurate fiber, origin, and responsible-business information. Wool products overlap with ordinary textile labeling but have distinct coverage and content rules.

## Coverage

A wool product includes a product or portion of a product that contains, or is represented to contain, wool or recycled wool. FTC guidance notes that some items exempt from the Textile Act, such as certain hats and slippers, can still be covered by the Wool Act when they contain wool.

Some products are outside Wool Act coverage, including carpets, rugs, and mats covered under the Textile Act, certain upholstery, and qualifying products made for export.

## Required labeling controls

A compliant process should determine and retain evidence for:

- fiber-content percentages, including wool and recycled wool;
- other fibers at reportable percentages;
- country-of-origin information; and
- the identity of the manufacturer or other responsible business, including a valid registered identification number where used.

Labels should remain attached through sale to the consumer unless a lawful substitute label is used.

## Fiber-content accuracy

Do not knowingly use manufacturing-tolerance concepts to justify an inaccurate label. FTC guidance explains that wool-content deviations may be excused only when they result from unavoidable manufacturing variation despite due care.

Claims such as `100% Wool`, `All Wool`, or equivalent exclusive-content statements require particular care because intentionally added non-wool fiber cannot be hidden through a tolerance rule.

## Recycled wool

Recycled wool is wool that has been returned to a fibrous state after having previously been woven, knitted, or felted into a wool product. It should be identified separately from wool where the Wool Act and Rules require that distinction.

## Mail-order and online descriptions

Mail-order promotional material for covered wool products must provide the origin disclosure required by the Wool Act. Online catalog and product-description controls should therefore keep origin and fiber representations synchronized with the physical product label.

## Governance pattern

1. Obtain fiber-content evidence from manufacturing or supplier records before approving labels.
2. Distinguish ordinary wool from recycled wool in product data.
3. Validate origin claims against actual processing/manufacturing facts.
4. Keep product-page, catalog, packaging, and physical-label information consistent.
5. Preserve supplier specifications and test or due-diligence records supporting material claims.
6. Re-review labels when a supplier, fabric composition, country of manufacture, or product construction changes.
7. Escalate specialty-wool names such as cashmere, mohair, alpaca, or similar claims when composition evidence is uncertain.

## Failure modes

- Using ordinary textile exemptions for a wool-containing product can miss Wool Act coverage.
- Labeling recycled wool simply as virgin wool misstates composition.
- Using `100% Wool` where another fiber was intentionally added is misleading.
- Updating a product page without updating the sewn label or vice versa creates inconsistent consumer information.
- Removing required identification before consumer sale can violate label-retention requirements.

## Scope note

This article summarizes U.S. federal wool-labeling governance. Product-specific exemptions, state law, customs/origin rules, and international labeling requirements may impose additional obligations.
