# Consumer Warranty Governance

## Purpose

The U.S. Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act governs written warranties on consumer products and is supported by FTC disclosure, pre-sale availability, and dispute-resolution rules. The FTC explains that businesses are not required to offer a written warranty, but once they do, specific federal requirements can apply.

## Practical controls

1. **Determine coverage.** Confirm whether the product and written warranty fall within the Act and applicable FTC rules.
2. **Use the correct designation.** Where required, identify a written warranty as "full" or "limited" consistently with the warranty terms.
3. **State terms clearly.** Put required coverage information in a single, understandable warranty document.
4. **Make terms available before sale.** Ensure consumers can review covered written warranties before purchase where the pre-sale availability rule applies.
5. **Coordinate advertising.** Marketing claims about guarantees or warranties should match the actual written terms and avoid misleading implications.
6. **Control service-contract interactions.** Review how service contracts affect implied-warranty obligations and customer remedies.
7. **Retain versions.** Keep the warranty version, effective dates, product coverage, approvals, and customer-facing distribution evidence.

## Source

- Federal Trade Commission — Businessperson's Guide to Federal Warranty Law: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/businesspersons-guide-federal-warranty-law

## Scope note

Warranty obligations also depend on state law, product type, transaction type, and jurisdiction. This is general U.S. commercial-governance guidance, not legal advice.
