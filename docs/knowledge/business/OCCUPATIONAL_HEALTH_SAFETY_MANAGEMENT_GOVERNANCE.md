# Occupational Health and Safety Management Governance

## Scope

This article addresses the umbrella governance discipline of an occupational health and safety management system (OH&S MS), which enables an organisation to provide safe and healthy workplaces, prevent work-related injury and ill health, and proactively improve OH&S performance. It draws on ISO 45001:2018 and the International Labour Organization's conventions and guidelines on occupational safety and health. It addresses the management system and its governance rather than workplace incident support workflows, which belong to the support collection.

## Workflow

ISO 45001:2018 is structured around the Plan-Do-Check-Act cycle and requires the participation of workers in the system's operation:

1. **Context, workers, and compliance obligations.** The organisation determines the issues, needs, and expectations of workers and other interested parties, and the legal and other requirements applicable to its OH&S hazards. Hazard identification is proactive and considers routine and non-routine activities, all persons having access to the workplace, and situations of emergency.
2. **Leadership and worker participation.** Top management demonstrates leadership and commitment, establishes an OH&S policy, and ensures consultation and participation of workers at all applicable levels. The standard assigns specific duties to the "top management" and requires that workers be involved in hazard identification, risk assessment, and the determination of controls.
3. **Planning: hazards, risks, and opportunities.** The organisation maintains a process for hazard identification and assessment of OH&S risks and opportunities, including changes to the organisation, its activities, or its processes. A hierarchy of controls governs the selection of control measures.
4. **Support and operation.** Resources, competence, awareness, communication, and documented information support the system. Operational planning and control cover the hierarchy of controls, management of change, procurement (including contractors and outsourced functions), and emergency preparedness and response.
5. **Performance evaluation.** The organisation monitors and measures OH&S performance, evaluates compliance with legal and other requirements, conducts internal audits, and holds management review.
6. **Improvement.** Incident investigation, nonconformity and corrective action, and continual improvement complete the cycle. Investigation of incidents is required to identify systemic causes rather than individual blame.

## Controls and evidence

Typical OH&S management evidence includes:

- An OH&S policy signed by top management and visibly communicated.
- Hazard registers and risk assessments, with review triggers for change, incident, or time interval.
- Documented application of the hierarchy of controls — elimination, substitution, engineering controls, administrative controls, and personal protective equipment, in that order of preference.
- Permits to work, lockout–tagout procedures, and confined-space entry controls where applicable.
- Contractor management records, including prequalification, induction, and supervision arrangements.
- Training and competence records for safety-critical roles, including first aid and emergency response.
- Occupational exposure monitoring, health surveillance records, and industrial-hygiene assessments.
- Incident and near-miss reports with investigation records and corrective actions.
- Emergency preparedness plans and exercise records.

## Validation

Validation sources include:

- Certification to ISO 45001 by an accredited certification body.
- Internal audits of the OH&S management system and of compliance obligations.
- Regulatory inspections by occupational safety authorities, including improvement and prohibition notices.
- Worker consultation records, safety-committee minutes, and workforce safety surveys.
- Independent audits by customers, insurers, or industry bodies, including cross-industry schemes such as those used in construction and energy.
- Statistical analysis of injury and ill-health data, including lagging indicators (injury rates) and leading indicators (inspections completed, hazards closed out).

## Failure correction

OH&S failures commonly present in these patterns:

- **Incidents recur at the same operation.** Repeated similar events indicate that corrective actions were narrowly scoped or that hazard identification did not capture the underlying energy source or task variability. Correction requires systemic investigation that examines task design, supervision, and production pressure.
- **Contractor incidents excluded from the system.** Outsourced work that is excluded from the OH&S MS leaves significant exposure. Corrective action extends procurement controls, induction, and reporting to contractors.
- **Paper controls that are not followed.** Procedures that workers find impractical will be bypassed. Correction relies on worker participation in redesign and on verification through observation.
- **Under-reporting of near-misses.** Weak near-miss reporting deprives the system of its most useful leading data. Corrective action addresses reporting culture, non-punitive treatment of reporters, and visible closure of reported hazards.

## Limitations

ISO 45001 certifies the management system and does not certify the absence of risk. National occupational safety and health law prevails over voluntary standards, and many jurisdictions impose specific duties — risk assessment, safety representatives, reporting of specified injuries and diseases, and licensing of high-hazard activities — that go beyond the standard's generic requirements. The ILO's instruments, including the Promotional Framework for Occupational Safety and Health Convention (No. 187), describe national-system obligations rather than enterprise certification. Differences in national frameworks, definitions of reportable events, and enforcement practice mean that OH&S governance must be localised even when the management system is global.
