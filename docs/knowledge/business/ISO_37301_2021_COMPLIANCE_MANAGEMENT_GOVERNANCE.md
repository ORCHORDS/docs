# ISO 37301:2021 Compliance Management System Governance

## Purpose

ISO 37301:2021, "Compliance management systems — Requirements with guidance for use," specifies the requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, reviewing, and improving a compliance management system (CMS). The standard uses the ISO high-level structure shared with ISO 9001 and ISO/IEC 27001, making it directly integrable with existing management systems. This article governs the application of ISO 37301 so an organization can establish a CMS that is aligned with its strategic objectives and integrated with its other management systems.

## Scope

The standard applies to any organization seeking to demonstrate the ability to manage compliance obligations. Within this knowledge base, the article covers the CMS scope, the compliance policy, the compliance obligations register, the compliance risk assessment, the controls, the awareness and competence requirements, the performance evaluation, and the continual improvement of the CMS. It does not replace legal advice; the organization remains responsible for identifying its legal obligations.

## Workflow

1. Determine the scope of the CMS: which parts of the organization, which obligations, which jurisdictions. Define the boundaries and interfaces with other management systems.
2. Establish the compliance policy at the appropriate level, supported by leadership, addressing the organization's commitment to compliance and continual improvement.
3. Identify compliance obligations: legal, regulatory, contractual, organizational, and voluntary codes. Maintain a compliance obligations register.
4. Conduct a compliance risk assessment for each obligation: likelihood of non-compliance, consequence, and the resulting risk.
5. Plan and implement compliance controls to treat identified risks. Document the controls in a compliance controls list aligned with Annex A guidance.
6. Provide awareness and competence: ensure staff understand the compliance policy, their obligations, and the consequences of non-compliance.
7. Monitor, measure, analyze, and evaluate the CMS: metrics, internal audits, noncompliance and corrective action.
8. Conduct management reviews on a planned cadence: review CMS performance, audit results, and improvement opportunities.

## Controls and evidence

CMS evidence includes the scope, the policy, the obligations register, the compliance risk assessments, the compliance controls, the awareness and training records, the monitoring records, the internal audit results, the management review minutes, and the noncompliance and corrective action records. The Statement of Applicability is a key document identifying applicable controls.

## Validation

Validation should confirm the CMS scope is documented and current, the policy is supported and communicated, the obligations register is comprehensive, the controls are implemented, internal audits cover the CMS scope, management reviews are conducted, and corrective actions are tracked to closure. Certification audits (where pursued) verify against the standard's requirements.

## Failure correction

Common failure modes: the obligations register is incomplete or outdated (corrective: implement a process to identify new obligations and review the register periodically); controls are listed but not operational (corrective: for each control, document the implementation and the evidence); noncompliance is reported but not analyzed (corrective: require root-cause analysis and corrective action); the CMS is not integrated with other management systems (corrective: align with the ISO high-level structure and reuse compatible processes).

## Limitations

ISO 37301 specifies the requirements; it does not prescribe how to comply with each specific legal obligation. The standard does not replace legal advice. The CMS does not guarantee compliance; it ensures the system is in place to manage compliance. Sector regulations may impose specific requirements beyond the standard.

## Scope note

This article summarizes project-neutral business use of ISO 37301:2021. It does not assert any specific organization's CMS certification or claim any compliance outcome.
