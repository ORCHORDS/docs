# COSO ERM 2017 Risk Appetite Governance

## Purpose

COSO's "Enterprise Risk Management — Integrating with Strategy and Performance" (2017) reframes ERM from a compliance exercise to a strategic discipline that integrates risk with strategy setting and performance. The framework defines five components (Governance and Culture; Strategy and Objective-Setting; Performance; Review and Revision; Information, Communication, and Reporting) and twenty-three principles. Risk appetite is a central concept that connects the framework's strategy and performance dimensions. This article governs how an organization establishes, expresses, and applies risk appetite so it can be used for decision-making at all levels.

## Scope

The framework applies to any organization seeking to integrate risk with strategy and performance. Within this knowledge base, the article covers the establishment of risk appetite (qualitative and quantitative statements, the relationship to risk capacity and risk tolerance), the communication of risk appetite throughout the organization, the use of risk appetite in decisions, and the review of risk appetite on strategy change. It does not replace a sector-specific risk framework; readers should overlay their sector requirements.

## Workflow

1. Define the organization's risk appetite in the context of its strategy. Risk appetite expresses the amount and type of risk the organization is willing to accept in pursuit of its strategy.
2. Express risk appetite at the strategic and operational levels. Strategic risk appetite is a high-level statement; operational risk appetite translates the strategic statement into actionable limits for specific categories (credit risk, operational risk, cyber risk, compliance risk, project risk).
3. Establish risk capacity as the boundary condition. Risk capacity is the maximum risk the organization can bear given its resources and obligations. Risk appetite should be set well within capacity.
4. Define risk tolerances for specific risk categories and metrics. Tolerances are the operational boundaries that trigger escalation when breached.
5. Communicate risk appetite and tolerances throughout the organization. Use multiple channels (policy, training, decision-support tools) so the appetite is consistently applied.
6. Apply risk appetite in decisions: project approval, investment, new product introduction, third-party engagement, and significant operational changes.
7. Review risk appetite at least annually and on material strategy change.

## Controls and evidence

Risk appetite evidence includes the documented risk appetite statement, the operational risk appetite for each category, the risk tolerances, the communication records, the decision records that cite risk appetite, and the periodic review minutes. Each decision at the operational level should be traceable to the relevant risk appetite and tolerance.

## Validation

Validation should confirm the risk appetite is documented and supported by leadership, the operational translations exist for material risk categories, risk appetite is consistently cited in material decisions, the periodic review occurs, and the appetite is updated when strategy changes. Spot checks should confirm operational decisions reference the relevant risk appetite and tolerance.

## Failure correction

Common failure modes: risk appetite is a single vague statement that does not translate to operational limits (corrective: develop operational appetite and tolerances for each material category); appetite is documented but not used in decisions (corrective: require a risk-appetite reference in the decision template for material decisions); appetite is set without consideration of risk capacity (corrective: assess risk capacity and ensure appetite is within capacity); appetite is not reviewed on strategy change (corrective: trigger a review on each material strategy change).

## Limitations

COSO ERM 2017 is a framework, not a measurement system. The framework does not prescribe specific risk metrics, risk models, or risk quantification methods. The framework's effectiveness depends on the organization's culture and on leadership commitment. The framework does not certify risk management; it provides a structure for designing and assessing risk management.

## Scope note

This article summarizes project-neutral business use of COSO ERM 2017. It does not assert any specific organization's risk appetite conformance or claim any ERM certification.
