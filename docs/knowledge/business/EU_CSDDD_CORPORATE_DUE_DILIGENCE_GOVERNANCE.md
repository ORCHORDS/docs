# EU CSDDD Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Governance

## Purpose

The EU Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD) requires in-scope companies to identify, prevent, mitigate, and account for adverse human rights and environmental impacts in their operations, subsidiaries, and across their chain of activities. Governance ensures that an in-scope company implements the directive, integrates due diligence into corporate strategy, and provides for remediation.

## Current context and source status

The EU adopted the CSDDD in 2024 (Directive (EU) 2024/1760). The directive has phased applicability starting with the largest companies in 2027 and extending to smaller in-scope companies in 2029. The directive is being transposed into national law by member states; transposition timelines and implementation guidance vary. Verify the current transposition status and the applicability schedule before treating any specific requirement as a current obligation.

## Governance workflow and controls

### 1. Determine applicability

Determine applicability based on the directive's criteria (EU thresholds on net turnover and number of employees; non-EU thresholds based on EU-generated turnover). Track phased applicability dates.

### 2. Integrate due diligence into policy

Adopt a due diligence policy approved at board level. The policy should describe the company's approach to actual and potential adverse impacts.

### 3. Identify actual and potential impacts

Identify actual and potential adverse human rights and environmental impacts. Use ongoing risk-based assessment. Document methodology and findings.

### 4. Prevent or mitigate

For identified impacts, develop and implement prevention or mitigation measures. Where impacts cannot be prevented or mitigated, consider terminating the business relationship.

### 5. Monitor and communicate

Monitor the effectiveness of due diligence measures. Communicate due diligence efforts to stakeholders and the public.

### 6. Provide remediation

Provide for remediation where the company has caused or contributed to impacts. Establish a complaints procedure accessible to affected stakeholders.

### 7. Apply climate transition plans

Adopt and put into effect a climate transition plan aligned with the 1.5°C objective of the Paris Agreement and the objective of achieving climate neutrality by 2050.

### 8. Manage director duties

Apply the directive's requirements for directors: integrating due diligence into corporate strategy, overseeing due diligence implementation, and reporting to the board.

## Validation and evidence

- Applicability assessment.
- Due diligence policy.
- Impact assessment.
- Prevention and mitigation plan.
- Complaints procedure and records.
- Climate transition plan.
- Board oversight records.

## Failure correction

Common defects include due diligence without board oversight, missing complaints procedures, and climate transition plans not aligned with 1.5°C. Corrective actions include a board reporting cadence, a complaints procedure design, and a climate target alignment review.

## Limitations

- The directive is being transposed; requirements may evolve.
- Phased applicability means requirements apply at different times to different companies.
- Chain of activities is broad; coverage may need prioritization.
- Penalties and civil liability apply; legal review is essential.
