# IIA Standards 2024 Internal Audit Governance

## Purpose

The Institute of Internal Auditors (IIA) International Standards for the Professional Practice of Internal Auditing provide the requirements for internal audit practice. Governance ensures that internal audit is independent, that the charter and scope are approved by the board, and that audit work conforms to the standards.

## Current context and source status

The IIA publishes the International Standards, currently the 2024 edition, comprising Attribute Standards (Series 1000, 1100, 1200, 1300) and Performance Standards (Series 2000, 2100, 2200, 2300, 2400, 2500, 2600). The standards are mandatory for internal audit functions that claim conformance. Verify the current edition before treating any standard identifier as a current requirement.

## Governance workflow and controls

### 1. Maintain independence

The chief audit executive (CAE) MUST report to a level within the organization that allows the internal audit activity to fulfill its responsibilities. The CAE reports functionally to the board (typically the audit committee) and administratively to senior management.

### 2. Establish the charter

Maintain an internal audit charter approved by the board. The charter defines the purpose, authority, and responsibility of the internal audit activity.

### 3. Manage the audit plan

Develop a risk-based audit plan. Consider the organization's strategy, objectives, risks, and stakeholder expectations. Submit the plan to the board for approval.

### 4. Conduct engagements

Plan each engagement, identify objectives, scope, criteria, and resources. Conduct the engagement with due professional care. Document findings and recommendations.

### 5. Communicate results

Issue engagement reports. Include objectives, scope, findings, conclusions, and recommendations. Distribute to relevant stakeholders. Obtain management responses.

### 6. Monitor and follow up

Track management actions to address findings. Report overdue actions to senior management and the board.

### 7. Quality assurance

Establish a quality assurance and improvement program. Conduct internal assessments periodically. Conduct an external assessment at least once every five years. Report results to the board.

## Validation and evidence

- Internal audit charter.
- Risk-based audit plan.
- Engagement planning, working papers, and reports.
- Management response tracking.
- Quality assurance and improvement program records.

## Failure correction

Common defects include lack of independence, audit plans that don't reflect risk, and overdue follow-up. Corrective actions include a charter review, an audit plan risk alignment check, and a follow-up report cadence.

## Limitations

- The IIA Standards apply to internal audit; they don't cover external audit.
- Conformance with the standards is voluntary unless the function claims conformance.
- The standards require judgement in application.
- Small audit functions may struggle to meet all attribute standards.
