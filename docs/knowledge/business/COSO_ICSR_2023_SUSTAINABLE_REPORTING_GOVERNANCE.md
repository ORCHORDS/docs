# COSO ICSR 2023 Sustainable Business Reporting Governance

## Purpose

Govern the application of the COSO guidance "Achieving Effective Internal Control over Sustainability Reporting" (ICSR, 2023), which adapts the COSO Internal Control — Integrated Framework to sustainability reporting, so that sustainability disclosures are produced under an internal control system with the same discipline as financial reporting: control environment, risk assessment, control activities, information and communication, and monitoring — applied to the sustainability disclosure process.

## Scope

Applies to the studio's sustainability reporting processes (GRI, IFRS S1/S2, regulatory sustainability disclosures). Covers the five COSO components as adapted for sustainability reporting, sustainability materiality assessment controls, and data quality controls. Does not cover the disclosure content standards themselves or financial reporting controls.

## Workflow

1. Establish the control environment for sustainability reporting: governance body oversight, defined roles with accountability for disclosure content, competence in the reporting team, and a culture that treats sustainability disclosure with financial-reporting seriousness.
2. Risk-assess the reporting process: identify where sustainability disclosures could be materially wrong or incomplete — estimation-heavy metrics, boundary decisions, data from uncontrolled sources — and prioritize control effort by misstatement risk.
3. Design control activities proportionate to risk: review controls over metric calculations, reconciliation of source data to disclosures, boundary/consolidation checks, and management review checkpoints before publication.
4. Control the information flow: define data owners, collection methods, transformation logic, and documentation for each disclosed metric; metrics flowing from spreadsheets without documented logic are uncontrolled.
5. Communicate and train: preparers know the standards' requirements and the control procedures; reviewers know what they are attesting to.
6. Monitor continuously: periodic evaluation of control operation, plus issue-triggered review when errors, restatements, or assurance findings occur.
7. Integrate with financial reporting controls where information overlaps: shared data (energy costs, headcount, compliance costs) uses one controlled source for both reporting streams, with reconciliation.

## Controls and evidence

- Governance and accountability records for sustainability disclosure roles.
- Reporting risk assessment identifying material misstatement risks by disclosure.
- Control activity documentation per material metric with review evidence.
- Metric data flow documentation: owners, sources, transformation logic.
- Training records for preparers and reviewers.
- Monitoring results and issue-triggered reviews.

## Validation

- Sample three material metrics and trace disclosure values through documented control activities to source data.
- Confirm the risk assessment covers estimation-heavy and boundary-sensitive disclosures specifically.
- Confirm overlapping data with financial reporting reconciles across both streams.

## Failure correction

- **Metric without documented data flow** → document owner, source, and logic; re-derive the current value; treat past values as unverified until re-derived.
- **Review control skipped before publication** → post-publication review with correction if needed, and enforce the checkpoint for the next cycle.
- **Restatement without root cause** → complete the analysis and add the missing control; restatement recurrence is a control design failure.

## Limitations

- ICSR adapts the COSO framework; it is guidance, not a certifiable standard.
- Sustainability data quality depends on value-chain sources outside the studio's control; document reliance and its limits.
- Regulatory regimes may mandate specific control or assurance arrangements beyond this guidance.
