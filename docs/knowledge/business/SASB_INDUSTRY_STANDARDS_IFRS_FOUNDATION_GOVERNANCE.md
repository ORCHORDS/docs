# SASB Industry Standards and IFRS Foundation Governance

## Purpose

Govern the application of the SASB Standards (now maintained by the IFRS Foundation through the ISSB) so that industry-specific sustainability metrics are selected from the correct industry standard, disclosed with the standard's defined metrics rather than improvised substitutes, and maintained against the IFRS Foundation's ongoing stewardship of the standards.

## Scope

Applies to the studio's industry-based sustainability disclosure where SASB standards serve as the industry layer (including as the source of industry metrics under IFRS S1/S2 application guidance). Covers industry standard identification, metric application, and change tracking under IFRS Foundation stewardship. Does not cover the full IFRS S1/S2 architecture or GRI impact reporting.

## Workflow

1. Identify the applicable SASB industry standard using the standards' industry classification (Sustainable Industry Classification System): match the studio's revenue-generating activities to the industry definitions, recording the classification rationale; activities spanning multiple industries take multiple standards.
2. Disclose the identified standard's metrics as defined: each metric has a defined unit, definition, and code; substituting a similar internal metric breaks comparability and is treated as a disclosure defect.
3. Report the metrics with the activity metric definitions and any topic-agnostic application guidance; where a metric is not applicable or data is unavailable, disclose that fact rather than silently omitting.
4. Build the metrics into recurring measurement: the data pipeline behind each metric is owned, documented, and stable across periods — one-off spreadsheets are not a disclosure pipeline.
5. Track the standards' evolution under IFRS Foundation stewardship: the ISSB has enhanced and continues to maintain the SASB standards; monitor revisions and apply them with effective-date discipline, restating comparatives where the revision requires.
6. Connect the industry metrics to IFRS S1/S2 disclosure where both apply: SASB metrics feed the industry-based metrics the ISSB standards reference, and the two disclosures must agree.
7. Retain the metric evidence trail per period: source data, calculation method, and preparer — sufficient for an assurer to re-perform the calculation.

## Controls and evidence

- Industry classification determination record with rationale.
- Metric disclosure worksheets using the standard's codes and definitions.
- Data pipeline ownership and documentation per metric.
- Standards revision tracking with effective dates applied.
- Reconciliation records between SASB and IFRS S1/S2 disclosures where both are prepared.
- Metric evidence trails per reporting period.

## Validation

- Confirm the industry standard(s) applied match the classification rationale and current activity mix.
- Sample five disclosed metrics and confirm the definition, unit, and code match the standard (not an internal approximation).
- Confirm metric values re-perform from the evidence trail.

## Failure correction

- **Internal substitute metric disclosed** → correct to the standard's metric definition, restate where material, and gate future disclosures on the standard's metric text.
- **Classification stale after activity change** → reclassify, add or drop industry standards, and disclose the change.
- **Revision applied late or early** → align to the effective date, restate comparatives if required, and fix revision monitoring.

## Limitations

- SASB metrics are industry-averaged materiality judgments; company-specific risks may need disclosure beyond the industry standard.
- The standards' maintenance under the ISSB means revisions arrive on the IFRS Foundation's cycle; monitor rather than assume stasis.
- Jurisdictional regimes may mandate or modify the ISSB/SASB stack; local requirements layer on top.
