# Business Continuity Management System Governance

## Scope

This article covers the umbrella governance discipline of business continuity management (BCM), which prepares an organisation to continue delivering products and services during and after a disruption. It draws on ISO 22301:2019, *Security and resilience — Business continuity management systems — Requirements*, and on the shorter ISO 22316:2017 guidance on organizational resilience. It addresses the continuity management system itself rather than the operational triage topics covered in the support collection.

## Workflow

ISO 22301 structures BCM around the Plan-Do-Check-Act cycle and a set of requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and improving a business continuity management system (BCMS):

1. **Establish context and leadership commitment.** The organisation determines the scope of the BCMS, the interested parties whose requirements apply, and top management's commitment to business continuity. Leadership must establish a policy, appoint competent roles, and ensure integration with other management systems.
2. **Business impact analysis (BIA).** The BIA identifies the organisation's products and services, the activities and resources that support them, and the impact over time of a disruption. Outputs include recovery time objectives (RTO), recovery point objectives (RPO) for data, and the minimum service levels that must be maintained.
3. **Risk assessment.** The organisation assesses threats to continuity, including natural hazards, technology failures, supply-chain interruptions, loss of key personnel, and civil emergencies. The risk assessment complements the BIA by identifying plausible disruption scenarios.
4. **Strategy selection.** The organisation selects continuity and recovery strategies proportionate to the identified impacts — for example, alternate work sites, redundant suppliers, backup and restore capability, cross-trained staff, and manual workarounds.
5. **Plans and procedures.** Business continuity plans, incident response structures, and recovery procedures are documented, with clear activation criteria, roles and responsibilities, and communication protocols.
6. **Exercise, test, and review.** Exercises and tests validate the strategies and plans. Performance evaluation, internal audit, management review, and corrective action complete the improvement cycle.

## Controls and evidence

Evidence of a functioning BCMS includes:

- A documented business continuity policy approved by top management.
- BIA and risk-assessment reports with methodology, assumptions, participants, and dates.
- Documented continuity and recovery plans, with version control and named owners.
- Call trees, contact lists, and communication templates maintained current.
- Exercise and test records, including scenario, participants, results, observations, and improvement actions.
- Management review minutes and internal audit findings with remediation tracking.
- Evidence of integration with incident-management and crisis-communication processes.

## Validation

Independent validation draws on:

- Certification to ISO 22301 by an accredited certification body.
- Internal audits against the standard's requirements and the organisation's own procedures.
- Regulator or customer assurance reviews, common in financial services and critical infrastructure.
- Unannounced or scenario-based exercises, including tabletop and live failover tests.
- Post-incident reviews from real disruptions, which provide the strongest evidence of whether plans work in practice.

## Failure correction

Continuity failures commonly take the following forms, each with corresponding correction:

- **Stale plans.** Plans that reference departed personnel, decommissioned systems, or obsolete suppliers fail when invoked. Corrective action includes assigning plan ownership with a fixed review cycle and verifying contact data at each review.
- **Untested strategies.** Backup arrangements that have never been exercised frequently reveal gaps — absent credentials, insufficient capacity, or unworkable runbooks. Corrective action is a graduated exercise programme that progresses from tabletop to full failover.
- **Unrealistic RTOs.** Recovery time objectives set by aspiration rather than evidence produce commitments the organisation cannot meet. Correction requires repeating the BIA with operational input and adjusting strategies or stakeholder expectations accordingly.
- **Poor activation decisions.** Ambiguous activation authority delays response. Corrective action is a defined decision authority, pre-agreed escalation triggers, and a duty-manager rota.

## Roles and responsibilities

A BCMS functions only when accountability for continuity is clear at each level of the organisation:

- **Top management.** Owns the BCMS, ensures alignment with the organisation's strategy, and chairs the management review that evaluates the system's continuing suitability.
- **Business continuity coordinator.** Maintains the programme, coordinates BIA and risk-assessment refresh, schedules exercises, and supports plan owners. Smaller organisations may combine this role with risk management or operations.
- **Process and resource owners.** Accountable for the continuity strategies and plans within their area, including supplier arrangements, technology recovery, and people-resource contingencies.
- **Crisis management team.** The body that convenes when an incident meets defined activation criteria, with decision authority for invoking plans, communicating with stakeholders, and authorising extraordinary expenditure.
- **All personnel.** Trained on their role in continuity plans, on the activation triggers relevant to them, and on the reporting channels used to escalate suspected events.

## Limitations

A BCMS provides a structured approach to continuity but cannot eliminate disruption, and certification indicates conformity of the management system rather than a guarantee of uninterrupted operations. Continuity planning addresses the organisation's own activities and, through contractual means, its critical suppliers, but wide-area disasters may affect suppliers, staff, and customers simultaneously. ISO 22301 is also distinct from the operational continuity topics in the support collection, which address individual service interruptions rather than the management system that governs organisational preparedness.
