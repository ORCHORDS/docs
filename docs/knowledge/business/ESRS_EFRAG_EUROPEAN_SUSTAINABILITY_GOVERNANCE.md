# ESRS EFRAG European Sustainability Reporting Governance

## Purpose

The European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS), developed by the European Financial Reporting Advisory Group (EFRAG), set out the disclosure requirements under the EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD). Governance ensures that an in-scope enterprise applies the ESRS, conducts the double materiality assessment, and integrates the disclosures with the management report.

## Current context and source status

The ESRS were adopted by the European Commission in 2023, with the first set (ESRS 1, ESRS 2, and topical standards ESRS E1 through ESRS S5, and governance standard ESRS G1) published as Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/2772. ESRS sector-specific standards and standards for non-EU undertakings are under development. Verify the current ESRS set and the phased CSRD effective dates before treating any disclosure requirement as a current requirement.

## Governance workflow and controls

### 1. Determine applicability

Determine CSRD applicability based on size, listed status, and EU turnover thresholds. Apply the phased effective dates (large undertakings already subject to NFRD in 2024 reporting year; other large undertakings in 2025 and 2026; listed SMEs in 2027). Track changes to the directive.

### 2. Conduct double materiality

Conduct a double materiality assessment per ESRS 1: impact materiality (effects on people and environment) and financial materiality (effects on the undertaking's development, performance, position, cost of capital, and access to finance). Document the assessment.

### 3. Apply ESRS 1 and ESRS 2

Apply ESRS 1 (general requirements) and ESRS 2 (general disclosures). Disclose the basis for preparation, governance, strategy, and material impacts, risks, and opportunities.

### 4. Apply topical standards

Apply the material topical standards: ESRS E (environmental: E1 climate change, E2 pollution, E3 water and marine resources, E4 biodiversity and ecosystems, E5 resource use and circular economy); ESRS S (social: S1 own workforce, S2 workers in the value chain, S3 affected communities, S4 consumers and end-users); ESRS G (governance: G1 business conduct).

### 5. Apply value chain disclosures

Apply the value chain disclosure requirements. Document the scope of the value chain and the data collection approach.

### 6. Integrate with management report

Integrate the sustainability statement with the management report per the CSRD. Apply the connection between the sustainability statement and other parts of the management report (for example, risk disclosures).

### 7. Manage assurance readiness

Prepare for limited (current) and reasonable (future) assurance requirements. Document data lineage, internal controls, and methodology.

## Validation and evidence

- CSRD applicability assessment.
- Double materiality assessment.
- ESRS disclosures with topical alignment.
- Value chain documentation.
- Assurance readiness review.

## Failure correction

Common defects include single materiality (impact only), missing value chain disclosures, and lack of integration with the management report. Corrective actions include a double materiality refresh, a value chain disclosure gap analysis, and an integration template.

## Limitations

- ESRS apply to specific EU undertakings; non-EU undertakings may apply under separate requirements.
- Double materiality is resource-intensive.
- Sector standards are not yet adopted; the cross-sector standards apply broadly.
- Assurance requirements evolve; track amendments.
