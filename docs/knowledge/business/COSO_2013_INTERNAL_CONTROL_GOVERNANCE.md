# COSO 2013 Internal Control — Integrated Framework Governance

## Purpose

The Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission (COSO) Internal Control — Integrated Framework (2013) provides principles-based guidance for designing, implementing, and assessing internal control. Governance ensures that internal control supports the achievement of objectives across operations, reporting, and compliance, and that the framework is applied consistently across the organization.

## Current context and source status

COSO published the Internal Control — Integrated Framework in 1992 and updated it in 2013. The 2013 framework organizes internal control into five components (control environment, risk assessment, control activities, information and communication, monitoring) and seventeen supporting principles. COSO also published the Enterprise Risk Management — Integrating with Strategy and Performance (2017) framework, which is distinct. Verify the current framework edition before treating any principle identifier as a current requirement.

## Governance workflow and controls

### 1. Define objectives in three categories

Define objectives in the categories the framework addresses:

- operations (effectiveness and efficiency);
- reporting (reliability of internal and external reporting);
- compliance (adherence to laws and regulations).

Each category requires specific consideration; internal control is judged against the chosen objectives.

### 2. Apply the five components

Apply the five components:

- control environment (tone at the top, integrity, ethical values);
- risk assessment (identifying, analyzing, and managing risks);
- control activities (policies and procedures that mitigate risks);
- information and communication (relevant, timely information);
- monitoring (ongoing and separate evaluations).

### 3. Apply the seventeen principles

Map controls to the seventeen principles. Document where each principle is applied and where it is not.

### 4. Assess internal control

Conduct ongoing monitoring and separate evaluations. Document the assessment methodology. Identify deficiencies and their severity (deficiency, significant deficiency, material weakness).

### 5. Report on internal control

Report on internal control effectiveness to senior management and the board. For external reporting, comply with applicable laws and regulations.

### 6. Use COSO with other frameworks

COSO is integrated with other frameworks (SOX, SOC reporting, ISO 31000 for risk management). Use COSO principles as the foundation and supplement with framework-specific requirements.

## Validation and evidence

- Internal control objectives.
- Mapping of controls to components and principles.
- Assessment methodology and results.
- Deficiency log with severity classification.
- Management and board reports.

## Failure correction

Common defects include incomplete principle coverage, deficiencies without classification, and reports that don't address all three objective categories. Corrective actions include a principle coverage matrix, a deficiency classification review, and a reporting template that requires all three categories.

## Limitations

- COSO 2013 is a framework, not a certifiable standard.
- The framework is principle-based; implementation requires judgement.
- Internal control effectiveness depends on the organization's culture and competence.
- The framework does not prescribe specific controls.
