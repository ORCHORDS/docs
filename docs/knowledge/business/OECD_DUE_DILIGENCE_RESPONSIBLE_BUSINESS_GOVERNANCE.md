# OECD Due Diligence Guidance for Responsible Business Conduct Governance

## Purpose

The OECD Due Diligence Guidance for Responsible Business Conduct (RBC) provides practical guidance to enterprises on implementing the OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises. Governance ensures that an enterprise conducts due diligence across its operations and supply chains, that findings are integrated into decision-making, and that adverse impacts are tracked.

## Current context and source status

The OECD published the Due Diligence Guidance for Responsible Business Conduct in 2018, building on sector-specific due diligence guidance (minerals, garment, agriculture, financial). The OECD updated the Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises in 2023. Verify the current OECD publications before treating any due diligence step as a current requirement.

## Governance workflow and controls

### 1. Embed RBC in policy

Adopt a responsible business conduct policy that addresses the OECD Guidelines. Communicate the policy to employees, suppliers, and other business partners. Integrate the policy with related policies (human rights, environment, anti-corruption).

### 2. Identify adverse impacts

Conduct a risk-based assessment to identify actual and potential adverse impacts in the enterprise's operations and supply chains. Consider geographic, sectoral, product, and enterprise-level risks.

### 3. Cease, prevent, or mitigate

For identified adverse impacts, take action to cease, prevent, or mitigate. Track actions and outcomes.

### 4. Track implementation

Track the effectiveness of due diligence actions. Use indicators that reflect both process (was due diligence conducted?) and outcome (were impacts addressed?).

### 5. Communicate

Communicate due diligence efforts publicly. Use the OECD reporting framework where applicable. Disclose to investors, customers, and other stakeholders.

### 6. Provide for remediation

Provide for or cooperate in remediation where the enterprise has caused or contributed to adverse impacts. Maintain a grievance mechanism.

### 7. Align with sector guidance

Apply sector-specific due diligence guidance where the enterprise operates in covered sectors. Examples include OECD minerals, garment, agriculture, and financial guidance.

## Validation and evidence

- RBC policy.
- Risk-based assessment of adverse impacts.
- Action plan with status.
- Public communication of due diligence.
- Grievance mechanism records.

## Failure correction

Common defects include due diligence that does not extend to supply chains, lack of remediation mechanisms, and communication that doesn't reach affected stakeholders. Corrective actions include a supply-chain mapping workshop, a remediation mechanism design, and a communication strategy.

## Limitations

- OECD guidance is non-binding; adherence is voluntary.
- Due diligence on supply chains is resource-intensive; prioritize high-risk areas.
- Sector guidance may not cover all sectors.
- Adverse impacts may require remediation beyond the enterprise's capacity.
