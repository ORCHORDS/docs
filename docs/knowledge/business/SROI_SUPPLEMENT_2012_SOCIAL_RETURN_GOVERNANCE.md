# SROI Supplement 2012 Social Return on Investment Governance

## Purpose

The Social Return on Investment (SROI) Supplement (2012), developed under the Cabinet Office and the UK Cabinet Office guidance lineage and now maintained as a SROI Network resource, provides a framework for measuring and reporting social, environmental, and economic value created by an organization's activities. The framework applies a principles-based approach: stakeholder engagement, understanding change, valuing change, only counting what matters, do not over-claim, be transparent, verify the result. This article governs the application of SROI principles so organizations can produce credible SROI analyses that are usable for decision-making and for stakeholder reporting.

## Scope

The SROI Supplement applies to any organization that wishes to measure its social value. Within this knowledge base, the article covers the SROI principles, the analysis stages (establishing scope, mapping stakeholders, establishing outcomes, evidencing outcomes, valuing outcomes, establishing impact, calculating SROI, reporting, embedding. The 2012 Supplement reduces these from earlier 2009 guidance but the principles apply at every stage), and the documentation of the analysis. It does not certify SROI; the credibility of the analysis depends on the evidence and the application of the principles.

## Workflow

1. Define the scope of the SROI analysis: which activities, which stakeholders, which outcomes, which time horizon.
2. Engage stakeholders to identify intended and unintended outcomes. Stakeholder engagement is a principle and a stage.
3. Map the outcomes and develop outcome indicators. Each outcome should have a defined indicator, a measurement method, and a target.
4. Evidencing outcomes: gather data to populate the indicators. Use mixed methods (quantitative and qualitative) appropriate to the outcome.
5. Value the outcomes. Apply financial proxies where market prices are not available; document the proxy choice and source.
6. Establish impact: deadweight (what would have happened anyway), attribution (who else contributed), displacement (would the outcome have happened elsewhere), and drop-off (durability over time).
7. Calculate the SROI ratio (total present value of impact / total present value of investment). Apply sensitivity analysis.
8. Report the analysis transparently, showing the assumptions and the evidence.
9. Embed the practice: use SROI in decision-making, share findings with stakeholders, and improve future analyses.

## Controls and evidence

SROI evidence includes the scope, the stakeholder engagement records, the outcome map, the outcome indicators, the data sources, the financial proxies with sources, the impact adjustments (deadweight, attribution, displacement, drop-off), the calculation, and the sensitivity analysis. Each principle should be verifiable in the analysis.

## Validation

Validation should confirm the principles are applied, stakeholders are engaged, evidence supports the outcomes, financial proxies are sourced and documented, impact adjustments are conservative and documented, the calculation is transparent, and the analysis is reviewed by an independent party. Sensitivity analysis confirms the result is not driven by a single assumption.

## Failure correction

Common failure modes: stakeholders are listed but not engaged (corrective: design engagement that is two-way and document the findings); financial proxies are used without source (corrective: source each proxy and document the source); impact is claimed without deadweight or attribution adjustments (corrective: apply the impact adjustments and document the evidence); the ratio is reported without sensitivity analysis (corrective: perform sensitivity analysis and report it); the analysis is performed once and never embedded (corrective: use the findings in decision-making and improve future analyses).

## Limitations

SROI is a framework for measuring social value; it does not produce a definitive number. The result depends on the assumptions and the proxies chosen; sensitivity analysis is essential. The framework does not replace other forms of evaluation; it complements them. Sector conventions for proxies vary; the analysis should document the choice.

## Scope note

This article summarizes project-neutral business use of the SROI Supplement (2012). It does not assert any specific organization's SROI analysis outcomes or claim any certification.
