# ISO/IEC 27014:2020 Governance of Information Security Governance

## Purpose

ISO/IEC 27014:2020, "Information security, cybersecurity and privacy protection — Governance of information security," provides guidance on the governance of information security for the governing body and top management of an organization. It complements ISO/IEC 27001 (information security management system) by addressing the strategic-level direction, oversight, and accountability for information security. This article governs the application of ISO/IEC 27014 so the strategic-level governance of information security is integrated with the operational ISMS and with the organization's overall governance.

## Scope

The standard applies to any organization's governing body and top management. Within this knowledge base, the article covers the governance principles, the roles and responsibilities of the governing body, the alignment of information security with the organization's strategy, the evaluation and direction of information security performance, and the integration with the ISMS. It does not replace ISO/IEC 27001; the two standards address different levels (governance vs. management system).

## Workflow

1. Establish the governing body's commitment to information security governance. Document the information security governance arrangements in the governance charter.
2. Apply the governance principles the standard identifies: responsibility, strategic alignment, risk-based orientation, value creation, resource optimization, performance evaluation, and continual improvement.
3. Define the information security objectives that support the organization's strategic objectives.
4. Direct the organization's information security: approve policies, allocate resources, set risk appetite, and approve significant changes.
5. Oversee the ISMS: receive performance reports, review audit results, and challenge top management on the adequacy of information security.
6. Be accountable: communicate the organization's information security posture to stakeholders; report material incidents; ensure compliance with applicable laws and regulations.
7. Evaluate information security governance on a planned cadence and improve it.

## Controls and evidence

Governance evidence includes the governance charter, the information security objectives, the approved policies, the resource allocation, the performance reports, the audit summaries, the incident reports, and the governance review records. The evidence should be at the level the governing body uses to make decisions.

## Validation

Validation should confirm the governing body has documented its information security governance arrangements, the principles are applied in practice, the governing body receives and challenges information security performance reports, the ISMS operates under the governing body's direction, and information security is aligned with strategy. Periodic governance review confirms the system remains effective.

## Failure correction

Common failure modes: information security governance is delegated to management without governing-body engagement (corrective: document the governing body's responsibilities and require periodic engagement); information security is treated as a technical or operational concern rather than a strategic one (corrective: align information security objectives with the organization's strategic objectives); the governing body approves policies without receiving performance reports (corrective: establish a reporting cadence with the metrics the governing body needs); governance review is performed but produces no change (corrective: require an improvement plan after each review).

## Limitations

ISO/IEC 27014 provides governance guidance; it does not replace the ISMS standard (ISO/IEC 27001). The standard does not prescribe specific metrics; the organization selects metrics appropriate to its context. The standard assumes a governance structure exists; for very small organizations, the governing body and management may be the same individuals, but the responsibilities remain.

## Scope note

This article summarizes project-neutral business use of ISO/IEC 27014:2020. It does not assert any specific organization's governance conformance or claim any certification outcome.
