# Export Working Capital Program Readiness

## Purpose

The U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA) **Export Working Capital Program (EWCP)** supports working-capital financing for eligible small-business exporters. Current SBA trade-finance guidance states that EWCP loans can be up to **$5 million** and can be arranged before an export sale or contract is finalized, helping exporters negotiate customer payment terms while financing the cash cycle required to perform the order.

EWCP is purpose-built for export working capital. It is distinct from Export Express, the domestic/export Working Capital Pilot, and the International Trade Loan.

## Export cash-cycle model

Export orders can require cash well before the buyer pays.

Map:

1. supplier deposits;
2. raw materials/inventory;
3. labor/production;
4. packaging;
5. inland transport;
6. freight/insurance;
7. shipment/documentation;
8. customer acceptance;
9. payment term; and
10. final collection.

The line amount should reflect peak eligible funding need across this cycle rather than the gross contract value alone.

## Current program size

SBA's current public export guidance lists EWCP loans up to **$5 million**.

SBA's export programs can provide high guaranty percentages to participating lenders under current rules, but the actual guaranty, advance structure, collateral, fees, and loan terms should be verified with the participating EWCP lender and current SBA guidance.

## Pre-sale flexibility

A useful EWCP feature is the ability to seek financing in advance of finalizing an export sale or contract.

This can help a business determine:

- what payment terms it can offer;
- how much inventory it can commit;
- whether it can finance a large order;
- whether advance payments are necessary; and
- whether credit insurance or another risk mitigant is needed.

Do not promise foreign buyers financing-dependent terms before the lender confirms the facility.

## Transaction readiness

For each major export transaction, maintain:

- buyer name/country;
- order/contract value;
- product/service;
- cost budget;
- shipment date;
- payment terms;
- expected collection date;
- currency;
- financing draw need; and
- repayment source.

Link financing advances to identifiable export business rather than mixing them with unrelated domestic uses unless the approved structure permits it.

## Buyer/payment risk

Assess:

- buyer creditworthiness;
- country risk;
- bank risk;
- payment method;
- trade-credit insurance;
- letter-of-credit terms;
- open-account exposure; and
- concentration.

A strong export order can still create a large loss if the buyer does not pay.

## Export compliance

Financing does not authorize a shipment.

Maintain separate controls for:

- export classification;
- licenses;
- sanctions/restricted parties;
- end-use/end-user restrictions;
- customs;
- product standards;
- documentation; and
- destination-specific legal requirements.

A financed order that cannot legally ship can create immediate repayment risk.

## Inventory and production controls

When financing pre-shipment costs, track:

- materials purchased for the order;
- production status;
- work in process;
- finished goods;
- customer-specific inventory;
- delays/rework; and
- cost-to-complete.

Export-specific inventory can be harder to redirect if the buyer cancels.

## Receivables controls

Post-shipment export receivables need clear monitoring.

Track:

- invoice date;
- due date;
- buyer;
- currency;
- shipping/acceptance evidence;
- disputes;
- credit-insurance coverage where applicable; and
- collection status.

If receivables are assigned or controlled under the loan agreement, ensure collections are applied as required.

## Currency exposure

Where invoices or costs are denominated in foreign currency, model exchange-rate changes between:

- purchase/production;
- shipment;
- invoicing; and
- collection.

A profitable export can become unprofitable after adverse currency movement.

## Logistics delays

Stress-test:

- port disruption;
- customs delay;
- vessel/airfreight delay;
- documentation error;
- damaged shipment; and
- delayed buyer acceptance.

Longer logistics time increases interest and working-capital duration.

## Lender-readiness package

A useful EWCP package can include:

- historical/interim financial statements;
- accounts-receivable/payable aging;
- export orders/contracts;
- cost budgets;
- buyer/payment information;
- inventory reports;
- cash-flow forecast;
- export experience;
- credit-insurance information where used; and
- requested line structure.

Exact requirements vary by participating lender.

## EWCP versus WCP

Compare EWCP with the Working Capital Pilot when the business has both domestic and export needs.

WCP can support domestic and export transactions under one facility where current rules permit, while EWCP remains a dedicated export working-capital structure with established export-lender expertise.

Consider:

- domestic financing needs;
- export volume;
- line amount;
- transaction/asset structure;
- lender authority;
- monitoring; and
- fees.

## Evidence to retain

Retain where appropriate:

- application/loan documents;
- export order records;
- draw support;
- inventory/receivable reports;
- shipping documents;
- customer payments;
- lender reports;
- credit-insurance documentation; and
- amendments/waivers.

## Scope note

This article provides general EWCP readiness guidance from current SBA public information. It does not guarantee eligibility, a $5 million loan, a particular guaranty percentage, export legality, buyer payment, or lender approval. Current SBA export-finance rules and participating lender underwriting control the facility.
