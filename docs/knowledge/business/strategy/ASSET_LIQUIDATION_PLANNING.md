# Asset Liquidation Planning

## Purpose

Asset liquidation is a distinct exit path in which business assets are converted to proceeds rather than transferred as a continuing business. It requires deliberate inventory, valuation, sale-method, creditor, tax, and wind-down planning.

## Planning sequence

The U.S. Small Business Administration presents liquidation as a last-resort strategy when buyers, mergers, or successors are not available and recommends a structured asset-sale process.

A practical sequence is:

1. Prepare a complete inventory and identify which assets are available for sale.
2. Secure merchandise, equipment, records, and other assets while the sale is being prepared.
3. Determine an appropriate liquidation value, using a qualified appraiser where necessary.
4. Estimate net proceeds after sale costs and use that estimate to re-evaluate the liquidation decision.
5. Choose a sale method appropriate to the assets and market, such as a negotiated sale, consignment, online sale, sealed bid, retail sale, auction, or another suitable process.
6. Select timing and location with regard to asset condition, carrying costs, buyer access, and contractual constraints.
7. Use qualified auctioneers, dealers, brokers, or other specialists where their expertise materially improves the process.
8. Document transfer terms and the allocation of risk for sold assets.

## Financial and operational controls

Before disposal begins:

- reconcile asset records with physical inventory;
- identify liens, security interests, leases, licenses, consignment rights, or other restrictions on disposal;
- separate assets owned by the business from property belonging to customers, employees, lessors, or other parties;
- estimate taxes, professional fees, storage, transport, commissions, and other costs that reduce gross sale proceeds;
- define how proceeds will be controlled and applied to obligations;
- preserve records needed for tax, employment, creditor, warranty, or legal purposes; and
- coordinate shutdown of contracts, permits, utilities, facilities, accounts, and access only when doing so will not impair asset value or required records.

## Decision discipline

Liquidation value is not automatically the same as book value, replacement cost, fair market value, or the value of a business as a going concern. Record the valuation basis and do not use one value label as a substitute for another.

## Scope note

This article is general planning guidance. Insolvency, bankruptcy, secured-creditor rights, taxes, employment obligations, and required sale procedures are jurisdiction- and fact-specific; obtain qualified professional advice when those issues apply.
