# Funding Needs Preparation

## Purpose

Funding preparation starts with understanding how much capital is actually required, when it is required, and what the capital will be used for. The financing method should follow from those needs rather than from a generic preference for debt, equity, or self-funding.

## Determine the requirement first

The U.S. Small Business Administration recommends calculating startup funding needs before selecting a funding source. Useful inputs include:

- one-time startup expenses;
- recurring operating costs;
- expected timing of revenue and collections;
- working-capital needs;
- contingency for uncertain costs; and
- milestones that could change later funding requirements.

The result should be a time-phased funding need, not only a single headline amount.

## Common funding routes

The SBA groups common approaches into broad categories such as:

- **self-funding**, using the owner's or founders' own financial resources;
- **investment capital**, where investors provide capital in exchange for an ownership or economic interest; and
- **loans**, where borrowed funds are repaid under agreed terms.

Each route can affect control, risk, repayment obligations, governance, and future financing flexibility differently.

## Preparation workflow

1. Reconcile the funding request to the startup-cost and operating-cost model.
2. State what each material portion of capital will fund.
3. Link the amount and timing to business-plan assumptions and milestones.
4. Distinguish minimum required capital from optional growth capital.
5. Document repayment capacity or investor-return assumptions without presenting projections as guarantees.
6. Identify constraints introduced by the proposed financing structure.
7. Revisit the requirement when timing, pricing, costs, or operating assumptions change.

## Decision quality

Avoid choosing a financing structure solely because capital appears available. Compare the obligations, dilution, cost, covenants, control implications, and downside scenarios that accompany each option.
