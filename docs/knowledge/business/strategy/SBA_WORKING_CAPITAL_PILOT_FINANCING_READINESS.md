# SBA Working Capital Pilot Financing Readiness

## Purpose

The U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA) **7(a) Working Capital Pilot (WCP)** is a monitored line-of-credit program for growing small businesses. Current SBA guidance describes both **transaction-based** financing for projects, contracts, or orders and **asset-based** financing against receivables and inventory. The program can support domestic and/or export working-capital needs under a single line.

WCP readiness depends heavily on financial reporting, project/contract evidence, collateral monitoring, and the borrower's ability to explain how advances convert back into cash.

## Current public terms

SBA's current 7(a) public guidance lists:

- maximum WCP loan amount: **$5,000,000**;
- maximum maturity: **60 months**;
- SBA guaranty percentage: **85% for loans of $150,000 or less and 75% above $150,000**; and
- monitored line-of-credit structures rather than an ordinary term loan.

Interest-rate caps, fees, processing methods, eligibility, and lender authority can change. Verify the live SBA page and Program Guide for a transaction.

## Core borrower readiness

Current SBA guidance highlights borrowers that:

- have at least one year of operating history;
- can produce timely and accurate financial statements;
- can produce accounts-receivable and accounts-payable aging reports;
- can produce inventory reports where applicable; and
- need financing for contracts/projects or borrowing against receivables/inventory.

These reporting capabilities are not paperwork afterthoughts. They are part of whether the lender can monitor the facility.

## Transaction-based WCP

A transaction-based WCP can finance costs associated with specific domestic or export projects, contracts, or orders.

For each financed transaction, maintain:

- contract/order/customer information;
- total value;
- direct-cost budget;
- performance schedule;
- supplier/subcontractor plan;
- invoicing milestones;
- customer payment terms;
- advance needs; and
- expected repayment source.

Current SBA manufacturing guidance states that Transaction-Based WCP can support up to 100% of related eligible expenses under program conditions. Do not assume every project receives 100% financing; lender underwriting and current program rules control the actual advance.

## Asset-based WCP

An asset-based WCP can provide advances against eligible short-term assets such as receivables and inventory.

Readiness requires reliable:

- receivable aging;
- invoice support;
- collection history;
- inventory reports;
- inventory valuation;
- customer concentration analysis;
- borrowing-base calculations; and
- reconciliation to financial statements.

The lender's borrowing-base formula determines actual availability. A $1 million receivable balance does not automatically provide $1 million of borrowing capacity.

## Domestic and export use

A distinctive WCP feature is the ability to support both domestic and export working-capital needs under one facility where program requirements are met.

For exporters, integrate:

- pre-shipment costs;
- foreign-buyer payment terms;
- currency exposure;
- shipping and logistics timing;
- export-documentation requirements;
- customer/country risk; and
- expected collection date.

Do not mix transactions in a way that makes it impossible to determine which advances support which order or collateral pool.

## Annual financial review

Current SBA lender guidance requires lenders to obtain updated borrower financial statements annually and perform a full credit analysis as part of renewal under the WCP framework.

A borrower should be prepared to explain:

- revenue changes;
- margin changes;
- working-capital trends;
- aging deterioration;
- inventory changes;
- new debt;
- owner distributions;
- material litigation or tax obligations; and
- changes to major customers or contracts.

## Monitoring controls

WCP requires operational controls appropriate to monitored credit facilities.

A useful internal cadence can include:

1. close books on time;
2. update receivables/payables aging;
3. update inventory;
4. reconcile borrowing-base or transaction availability;
5. review covenant compliance;
6. identify material exceptions;
7. forecast collections and repayments; and
8. submit lender reporting by the required deadline.

Late or unreliable reporting can reduce lender confidence and may restrict line availability.

## Transaction-based cost controls

For project/order financing, prevent cross-subsidization between unrelated transactions.

Use:

- cost codes;
- purchase-order linkage;
- payroll allocation;
- supplier invoice matching;
- subcontractor tracking;
- contract change-order controls; and
- cost-to-complete updates.

A transaction that overruns its budget should be reforecast promptly rather than silently consuming availability intended for another order.

## Asset quality

For asset-based financing, collateral quality matters as much as nominal balance.

Monitor:

- receivable aging;
- disputed invoices;
- customer concentration;
- credits/returns;
- inventory aging;
- obsolescence;
- damaged or slow-moving inventory; and
- existing liens or claims.

Availability can shrink quickly when assets become ineligible.

## Cash-control discipline

Understand how collected proceeds must be handled under the lender's agreement.

Some monitored facilities require collections to reduce outstanding advances before funds become available again. Avoid diverting contract or receivable proceeds into unrelated spending when the loan agreement requires repayment or controlled cash application.

## Downside scenarios

Model WCP needs under scenarios such as:

- customer pays 30-60 days late;
- project cost rises 15%;
- major receivable is disputed;
- inventory becomes obsolete;
- order is canceled;
- export buyer delays acceptance;
- supplier changes to cash-in-advance; or
- lender reduces collateral eligibility.

The business should know how it will finish financed work and repay the facility under a plausible adverse scenario.

## WCP versus CAPLines

The WCP is a separate pilot delivery method from the permanent CAPLines products.

Compare:

- eligible uses;
- maximum maturity;
- monitoring requirements;
- transaction versus asset structures;
- export flexibility;
- guaranty-fee framework; and
- lender experience/authority.

Use the program that matches the financing need rather than assuming the newest program is automatically preferable.

## Lender-readiness package

A WCP package can include:

- historical and interim financial statements;
- receivables/payables aging;
- inventory reports;
- debt schedule;
- cash-flow forecast;
- transaction or contract schedules;
- work-in-progress information;
- customer concentration analysis;
- collateral information; and
- requested line structure and rationale.

Exact requirements vary by lender and transaction.

## Evidence to retain

Retain where appropriate:

- WCP application/loan documents;
- financial statements;
- aging and inventory reports;
- borrowing-base or transaction calculations;
- draw records;
- lender reports;
- annual review materials;
- repayments;
- waivers/amendments; and
- current Program Guide or policy references used for material decisions.

## Scope note

This article provides general WCP readiness guidance from current SBA public information. It does not guarantee eligibility, 100% project financing, loan approval, advance rates, fees, or lender terms. The current WCP Program Guide, SBA SOP, and lender underwriting control the actual facility.
