# SBA Express Revolving-Credit Readiness

## Purpose

The U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA) **SBA Express** delivery method allows participating lenders to use more of their own processes and procedures while receiving a lower SBA guaranty percentage than many other 7(a) delivery methods. Current SBA lender guidance permits SBA Express loans up to **$500,000**, with a maximum SBA guaranty of **50%**, and allows revolving lines of credit with maturities up to **10 years**.

SBA Express can fit smaller, flexible working-capital needs, but the business still needs to demonstrate repayment capacity and understand the lender's own underwriting, collateral, monitoring, and renewal practices.

## Current public terms

SBA's current public lender summary lists:

- maximum SBA Express loan amount: **$500,000**;
- maximum SBA guaranty: **50%**;
- revolving lines of credit: up to **10 years**;
- interest rates negotiated between borrower and lender subject to SBA maximums; and
- lender credit decisions under delegated authority.

SBA also states that lenders are not required to take collateral for loans up to $50,000 and may use their existing collateral policy for larger SBA Express loans, subject to current SBA conditions.

Verify the live SBA page and current SOP before relying on any threshold or collateral rule.

## Revolving-credit use case

A revolving SBA Express line can support short-term, recurring needs such as:

- inventory purchases;
- receivable timing gaps;
- payroll timing;
- recurring operating cycles;
- small project mobilization; and
- temporary liquidity needs.

The appropriate use depends on current 7(a) eligible-use rules and lender policy.

Do not use a revolving facility as a substitute for permanent equity when the business cannot generate enough cash to repay draws.

## Line-sizing discipline

Size the requested line from the cash cycle rather than simply requesting the maximum.

A useful calculation considers:

1. peak inventory or operating build;
2. receivables generated;
3. expected collections;
4. existing cash reserves;
5. supplier terms;
6. payroll and tax timing;
7. downside delay; and
8. other available credit.

Requesting significantly more than the demonstrated need can increase cost or underwriting difficulty without improving operations.

## Lender-process boundary

SBA Express lenders generally use their own processes and procedures more extensively than non-delegated 7(a) lending.

Borrowers should expect variation in:

- application forms;
- financial information requested;
- credit scoring;
- collateral requirements;
- covenant structure;
- renewal practices;
- line-cleanup expectations; and
- monitoring.

Do not assume one lender's SBA Express terms represent the entire program.

## Revolving-line governance

For a revolving line, document:

- who can request draws;
- maximum availability;
- minimum draw rules if any;
- interest calculation;
- annual or periodic review;
- financial-reporting deadlines;
- maturity and renewal;
- conditions for suspension; and
- how the balance must be repaid at maturity.

A revolving line should have internal controls comparable to other business debt even if the draw process is operationally simple.

## Cash-flow monitoring

Track whether the line is cycling as intended.

Warning signs include:

- balance continually near maximum;
- no meaningful pay-down after collections;
- draws funding losses rather than temporary assets;
- increasing use while sales decline;
- late lender reporting; and
- reliance on new draws to make old loan payments.

A line that never revolves may indicate the financing need has become permanent.

## Collateral readiness

Even where SBA does not require collateral at a lower threshold, the lender's policy and current SBA rules still matter.

Maintain current information on:

- business assets;
- existing liens;
- receivables;
- inventory;
- equipment;
- real estate where relevant; and
- other pledged obligations.

Confirm lien and collateral terms before closing rather than assuming the SBA guaranty eliminates collateral requirements.

## Interest-rate sensitivity

Many lines use variable rates tied to a base rate plus a lender spread subject to SBA caps.

Model debt service after a higher base rate, especially if the line could remain outstanding for years.

A useful sensitivity analysis can compare:

- current rate;
- +1 percentage point;
- +2 percentage points; and
- a high-balance scenario.

The business should remain able to service interest during a slower collection period.

## Annual review readiness

Even where the program does not prescribe the same monitored structure as an asset-based facility, lenders can require periodic financial review.

Keep ready:

- current financial statements;
- tax information required by the lender;
- receivable/payable aging;
- debt schedule;
- liquidity forecast; and
- explanation of material business changes.

Strong reporting helps preserve access to credit before the business urgently needs it.

## SBA Express versus other line programs

Compare SBA Express with other SBA line-of-credit structures where appropriate:

- CAPLines for specific seasonal, contract, builder, or asset-based needs;
- Working Capital Pilot for monitored transaction/asset-based needs up to current WCP limits;
- Export Express for smaller export-related needs; and
- MARC for eligible manufacturers.

The smaller SBA Express maximum can be a feature when the business values simpler lender processing and does not need a specialized monitored structure.

## Downside scenarios

Model:

- slower receivable collections;
- inventory write-down;
- loss of a key customer;
- interest-rate increase;
- lender declining renewal;
- line converted or required to amortize; and
- unexpected cash demand at the same time availability is constrained.

Maintain a plan for repaying the outstanding balance if renewal is unavailable.

## Lender-readiness package

A useful SBA Express package can include:

- historical/interim financial statements;
- debt schedule;
- cash-flow forecast;
- intended uses of the line;
- receivables/inventory information where relevant;
- ownership information;
- collateral information; and
- requested amount with a cash-cycle explanation.

Exact lender requirements vary.

## Evidence to retain

For a material SBA Express line, retain where appropriate:

- application and loan agreement;
- lender disclosures;
- draw/repayment history;
- financial reporting;
- collateral/lien documents;
- annual or periodic reviews;
- amendments/waivers; and
- maturity/renewal decisions.

## Scope note

This article provides general SBA Express readiness guidance based on current SBA public information. It does not guarantee approval, a revolving structure, a 10-year maturity, collateral treatment, or a specific interest rate. Participating lender underwriting and current SBA requirements control the actual loan.
