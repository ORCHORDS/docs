# SBA 7(a) Small Term Financing Readiness

## Purpose

The U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA) **7(a) Small** delivery method provides non-revolving term loans for eligible small-business needs at lower amounts than Standard 7(a). Current SBA lender guidance states that 7(a) Small loans are **$350,000 or less** and exclude specialized delivery methods such as SBA Express, Export Express, CAPLines, Export Working Capital, and pilot programs.

This program can fit borrowers that need a defined amount of term financing without the larger Standard 7(a) size or a revolving facility.

## Current public terms

SBA's current public lender summary lists:

- maximum loan amount: **$350,000**;
- term, non-revolving structure;
- maximum SBA guaranty: **85% for loans up to $150,000 and 75% for loans above $150,000**;
- interest rates negotiated with the lender subject to SBA maximums; and
- processing through delegated or non-delegated lender channels.

Verify current thresholds and SOP requirements at the time of application.

## Uses of proceeds

7(a) Small follows the broader 7(a) framework for eligible uses, which can include areas such as:

- working capital;
- equipment;
- furniture/fixtures/supplies;
- eligible business debt refinancing;
- real-estate or facility needs within program rules;
- ownership changes; and
- multi-purpose financing.

Document each use precisely enough for the lender to determine eligibility.

## Loan-size discipline

Do not default to the $350,000 maximum.

Calculate the requested amount from:

1. specific project or purchase costs;
2. borrower contribution;
3. existing cash resources;
4. working-capital reserve;
5. closing/transaction costs; and
6. realistic contingency.

Borrowing more than necessary adds debt service without improving project economics.

## Collateral boundary

Current SBA lender guidance states that for 7(a) Small loans:

- SBA does not require collateral for loans of **$50,000 or less**, subject to program exceptions; and
- for larger loans in the applicable range, the lender follows its written collateral policies for similarly sized non-SBA commercial loans under current SBA conditions.

Collateral requirements can change and lender policy matters. Verify the live terms for the transaction.

## Repayment capacity

Prepare a debt-service model using realistic cash flow.

Include:

- existing debt payments;
- proposed loan payment;
- payroll;
- taxes;
- rent/occupancy;
- major supplier obligations;
- owner distributions; and
- planned capital spending.

Stress-test at lower revenue or higher costs before assuming the business can comfortably support the debt.

## Working-capital use

When financing working capital, explain whether the need is:

- startup/launch;
- permanent expansion capital;
- one-time operating transition;
- inventory build;
- receivable growth; or
- another eligible purpose.

If the need is recurring and short-term, compare a revolving product rather than forcing a repeated need into a one-time term loan.

## Equipment use

For equipment financing, prepare:

- quote/invoice;
- useful life;
- installation cost;
- expected capacity/productivity effect;
- maintenance cost; and
- cash-flow benefit.

Avoid financing equipment with no clear operational purpose merely because credit is available.

## Acquisition or ownership change

For an eligible business acquisition or ownership change, separate:

- purchase price;
- inventory;
- equipment;
- working capital;
- real estate if any;
- intangible/business value; and
- transaction costs.

Provide historical financials and a post-close management/cash-flow plan.

## Lender-readiness package

A useful package can include:

- business/owner information;
- historical and interim financial statements;
- debt schedule;
- tax information requested by the lender;
- use-of-proceeds schedule;
- quotations/purchase agreements;
- projections; and
- collateral information where relevant.

Exact requirements vary by lender and transaction.

## Processing expectations

SBA's lender-facing public summary currently describes a typical SBA turnaround range of **2-10 business days** for 7(a) Small processing after a complete submission through the applicable channel.

That is not a borrower promise or total closing timeline. Lender underwriting, document collection, collateral, legal review, and closing conditions can take longer.

## Evidence to retain

Retain where appropriate:

- application and lender/SBA forms;
- use-of-proceeds schedule;
- loan agreement;
- collateral documents;
- invoices/purchase records;
- closing records;
- financial reporting; and
- amendments/waivers.

This article provides general 7(a) Small financing-readiness guidance from current SBA public information. It does not guarantee eligibility, approval, a $350,000 loan, collateral treatment, processing time, maturity, or interest rate. Current SBA policy and lender underwriting control the transaction.
