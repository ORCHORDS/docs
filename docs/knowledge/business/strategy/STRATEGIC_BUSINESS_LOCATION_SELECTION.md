# Strategic Business Location Selection

## Purpose

Business location is a strategic choice because it affects access to customers and partners, operating costs, taxes, licensing, zoning, and other local requirements. The U.S. Small Business Administration explicitly recommends considering target-market location together with regional costs, benefits, and restrictions.

## Decision framework

Evaluate candidate locations against:

1. **Customer access.** Determine whether the location improves reach to the target market or materially changes delivery, service, or acquisition costs.
2. **Partner and workforce access.** Consider suppliers, logistics, talent, professional services, and other dependencies.
3. **Operating costs.** Compare rent, property values, salaries, utilities, insurance, licenses, and fees rather than using headline rent alone.
4. **Regulatory fit.** Check zoning, permits, taxes, registrations, and sector-specific restrictions before committing.
5. **Scalability.** Assess whether the location can support realistic growth without forcing a premature move.
6. **Resilience.** Consider transport, utilities, connectivity, and concentration risk where these are material to operations.

## Evidence to retain

Keep the assumptions, cost comparisons, market data, regulatory checks, and decision rationale used to select the location. Revisit them before major expansion or relocation.

## Source

- U.S. Small Business Administration — Launch your business / Pick your business location: https://www.sba.gov/counseling/launch-your-business/

## Scope note

Location-specific tax, zoning, licensing, employment, and property rules vary by jurisdiction. This article is a general planning framework, not legal or tax advice.
