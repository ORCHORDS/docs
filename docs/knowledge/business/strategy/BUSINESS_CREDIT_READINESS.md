# Business Credit Readiness

## Purpose

Business credit readiness is the ability to present a credible, internally consistent financial picture before seeking loans, lines of credit, supplier terms, or other financing. Current U.S. Small Business Administration guidance notes that poor credit history is a common reason small-business loan applications are declined and that credit history can also affect insurance, supplier, vendor, and partner decisions.

## Readiness checks

1. **Review both business and personal credit where relevant.** New businesses may be assessed partly on an owner's personal credit history, while established businesses can rely more heavily on their own financial and credit record.
2. **Correct reporting errors before a major application.** Review available credit files early enough to dispute inaccurate information rather than discovering it during underwriting.
3. **Maintain current financial statements.** Keep revenue, expenses, cash flow, debt obligations, and other material financial records current and reconcilable.
4. **Separate business and personal activity where the legal and operating structure supports it.** Clear records make it easier to explain the business's actual obligations and performance.
5. **Define the funding need.** Record the amount sought, intended use of proceeds, expected business effect, and how repayment will be supported.
6. **Test repayment capacity.** Consider existing debt service, cash-flow variability, downside scenarios, and the effect of the proposed borrowing on future liquidity.
7. **Prepare consistent application evidence.** Business plans, financial projections, credit history, amount and use of funds, and supporting records should tell the same story.
8. **Compare financing terms rather than only approval.** Evaluate rates, fees, collateral requirements, repayment periods, guarantees, covenants, and other material terms.

## SBA lending context

SBA-guaranteed lending does not remove normal credit assessment. SBA's current 7(a) guidance states that an eligible borrower must be creditworthy and demonstrate a reasonable ability to repay. SBA lender guidance also notes that lenders may consider credit scoring or credit history together with cash flow, equity, collateral, associates, and guarantors.

Lender Match likewise advises applicants to be ready with a business plan where expected, the amount and intended use of funds, and credit history before approaching lenders.

## Practical evidence record

A reusable credit-readiness file can include:

- latest business credit report or review date;
- material errors identified and dispute status;
- current debt and repayment schedule;
- recent financial statements;
- funding amount and use-of-funds statement;
- repayment assumptions and sensitivity notes;
- key application documents prepared; and
- offers reviewed, with material rates, fees, and conditions compared.

## Scope note

Credit decisions depend on the lender, product, jurisdiction, business history, owners or guarantors, and current underwriting criteria. This article describes readiness practices; it does not promise approval, a particular interest rate, or eligibility for an SBA program.
