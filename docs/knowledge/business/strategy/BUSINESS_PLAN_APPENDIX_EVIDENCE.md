# Business Plan Appendix Evidence

## Purpose

A business-plan appendix keeps supporting evidence available without forcing the main plan to carry every source document, calculation, record, or reference. The appendix should help a reviewer verify important claims and assumptions while keeping the core plan readable.

The appendix is not a dumping ground. Include evidence because it supports a decision, projection, capability, qualification, or material statement in the plan.

## SBA planning basis

Current U.S. Small Business Administration business-plan guidance describes the business plan as a roadmap for structuring, running, and growing a business. SBA guidance for traditional plans expects detailed market, organization, product or service, marketing, funding, and financial-projection information, and SBA provides sample business plans to illustrate how supporting detail can be organized.

The exact appendix structure is not mandated by this article. The governance principle is to keep supporting evidence attributable, current, relevant, and safely distributable.

## What belongs in supporting evidence

Depending on the plan and audience, useful supporting material can include:

- market-research source extracts or reference details;
- customer-research methodology and summarized findings;
- material assumptions and calculation notes;
- resumes or capability summaries for key leadership where appropriate;
- organizational or ownership diagrams;
- licenses, permits, registrations, or qualification evidence where relevant and safe to share;
- product, service, technical, or operational supporting information;
- supplier quotations or cost evidence;
- letters of intent, contracts, or pipeline evidence when disclosure is authorized;
- financial assumptions and projection workpapers;
- capital-expenditure support;
- insurance or risk-planning summaries;
- property, location, or equipment information; and
- other evidence directly supporting a material plan statement.

Do not include material merely because it exists. Every appendix item should have a clear reason for inclusion.

## Evidence register

For material appendix items, maintain enough metadata to understand what the evidence is and whether it is still usable. Record where practical:

- evidence title or identifier;
- plan section, claim, or assumption supported;
- source or origin;
- source date and retrieval date where relevant;
- owner or custodian;
- version or effective period;
- distribution classification or sensitivity;
- expiry or review trigger; and
- notes on material limitations.

This allows stale evidence to be identified without rereading the whole plan.

## Provenance and attribution

Evidence should be attributable to a source. For externally verifiable facts, prefer primary authoritative sources where available.

When the appendix contains internal analysis, distinguish:

- observed facts;
- third-party source material;
- calculated outputs; and
- management assumptions or judgment.

Do not present an internal estimate as though it came from an independent external authority.

For a calculation or model, preserve enough information to reproduce or understand the result, including the relevant source inputs, formula or method, period, and material assumptions.

## Link evidence to claims

Supporting material is most useful when a reviewer can tell what it supports. Use references from the main plan or an evidence register to connect appendix items to claims such as:

- market demand;
- customer need;
- pricing;
- expected volume;
- operating cost;
- funding requirement;
- capacity;
- qualification or licensing status;
- supplier availability; or
- financial projections.

Avoid attaching large collections of evidence with no clear relationship to the plan.

## Currency and stale evidence

Evidence should be reviewed when the underlying claim is sensitive to time.

Refresh or retire material when:

- the source has been superseded;
- the market period is no longer representative;
- prices or supplier terms changed;
- a license, permit, certification, or qualification expired or changed;
- a forecast assumption changed materially;
- ownership or leadership changed;
- customer or pipeline evidence is no longer current; or
- a regulator, standards body, or authoritative source changed the applicable information.

Do not leave superseded evidence in the active appendix without clearly marking its historical status.

## Version control

When the business plan is used for material decisions or external review, align the appendix with a specific plan version.

A practical approach is to record:

- plan version or date;
- appendix version or date;
- evidence-register version; and
- material changes since the prior version.

This helps prevent a current narrative from being distributed with old projections, quotes, or supporting records.

## Sensitive-information boundary

Before distributing an appendix, review every item for information that should not be included in that audience's copy.

Potentially sensitive material can include:

- personal information;
- bank or payment details;
- tax identifiers;
- credentials or secrets;
- private keys or tokens;
- confidential customer information;
- private contractual terms;
- non-public security or infrastructure details;
- confidential transaction information; and
- information restricted by law or agreement.

Create a redacted or audience-specific evidence pack where necessary rather than distributing an unrestricted internal appendix.

Do not redact material in a way that makes the remaining evidence misleading.

## Third-party material

Respect copyright, confidentiality, license, and contractual restrictions. Prefer summarizing or citing authoritative material rather than reproducing entire third-party documents when full reproduction is unnecessary or unauthorized.

For public sources, retain the source reference and retrieval context. For confidential third-party material, record access restrictions and do not assume the business plan grants permission to redistribute it.

## Evidence quality review

Before relying on an appendix item, check where relevant:

1. Does it support the stated claim?
2. Is the source authoritative enough for the decision?
3. Is it current for the relevant period?
4. Is its scope consistent with the claim?
5. Are limitations or contrary evidence visible?
6. Can the evidence be lawfully and appropriately distributed?
7. Has a later source superseded it?
8. Does it create an unsupported implication of certification, approval, or assurance?

A document's existence is not proof that its contents establish the claim being made.

## External review packages

A lender, investor, partner, grant program, regulator, or other reviewer may require a different evidence set. Build the package from the controlled evidence register rather than copying the complete internal appendix by default.

For each external package, record where appropriate:

- intended audience;
- plan version;
- evidence included;
- redactions or omissions;
- date supplied; and
- accountable approver.

This reduces accidental disclosure and makes later questions easier to reconcile with the exact material provided.

## Maintenance workflow

1. Identify material plan claims that require supporting evidence.
2. Add evidence to the register with provenance, date, owner, and scope.
3. Check sensitivity and distribution restrictions.
4. Link the evidence to the relevant plan section or assumption.
5. Review evidence when the plan or underlying facts change.
6. Mark superseded items clearly and remove them from active external packages.
7. Confirm the appendix version matches the distributed plan version.
8. Preserve historical evidence only where there is a legitimate records or decision-history need.

## Scope note

This article describes reusable evidence-governance practices for business plans. It does not prescribe a mandatory SBA appendix format, does not guarantee that a reviewer will accept particular evidence, and does not provide legal, accounting, tax, investment, or records-management advice.
