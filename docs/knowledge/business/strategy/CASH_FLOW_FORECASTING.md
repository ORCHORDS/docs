# Cash Flow Forecasting

## Purpose

A business can be profitable on paper and still fail to meet obligations if cash arrives later than expenses are due. Cash-flow forecasting makes timing visible before it becomes a crisis.

## Forecast pattern

- Start with opening cash available.
- Forecast expected cash receipts by realistic collection date, not invoice date alone.
- Forecast payroll, suppliers, rent, taxes, debt service, capital expenditure, and other cash outflows.
- Separate recurring commitments from discretionary spending.
- Build monthly detail for the near term and extend the outlook as business needs require.
- Compare actual cash movement against forecast and update assumptions.
- Model downside cases such as delayed receipts, lower sales, or unexpected costs.
- Identify the minimum liquidity buffer and decision points before the forecast becomes negative.

## Management use

A cash-flow forecast should inform payment timing, hiring, purchasing, financing, and investment decisions. It should be updated when material assumptions change.
