# International Trade Loan Financing Readiness

## Purpose

The U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA) **International Trade Loan (ITL)** program helps eligible small businesses enter or expand in international markets and make investments needed to compete with foreign sellers or import competition. Current SBA public guidance states that International Trade loans can provide up to **$5 million in total financing** with SBA's maximum guaranty of **90%**, combining fixed-asset financing, working capital, and eligible debt refinancing.

SBA expanded participation in the ITL program through Policy Notice 5000-877629, effective **May 1, 2026**, to make the enhanced 90% guaranty easier for lenders to use. A borrower should therefore evaluate the ITL against current rules rather than older descriptions of the program.

## Program fit

An ITL can be relevant when the financing supports a credible international-trade business case, such as:

- entering a foreign market;
- expanding export sales;
- increasing capacity to serve international customers;
- improving productivity or competitiveness against imported goods;
- financing fixed assets connected to trade growth;
- providing eligible working capital; or
- refinancing eligible debt where current ITL rules permit it.

Financing approval does not itself prove that an export strategy is commercially viable or legally permitted.

## Current public terms

SBA's current international-sales guidance states:

- maximum International Trade loan: **$5 million**;
- maximum SBA guaranty: **90% of the total loan amount**, subject to current program limits and rules; and
- potential combination of fixed assets, working capital financing, and debt refinancing.

The current policy notice effective May 1, 2026 states that SBA is easing the burdens of participation and expanding ways lenders can use the ITL program.

Verify the current SOP, policy notices, guaranty-exposure rules, and participating lender requirements before relying on these figures for a live transaction.

## International-market business case

A strong financing request should explain how the investment changes the company's international competitiveness.

Document:

1. target markets or sources of import competition;
2. product/service involved;
3. current and expected sales;
4. existing production/service capacity;
5. investment required;
6. customer or channel strategy;
7. international pricing and margin;
8. implementation timeline; and
9. expected effect on cash flow and debt-service capacity.

Avoid unsupported claims that international demand will automatically materialize after financing closes.

## Fixed-asset component

For equipment, facility, or other eligible fixed-asset investments, maintain:

- asset description;
- vendor/property information;
- purchase or project cost;
- installation/renovation cost;
- useful life;
- capacity or productivity impact;
- expected trade-related use; and
- implementation schedule.

If the project is predominantly long-term fixed assets, compare ITL with 504 financing and the July 2026 cumulative 7(a)/504 coordination rules.

## Working-capital component

International growth often increases cash requirements before revenue is collected.

Model:

- inventory build;
- supplier deposits;
- production costs;
- payroll;
- freight;
- customs and documentation;
- foreign-receivable terms;
- customer acceptance; and
- collection timing.

Separate permanent working capital from short-term order-specific financing. If the need is primarily revolving export working capital, compare EWCP or WCP.

## Debt-refinancing component

If ITL proceeds refinance eligible debt, identify:

- debt being refinanced;
- original use;
- outstanding balance;
- lender;
- interest rate;
- maturity;
- collateral;
- payment history; and
- business/trade benefit from the refinance.

Confirm eligibility under current SBA rules rather than assuming any existing business debt can be included.

## Competing with imports

SBA's ITL framework can also support eligible businesses investing to compete more effectively against foreign competition.

Build an evidence-based case using information such as:

- market-share changes;
- imported-product competition;
- price/cost comparisons;
- customer loss or pressure;
- production bottlenecks;
- technology gaps;
- quality/capacity needs; and
- investment expected to improve competitiveness.

Do not frame ordinary domestic competition as import competition without supporting market evidence.

## Export-demand evidence

Useful evidence can include:

- signed export orders;
- letters of intent;
- distributor agreements;
- historical export sales;
- customer pipeline;
- market research;
- trade-show or buyer engagement; and
- indirect export opportunities through U.S. supply chains.

The strength needed depends on the borrower, project, lender, and current SBA rules.

## Export compliance

An ITL does not authorize restricted trade.

Maintain separate compliance for:

- export controls;
- sanctions/restricted parties;
- licenses;
- customs;
- product standards;
- end-use/end-user controls;
- anti-boycott requirements where applicable; and
- destination-country legal/tax requirements.

Regulated products or destinations can require specialist trade/legal review.

## Currency and payment risk

International growth can create risks beyond ordinary domestic lending.

Assess:

- invoice currency;
- foreign-exchange exposure;
- buyer creditworthiness;
- payment method;
- political/country risk;
- transfer restrictions;
- credit insurance; and
- collection/enforcement practicalities.

Financing can fund the sale but does not eliminate nonpayment risk.

## Collateral and project coordination

Maintain a complete collateral and financing schedule showing:

- assets acquired with proceeds;
- existing liens;
- working-capital collateral;
- real estate/equipment;
- other SBA debt;
- proposed lien positions; and
- material cross-default or intercreditor arrangements.

Where ITL is combined with other financing, ensure no project cost is financed twice.

## Repayment capacity

Model combined debt service using realistic operating cash flow.

Stress-test:

- export sales 20% below forecast;
- delayed foreign collections;
- exchange-rate movement;
- equipment/project delay;
- margin compression;
- increased freight/tariffs; and
- loss of a major international customer.

The borrower's ability to repay should not depend on one optimistic export scenario.

## Lender-readiness package

A useful package can include:

- historical/interim financial statements;
- debt schedule;
- use-of-proceeds schedule;
- export/international business plan;
- market/import-competition evidence;
- equipment/property quotes;
- working-capital forecast;
- customer/order evidence;
- collateral schedule; and
- management/export experience.

Exact requirements depend on the lender and current SBA rules.

## Choosing among SBA export programs

Compare the ITL with:

- **Export Express** — smaller export-related financing up to the current program limit;
- **Export Working Capital Program** — dedicated export working-capital financing;
- **Working Capital Pilot** — monitored domestic/export line of credit; and
- other 7(a)/504 structures for fixed assets or broader financing needs.

The ITL is especially relevant when the plan combines significant fixed-asset investment with working capital or eligible refinancing under an international-trade business case.

## Current-policy verification

Because SBA materially updated ITL participation in 2026, verify at application:

- Policy Notice 5000-877629 and any superseding notice;
- effective SOP 50 10 version;
- maximum SBA exposure;
- working-capital sublimits if applicable;
- eligible fixed-asset/refinancing uses;
- borrower/export or import-competition requirements;
- collateral;
- fees; and
- lender authority.

Do not apply future-effective guidance before its stated effective date.

## Evidence to retain

Retain where appropriate:

- ITL application and loan documents;
- trade/business case;
- sources-and-uses schedule;
- fixed-asset purchase records;
- working-capital calculations;
- debt-refinancing schedule;
- customer/market evidence;
- collateral/lien records;
- export compliance records relevant to financed activity; and
- policy/SOP version used for the transaction.

## Scope note

This article provides general ITL readiness guidance based on current SBA public information. It does not guarantee eligibility, a $5 million loan, a 90% guaranty, export legality, refinancing eligibility, or lender approval. Current SBA policy, effective SOP, and lender underwriting control the actual transaction.
