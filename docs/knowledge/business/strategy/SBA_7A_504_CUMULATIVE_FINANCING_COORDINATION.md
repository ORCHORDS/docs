# SBA 7(a) and 504 Cumulative Financing Coordination

## Purpose

Effective **July 4, 2026**, the U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA) changed its policy for coordinating maximum financing across the 7(a) and 504 loan programs. Current SBA public guidance states that eligible borrowers can combine 7(a) and 504 financing for up to **$10 million in SBA-backed financing**, replacing the previous $5 million cumulative limit.

This does not turn the programs into one loan. The 7(a) and 504 programs continue to have different purposes, structures, lenders, collateral rules, eligible uses, maturities, and underwriting requirements. Businesses considering both should coordinate the financing plan at the project level and verify current program rules before committing to a transaction.

## Current policy change

SBA announced the cumulative-limit change in May 2026 and stated that it became effective July 4, 2026. The agency subsequently announced that small businesses were eligible for the new $10 million combined financing ceiling.

The change increases the amount of SBA-backed financing that an eligible borrower may potentially access across the two programs. It does not guarantee that a borrower qualifies for the maximum or that every combination of uses is permitted.

## Program roles

### 7(a)

SBA's 7(a) program supports a broad range of eligible business purposes, which can include areas such as:

- working capital;
- equipment;
- business expansion;
- real estate;
- acquisitions; and
- other eligible uses under current policy.

7(a) financing is generally delivered through participating lenders with an SBA guarantee.

### 504

SBA's 504 program provides long-term, fixed-rate financing for eligible major fixed assets that support business growth and job creation. 504 loans are delivered through Certified Development Companies (CDCs) together with other project financing.

The different program purposes matter when deciding which costs belong in each financing component.

## Do not simply split a project to maximize SBA dollars

A financing plan should be driven by eligible uses, project economics, repayment capacity, and the program structures—not by an attempt to force every cost under an SBA-backed ceiling.

For each material use of proceeds, identify:

1. the business purpose;
2. which program can legally and operationally support that use;
3. amount;
4. expected term;
5. collateral;
6. repayment source;
7. required borrower contribution;
8. fees and closing costs; and
9. timing.

Confirm the allocation with the participating 7(a) lender and CDC/lenders involved in the 504 project.

## Cumulative financing register

Maintain a current view of the borrower's SBA-backed exposure across relevant 7(a) and 504 loans.

A useful register can include:

- loan/program type;
- lender or CDC;
- original approved amount;
- current outstanding amount;
- SBA-backed amount relevant to the cumulative rule;
- project/use of proceeds;
- approval date;
- maturity;
- collateral;
- guarantors; and
- material cross-default or intercreditor terms.

Do not rely on memory when the business has multiple SBA facilities or related projects.

## Project financing sequence

For a transaction that may use both programs, a reusable planning sequence is:

1. define the total project cost;
2. separate working-capital and fixed-asset needs;
3. identify eligible 504 project costs;
4. identify eligible 7(a) uses that are not appropriately financed in the 504 structure;
5. model non-SBA financing and borrower equity;
6. calculate the cumulative SBA-backed exposure under current policy;
7. model debt service for all components;
8. confirm collateral and lien positions;
9. coordinate closing timing; and
10. recheck the cumulative limit immediately before approval/closing if other SBA financing changes.

## Avoid double financing

No cost should be financed twice.

Controls should ensure:

- invoices or assets assigned to one financing source are not also funded by another;
- borrower contributions are traceable;
- acquisition/real-estate costs reconcile to closing documents;
- equipment schedules match financed assets;
- working-capital proceeds are not represented as fixed-asset costs; and
- refinanced obligations are removed from duplicate sources-and-uses schedules.

## Repayment capacity

A higher cumulative ceiling increases possible financing but does not increase the business's underlying cash flow.

Model combined debt service using:

- realistic operating cash flow;
- all existing debt;
- new 7(a) payment requirements;
- new 504-related payment requirements;
- variable-rate exposure where applicable;
- seasonal or cyclical volatility;
- downside revenue and margin scenarios; and
- major capital or working-capital needs not covered by the project financing.

Do not justify the maximum available financing solely because the project appraises or costs enough to support it.

## Collateral and lien coordination

Using more than one financing program can create collateral and intercreditor complexity.

Identify:

- assets acquired or improved;
- existing liens;
- proposed lien priority;
- business assets pledged under the 7(a) facility;
- real estate and fixed assets in the 504 project;
- personal guarantees required under applicable rules; and
- any lender/CDCs agreements needed to coordinate security interests.

Borrowers should understand that the combined financing limit does not override ordinary collateral, credit, or closing requirements.

## Timing and closing risk

7(a) and 504 financing components can have different approval and closing processes.

Build a closing plan that addresses:

- purchase or project deadlines;
- construction or equipment delivery timing;
- interim financing if applicable;
- appraisal and environmental review;
- title and lien work;
- borrower equity availability;
- insurance;
- landlord or seller consents; and
- expiration of approvals.

A project can fail even after credit approval if the components cannot close in the required sequence.

## Business-acquisition and expansion planning

Where 7(a) financing supports an acquisition or working-capital component while 504 supports qualifying fixed assets in a broader expansion plan, preserve a clear allocation between business-value, intangible, working-capital, equipment, and real-estate components.

Use professional accounting, legal, valuation, and tax advice where the transaction structure creates material consequences.

## Manufacturing note

SBA's 2026 announcement also highlighted special financing policy for small manufacturers, including the ability to use 7(a) financing in addition to multiple qualifying 504 projects under current rules.

Manufacturing-specific eligibility and incentives can change rapidly. Verify the current SBA manufacturing pages and lender guidance rather than assuming every borrower receives the same treatment.

## Current-rule verification

The cumulative-limit policy is recent. For live transactions, verify:

- current policy notice;
- current SOP 50 10 version that is effective on the relevant date;
- program maximums;
- eligibility rules;
- ownership/residency or other borrower requirements;
- collateral requirements;
- guaranty fees; and
- lender/CDCs procedures.

SBA has published a newer SOP version with a future effective date. Do not apply a future-effective SOP before its effective date merely because it is available online.

## Evidence to retain

For coordinated 7(a)/504 financing, retain where appropriate:

- complete sources-and-uses schedule;
- cumulative SBA exposure calculation;
- 7(a) approval and loan documents;
- 504 project/CDC documents;
- current SBA policy notice used for the limit analysis;
- lender/CDCs communications;
- debt-service model;
- collateral/lien schedule;
- borrower contribution evidence; and
- closing reconciliation.

## Scope note

This article summarizes general planning implications of SBA's July 2026 cumulative financing change. It does not guarantee eligibility for $10 million, define every eligible use, or replace the current SBA SOP, policy notices, lender underwriting, CDC requirements, legal advice, or transaction-specific closing guidance.
