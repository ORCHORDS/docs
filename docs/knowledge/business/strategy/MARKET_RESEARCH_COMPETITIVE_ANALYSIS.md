# Market Research and Competitive Analysis

## Purpose

Market research reduces uncertainty about customers, demand, market size, and buying behavior. Competitive analysis examines the alternatives available to those customers and the conditions that can affect entry and differentiation.

## Evidence to collect

The U.S. Small Business Administration recommends combining market research with competitive analysis. Useful questions include:

- Is there demonstrated demand for the product or service?
- How large is the addressable market?
- What demographic or economic characteristics matter to the offering?
- Where are prospective customers located?
- What prices do customers currently pay for alternatives?
- Which direct and indirect competitors serve the same need?
- What strengths, weaknesses, market share, barriers to entry, and windows of opportunity characterize the competitive landscape?

## Primary and direct research

Secondary research can use public economic, demographic, industry, and market data. Direct research can add first-hand evidence through methods such as surveys, questionnaires, focus groups, and interviews.

Do not treat one research method as sufficient by default. Triangulate material conclusions where practical and record the source date, population, geography, and assumptions behind important figures.

## Competitive analysis workflow

1. Define the customer problem and market segment before naming competitors.
2. Identify direct competitors and indirect substitutes.
3. Compare customer segments, offers, pricing, distribution, strengths, and weaknesses.
4. Identify entry barriers and market changes that could alter the analysis.
5. Separate observed facts from assumptions and hypotheses.
6. Revisit the analysis when customer behavior, regulation, technology, or major competitors change.

## Avoiding false precision

Market-size estimates are highly sensitive to definitions and assumptions. Document how an estimate was derived rather than presenting a single number without methodology. Distinguish total market estimates from the portion realistically reachable by the business.
