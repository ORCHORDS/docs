# County Business Patterns for Competitive Density

## Purpose

County Business Patterns (CBP) is a U.S. Census Bureau program that provides annual subnational business statistics, including establishment counts, employment, and payroll by industry and geography. Those measures can help a business-planning process estimate local competitive density and employer scale.

## Practical use

1. Identify the relevant NAICS industry before comparing markets.
2. Compare establishment counts across candidate counties, ZIP Codes, or other available geographies.
3. Use employment and payroll alongside establishment counts to distinguish a market with many very small establishments from one dominated by larger employers.
4. Normalize counts against population, households, or another relevant demand measure when comparing differently sized markets.
5. Compare multiple years where possible instead of treating one annual snapshot as a durable trend.
6. Document suppression, disclosure-avoidance, classification, and coverage limitations when they affect the interpretation.
7. Combine CBP with customer-demographic and spending research before making a location or expansion decision.

## Sources

- U.S. Census Bureau — Powerful Data for Your Small Business: https://www.census.gov/topics/business-economy/small-business/about/powerful-data.html
- U.S. Census Bureau — Census Business Builder: https://www.census.gov/data/data-tools/cbb.html

## Scope note

Business density is not the same as market saturation. Establishment, employment, and payroll statistics should be interpreted together with demand, pricing, differentiation, costs, and local operating conditions.
