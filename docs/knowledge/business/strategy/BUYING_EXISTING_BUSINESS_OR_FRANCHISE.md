# Buying an Existing Business or Franchise

## Purpose

Buying an existing business or franchise can reduce some startup uncertainty, but it changes the due-diligence questions. SBA guidance emphasizes understanding control, contracts, financial condition, permits, location constraints, and the value of the opportunity before purchase.

## Practical review

1. Decide whether the desired model is independent ownership or a franchise relationship with ongoing brand and operating requirements.
2. Review financial records, customer base, operating expenses, assets, liabilities, staffing, and key dependencies.
3. Verify licenses, permits, zoning, environmental considerations, and transferability of material rights.
4. Review contracts, franchise restrictions, territory, supply obligations, training, fees, renewal, transfer, and termination terms where applicable.
5. Use an appropriate valuation approach and test the assumptions behind the asking price.
6. Record unresolved liabilities, required consents, transition needs, and conditions that must be satisfied before closing.

## Source

- U.S. Small Business Administration — Plan your business: Buy an existing business or franchise: https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/

## Scope note

Business acquisitions and franchises can involve legal, tax, financing, disclosure, and sector-specific requirements. Obtain appropriate professional advice for a specific transaction.
