# SBA Size-Standard Qualification Planning

## Purpose

SBA size standards determine whether a business qualifies as small for many U.S. federal contracting and assistance programs. The applicable threshold varies by industry and is generally based on average annual receipts or number of employees, with affiliation rules affecting the calculation.

## Planning workflow

1. Identify the correct NAICS industry for the activity or procurement being evaluated.
2. Look up the current SBA size standard for that code rather than relying on an old threshold copied into an internal document.
3. Determine whether the standard is based on receipts, employees, or another stated measure.
4. Include affiliates where SBA's affiliation rules require their employees or receipts to be counted.
5. Preserve the effective date and source of the threshold used in the analysis.
6. Re-check the current rule before a certification, bid, or eligibility decision because size standards can change.
7. Treat proposed rules separately from standards that are legally effective.

## Current-status caution

SBA's current reference materials continue to point users to the effective size-standard table and regulations. In August 2026 SBA also announced a proposed overhaul of size standards. A proposal should not be substituted for the standards currently in effect until a final rule becomes effective.

## Scope note

This article is planning guidance, not an eligibility determination. Federal program rules, affiliation analysis, and procurement-specific requirements should be checked for the actual transaction or application.
