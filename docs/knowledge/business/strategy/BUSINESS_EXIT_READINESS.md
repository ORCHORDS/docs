# Business Exit Readiness

## Purpose

Exit readiness is the preparation needed before a business is sold, transferred, wound down, or liquidated. It should be treated as an ongoing planning discipline rather than a checklist created only after an owner has already decided to leave.

Current U.S. Small Business Administration guidance distinguishes several exit paths, including sale, gradual transfer, lease-based transfer, closure, bankruptcy, and liquidation. The correct path depends on ownership structure, financial condition, contracts, tax obligations, employees, licenses, and the intended continuity of the business.

## Readiness areas

1. **Ownership and decision authority** — document who can approve a sale, transfer, dissolution, or other exit action under the entity's governing documents and applicable law.
2. **Financial records** — maintain current financial statements, debt schedules, tax records, working-capital information, and explanations for material liabilities or contingencies.
3. **Assets and liabilities** — maintain an inventory of material physical and intangible assets, liens, leases, debts, guarantees, and obligations that could affect valuation or transfer.
4. **Contracts and consents** — identify customer, supplier, financing, licensing, tenancy, and other agreements that restrict assignment, require consent, or create post-exit obligations.
5. **Intellectual property and data** — establish what is owned, licensed, transferable, retained, or subject to confidentiality, privacy, retention, or deletion duties.
6. **Employees and contractors** — identify wage, benefit, notice, employment-tax, contractor-payment, and transition obligations that may arise during a sale or closure.
7. **Licenses and registrations** — determine which permits, registrations, trade names, tax accounts, and licenses must be transferred, cancelled, replaced, or separately closed.
8. **Customer and supplier continuity** — plan how open commitments, prepaid amounts, warranties, support duties, records, and communications will be handled.
9. **Valuation and transaction structure** — understand the valuation basis and whether the contemplated transaction is an asset sale, stock or ownership-interest sale, gradual transfer, lease arrangement, or another structure.
10. **Records retention** — identify which records must remain available after the operating business stops or changes ownership.

## Sale and transfer readiness

SBA guidance recommends establishing a business valuation before marketing a business and identifies income, market, and asset approaches as common valuation methods. A transaction record should also identify the assets and liabilities included, operational arrangements before closing, access to information during diligence, and material fees or adjustments.

Ownership transfer can take several forms. SBA currently describes outright sale, gradual sale, and lease-based transfer as examples. These structures have materially different cash-flow, control, tax, liability, and continuity implications, so the chosen structure should be documented rather than inferred from informal arrangements.

## Closure readiness

Closing an entity is not the same as merely ceasing operations. SBA guidance includes documenting the decision, filing dissolution documents where required, cancelling registrations and licenses, addressing employees, resolving financial and tax obligations, and retaining required records.

From the U.S. federal tax perspective, the IRS separately identifies final returns, employee obligations, taxes due, contractor reporting, EIN/account deactivation, and records retention as closure tasks. Exact forms depend on the entity type and facts.

## Liquidation readiness

Liquidation is generally a forced or last-resort exit rather than an ordinary sale. SBA guidance recommends inventorying and securing assets, determining liquidation value, estimating net proceeds, choosing an appropriate sale method, and using qualified professional assistance where needed.

Do not assume book value, replacement cost, or going-concern value equals liquidation value. The likely proceeds can depend heavily on timing, condition, encumbrances, market depth, and sale method.

## Evidence record

A reusable exit-readiness file can include:

- governing approval requirements;
- current asset and liability register;
- ownership and capitalization records;
- valuation date, method, and assumptions;
- material contracts and required consents;
- licenses, registrations, and tax accounts;
- employee and contractor obligations;
- data, records, and IP transition requirements;
- customer and supplier continuity actions;
- unresolved disputes or contingent liabilities;
- proposed exit structure and professional-review status; and
- post-close or post-closure obligations and retention dates.

## Scope note

Exit requirements vary by entity type, jurisdiction, transaction structure, workforce, contracts, tax position, and regulated activities. This article is reusable planning guidance, not legal, tax, valuation, or transaction advice, and it does not assert that any specific business is being sold or closed.
