# Surety-Bond Readiness for Contract Bidding

## Purpose

Many public and private contracts require surety bonds before a business can bid, receive an award, begin performance, or satisfy payment obligations. The U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA) Surety Bond Guarantee Program can help qualified small businesses obtain contract surety bonds when they may not meet conventional surety criteria on their own.

Bond readiness is therefore part of contract-market readiness. A business should understand the required bond, its own financial and operational capacity, the surety underwriting process, and the current SBA program limits before committing to a bid.

## Contract surety versus commercial bonds

SBA guarantees **contract bonds**, not commercial bonds.

Contract bonds relate to performance of a specific contract. Commercial bonds generally support compliance with laws or licensing requirements and are outside the SBA Surety Bond Guarantee program.

Confirm which type the solicitation or customer actually requires before assuming SBA support is available.

## Major contract-bond categories

SBA currently describes several contract-bond categories:

- **Bid bond** — supports the bidder's commitment and the ability to provide required payment and performance bonding if awarded.
- **Performance bond** — supports completion of the contract by the bonded business.
- **Payment bond** — supports payment to suppliers and subcontractors.
- **Ancillary bond** — supports additional contract obligations outside the main performance or payment bond, such as certain maintenance requirements.

A solicitation can require more than one bond. Build the expected bond package into the bid plan rather than waiting until award.

## Current SBA program limits

As of the current SBA public guidance, the Surety Bond Guarantee Program can support qualified small-business contracts up to:

- **$9 million for non-federal contracts**; and
- **$14 million for federal contracts**.

The federal ceiling can depend on the applicable SBA and contracting requirements. Verify the current program page and solicitation before relying on a limit in a live bid because statutory or policy limits can change.

## Core eligibility and underwriting boundary

SBA's public eligibility summary requires the business to:

- qualify as a small business under SBA size standards;
- stay within the applicable contract limit; and
- meet the surety company's evaluation of **credit, capacity, and character**.

An SBA guarantee does not mean every applicant automatically receives a bond. The participating surety still underwrites the contractor and the specific bonded obligation.

## Readiness package

Before approaching a surety agent for an important bid, prepare a current package that can explain the business and the project.

Useful materials can include:

- current and historical business financial statements;
- work-in-progress schedule;
- completed-project history;
- ownership and management information;
- key-person experience;
- contract and solicitation documents;
- project schedule;
- subcontractor and supplier plan;
- financing and working-capital sources;
- bank relationship or credit information;
- existing bonded backlog;
- claims, defaults, or disputes that require explanation; and
- realistic cost estimates and contingency assumptions.

Exact documents depend on the surety, bond type, program path, contract size, and business circumstances.

## Credit

Credit review considers whether the business has the financial reliability to support the obligation.

Readiness actions include:

- keep business financial statements current and internally consistent;
- reconcile major liabilities and contingent obligations;
- explain unusual losses, arrears, or one-time events;
- understand existing borrowing and collateral commitments;
- correct known reporting errors before a critical application; and
- avoid taking on hidden short-term obligations that undermine the financial picture presented to the surety.

Do not manipulate timing or classification merely to make the business appear stronger. Surety underwriting depends on credible information.

## Capacity

Capacity is not only revenue size. The business needs to show that it can perform the particular contract while handling its existing backlog.

Review:

- technical experience with comparable work;
- staffing and supervision;
- equipment and facilities;
- subcontractor availability;
- supplier capacity;
- project-management controls;
- schedule realism;
- working capital;
- access to contract financing; and
- concentration risk across simultaneous projects.

A profitable-looking contract can still create unacceptable capacity risk if it stretches management, labor, cash, or equipment beyond realistic limits.

## Character and management credibility

Sureties also evaluate the people responsible for performance.

Build credibility through:

- complete and accurate disclosures;
- a consistent performance history;
- timely explanation of prior problems;
- reliable internal controls;
- demonstrated contract-management discipline; and
- prompt communication with the surety when material conditions change.

Attempting to conceal a default, claim, ownership issue, or significant project problem can damage the relationship more than an explainable adverse event.

## Working capital and cash timing

Bonding and financing are related but not identical. A bond guarantees performance or payment obligations; it does not itself provide the cash needed to execute the contract.

Before bidding, model:

1. mobilization costs;
2. payroll timing;
3. supplier and subcontractor payment timing;
4. retainage;
5. customer invoicing and approval delays;
6. change-order exposure;
7. disputed-cost scenarios; and
8. the working-capital gap until collections arrive.

Where external financing is needed, arrange it early enough that an award does not create an immediate liquidity crisis.

## SBA guarantee fee

Current SBA public guidance states that small businesses pay SBA a **0.6% fee of the contract price** for performance and payment bond guarantees.

SBA does **not** charge a guarantee fee for bid bond guarantees.

Bond premiums and other permitted surety/agency costs are separate from the SBA guarantee fee and can vary. Use current quotes rather than treating the SBA fee as the total cost of bonding.

## Quick Bond Guarantee boundary

SBA provides a simplified Quick Bond Guarantee path for certain smaller contracts through participating prior-approval sureties. Current SBA partner guidance describes the quick path for eligible contracts up to **$500,000**, subject to exclusions and required forms.

A contractor should work through an SBA-authorized surety agent to determine which application path applies. Do not assume contract value alone makes a bond eligible for the quick process.

## Bid decision workflow

A reusable pre-bid sequence is:

1. Identify all bond requirements in the solicitation.
2. Confirm contract value against current SBA program limits if an SBA guarantee may be needed.
3. Review size-standard eligibility.
4. Ask an authorized surety agent about likely underwriting requirements before the bid deadline.
5. Model project capacity and working-capital demand.
6. Prepare the financial and work-in-progress package.
7. Identify material risks that must be disclosed or mitigated.
8. Confirm expected bond premium, SBA guarantee fee where applicable, and related financing costs.
9. Obtain the required bid bond or bonding commitment.
10. Reconfirm final performance/payment requirements immediately after award.

Do not submit a bid that assumes required bonding will somehow be solved later when the solicitation makes bonding a condition of award or performance.

## Changes after bond approval

Material project changes can affect the surety's risk.

Escalate changes such as:

- large scope changes;
- contract-price increases;
- major schedule extensions;
- ownership or control changes;
- loss of key personnel;
- significant financial deterioration;
- unexpected litigation or claims;
- major subcontractor failure; or
- substantial increases in bonded backlog.

Confirm whether the bond, SBA guarantee, or surety approval needs amendment before relying on the original approval.

## Evidence to retain

For material bonded contracts, retain where appropriate:

- solicitation bond requirements;
- bond applications;
- underwriting submissions;
- final bonds and riders;
- contract value;
- SBA guarantee information where used;
- bond premiums and guarantee fees;
- work-in-progress schedules;
- financing assumptions;
- surety communications; and
- notices of material project changes.

## Scope note

This article provides general contract-bond readiness guidance. It does not guarantee eligibility, bonding capacity, award of a contract, or SBA approval. Surety underwriting, solicitation requirements, program rules, insurance regulation, and legal obligations can change and should be verified for the specific transaction.
