# Strategy Integrated Reporting Capitals Linkage

Integrated reporting asks an organization to explain how it creates value over time by drawing on and affecting six forms of capital: financial, manufactured, intellectual, human, social and relationship, and natural. For a strategy function, the six-capitals frame is not a reporting chore — it is a discipline for making resource claims explicit. Every strategy consumes capitals in combinations and replenishes or depletes them; the value-creation narrative is the account of those flows and their linkage to outcomes. This article covers how to connect strategy to a capitals-based disclosure: mapping strategy to capital stocks and flows, maintaining the linkage between narrative and measurement, and avoiding the greenwashing-adjacent failure of decorative capitals talk.

## Scope

This article covers the construction of a six-capitals linkage between strategy and integrated-style reporting: capital mapping, flow definition, narrative construction, internal control, and board-level governance. It applies to organizations preparing integrated or sustainability-linked strategy disclosures. It addresses the linkage discipline rather than the accounting recognition questions of any particular reporting standard, which follow the financial reporting and sustainability disclosure governance articles.

## Workflow or implementation guidance

**Map the strategy onto capital stocks and flows.** Take the strategy's principal initiatives and, for each, record which capitals it draws down — financial capital committed, human capital hours and skills, manufactured capacity, relationship capital with partners and regulators — and which capitals it builds or depletes as a consequence of operation, including natural capital effects where material. The output is a capital flow table tied to named initiatives, not to the organization in the abstract. An initiative that consumes relationship capital with regulators while claiming a license-to-operate benefit is exactly the contradiction this mapping exists to surface.

**Define each capital's measurement basis honestly.** Financial and manufactured capitals have established measurement; the others do not, so each requires a stated proxy and its limits: human capital in skills coverage and stability metrics per the human-capital reporting discipline; intellectual capital in protected and codified assets; social and relationship capital in dependency concentration and partner-verified outcomes; natural capital in quantified inputs and emissions with method references. State proxies as proxies. Precision language on soft capitals is the marker of an unreliable narrative.

**Write the value-creation narrative as a causal chain, not a collage.** The narrative states: the business model and its inputs in capital terms; the strategy's allocation choices; the outputs produced; and the outcomes — capital changes — expected over short, medium, and long horizons. Every claim in the narrative must point to a measure in the flow table or to a disclosed judgment with its basis. Sentences that neither rest on measurement nor declare themselves judgments are edited out.

**Link capitals to risk and dependency.** For each material capital dependency — a scarce skill, a watershed, a concentration of relationship capital in one partner — record the depletion scenario, its monitoring indicator, and the mitigation or substitution plan. This converts the capitals frame into strategic risk management rather than descriptive accounting, and it feeds the enterprise risk register with structure.

**Govern the linkage internally like a control.** Assign each capital's measures to an owning function with a named accountable executive. Reconcile the narrative's figures to the underlying systems each cycle. Version the narrative so changes in claims are traceable to changes in evidence, and require that significant negative capital movements appear in the same document that claims the positives — selective narration is the failure this control prevents.

**Connect to board governance.** The board or its designated committee reviews the capitals linkage annually alongside strategy approval, testing whether allocation decisions reflect the stated dependencies — a strategy narrative claiming human capital as core while cutting development spend fails board review on its face.

**Reconcile with external reporting requirements.** Where the organization also reports under mandated sustainability or financial frameworks, map the capitals measures to the mandated disclosures so one underlying dataset serves both, with differences in boundary and basis documented rather than silently diverging.

## Controls

- **Capital flow table with initiative linkage.** No narrative claim without a row: initiative, capitals consumed, capitals affected, measure, owner.
- **Proxy disclosure rule.** Every non-financial capital measure publishes its proxy definition and known limitations.
- **Two-sided narration.** Positive and negative capital movements appear together; omission of a known material negative movement is a reportable defect.
- **Version and variance audit.** Narrative versions are archived; year-over-year claim changes are reconciled to evidence changes.
- **Board review gate.** Annual review tests strategy-allocation consistency with stated capital dependencies, with objections minuted.
- **Boundary reconciliation.** Capitals measures used internally and in external disclosures share sources; boundary differences are documented.

## Validation evidence

Evidence includes: the capital flow table with owners and measures; reconciliation of narrative figures to source systems for sampled claims; the version archive showing traceable change; board minutes recording the review and any inconsistency findings; and the boundary reconciliation notes against mandated disclosures. External validation is assurance where obtained, and internal audit sampling that recomputes published capital measures from underlying records. A linkage that survives audit sampling without claim reversals is the standard of success.

## Failure modes and correction

- **Decorative capitals.** The six labels used as section headers with generic prose. Correct via the flow-table requirement and measure linkage.
- **Proxy precision.** Soft-capital numbers presented with false accuracy. Correct through the proxy disclosure rule.
- **Selective narration.** Reporting built capitals while omitting depleted ones. Correct with the two-sided narration control.
- **Narrative drift.** Claims rewritten annually without evidence linkage. Correct via version and variance audit.
- **Board rubber stamp.** Annual review that never finds inconsistencies. Correct by requiring the review to test at least the three largest capital dependencies against allocation data.

## Limitations

Capitals aggregation risks false commensurability — capitals are not fungible, and summing them implies trade-offs that ethics and law may not permit. Natural and social capital measurement methods remain contested and evolve; narrative stability is limited by method change. The frame is voluntary in many jurisdictions and sits alongside, not instead of, mandated reporting. It improves transparency of strategy's resource claims; it does not by itself improve the strategy.
