# Business Plan Assumption Refresh

## Purpose

A business plan is only useful while its important assumptions still describe the environment in which decisions are being made. Assumption refresh is the disciplined process of reviewing market, pricing, cost, funding, operating, and capacity assumptions when new evidence appears or on a defined review cycle.

The objective is not to rewrite the entire business plan constantly. It is to identify which assumptions materially affect decisions, keep their evidence current, and make changes visible when reality diverges from the plan.

## SBA planning basis

Current U.S. Small Business Administration business-plan guidance describes the plan as a roadmap for structuring, running, and growing a business and notes that marketing strategy should evolve as needs change. SBA financial-projection guidance also recommends explaining projections and matching them to funding requests.

These principles support maintaining a traceable connection between business assumptions, financial projections, and the decisions they inform.

## Assumption register

For material assumptions, record where practical:

- assumption statement;
- business-plan section or decision affected;
- evidence or source used;
- effective date or observation period;
- accountable owner;
- expected range or confidence level;
- trigger for early review;
- dependent forecasts or decisions; and
- date last refreshed.

An assumption should be specific enough to test. "Demand will grow" is less useful than an assumption about expected customer volume, conversion, renewal, price, or market condition over a defined period.

## Assumption categories

Common categories include:

- customer demand and market size;
- pricing and discounting;
- conversion, retention, churn, or repeat purchase;
- sales-cycle timing;
- supplier pricing and availability;
- staffing cost and hiring timing;
- operating expenses;
- capital expenditure;
- working-capital timing;
- collection periods;
- financing availability and cost;
- regulatory or licensing dependencies;
- implementation schedules;
- capacity and utilization; and
- partner or channel performance.

Do not create a register so large that material assumptions become harder to find. Prioritize assumptions capable of changing an important decision.

## Refresh triggers

Review assumptions after events such as:

- actual performance materially misses forecast;
- a significant customer, supplier, or partner changes;
- pricing changes;
- financing terms or availability change;
- major hiring or capacity plans move;
- a product launch is delayed or accelerated;
- regulation, licensing, or market-access conditions change;
- material economic or industry conditions change;
- a major incident or disaster changes operating capacity;
- a new location or acquisition changes the operating model; or
- the evidence supporting an assumption becomes stale or invalid.

A periodic review can supplement event-driven review, especially for assumptions that may drift gradually.

## Refresh workflow

1. **Identify the assumption.** State exactly what is being tested and which decisions depend on it.
2. **Retrieve the current basis.** Locate the evidence, source data, or rationale used previously.
3. **Collect newer evidence.** Prefer current primary or authoritative sources for externally verifiable facts.
4. **Compare assumption with reality.** Determine whether the assumption remains reasonable, needs a narrower range, or should be replaced.
5. **Assess downstream impact.** Identify which forecasts, funding needs, targets, staffing plans, contracts, or risk decisions change.
6. **Update connected models.** Reconcile sales, cost, cash-flow, capital, working-capital, or funding models rather than changing the narrative alone.
7. **Record the change.** Preserve the previous value, new value, reason, evidence, date, and approver where the assumption is material.
8. **Communicate material consequences.** Make sure affected decision owners use the revised assumptions.
9. **Monitor outcome.** Compare later actual results against the refreshed assumption.

## Evidence discipline

Use evidence appropriate to the assumption. Sources can include:

- internal operating history;
- signed contracts or qualified pipeline evidence;
- current supplier quotes;
- current government or regulator data;
- authoritative industry data;
- customer research;
- technical capacity measurements; and
- current financing proposals or commitments.

Distinguish facts from management judgment. A judgment can be legitimate, but it should not be presented as externally verified data.

## Forecast consistency

A changed assumption may affect several models simultaneously. For example:

- lower unit sales may reduce revenue but also variable cost;
- slower collections may leave profit unchanged while worsening cash flow and working-capital need;
- delayed hiring may reduce expense but also constrain delivery capacity;
- higher supplier prices may affect margin, pricing, funding, and break-even point; and
- delayed equipment may move capital spending while also reducing available capacity.

Reconcile connected models so different parts of the plan do not quietly rely on conflicting versions of the same assumption.

## Scenario use

When uncertainty cannot be resolved with better evidence, use ranges or scenarios instead of forcing a false single-point estimate.

A practical scenario set can include:

- downside;
- expected/base; and
- upside.

State which assumptions differ between scenarios. Avoid building a downside case that changes every variable arbitrarily; focus on plausible drivers and the decisions those scenarios are intended to test.

## Governance

Define which assumption changes require formal review. A minor forecast refinement may not need executive approval, while a change that affects funding need, liquidity runway, major capital expenditure, market entry, or customer commitments may require a higher decision authority.

Do not silently overwrite a material assumption that supported an earlier decision. Preserve enough history to explain why the plan changed.

## Evidence to retain

For material assumption refreshes, retain where appropriate:

- prior assumption;
- refreshed assumption;
- evidence used;
- source date;
- owner and reviewer;
- affected models or decisions;
- scenario impact;
- approval or decision record; and
- date for next review or trigger condition.
