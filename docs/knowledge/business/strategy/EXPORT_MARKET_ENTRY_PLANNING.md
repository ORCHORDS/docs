# Export Market Entry Planning

## Purpose

Exporting creates access to new customers but introduces additional market, logistics, payment, regulatory, and financing questions. SBA guidance treats exporting as a planned growth path rather than a simple extension of domestic sales.

## Practical steps

1. Select target markets using demand, competition, distribution, legal, and operational evidence.
2. Confirm the product or service can be sold, delivered, supported, and paid for in the target market.
3. Identify export controls, customs, labeling, documentation, tax, sanctions, and local-market requirements that may apply.
4. Choose a route to market, such as direct sales, distributors, representatives, marketplaces, or local partners, and document responsibilities.
5. Model landed cost, currency, payment terms, insurance, logistics, support, returns, and expected margin.
6. Start with measurable market tests and revise the export plan using actual demand and delivery evidence.

## Source

- U.S. Small Business Administration — Business guide, including export and trade assistance: https://www.sba.gov/counseling/

## Scope note

Export controls and trade requirements vary by product, destination, transaction, and jurisdiction. Specialist legal, customs, tax, or trade advice may be required.
