# Business Insurance Risk Planning

## Purpose

Business insurance is a risk-transfer tool for losses an organization may not be able to absorb itself. Coverage decisions should begin with the actual risks created by the business model, location, assets, products, services, workforce, and legal requirements rather than by renewing the same policies automatically.

## Practice

1. **Assess material risks.** Identify accidents, property loss, liability, interruption, professional errors, product risks, workforce obligations, and location-specific hazards that could create losses the business could not comfortably self-fund.
2. **Separate required and optional coverage.** Determine which insurance is required by applicable law and which coverage is a risk-management choice.
3. **Match coverage to the activity.** Consider the forms of coverage relevant to the business, such as general liability, product liability, professional liability, commercial property, home-based business coverage, or a business owner's policy.
4. **Compare terms, not only premiums.** Review limits, deductibles, exclusions, covered events, and policy conditions when comparing offers.
5. **Use qualified assistance where useful.** A licensed commercial insurance professional can help identify gaps, but the organization should still understand the assumptions behind the recommendation.
6. **Reassess after change.** Revisit insurance needs after material changes in assets, locations, products, services, staffing, contracts, or risk exposure.

Insurance requirements and available products vary by jurisdiction and business activity. This article is general planning guidance and is not legal, tax, or insurance advice.
