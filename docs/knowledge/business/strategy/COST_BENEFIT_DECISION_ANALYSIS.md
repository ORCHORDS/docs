# Cost-Benefit Decision Analysis

## Purpose

Cost-benefit analysis provides a repeatable way to compare significant business decisions instead of relying only on intuition or headline price.

## Analysis pattern

1. Define the decision and realistic alternatives, including doing nothing.
2. List one-time and recurring costs for each option.
3. Identify expected benefits, savings, revenue effects, risk reductions, and operational improvements.
4. Separate measurable financial effects from qualitative factors that still matter.
5. State assumptions, time horizon, dependencies, and uncertainty.
6. Test sensitivity to the assumptions most likely to change.
7. Record the preferred option and why it provides the best overall value.
8. After implementation, compare actual outcomes with the original assumptions.

## Common omissions

Include implementation effort, training, migration, support, maintenance, switching costs, downtime risk, and exit costs where relevant. A cheap acquisition can become an expensive lifecycle decision.

## Boundary

Cost-benefit analysis supports judgment; it does not remove the need to consider legal, security, safety, accessibility, or strategic constraints that may outweigh a financial result.
