# Break-Even Analysis

## Purpose

Break-even analysis estimates the sales level at which total revenue equals total cost. It is a planning tool for understanding the relationship between price, fixed costs, variable costs, and sales volume.

## Core formulas

For a single product or service, the U.S. Small Business Administration presents the unit break-even formula as:

`Break-even units = Fixed costs / (Selling price per unit - Variable cost per unit)`

The difference between selling price and variable cost per unit is the unit contribution toward fixed costs and profit.

A sales-value approach can use contribution margin:

`Break-even sales = Fixed costs / Contribution margin ratio`

## Classify costs carefully

- **Fixed costs** generally do not change with production or service volume over the analysis period.
- **Variable costs** change with production or sales volume.
- **Semi-variable costs** include both fixed and variable components and should be separated where practical.

Misclassifying costs can materially distort the result.

## Use the result as an estimate

Break-even analysis can support pricing, revenue targets, cost reviews, business plans, and funding discussions. It should not be treated as a guarantee of profitability. Real results can differ because of product mix, discounts, taxes, demand changes, step costs, capacity constraints, and forecast error.

## Practical workflow

1. Define the period and product or service being analyzed.
2. Identify total fixed costs for that period.
3. Estimate selling price and variable cost per unit on consistent assumptions.
4. Calculate the break-even point.
5. Stress-test the result using plausible changes in price, volume, and cost.
6. Recalculate when material assumptions change.
