# SBA 504 Fixed-Asset Financing Readiness

## Purpose

The U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA) **504 loan program** provides long-term, fixed-rate financing for eligible major fixed assets that support business growth and job creation. Current SBA public guidance lists a maximum 504 loan amount of **$5.5 million** and explains that Certified Development Companies (CDCs) originate 504 financing in collaboration with a senior lender.

504 financing is designed for fixed-asset projects rather than general operating liquidity. Readiness therefore starts with a clear project, eligible assets, credible costs, borrower contribution, collateral, and repayment capacity.

## Program boundary

Current SBA guidance describes common 504 uses around major fixed assets, such as eligible real estate, buildings, renovations, and long-lived equipment.

The program is not a general-purpose working-capital line. Separate project costs into:

- fixed assets potentially eligible for 504 financing;
- working capital;
- inventory;
- operating expenses;
- acquisition goodwill/intangibles; and
- other costs that may need a different financing source.

Confirm current eligible and ineligible uses with a CDC before signing binding project commitments.

## Financing parties

A typical 504 project involves multiple parties:

- the borrower;
- a Certified Development Company;
- a senior/private-sector lender;
- SBA-backed 504 financing; and
- the borrower's required contribution.

The exact percentages and contribution requirements depend on the project and current program rules. Do not assume a standard structure applies unchanged to every startup, special-purpose property, or transaction.

## Project definition

Prepare one complete project budget before approaching financing sources.

Include where applicable:

- property purchase;
- eligible construction or renovation;
- equipment;
- professional/closing costs eligible under current policy;
- contingency;
- interim financing costs;
- borrower contribution; and
- non-504 costs funded separately.

All financing parties should be working from the same total-project definition.

## Real-estate readiness

For real-estate projects, prepare for issues such as:

- purchase contract;
- appraisal;
- title;
- survey;
- zoning;
- environmental review;
- occupancy/use requirements;
- insurance;
- construction scope; and
- permits.

Resolve material site or legal issues early because a financing approval cannot cure an ineligible or unusable property.

## Equipment readiness

For equipment-heavy projects, maintain:

- vendor quotations;
- equipment description;
- purchase price;
- installation costs;
- useful-life assumptions;
- serial/asset identification where available;
- delivery schedule; and
- expected productivity/capacity impact.

Match loan maturity and project economics to the useful life and business value of the assets.

## Borrower contribution

Plan the required borrower injection well before closing.

Evidence can include:

- bank statements;
- documented owner equity;
- eligible project deposits already paid;
- approved subordinate financing where allowed; and
- other sources accepted under current program rules.

Do not count the same funds as both working-capital reserves and required project equity.

## Interim financing

Some 504 projects require interim financing before the SBA-backed debenture component is funded.

A closing plan should identify:

1. when project costs must be paid;
2. who provides interim funds;
3. what conditions must be satisfied for final 504 funding;
4. interest/carrying costs during the interim period;
5. risk if final funding is delayed; and
6. how liens transition at permanent closing.

Borrowers should understand the complete financing sequence, not only the final fixed rate.

## Debt-service capacity

Long-term fixed rates improve payment predictability but do not eliminate credit risk.

Model combined debt service across:

- senior lender debt;
- 504/SBA-backed component;
- existing business debt;
- equipment or vehicle financing;
- leases; and
- other material obligations.

Stress-test lower revenue, margin compression, occupancy delays, and project cost overruns.

## Occupancy and business-use planning

504 eligibility can depend on how the business uses the financed property.

Before closing:

- document current and planned occupancy;
- separate owner-occupied business use from investment/rental use;
- identify affiliates or related-party occupants;
- review leases; and
- confirm current SBA occupancy rules with the CDC.

Do not assume any commercially zoned property qualifies simply because the borrower operates a business.

## Construction and renovation controls

For construction projects, maintain:

- approved budget;
- plans/specifications;
- contractor information;
- schedule;
- change orders;
- draw records;
- inspections;
- cost-to-complete; and
- contingency.

A major scope or cost change can affect appraisal, financing structure, borrower contribution, and approval.

## 504 versus 7(a)

The programs overlap in some fixed-asset financing use cases but are structurally different.

Compare:

- project purpose;
- fixed-asset versus working-capital need;
- loan amount;
- term/rate structure;
- collateral;
- borrower contribution;
- closing timeline; and
- need for multiple financing parties.

As of July 4, 2026, SBA's cumulative-limit policy allows qualified borrowers to coordinate 7(a) and 504 financing up to the current combined ceiling under applicable rules. This can support projects where long-term assets and operating/working-capital needs require different financing components.

## Current 504 amount boundary

SBA's current general 504 page states a maximum 504 loan amount of **$5.5 million**.

Certain energy/manufacturing or other qualifying situations can have special project rules. Verify current SBA policy rather than applying a generic $5.5 million assumption to every transaction.

## Lender/CDCs readiness package

A useful package can include:

- business plan or project rationale;
- historical/interim financial statements;
- tax information required by lenders;
- debt schedule;
- project sources and uses;
- purchase contract;
- appraisal/property information;
- environmental information;
- construction budget where applicable;
- equipment quotes;
- ownership/entity documents;
- borrower contribution evidence; and
- cash-flow projections.

Exact requirements vary by transaction and CDC/lender.

## Closing coordination

Track:

- approval conditions;
- senior-lender commitment;
- CDC/SBA requirements;
- appraisal and environmental completion;
- borrower equity;
- insurance;
- title/lien documents;
- construction/interim funding; and
- expected permanent funding date.

A missed condition can delay project closing even when the business itself remains creditworthy.

## Evidence to retain

For a material 504 project, retain where appropriate:

- project budget;
- lender/CDCs approvals;
- loan documents;
- appraisal;
- environmental/property records;
- borrower contribution evidence;
- closing statement;
- equipment/real-estate invoices;
- construction draws and change orders; and
- final project reconciliation.

## Scope note

This article provides general 504 financing-readiness guidance from current SBA public information. It does not guarantee eligibility, a $5.5 million loan, a particular contribution percentage, project approval, or closing. Current SBA policy, CDC review, senior-lender underwriting, and transaction-specific requirements control the actual financing.
