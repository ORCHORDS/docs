# Export Express Financing Readiness

## Purpose

The U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA) **Export Express** delivery method provides smaller SBA-backed financing for eligible businesses that need capital related to export development or international sales. Current SBA trade-finance guidance states that Export Express loans can be up to **$500,000** and that participating Export Express lenders can directly underwrite loans without obtaining prior SBA approval, supporting faster lender-driven processing.

Export Express can fit smaller export-market needs that do not require the larger Export Working Capital or International Trade structures.

## Current public boundary

Current SBA export guidance identifies Export Express as one of SBA's export finance programs and states:

- maximum loan amount: **$500,000**;
- participating lenders can directly underwrite under delegated authority; and
- financing should support eligible export-development or international-business purposes under current program rules.

Verify the current SBA lender/SOP terms before relying on any guaranty percentage, maturity, fee, or use of proceeds.

## Export purpose

A borrower should be able to explain how the financing supports export activity.

Examples of planning needs can include:

- export market development;
- trade-show participation;
- export-related working capital;
- equipment or capacity that supports international sales;
- translation/localization;
- export inventory;
- foreign-sales travel; or
- other eligible export-related purposes under current policy.

Do not assume any international expense automatically qualifies.

## Export plan linkage

Connect the financing request to an export plan that identifies:

1. target market;
2. product/service;
3. customer or distribution channel;
4. pricing;
5. payment terms;
6. logistics;
7. compliance/export-control requirements;
8. expected timeline; and
9. cash-flow impact.

The financing should solve a specific constraint in the export plan.

## Smaller-ticket positioning

Export Express can be useful where the needed amount is below the program's current $500,000 maximum and the business values delegated lender processing.

If the business needs substantially more export working capital, compare:

- Export Working Capital Program (EWCP);
- Working Capital Pilot (WCP); or
- International Trade Loan (ITL).

Program choice should match the use, amount, cash cycle, and asset/project structure.

## Working-capital readiness

For export working capital, model:

- supplier prepayments;
- production lead time;
- freight;
- customs/documentation;
- foreign-buyer payment terms;
- receivable collection;
- currency exposure; and
- delay scenarios.

International transactions can have longer cash cycles than domestic sales.

## Payment risk

Before financing a foreign sale, understand how the business will be paid.

Evaluate:

- cash in advance;
- letter of credit;
- documentary collection;
- open account;
- credit insurance; and
- other agreed payment methods.

A loan finances the business; it does not eliminate buyer or country payment risk.

## Compliance boundary

Financing approval does not authorize an export.

Separately evaluate:

- export controls;
- sanctions;
- restricted parties;
- customs/documentation;
- product standards;
- tax/VAT; and
- local-market legal requirements.

Use appropriate legal/trade expertise for regulated products or destinations.

## Lender-readiness package

A useful package can include:

- historical/interim financial statements;
- tax information requested by lender;
- debt schedule;
- export plan;
- customer/order evidence where available;
- use-of-proceeds schedule;
- cash-flow forecast;
- ownership information; and
- export experience/management background.

Exact requirements vary by lender.

## Downside scenarios

Model:

- buyer delay;
- canceled order;
- freight increase;
- currency movement;
- customs delay;
- export-license delay;
- returned goods; and
- customer default.

The business should remain able to service debt if the first export cycle is slower than planned.

## Evidence to retain

Retain where appropriate:

- application/loan documents;
- export-purpose analysis;
- customer/order information;
- use-of-proceeds records;
- export documentation;
- draw/payment history; and
- material amendments.

## Scope note

This article provides general Export Express readiness guidance from current SBA public information. It does not guarantee eligibility, a $500,000 loan, delegated approval, export legality, a particular guaranty percentage, or closing speed. Current SBA policy and participating lender underwriting control the actual financing.
