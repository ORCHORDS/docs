# Business Valuation Approaches

## Purpose

Business valuation provides a reasoned estimate of a business's monetary value for decisions such as a sale, acquisition, ownership transfer, or financing discussion. It is a decision input, not a guarantee of the price a buyer will pay.

## Common approaches

The U.S. Small Business Administration describes three common high-level approaches when preparing to sell a business:

1. **Income approach** — considers projected revenue together with the risks affecting those projections.
2. **Market approach** — compares the business with similar businesses that have recently sold.
3. **Assets approach** — subtracts total liabilities from the value of the business's assets.

The SBA also notes that valuation may need to account for intangible assets such as brand presence, intellectual property, customer information, and future-revenue potential.

## Practical workflow

1. Define the valuation purpose, effective date, and ownership interest being considered.
2. Inventory material assets and liabilities, including relevant intangible assets.
3. Assemble traceable financial inputs and document the source period for each figure.
4. Select an approach that fits the business economics and the quality of available evidence rather than forcing every method onto every business.
5. Where more than one approach is reasonable, compare the results and investigate large differences instead of averaging them mechanically.
6. Record assumptions, adjustments, comparable-company selection criteria, and material uncertainties.
7. Use sensitivity analysis for assumptions that materially change the result, such as growth, margins, risk, or asset values.
8. Obtain qualified valuation, accounting, tax, or legal advice when the estimate will support a real transaction or regulated filing.

## Evidence discipline

Do not treat unsupported forecasts, unverified comparables, or undocumented asset values as objective facts. A useful valuation record distinguishes observed data from assumptions and explains why the chosen method is appropriate for the decision.
