# Business Plan Structure

## Purpose

A business plan is a structured way to describe what a business does, the market it serves, how it operates, and how it expects to sustain itself. The format should match the decision it needs to support rather than follow a template mechanically.

## Two common formats

The U.S. Small Business Administration describes two common approaches:

- **Traditional plans** are detailed and commonly used when a lender, investor, or other stakeholder expects a comprehensive document.
- **Lean startup plans** summarize the most important elements and are useful when the business model is simpler or expected to evolve frequently.

There is no single required structure for every business. Use the sections that support the intended audience and decision.

## Common traditional sections

A comprehensive plan commonly covers:

1. **Executive summary** — what the business is, what it offers, and the high-level case for viability.
2. **Company description** — the problem being solved, customers served, and relevant advantages.
3. **Market analysis** — industry conditions, target market, trends, and competitive research.
4. **Organization and management** — structure, responsibilities, and relevant leadership capabilities.
5. **Product or service line** — what is sold, customer benefit, lifecycle considerations, and relevant intellectual property or research activity.
6. **Marketing and sales** — how customers are reached, acquired, retained, and converted.
7. **Funding request** — if applicable, the amount, purpose, timing, and form of financing sought.
8. **Financial projections** — assumptions and forward-looking financial information appropriate to the audience.
9. **Appendix** — supporting material that would distract from the main narrative.

## Planning discipline

- Link projections to explicit assumptions.
- Keep market claims traceable to sources.
- Distinguish committed facts from intentions and scenarios.
- Revise the plan when material assumptions change.
- Avoid treating the plan as proof that the business will succeed; it is a decision-support document.
