# Strategy Value Proposition Assumption Evidence

A value proposition is a stack of untested claims: that the target buyer experiences the problem, that the proposed mechanism removes it, that the buyer will pay the implied price, and that they will switch from whatever they do today. Each claim is an assumption with an evidence state — and until evidence accrues, the proposition is a hypothesis, not a fact. This article establishes the assumption register for value propositions: how to decompose the proposition into falsifiable claims, track each claim's evidence state, and attach kill criteria so that a disproven assumption retires the proposition instead of accumulating patches.

## Scope

This article covers the construction and maintenance of a value-proposition assumption register for new offerings and material revisions to existing ones. It addresses claim decomposition, evidence-state classification, evidence acquisition sequencing, and kill criteria. It applies to business-to-business and business-to-business-to-consumer propositions as well as direct consumer ones. It does not cover pricing mechanics, conjoint analysis procedure, or market sizing, which are governed by separate articles, and it does not replace regulatory substantiation obligations for objective product claims, which follow the applicable consumer-protection and advertising rules.

## Workflow or implementation guidance

**Decompose the proposition into atomic claims.** Take the one-sentence proposition — for whom, what pain, what mechanism, what measurable benefit, versus which alternative — and split it into separately falsifiable assumptions. A workable set covers: problem existence and frequency for the named segment; the segment's current workaround and its cost; causal efficacy of the proposed mechanism; perceptibility of the benefit to the buyer; willingness to pay at the implied price point; switching friction; and deliverability at target unit cost. Each assumption gets an identifier, an owner, and a written statement of what would make it false.

**Assign an evidence state to each assumption.** Use a fixed scale and resist grade inflation: hypothesis — no relevant evidence; anecdote — unsolicited or exploratory signals only; indirect evidence — adjacent markets, proxies, or archival data; direct evidence — structured observation of the target segment, such as measured behavior in pilots or paid tests; corroborated — at least two independent direct sources agreeing. State the date the evidence was obtained, because stale evidence reverts one level at each review.

**Sequence evidence acquisition by cost of being wrong.** Rank assumptions by the product of their financial exposure and their current weakness. The assumptions that should be tested first are those where being wrong kills the economics and where a cheap, fast test exists. Willingness to pay is chronically under-tested because survey answers are comfortable; it requires transactional evidence — deposits, letters of intent with prices, or paid pilots — to reach direct status.

**Pre-register the test before running it.** For each planned test, record the assumption, the method, the sample, the success threshold, and the decision rule before data collection begins. Pre-registration is the only reliable defense against post-hoc rationalization, in which a failed result is reframed as a success on a different metric nobody wrote down in advance.

**Attach kill criteria to the proposition as a whole.** Define in advance the combinations that stop or pivot the work: for example, problem frequency below a stated floor in the segment, or willingness to pay below the deliverability cost for two consecutive structured tests. Kills are recorded with what was learned, and the learning is folded into successor propositions rather than discarded.

**Review on a fixed cadence.** A monthly register review updates evidence states, downgrades stale entries, and flags assumptions whose evidence has not advanced for two cycles — a stalled assumption is a silent risk. Any proposition carrying zero corroborated assumptions beyond a defined age must either commission its next test or be parked.

## Controls

- **Register completeness.** A proposition cannot enter development planning without all seven assumption classes populated, even if every state is hypothesis.
- **Evidence-grade discipline.** Evidence-state upgrades require a citation to a dated artifact — transcript, dataset, test log — reviewed by someone other than the proposition's sponsor.
- **Pre-registration gate.** Tests lacking a written threshold and decision rule before launch produce evidence capped at anecdote grade.
- **Staleness rule.** Evidence older than a stated period, set per market velocity, automatically reverts one grade until refreshed.
- **Kill authority.** The authority to declare kill criteria met sits with a review role independent of the proposition team; the team may argue, but cannot veto.
- **Change log.** Any redefinition of an assumption — rewording, segment shift, threshold change — is versioned; evidence attached to the old wording does not carry over silently.

## Validation evidence

Evidence the register is functioning includes: registers with dated evidence citations and grades for each assumption; pre-registration records preceding their test dates; review minutes showing downgrades and stalled-assumption flags; and a kill log with recorded learnings. Periodic audit should sample upgraded assumptions and confirm the underlying artifact exists and supports the grade claimed. A healthy history includes at least some propositions killed or pivoted on evidence — a register in which everything always passes suggests grades are decorative rather than disciplined.

## Failure modes and correction

- **Survey-grade inflation.** Treating stated intent as willingness to pay. Correct by requiring transactional evidence for pricing assumptions to reach direct grade.
- **Friendly-sample capture.** Tests run on existing champions. Correct by sampling rules requiring disinterested or new buyers for grade-earning tests.
- **Moving-target assumptions.** Redefining the segment or benefit after weak results. Correct through versioning and the change log.
- **Zombie propositions.** Low evidence states persisting indefinitely because no one commissions tests. Correct with the stalled-assumption rule and park-or-test decisions.
- **Post-hoc success reframing.** New success metrics invented after data arrives. Correct via pre-registration.

## Limitations

Evidence grades compress heterogeneous realities: a structured pilot in one city and a national launch differ in more than grade. Behavioral tests alter the environment they measure, and early adopters over-represent enthusiasm. Assumptions interact — a validated problem plus a validated mechanism can still fail on switching friction in combination — so per-assumption passing does not prove the whole proposition. The register disciplines belief; it does not substitute for the eventual market verdict.
