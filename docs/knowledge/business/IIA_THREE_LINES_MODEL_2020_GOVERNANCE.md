# IIA Three Lines Model 2020 Governance

## Purpose

Govern the application of the IIA's Three Lines Model (2020 update) so that roles and responsibilities for risk management and control are structured clearly: management owns and manages risk (first and second line roles), internal audit provides independent assurance (third line), and the governing body oversees the whole — with the model applied as an organizing principle rather than a bureaucratic department map.

## Scope

Applies to the studio's organizational structure for risk, control, and assurance functions. Covers role allocation across the model's lines, the governing body's oversight role, and external assurance's relationship to the model. Does not prescribe specific risk frameworks (ISO 31000 governs risk method) or audit technique.

## Workflow

1. Map the studio's functions to the model: first line (functions that own and manage risk in daily operations), second line (functions providing expertise, support, monitoring, and challenge — risk, compliance, security), third line (internal audit providing independent, objective assurance).
2. Assign the governing body's oversight: approving risk appetite and structure, overseeing the model's implementation, and holding accountability for effective risk management — recorded in governance charters.
3. Protect third-line independence: internal audit reports to the governing body (or its audit committee) functionally, with unrestricted scope and access; organizational reporting arrangements that compromise independence are flagged at governance level.
4. Define second-line challenge explicitly: second-line functions set frameworks and monitor but do not own first-line risks; where second line takes over risk decisions, ownership blurs and the model degrades.
5. Position external assurance (external audit, regulators, certification bodies) relative to the model: they complement but do not replace the third line.
6. Review the mapping when the organization changes: new functions, restructures, or outsourced processes re-enter the map with their line assignment and rationale.
7. Report model health: assurance coverage across the three lines (what the first line self-assesses, the second line monitors, the third line audits) is summarized for the governing body on a defined cadence.

## Controls and evidence

- Function-to-line mapping document with rationale per function.
- Governance charters recording oversight roles and risk appetite approval.
- Internal audit charter with functional reporting line and unrestricted access.
- Assurance coverage summary across the three lines.
- Mapping review records triggered by organizational change.

## Validation

- Confirm internal audit's functional reporting line reaches the governing body without executive filtration.
- Sample three functions and confirm their line assignment matches their actual risk responsibilities (not just the org chart label).
- Confirm the assurance coverage summary identifies gaps — areas with no second-line monitoring or third-line audit — with remediation plans.

## Failure correction

- **Second line making first-line risk decisions** → return decision ownership to the first line, redefine the second-line role, and record the boundary.
- **Independence breach attempt (audit scope restriction)** → escalate to the governing body per the audit charter; the attempt itself is a finding.
- **Unmapped new function** → assign a line with rationale at creation, not after its risks materialize.

## Limitations

- The model organizes roles; it does not make controls effective — each line still executes its own discipline.
- Small organizations combine roles across lines; the independence requirement for the third line is the last compromise acceptable, with compensating governance attention.
- The 2020 update reframed the model (structures replaced by roles, external assurance positioned separately); older "three lines of defense" literature describes the prior framing.
