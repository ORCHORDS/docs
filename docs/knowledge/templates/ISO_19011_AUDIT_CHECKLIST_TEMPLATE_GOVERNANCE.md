# ISO 19011 Audit Checklist Template Governance

## Purpose

ISO 19011:2018 (under revision as ISO 19011:2026 in DIS stage), *Guidelines for auditing management systems*, defines a six-stage audit process (initiating, preparing, conducting, reporting, completing, follow-up) and provides guidance on audit-program management, auditor competence, and audit evidence evaluation. A reusable audit checklist template records, for each audit objective, the audit criterion (clause or control), the evidence requested, the evidence observed, the audit finding (conformity, minor nonconformity, major nonconformity, opportunity for improvement), and the cited evidence reference. The template converts a recurring audit activity into a reproducible artifact suitable for surveillance audits, certification audits, and internal compliance reviews.

The template must remain generic: it MUST NOT embed real auditee names, audit team members, customer identifiers, or proprietary audit findings.

## Scope

This template applies to management-system audits (ISO 9001, ISO 14001, ISO/IEC 27001, ISO 45001, ISO 22301, ISO/IEC 42001, and others that follow the ISO management-system structure). It does not replace the formal audit report required by the certification body; it produces the per-clause working notes that feed into that report. The template does not address financial-statement auditing (governed by IAASB ISAs) or compliance auditing (governed by IIA Standards), which have separate professional and reporting conventions.

## Workflow

1. Open the template and complete the header with the audit identifier, the management system(s) being audited, the audit type (internal, second-party supplier, third-party certification), the audit dates, the lead auditor, and the audit team.
2. For each clause or control to be audited, populate:
   - Reference citation: ISO clause, control identifier, or standard section.
   - Audit objective: what the audit intends to verify.
   - Evidence requested: documents, records, interviews, observations.
   - Evidence observed: documents reviewed, records sampled, people interviewed, activities observed.
   - Audit finding: conformity, minor nonconformity, major nonconformity, observation, or opportunity for improvement.
   - Cited evidence reference: document identifier, record identifier, or interview note identifier.
3. Document the audit evidence sampling method (random, judgmental, risk-based) and sample size.
4. Document any deviations from the audit plan with justification.
5. Identify follow-up actions for nonconformities, including corrective action owner and target date.
6. Save the completed checklist alongside the audit report, with access restricted to the audit team, the auditee's management representative, and the certification body (for third-party audits).

## Controls and evidence

- Header records audit identifier, type, dates, lead auditor, and team.
- Per-clause rows record criterion, objective, evidence, finding, and evidence reference.
- Sampling method and size documented.
- Deviation log records any deviations from the audit plan.
- Follow-up section lists nonconformities with owners and target dates.

## Validation

- Every clause or control planned is addressed; no audit point is left blank without a documented justification.
- Every nonconformity has a corrective action owner and target date.
- Evidence references are precise (document identifier and section, record date, or interview note).
- The audit team signs off on the checklist before the closing meeting.
- The auditee's management representative signs off on the findings before report finalization.

## Failure correction

Common defects include leaving audit points blank, recording findings without cited evidence, omitting the sampling method, and recording nonconformities without owners. Corrective actions include restoring the evidence-citation discipline, requiring sampling rationale in the audit plan, and routing nonconformities into the corrective-action system before report finalization.

## Limitations

- The template does not substitute for the formal audit report.
- It does not address the certification body's specific reporting templates; those templates must be used in parallel.
- It does not address remote-auditing techniques (for example video walkthroughs), which require additional documentation.
- It does not cover the auditor competence assessment required by ISO 19011 §7; a separate competence matrix is required.

## Scope note

This template is part of the **templates** leaf. Sibling leaves cover: **standards** (the underlying management-system standards), **security** (audit findings for security controls), **business** (supplier audits and second-party reviews), and **operations** (corrective action records). The template should be used together with those sibling-leaf articles.
