# ISO 30301 Records Management System Governance

## Purpose

ISO 30301 ("Information and documentation — Records management systems — Requirements and guidance") specifies the requirements for a records management system (RMS) within an organisation. It adopts the harmonised structure of ISO's management-system standards (the so-called Annex SL high-level structure) so that records management can be integrated with other management systems such as quality, information security, and business continuity.

This article provides a public, project-neutral method for designing, operating, and auditing a records management system that satisfies the requirements of ISO 30301:2019. It does not certify any specific organisation; conformity is determined by accredited certification bodies against the standard's normative text.

## Scope

The standard applies to any organisation that wishes to establish, maintain, and improve a records management system, regardless of its type, size, or the nature of its records. The scope of this article covers:

- the high-level structure that makes an RMS auditable and certifiable alongside other ISO management systems;
- the relationship between organisational context, leadership commitment, planning, support, operation, performance evaluation, and improvement;
- the records-related policy and objectives that align with business strategy and applicable obligations; and
- the documented controls and retained records that demonstrate conformity.

ISO 30301 is grounded in ISO 15489-1, and an RMS following ISO 30301 is expected to apply the principles of records management defined therein.

## Workflow

ISO 30301 organises the RMS into a recurring cycle whose chapters mirror the management-system structure of ISO's higher-level standards. The workflow below summarises the chapters and the controls each generates.

1. **Context of the organisation.** Determine the scope of the RMS, the internal and external issues that affect it, and the needs and expectations of interested parties. Records-related requirements from regulators, courts, customers, and other stakeholders are recorded as part of this analysis.
2. **Leadership.** Top management demonstrates leadership by ensuring the records policy and objectives are established, communicating the importance of conformance, and assigning responsibility and authority for the RMS.
3. **Planning.** Identify records-related risks and opportunities; set measurable records objectives; and plan actions to address them. Planning outputs include target values, responsibilities, resources, timelines, and evaluation methods.
4. **Support.** Provide the resources, competence, awareness, communication, and documented information needed for the RMS. Documented information required by the standard is created, updated, and controlled as evidence.
5. **Operation.** Plan, implement, and control the records processes needed to meet records requirements. Operational controls include creation, capture, storage, protection, retrieval, retention, and disposition of records.
6. **Performance evaluation.** Monitor, measure, analyse, and evaluate the RMS; conduct internal audits at planned intervals; and review the system at management review with documented inputs and outputs.
7. **Improvement.** Address nonconformities with documented corrective action; continually improve the suitability, adequacy, and effectiveness of the RMS.

## Controls and evidence

Documented evidence supporting an ISO 30301 RMS includes:

- a formal records-management policy approved by top management, with publication, communication, and review records;
- the scope statement of the RMS, supported by context analysis and interested-party requirements;
- records-related objectives and the plans to achieve them;
- records of competence, training, and awareness for personnel with records responsibilities;
- controlled documented information, including retention and disposition schedules and processes;
- operational records of records-related activities and their outcomes;
- internal audit programme, audit reports, and follow-up actions; and
- management review minutes, including required inputs (status of previous actions, evaluation of compliance, audit results, stakeholder feedback, risks, opportunities) and decisions.

## Validation

Validation that the RMS conforms to ISO 30301 should rely on:

- internal audits conducted by personnel independent of the area being audited;
- second-party audits by external parties where contracts impose obligations;
- accredited third-party certification audits where recognised attestation is sought;
- management review with documented inputs, outputs, and action tracking; and
- cross-management-system integration audits where the RMS is implemented alongside information security, quality, or business-continuity management systems.

## Failure correction

Failure modes commonly associated with implementing ISO 30301 include:

- **Scope defined by tool, not by organisational context.** The corrective action is to revisit context analysis, republish the scope, and reassess which records fall within the RMS.
- **Policy endorsed but not communicated.** The corrective action is to verify comprehension through documented awareness activities and to record the method and outcome of communication.
- **Objectives not measured.** The corrective action is to set measurable objectives with evidence of monitoring and to remove unmeasurable objectives from the policy.
- **Audit findings without closure.** The corrective action is to track findings to corrective-action completion with verified effectiveness.
- **Hold-and-release failures.** Legal holds are inconsistently applied. The corrective action is to centralise hold management and integrate it with disposition automation.

## Limitations

ISO 30301 establishes what an organisation must do to manage records; it does not determine how records are stored, addressed, or formatted. Decisions about repositories, indexing, archival formats (for example, PDF/A, ISO 18923-preserved microforms), and access-control mechanisms remain operational. Certification against ISO 30301 attests that the system is documented, implemented, and maintained; it does not attest to the legal admissibility of any specific record.
