# ISO 15489 Records Management Governance

## Purpose

Records management establishes the policies, processes, and controls needed to create, capture, organise, use, retain, and dispose of records in a way that supports business continuity, accountability, regulatory compliance, and the evidentiary value of organisational memory. ISO 15489 is the international standard that defines records management as a discipline distinct from general information management and document control.

This article provides a public, project-neutral method for adopting the records-management controls described in ISO 15489-1:2016 ("Information and documentation — Records management — Part 1: Concepts and principles") and the implementation guidance in ISO 15489-2:2018 ("Part 2: Guidance on the application of ISO 15489-1"). It does not certify any organisation and does not replace the published standard's normative text.

## Scope

The standard applies to any organisation that creates or receives records in the course of its business. Records are defined as information created, received, and maintained as evidence and as an asset by an organisation in pursuance of legal obligations or in the course of conducting business. The scope of this article covers:

- the distinction between records, documents, and information;
- the policy and accountability framework that binds records management to organisational risk;
- the business-driven process of designing records controls before systems or repositories are procured;
- the metadata needed to make records authentic, reliable, usable, and integral across their lifecycle;
- the retention, disposition, and disposition authorisation controls that close the lifecycle; and
- the limits of the standard: ISO 15489 is a management-system and process standard, not a technical repository specification.

## Workflow

Records management is treated as a strategic business function with a recurring lifecycle. The workflow below distils the principles of ISO 15489-1 into a sequence that can be audited and reproduced.

1. **Establish accountability and policy.** Top management assigns records-management responsibilities to a designated role with sufficient authority, visibility, and resources. A records-management policy is approved by leadership, communicated across the organisation, and reviewed at defined intervals.
2. **Identify records requirements from business context.** Records requirements are derived from business activities, regulatory obligations, contractual commitments, and the expectations of stakeholders — not from the features of any tool or repository.
3. **Assign responsibilities and skills.** Each records class is assigned a custodian, an owner, and rules of access. Personnel handling records receive training appropriate to their records responsibilities.
4. **Design records controls before procurement.** Storage, retrieval, retention, disposition, and protection controls are specified prior to the selection of any system. The integrity, authenticity, and usability of records must be preserved across the lifecycle and across technology changes.
5. **Capture and classify records.** Records are captured into the records system at the point of business activity, classified against a controlled taxonomy, and described with metadata that supports retrieval, retention, and disposition.
6. **Maintain, use, and protect records.** Access controls, audit trails, and integrity checks apply during active use. Records are protected against unauthorised alteration, deletion, or loss for as long as they are retained.
7. **Retain and dispose.** Retention schedules trigger disposition. Disposition actions include transfer of custody, destruction, or archival preservation, and each action must be authorised and audited.
8. **Monitor and audit.** Performance is monitored against records objectives; non-conformities are documented and corrected in a manner consistent with the policy.

## Controls and evidence

ISO 15489-1 lists six characteristics of records that should be controlled: authenticity, reliability, integrity, usability, content, and context. Evidence supporting the demonstration of these characteristics includes:

- a published records-management policy, including scope, objectives, roles, and approved deviations;
- documented records requirements derived from business analysis, including statutory, regulatory, and contractual obligations;
- a controlled records classification schema with version history;
- retention and disposition schedules covering each records class, including triggers, retention periods, disposition actions, and authorisation;
- records-system audit trails that demonstrate who created, modified, accessed, or disposed of a record and when;
- integrity protection measures (for example, cryptographic hashes, write-once storage, or change-controlled environments);
- documented disposition authorities and certificates of destruction or transfer; and
- monitoring and audit reports with corrective actions linked to non-conformities.

## Validation

Confirmation that the records-management programme conforms to ISO 15489 should rely on:

- internal audit against the records-management policy and the standard's principles;
- management review with records of decisions, attendees, and inputs;
- independent certification by accredited certification bodies, where the organisation seeks recognised attestation;
- sampling tests that confirm authentication, reliability, integrity, and usability of records maintained over time;
- legal hold and litigation-readiness exercises that demonstrate records remain retrievable, complete, and unaltered; and
- cross-functional walkthroughs that demonstrate records are actually captured and used in the operations that depend on them.

## Failure correction

Failure modes commonly observed when implementing the standard include:

- **Procurement-led records management.** Records controls specified by the chosen tool rather than by business analysis. The corrective action is to revisit business analysis, republish the records requirements, and revalidate the tool against them.
- **Policy without ownership.** A policy exists but no role is empowered to enforce it. The corrective action is to formally allocate responsibility with the authority and resources necessary to act.
- **Retention without disposition.** Retention schedules exist but disposition is delayed indefinitely. The corrective action is to authorise disposition actions, record certificates, and audit the resulting actions.
- **Metadata decoupled from records.** Records are stored but their metadata is incomplete. The corrective action is to introduce capture-time validation and reject records that lack required metadata.
- **Legal hold ignored.** Active disposition continues despite a legal hold. The corrective action is to maintain a centralised hold register, suspend automated disposition, and preserve evidence of the suspension.

## Limitations

ISO 15489 is a management and process standard. It does not prescribe any specific storage technology, repository architecture, or technical record format. Conformance depends on documented analysis and audit, not on the purchase of any product. Records that exist outside the records system (for example, in unmanaged file shares) are not compliant simply because a policy exists; they must be brought into scope. The standard is technology-neutral and so does not address digital-preservation formats such as PDF/A or ISO 18923; those decisions belong to companion standards and to a separately maintained digital-preservation plan.
