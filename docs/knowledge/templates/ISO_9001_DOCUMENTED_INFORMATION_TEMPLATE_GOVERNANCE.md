# ISO 9001 Documented Information Template Governance

## Purpose

ISO 9001 ("Quality management systems — Requirements") requires organisations to maintain a quality management system and to retain and control "documented information." The standard does not prescribe a document control system or a particular template; instead, it sets requirements about what documented information must do — fit its purpose, be controlled, and be reviewed and approved — and leaves implementation to the organisation. A documented-information template therefore has to encode those requirements so that documents produced under the template are auditable without a separate quality review step.

This article provides a public, project-neutral method for governing a documented-information template under ISO 9001:2015. It does not certify any quality management system or assert conformity for any organisation.

## Scope

The scope covers the documented-information requirements of ISO 9001:2015, including:

- the distinction between documents retained as evidence ("documents that are required to be retained") and documents retained for the operation of the system ("documents required by the standard to be maintained");
- the general control requirements: documents are reviewed and approved for suitability and adequacy, distributed for use, controlled against unintended alterations, and identified, marked, and stored appropriately;
- records as a special class of documented information: objective evidence of conformity and effective operation that must be controlled against loss of integrity and made retrievable;
- the requirements arising from specific clauses (for example, the scope statement, quality policy, quality objectives, audit results, management review, design and development outputs, and nonconformity and corrective-action information); and
- the integration of documented information with the organisation's other management systems through the high-level structure.

## Workflow

Governing a documented-information template requires treating documentation as part of a controlled lifecycle, not as output:

1. **Identify what documented information is required.** Map each ISO 9001 clause that requires documented information to the artefact that satisfies it. The result is a documented-information register listing every required document and record, its owner, its retention rule, and the clause it supports.
2. **Design each document for its purpose.** Decide whether the document is a work instruction, a policy, a plan, an operational procedure, a specification, or a record. Each has a different audience, lifecycle, and approval pattern; one template cannot serve all equally.
3. **Define the metadata that makes control possible.** For each document class, define the required metadata: identifier, version, title, owner, status (draft, approved, withdrawn), reviewer, approver, effective date, and retention rule. Hold the metadata as data so that a document's state can be queried without parsing the document.
4. **Apply review and approval as a gate.** Documents cannot be released in an uncontrolled state. The template records who reviewed, who approved, and when, before the document enters the controlled set.
5. **Control distribution.** Documents must be available where needed and protected against uncontrolled distribution. Where paper and electronic versions both exist, identify the controlled set as authoritative.
6. **Manage changes deliberately.** Changes follow a defined change-control procedure. The template allows changes to be proposed, reviewed for impact on other documents and on the quality management system, and approved by the same authority as the original.
7. **Identify and store records.** Records carry the additional requirement of being protected against unauthorised alteration. The template applies access controls and integrity protection appropriate to each record class.
8. **Retain and dispose.** Documented information is retained for the period required by the standard and by the organisation's retention schedule. Disposition is documented; records that are legally required to be retained are not disposed while the obligation exists.

## Controls and evidence

Evidence that documented-information governance operates correctly includes:

- the documented-information register, listing every required document and record with its owner, retention, and the clause it supports;
- approval records showing who approved each document version and when;
- review records demonstrating periodic reviews of suitability and adequacy;
- distribution records identifying where the controlled copy is available;
- change-control records linking each change to its impact assessment and approval;
- retention and disposition records, including certificates of destruction where applicable; and
- records themselves, protected against unauthorised alteration and retrievable throughout the retention period.

## Validation

A governed documented-information template is validated by:

- conformity checks confirming every ISO 9001 clause that requires documented information is satisfied by a known document with current approval;
- completeness checks confirming each document class contains the required elements;
- traceability checks confirming the documented-information register matches the documents that actually exist;
- integrity checks confirming records are protected against alteration and remain retrievable;
- audit checks confirming internal audits of the quality management system find documents in the correct state; and
- external certification audits, where sought, examining the same evidence.

## Failure correction

Common failure modes in ISO 9001 documented-information governance include:

- **Documents not on the register.** The corrective action is to update the register and the version control in one step, not to publish the document first and catch up later.
- **Approval authority unclear.** The corrective action is to document the approval authority for each document class and to re-approve documents whose authority does not match the rule.
- **Uncontrolled copies in use.** The corrective action is to identify the controlled set, recall uncontrolled copies, and explain how to obtain the current version.
- **Records altered retroactively.** The corrective action is to apply integrity protection, treat the alteration as a nonconformity, and respond with documented corrective action.
- **Documents retained past their schedule.** The corrective action is to route the document through the retention schedule, including authorised disposition, and to record the outcome.

## Limitations

ISO 9001 sets requirements about documented information, not about tooling. The choice of document management system, electronic signatures, and workflow tools is operational and must be justified on its own merits. The standard does not specify retention periods; they are set by the organisation subject to regulation and sector practice. Compliance with ISO 9001's documented-information requirements does not ensure that the quality management system itself is effective: documents can exist in the right state while the underlying processes are broken. The standard is technology-neutral and so does not specify format requirements such as PDF/A; those belong to separate standards if long-term preservation is needed.
