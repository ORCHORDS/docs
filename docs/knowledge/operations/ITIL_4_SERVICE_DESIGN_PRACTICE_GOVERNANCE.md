# ITIL 4 Service Design Practice Governance

## Purpose

ITIL 4, the latest evolution of ITIL, structures service management around the Service Value System (SVS) and 34 management practices. The Service Design practice governs the design of new or changed services for introduction into the live environment. This article governs the application of the ITIL 4 Service Design practice so the organization designs services that meet the organization's objectives, meet quality requirements, and are designed for life-cycle manageability.

## Scope

The Service Design practice applies to the design of any service or significant change to an existing service. Within this knowledge base, the article covers the four dimensions of service design (organizations and people, information and technology, partners and suppliers, value streams and processes), the design activities (requirements gathering, design of value streams, design of the service solution, design of supporting systems), and the documentation of the design. It does not cover the Service Strategy, Service Transition, or other ITIL 4 practices; readers should consult those separately.

## Workflow

1. Establish the design context: the organization's objectives, the service's purpose, the scope, the constraints, the stakeholders, and the value the service is expected to deliver.
2. Apply the four dimensions in design:
   - Organizations and people: who is responsible, who has the skills, what training is needed, and how the design integrates with the organization's culture.
   - Information and technology: the data, the information management, the technology architecture, and the integration points.
   - Partners and suppliers: the external dependencies and the relationships that must be designed or maintained.
   - Value streams and processes: how the service is produced end-to-end, including the steps, the inputs, the outputs, and the interfaces.
3. Design the service solution: components, interfaces, service levels, the quality characteristics (availability, performance, capacity, security, continuity), the operational support, and the cost model.
4. Design the supporting systems: the monitoring, the service management tools, the ticketing, and the reporting.
5. Produce the service design package: requirements, design descriptions, test plans, service acceptance criteria, and the operational documentation.
6. Review and approve the design before the transition stage.

## Controls and evidence

Service Design evidence includes the design context, the four-dimension analysis, the service design package, the review records, and the approval. The design package should be sufficient for the transition stage to produce the service without further design work.

## Validation

Validation should confirm the four dimensions are addressed, the design meets the requirements, the design is approved, the design package is sufficient for transition, and the design aligns with the organization's architecture and operational capabilities. Service acceptance testing confirms the service meets the design.

## Failure correction

Common failure modes: one or more dimensions are ignored (correct: review the design against the four dimensions and address gaps); the design does not address operational support (correct: include monitoring, alerting, and runbooks in the design); the design package is incomplete (correct: enforce completeness before transition); design reviews are nominal (correct: review with concrete acceptance criteria); the design is not aligned with the architecture (correct: review against the architecture standards).

## Limitations

ITIL 4 is a guidance framework, not a management system standard; it does not offer certification of its own. The Service Design practice must be applied with the organization's other management practices. The framework does not prescribe specific design notations or tools.

## Scope note

This article summarizes project-neutral operations use of ITIL 4 Service Design. It does not assert any specific organization's service design conformance or claim any certification outcome.
