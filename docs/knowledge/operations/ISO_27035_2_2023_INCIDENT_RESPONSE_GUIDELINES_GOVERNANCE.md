# ISO/IEC 27035-2:2023 Incident Response Guidelines Governance

## Purpose

ISO/IEC 27035-2:2023, "Information technology — Information security incident management — Part 2: Guidelines to plan and prepare for incident response," updates the supporting guidance for the "plan and prepare" stage of the ISO/IEC 27035 incident management process. The guidance covers the establishment of the information security incident management policy, the assessment of information security incidents and their consequences, the definition of incident response teams (CSIRT), the incident response plan, and the training and awareness. This article governs the application of ISO/IEC 27035-2 so the "plan and prepare" activities of incident management are conducted with the discipline the standard requires.

## Scope

The guidance applies to the "plan and prepare" stage of the incident management process. Within this knowledge base, the article covers the incident management policy, the incident response team structure, the incident response plan, the training and awareness activities, and the documentation of the "plan and prepare" stage. It does not cover the "detect and report," "respond," or "learn" stages (those are covered by ISO/IEC 27035-1 and the operational incident response).

## Workflow

1. Establish the incident management policy: scope, objectives, authority, alignment with the information security policy, and the approval process.
2. Conduct an incident scenarios assessment: identify the most likely and most damaging incident types the organization faces, considering threats, vulnerabilities, and impact.
3. Define the incident response team structure:
   - Roles: incident manager, technical responders, communications lead, legal, business liaison, executive sponsor.
   - Authority: the team's authority to act during an incident.
   - On-call: coverage across time zones and shifts.
   - Escalation paths to executives and external responders.
4. Develop the incident response plan: the policy, the scenarios, the team's roles, the procedures for each scenario type, the tools, the communication plan, the external coordination plan, and the post-incident review process.
5. Train the incident response team and broader staff:
   - Team training on the plan, the procedures, and the tools.
   - Awareness training for all staff on reporting incidents.
   - Exercises to test the plan.
6. Maintain the plan: review after each major incident, on changes to the organization's context, and on a planned cadence.

## Controls and evidence

"Plan and prepare" evidence includes the incident management policy, the scenario assessment, the team charter, the incident response plan, the training records, the exercise records, and the plan review records. The plan should be testable through tabletop or full exercises.

## Validation

Validation should confirm the policy and plan are current, the team structure operates, training has been delivered, exercises have been performed and produced improvements, and the plan is reviewed on the planned cadence. Exercises provide the strongest evidence of "plan and prepare" capability.

## Failure correction

Common failure modes: the plan is written but the team has never been exercised (correct: schedule exercises covering major incident types); the team lacks authority to act during an incident (correct: define the team's authority in the policy and confirm it at senior level); training is one-time (correct: schedule recurring training and exercises); the plan is not updated (correct: review after major incidents and on changes); the scenarios are unrealistic (correct: ground scenarios in the actual threat model and recent incidents).

## Limitations

ISO/IEC 27035-2 provides guidance for the "plan and prepare" stage; it does not prescribe specific tools or response techniques. The standard does not guarantee incident response outcomes; it ensures the preparation is consistent with the incident management policy. Sector regulations may impose specific preparation obligations.

## Scope note

This article summarizes project-neutral operations use of ISO/IEC 27035-2:2023. It does not assert any specific organization's incident response conformance or claim any certification outcome.
