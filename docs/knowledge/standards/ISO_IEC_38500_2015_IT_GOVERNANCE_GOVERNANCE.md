# ISO/IEC 38500:2015 IT Governance Governance

## Purpose

ISO/IEC 38500:2015 provides principles for the governance of information technology (IT) used by organizations. The standard is directed at governing bodies (boards, executives) and prescribes six principles: responsibility, strategy, acquisition, performance, conformance, and human behavior. Governance ensures that IT decisions are made within an explicit governance framework, that the board receives the information needed to govern, and that IT operations support organizational objectives.

## Current context and source status

ISO/IEC 38500:2015 was published as the second edition, replacing ISO/IEC 38500:2008. The standard is a governance standard, not a management system standard; it is not certifiable. It is supported by ISO/IEC 38500-1 through ISO/IEC 38500-7 implementation guides, some of which are still in draft. Verify the current ISO/IEC 38500 family status before treating any sub-standard identifier as a current requirement.

## Governance workflow and controls

### 1. Apply the six principles

Governing bodies MUST apply the six principles when directing IT:

- responsibility (individuals and groups understand and accept their responsibilities);
- strategy (IT strategy aligns with organizational strategy);
- acquisition (acquisitions are made for valid reasons, at the right price, with appropriate analysis);
- performance (IT delivers the service levels required);
- conformance (IT complies with applicable laws, regulations, and policies);
- human behavior (IT respects human factors).

### 2. Establish a governance framework

Assign responsibility for IT governance. Define decision rights. Establish a governance committee with documented terms of reference. Define escalation paths.

### 3. Direct IT strategy

Direct the development of IT strategy. Approve the strategy. Monitor progress against the strategy. Review the strategy at least annually.

### 4. Direct acquisitions

For material IT acquisitions, require a business case, alternatives analysis, risk assessment, and total cost of ownership. Approve or reject the acquisition. Document the decision.

### 5. Monitor performance

Define key performance indicators and key risk indicators for IT. Report to the governing body at a defined cadence. Investigate deviations.

### 6. Monitor conformance

Report conformance with laws, regulations, and policies. Maintain a register of applicable obligations. Audit conformance independently.

### 7. Monitor human behavior

Ensure IT policies address acceptable use, ethics, training, and human factors. Track training completion. Survey users periodically.

## Validation and evidence

- Governance charter.
- Decision rights matrix.
- IT strategy document.
- Material acquisition approvals.
- KPI and KRI reports.
- Conformance register.
- Training records.

## Failure correction

Common defects include absent governance committee, IT strategy misalignment, and acquisitions without business cases. Corrective actions include charter refresh, strategy alignment workshops, and a mandatory business case template for material acquisitions.

## Limitations

- ISO/IEC 38500 is a governance standard, not a management system standard.
- The standard is high-level; it requires translation to the organization's context.
- Some sub-standards in the ISO/IEC 38500 family are still in development.
- The standard does not prescribe specific KPIs.
