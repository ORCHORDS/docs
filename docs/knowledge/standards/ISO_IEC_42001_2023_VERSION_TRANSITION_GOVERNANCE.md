# ISO/IEC 42001:2023 Version Transition Governance

## Purpose

This article describes how an organisation records, governs, and transitions between editions of **ISO/IEC 42001**, *Information technology — Artificial intelligence — Management system*. ISO/IEC 42001:2023 is the first edition of the world's AI management-system standard. It adopts the ISO Harmonized Structure (Annex SL), defining the requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an Artificial Intelligence Management System (AIMS).

The article is governance guidance. It is not a substitute for the operative edition, an accredited certification body, or the organisation's AI management system documentation. ISO/IEC 42001 is not, on its own, a regulation, a conformity assessment for individual AI systems, or a guarantee of trustworthy AI outcomes.

## Scope

ISO/IEC 42001 was published in December 2023 by ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42, the joint technical committee sub-committee responsible for artificial intelligence. It is the first ISO/IEC management-system standard dedicated to AI and is structured under Annex SL alongside ISO 9001, ISO 14001, ISO/IEC 27001, and ISO 45001. Because the standard is at the first edition, transition governance focuses on stabilising the edition pin, mapping adjacent AI standards, and preparing for amendments or corrigenda that are likely in the early life of the standard.

The standard applies to any organisation that provides or uses AI-based products or services. It does not prescribe the technical implementation of AI systems, the algorithmic choice, or the sector in which AI is deployed. Organisations developing high-risk AI systems typically also have obligations under sectoral AI regulation (for example, the EU AI Act and analogous national regimes) that are governed independently of ISO/IEC 42001.

## Version governance workflow

### 1. Pin the operative edition and the date consulted

Every reference to ISO/IEC 42001 in policy, AI policies, audit reports, control matrices, or customer-facing material should record the exact edition (for example, ISO/IEC 42001:2023) and the publication date consulted. The publication date of the first edition is December 2023, and subsequent editions, amendments, or corrigenda should be tracked separately.

The first edition of a management-system standard is the most exposed to amendment, corrigendum, and interpretation, and an organisation that pins the operative edition now should plan for an audit or re-certification cycle that may need to consume an amendment.

### 2. Distinguish ISO/IEC 42001 from adjacent AI standards

ISO/IEC 42001 is a management-system standard. It is one of several AI-related ISO/IEC standards published by JTC 1/SC 42, and the relationships between ISO/IEC 42001 and the adjacent standards should be recorded explicitly:

- ISO/IEC 22989 — AI concepts and terminology;
- ISO/IEC 23053 — Framework for AI systems using machine learning;
- ISO/IEC 23894 — Guidance on AI risk management;
- ISO/IEC 23984 — Architecture framework for AI systems;
- ISO/IEC TS 4213 — Assessment of AI performance;
- ISO/IEC 42005 — Change management for AI systems (planned or in development);
- ISO/IEC TS 8200 — Controllability of automated systems (in development);
- ISO/IEC 5338 — AI system lifecycle processes;
- ISO/IEC 5339 — Guidance for AI applications; and
- ISO/IEC TR 5469 — Functional safety and AI systems.

A claim that an AI system complies with ISO/IEC 42001 does not imply compliance with any of these adjacent standards. Each adjacent standard has its own edition cycle and should be tracked independently.

### 3. Treat sectoral AI regulation as separate from ISO/IEC 42001 conformance

ISO/IEC 42001:2023 establishes requirements for the AIMS itself, not for the conformity of individual AI systems with sectoral AI regulation. Organisations operating in regulated jurisdictions typically have separate obligations under AI-specific laws that may impose conformity-assessment, transparency, human oversight, risk-management, data-governance, and quality requirements that are distinct from the AIMS requirements.

Public claims about AI products or services should distinguish ISO/IEC 42001 AIMS certification from regulatory compliance. Conflating these claims is a common AI-governance failure mode.

### 4. Map AIMS controls to existing management systems

Many organisations will operate the AIMS as part of an integrated management system alongside ISO/IEC 27001 (information security), ISO 9001 (quality), ISO/IEC 27018 (privacy), and ISO 31000 (risk). The AIMS should reference each operative edition of each underlying standard. A shared integrated-management-system document should retain the operative edition pin of each standard separately.

The AIMS uses Annex SL terminology and structure, so integration with other Annex SL standards is structurally straightforward but should not be done by reference alone; the AI-specific obligations (AI risk identification, AI impact assessment, lifecycle management, data-quality controls, AI policy) should be visible in the AIMS documentation.

### 5. Sequence certification transitions

ISO/IEC 42001 is at the first edition. Certification bodies are still calibrating their audit expectations against the standard, and accreditation bodies are still calibrating their accreditation expectations. An organisation pursuing ISO/IEC 42001 certification should expect:

- audit cycle definitions from certification bodies to vary in the early period;
- accreditation decisions and surveillance expectations to evolve as IAF and accreditation bodies publish guidance; and
- amendments, corrigenda, and interpretations to be issued against the first edition rather than via immediate re-edition.

The certification body's transition bulletin (or its analogue for a first edition) is the operative source for transition governance.

### 6. Preserve historical evidence under the edition it was created for

Internal audit reports, corrective-action records, AI risk registers, and impact assessments that were assessed against ISO/IEC 42001:2023 should remain labelled with the edition under which they were created. Once an amendment or a second edition is published, legacy findings should be retained under the original edition reference.

### 7. Monitor amendments, corrigenda, and JTC 1/SC 42 output

ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 publishes standards, technical reports, and interpretations relating to AI. Governance should subscribe to the SC 42 publications feed, the ISO catalogue alerts, and the publications of national mirror committees. A change-log artefact should record the date of each change, the operative edition affected, and the affected clause numbers.

## Controls and evidence

Version-transition evidence typically includes:

- a dated edition register recording the ISO/IEC 42001 edition consulted for each artefact;
- an AI policy that explicitly references the operative edition and the date issued;
- a documented AIMS scope, context-of-the-organisation analysis, and AI policy approved at the leadership level under the operative edition;
- an AI risk register and AI impact assessment artefacts stored with the edition reference under which they were created;
- adjacent-standards map listing each AI-related ISO/IEC standard consulted and its operative edition;
- certification body bulletins (or first-edition audit-programme announcements), planned audit dates, and certificate-issuance conditions;
- internal audit reports stored with the edition reference under which they were assessed; and
- training and competency records showing staff were briefed on Annex SL and on AI-specific obligations.

## Validation

Validation that the AIMS continues to meet ISO/IEC 42001:2023 requirements typically draws on:

- internal audits against the operative edition by auditors trained on Annex SL and on AI-specific obligations;
- external certification audits by an accredited certification body, where the organisation elects to pursue certification;
- management review minutes that explicitly reference the operative edition and the transition status;
- corrective action closure and effectiveness review under the operative edition;
- certification body's public register of certified clients, where certification is granted;
- accreditation-body surveillance reports that oversee certification bodies; and
- where applicable, sector-specific accreditation requirements or sectoral AI regulation.

## Failure correction

Common transition failures include:

- citing ISO/IEC 42001 without an edition in policy or customer-facing material;
- claiming that ISO/IEC 42001 certification implies compliance with sectoral AI regulation;
- conflating AIMS conformance with the technical performance or conformity of an individual AI system;
- mixing Annex SL standards into a single document that fails to identify the operative edition of each standard;
- assuming the first edition is stable and ignoring amendments, corrigenda, and interpretations;
- failing to track adjacent AI standards (ISO/IEC 22989, ISO/IEC 23894, ISO/IEC 5338) and their own edition cycles;
- making AI-system claims that rely on AIMS certification to imply technical, ethical, or regulatory conformity beyond the AIMS scope; and
- losing historical evidence under the edition it was created for.

A corrective action should document the edition under which the failure occurred, the operative edition that should have been used, the disposition of historical evidence, and the owner of the re-issued artefact.

## Limitations

ISO/IEC 42001:2023 is a management-system standard. Conformance with ISO/IEC 42001 does not equate to compliance with sectoral AI regulation, with contractual AI-related obligations, or with internal ethics commitments. The standard does not prescribe algorithmic choice, AI model architecture, or sector-specific risk thresholds.

Because the standard is at the first edition, the certification landscape is still maturing. Audit expectations, accreditation expectations, and certification body offerings will evolve, and an organisation that pursues certification should plan for revisions to its AIMS documentation as the certification ecosystem matures.
