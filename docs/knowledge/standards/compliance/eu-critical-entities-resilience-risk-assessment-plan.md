# EU critical-entities resilience risk assessment plan

**Issue:** An organization identified by a Member State as a critical entity needs an all-hazards resilience program, not only a cybersecurity plan. Directive (EU) 2022/2557 links entity risk assessment to proportionate prevention, physical protection, response, recovery, personnel-security, and awareness measures.

**Date:** 2026-08-18
**Author:** ORCHORDS
**Status:** documented

## Applicability and controls

- Confirm whether a competent Member State authority has identified and notified the legal entity under the national law implementing the Directive; sector membership alone is not the final designation.
- Map the essential service, critical infrastructure, premises, people, suppliers, cross-border dependencies, and interdependencies on which delivery relies.
- Maintain an entity risk assessment covering relevant natural and human-made, accidental and intentional, public-health, hybrid, and cross-sector risks using information supplied by the Member State.
- Link each risk to appropriate and proportionate technical, security, and organizational measures for prevention, physical protection, crisis response, resistance and mitigation, business continuity, recovery, alternative supply chains, personnel security, training, and exercises.
- Record measures in a resilience plan or equivalent controlled documents, with owners, review dates, acceptance rationale, and evidence.
- Coordinate the plan with NIS2 cybersecurity governance without assuming one assessment automatically satisfies the other.
- Apply privacy, employment, equality, and national-law safeguards to access controls and any background-check process.

## Implementation and tests

Build a service-impact model and exercise loss of a site, utility, critical supplier, workforce, communications channel, and key physical control. Verify decision authority, alerting, manual workarounds, recovery priorities, alternative suppliers, and restoration of the essential service. Preserve exercise results, observed gaps, remediation owners, and retest evidence.

Cross-reference existing risk and continuity documents where the competent authority permits, while keeping a traceable mapping to the national obligations.

## Gotchas and legal caveat

The Directive is implemented and supervised through Member State law, so designation, competent authority, evidence format, deadlines, and sanctions can differ. Other sector-specific Union rules may affect overlap. This article is an engineering readiness pattern, not legal advice; obtain jurisdiction-specific counsel and current authority guidance.
