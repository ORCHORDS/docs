# NIST SP 800-161 Rev. 1 Update 1 C-SCRM Governance

## Publication and scope

This article operationalizes **NIST Special Publication 800-161 Revision 1 Update 1, Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management Practices for Systems and Organizations**. It describes governance evidence and decisions; it does not make the publication mandatory or claim certification. Applicability depends on the organization, system, contract, and legal context.

## Multilevel C-SCRM and enhanced controls

SP 800-161r1 applies C-SCRM across organization, mission/business process, and operational levels. It calls for a C-SCRM strategy and implementation plan, policies, roles, criticality analysis, supplier and product risk assessment, requirements in acquisition, monitoring, incident response, and disposal. Its enhanced control catalog tailors SP 800-53 Rev. 5 controls for supply-chain concerns.

## Publication-specific workflow

Establish governance and a strategy; identify critical systems, components, services, and suppliers; analyze dependencies, provenance, foreign ownership or control where relevant, counterfeits, tampering, vulnerabilities, and concentration; define acquisition requirements and acceptance criteria; perform due diligence; monitor changes and incidents; maintain contingency and exit options.

Assign named owners for each decision and define review triggers. Tailor implementation to mission impact and architecture, but preserve the publication's named concepts so reviewers can trace local practice back to the source. Document assumptions, exclusions, inherited capabilities, and residual risk rather than presenting partial coverage as full implementation.

## Evidence to retain

Keep the C-SCRM strategy and implementation plan, criticality analysis, supplier inventory, sub-tier visibility, product provenance, bills of materials where available, due-diligence results, solicitation and contract clauses, acceptance tests, vulnerability and incident notices, exceptions, monitoring, and disposal or return records.

Evidence must identify scope, collection date, source, owner, and covered population. Preserve raw results separately from interpretation. When remediation occurs, retain the original finding and append verification rather than rewriting history.

## Review and metrics

Review after material system, supplier, threat, mission, or organizational changes and at the stated document cycle. Metrics must include denominators and blind spots. Track overdue high-impact decisions, evidence age, exceptions approaching expiry, failed tests, and time to verified closure. Management review should focus on consequences and unresolved risk, not a context-free completion percentage.

## Failure modes

Do not assume a reputable supplier makes every product low risk, treat questionnaires as verification, stop analysis at the first tier, demand artifacts without a use process, omit end-of-life risk, or confuse supplier financial criticality with cybersecurity criticality.

Also avoid unsupported claims based only on policy text, a product purchase, or one convenience sample. If evidence is unavailable, record the unknown, affected scope, interim safeguard, accountable owner, and decision deadline.
