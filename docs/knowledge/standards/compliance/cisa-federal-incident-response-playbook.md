# CISA Federal Government Cybersecurity Incident Response Playbook

## Publication and scope

This article operationalizes **CISA Federal Government Cybersecurity Incident Response Playbook**. It describes governance evidence and decisions; it does not make the publication mandatory or claim certification. Applicability depends on the organization, system, contract, and legal context.

## Incident response phases and checklists

The playbook structures work as Preparation; Detection and Analysis; Containment, Eradication, and Recovery; and Post-Incident Activities. It supplies checklists for coordination, incident tracking, evidence, scoping, reporting, containment, eradication, recovery, and lessons learned, with federal reporting and CISA coordination considerations that nonfederal adopters must adapt.

## Publication-specific workflow

Prepare contacts, tools, logging, authority, and exercises. On detection, open an incident record, preserve evidence, determine category and severity, scope affected entities, and notify required parties. Choose short- and long-term containment, eradicate persistence and root causes, restore from trusted sources, increase monitoring, and validate operations. Conduct an after-action review and track corrective actions.

Assign named owners for each decision and define review triggers. Tailor implementation to mission impact and architecture, but preserve the publication's named concepts so reviewers can trace local practice back to the source. Document assumptions, exclusions, inherited capabilities, and residual risk rather than presenting partial coverage as full implementation.

## Evidence to retain

Keep the incident ID, initial report, timeline, affected indicators and assets, evidence chain of custody, hypotheses, scoping queries, notification decisions, containment approvals, eradication proof, restoration validation, communications, final report, and corrective-action closure.

Evidence must identify scope, collection date, source, owner, and covered population. Preserve raw results separately from interpretation. When remediation occurs, retain the original finding and append verification rather than rewriting history.

## Review and metrics

Review after material system, supplier, threat, mission, or organizational changes and at the stated document cycle. Metrics must include denominators and blind spots. Track overdue high-impact decisions, evidence age, exceptions approaching expiry, failed tests, and time to verified closure. Management review should focus on consequences and unresolved risk, not a context-free completion percentage.

## Failure modes

Do not destroy volatile evidence during containment, declare scope from one indicator, restore before removing persistence, let public messaging outrun verified facts, or close lessons learned without owners and deadlines.

Also avoid unsupported claims based only on policy text, a product purchase, or one convenience sample. If evidence is unavailable, record the unknown, affected scope, interim safeguard, accountable owner, and decision deadline.
