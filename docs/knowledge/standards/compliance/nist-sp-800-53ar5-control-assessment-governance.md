# NIST SP 800-53A Rev. 5 Control Assessment Governance

## Publication and scope

This article operationalizes **NIST Special Publication 800-53A Revision 5, Assessing Security and Privacy Controls in Information Systems and Organizations**. It describes governance evidence and decisions; it does not make the publication mandatory or claim certification. Applicability depends on the organization, system, contract, and legal context.

## Methods, objects, attributes, and procedures

Assessment procedures use three methods: examine, interview, and test. Objects include specifications, mechanisms, activities, and individuals. Method attributes are depth—basic, focused, or comprehensive—and coverage—basic, focused, or comprehensive. Procedures contain assessment objectives and determination statements tied to SP 800-53 controls and enhancements; assessors tailor procedures to the assurance needed and available evidence.

## Publication-specific workflow

Build an assessment plan defining scope, controls, procedures, methods, objects, depth, coverage, sampling, logistics, roles, and rules for evidence. Execute each determination statement, record satisfied or other-than-satisfied results and supporting evidence, analyze deficiencies, report results, and feed remediation and authorization. Reassess corrected controls rather than changing the original result.

Assign named owners for each decision and define review triggers. Tailor implementation to mission impact and architecture, but preserve the publication's named concepts so reviewers can trace local practice back to the source. Document assumptions, exclusions, inherited capabilities, and residual risk rather than presenting partial coverage as full implementation.

## Evidence to retain

Keep the approved assessment plan, procedure version, tailored determination statements, object populations and samples, examination references, interview role and date, test conditions and raw output, assessor notes, findings, report, remediation evidence, and retest result.

Evidence must identify scope, collection date, source, owner, and covered population. Preserve raw results separately from interpretation. When remediation occurs, retain the original finding and append verification rather than rewriting history.

## Review and metrics

Review after material system, supplier, threat, mission, or organizational changes and at the stated document cycle. Metrics must include denominators and blind spots. Track overdue high-impact decisions, evidence age, exceptions approaching expiry, failed tests, and time to verified closure. Management review should focus on consequences and unresolved risk, not a context-free completion percentage.

## Failure modes

A document examination cannot prove a mechanism works. One interview cannot establish population-wide operation. Avoid defaulting every method to basic depth and coverage, hiding omitted determination statements, sampling only easy assets, or marking a control satisfied when one required objective failed.

Also avoid unsupported claims based only on policy text, a product purchase, or one convenience sample. If evidence is unavailable, record the unknown, affected scope, interim safeguard, accountable owner, and decision deadline.
