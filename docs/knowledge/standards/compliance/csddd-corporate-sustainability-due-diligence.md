# CSDDD — Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive

**Date:** 2026-08-16
**Author:** the platform team
**Status:** published

## Symptom

Your company has 5,000+ employees and EUR 1.5B+ net turnover (or is a
non-EU company with EUR 1.5B+ net turnover generated in the EU) and you
have no systematic process to identify, prevent, and mitigate human rights
and environmental impacts across your value chain. Supply chain due
diligence is ad hoc, and you cannot demonstrate compliance to regulators
or investors.

## Context

The Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (Directive 2024/1760,
"CSDDD" or "CS3D") entered into force on 25 July 2024. It establishes
legal accountability for businesses regarding human rights and
environmental transgressions across their entire value chain, both within
the EU and globally. The Omnibus I simplification package (December 2025)
significantly narrowed the scope and reduced reporting frequency.

## Scope and thresholds (post-Omnibus I)

| Entity type | Employees | Turnover | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| EU companies | 5,000+ | EUR 1.5B+ net worldwide | Both thresholds must be met |
| Non-EU companies | No employee threshold | EUR 1.5B+ net in EU | EU-generated turnover only |
| Financial sector | Deferred | Deferred | Excluded pending sector review |

## Key timelines

- **26 July 2028** — member states must adopt and publish national
  transposition measures.
- **26 July 2029** — transposed rules apply to in-scope companies.
- **1 January 2030** — Article 16 reporting measures apply (for financial
  years starting on or after this date).

## Core obligations

### 1. Due diligence process

Companies must integrate due diligence into all corporate policies and
establish a process to:

- **Identify** actual and potential adverse human rights and environmental
  impacts in their own operations and those of subsidiaries and business
  partners in the value chain.
- **Prevent and mitigate** potential adverse impacts through corrective
  action plans, contractual cascading, and capacity building.
- **Bring to an end** actual adverse impacts and provide remediation.
- **Monitor** the effectiveness of due diligence measures.
- **Communicate** publicly on due diligence.

### 2. Supplier assessment scope

Post-Omnibus I, in-depth assessment of adverse impacts is limited to
**Tier 1 suppliers only**, unless there is plausible information about
adverse impacts in Tier 2+ supply chains. Periodic assessment frequency
is reduced from annual to **at least every five years**, with ad hoc
assessments where necessary.

### 3. Climate transition plan

In-scope companies must adopt and implement a climate transition plan
aligned with the Paris Agreement 1.5°C target, including time-bound
decarbonization targets.

### 4. SME support

Companies must provide "proportionate support" to SME suppliers that
struggle with due diligence requirements: training programs, capacity-
building initiatives, or financial assistance for implementing
environmental management systems.

## Anti-patterns

- **Contractual cascading without support** — pushing due diligence
  requirements down to SME suppliers via contract clauses without
  providing training or resources. The directive explicitly requires
  proportionate support.
- **Checkbox compliance** — treating due diligence as a one-time audit
  rather than an ongoing process integrated into corporate governance.
- **Ignoring Tier 2+ signals** — the Omnibus I narrowed the default scope
  to Tier 1, but companies must still act on "plausible information" about
  deeper supply chain impacts. Ignoring credible reports is non-compliant.
- **Conflating CSDDD with CSRD** — CSRD is sustainability reporting (what
  you disclose); CSDDD is due diligence (what you do). Both apply but
  serve different purposes.

## Gotchas

- **Civil liability** — the directive includes a civil liability mechanism.
  Affected parties can bring claims against companies that fail to comply
  with due diligence obligations. Member states must ensure access to
  justice and adequate limitation periods.
- **Director duties** — directors have a duty of care to set up and oversee
  the due diligence process. This is a board-level obligation, not just an
  ESG team task.
- **Interaction with other frameworks** — CSDDD overlaps with the German
  Supply Chain Due Diligence Act (LkSG), French Duty of Vigilance Law,
  and Norwegian Transparency Act. Companies already complying with
  national laws will need to gap-assess against CSDDD requirements.
- **Sector exclusions** — financial services are currently excluded pending
  review. This may change before 2029.

## Verification

- Due diligence policy is board-approved and publicly available.
- Tier 1 supplier assessment is documented and on a 5-year review cycle.
- Climate transition plan is adopted with time-bound targets.
- SME supplier support programs are documented and budgeted.
- Grievance mechanism is operational and accessible to affected
  stakeholders.
- Legal team has mapped CSDDD requirements against existing national due
  diligence laws.
