# ISO/IEC 27005:2022 Information Security Risk Management Governance

## Purpose

ISO/IEC 27005:2022 provides guidelines for information security risk management, supporting the requirements of ISO/IEC 27001. Governance ensures that risk management is integrated with the organization's enterprise risk management, that risk acceptance criteria are explicit, that risk treatment plans are tracked, and that residual risk is communicated to decision-makers.

## Current context and source status

ISO/IEC 27005 was published in 2022 as the third edition, replacing the 2018 second edition. The standard is normative at the level of guidance: it does not prescribe a single risk methodology, but it does require certain activities (context establishment, risk assessment, risk treatment, monitoring, communication). Conformance with ISO/IEC 27001 does not require conformance with ISO/IEC 27005, but ISO/IEC 27005 is a recognized implementation guide.

## Governance workflow and controls

### 1. Establish context

Define the scope of the risk management program: organizational context, regulatory context, technical context, and information security criteria. Document the risk acceptance criteria, the impact criteria, and the likelihood criteria. Align the program with the organization's enterprise risk management.

### 2. Maintain a risk inventory

Maintain a risk register that records:

- risk identifier;
- threat and vulnerability;
- asset affected;
- impact;
- likelihood;
- inherent risk score;
- treatment decision;
- residual risk score;
- owner;
- review date.

Risk acceptance MUST be at the appropriate management level based on residual risk severity.

### 3. Conduct risk assessment

Use a documented methodology. ISO/IEC 27005 supports qualitative, semi-quantitative, and quantitative approaches. Document the methodology, scales, and rationale. Repeat the assessment when context changes.

### 4. Develop risk treatment plans

For each unacceptable risk, choose a treatment: modify, avoid, share, or accept. For modify, define controls (from ISO/IEC 27002 Annex A or otherwise), an owner, an implementation date, and a target residual risk score. For share, document the transfer mechanism (insurance, contract). For accept, document the acceptance rationale and the approver.

### 5. Monitor and review

Track treatment plan progress. Review residual risk periodically. Update the risk register when context changes, when incidents occur, or when new threats emerge.

### 6. Communicate and consult

Communicate risk to stakeholders. Consult stakeholders when defining acceptance criteria and treatment options. Document the communication plan.

## Validation and evidence

- Risk management policy.
- Context document.
- Risk register with current scoring.
- Risk treatment plans with progress evidence.
- Risk acceptance records.
- Communication and consultation records.

## Failure correction

Common defects include stale risk registers, treatment plans without owners, and risk acceptance without appropriate authority. Corrective actions include a quarterly risk register review, automated treatment plan status reporting, and an escalation path for risk acceptance above defined thresholds.

## Limitations

- ISO/IEC 27005 is guidance, not a certifiable standard.
- The standard does not prescribe specific scales; tailor them to your context.
- Quantitative risk assessment requires data the organization may not have.
- Integration with enterprise risk management requires coordination across functions.
