# ISO/IEC 27043:2015 Incident Investigation Principles Governance

## Purpose

ISO/IEC 27043:2015, *Information technology — Security techniques — Incident investigation principles and processes*, defines the principles and processes for incident investigation. The publication establishes the Investigate Process Model with eleven sub-processes that integrate digital evidence acquisition, analysis, and interpretation. Profiles that govern incident investigation should reference ISO/IEC 27043 and bind it to ISO/IEC 27037, ISO/IEC 27042, NIST SP 800-86, and the applicable legal framework.

## Current context and source status

ISO/IEC 27043:2015 was published in March 2015 and remains the current edition as of September 2026. Profiles should call out the 2015 publication date and identify the companion standards. ISO/IEC 27037 (2012 + 2018 corrigendum) and ISO/IEC 27042 (2015) are the primary companion publications.

## Governance workflow and controls

1. Plan: identify the investigation objective, the legal framework, the rules of engagement, and the relationship to the broader incident response.
2. Investigate Process Model: the eleven sub-processes are pre-incident preparation; detection; initial response; planning; investigation; response; recovery; closure; post-investigation; implementation; and review.
3. Digital evidence: integrate ISO/IEC 27037 acquisition and ISO/IEC 27042 analysis methods into the Investigate Process Model.
4. Chain of custody: maintain the chain of custody for all digital evidence throughout the investigation.
5. Reporting: report findings with the supporting evidence, the analytical methods, the limitations, and the recommendations.
6. Closure: define the closure procedure for the investigation, including the corrective-action tracking and the lessons-learnt review.
7. Document deviations with the approver, scope, expiration, compensating controls, and review schedule.

## Validation and evidence

- Incident investigation policy aligned with ISO/IEC 27043, ISO/IEC 27037, ISO/IEC 27042, and the applicable legal framework.
- Records of the eleven sub-processes for representative investigations.
- Digital evidence records, chain of custody, and tool validation.
- Closure records with the corrective-action tracking and the lessons-learnt review.
- Periodic competency assessment for the investigators.

Evidence that omits the eleven sub-process records, the chain of custody, or the closure records does not establish ISO/IEC 27043 conformance.

## Failure correction

Common defects include ad-hoc investigation procedures without the Investigate Process Model integration, missing chain-of-custody records, and missing closure records. Corrective actions include an explicit Investigate Process Model implementation, an evidence-tracking system, and a closure procedure with corrective-action tracking.
