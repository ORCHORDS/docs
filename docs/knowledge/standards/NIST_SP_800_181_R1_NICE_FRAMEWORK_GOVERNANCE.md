# NIST SP 800-181 Rev. 1 — NICE Workforce Framework for Cybersecurity Governance

## 1. Scope

This card governs the application of NIST Special Publication 800-181 Revision 1, "Workforce Framework for Cybersecurity (NICE Framework)" (November 2020), to cybersecurity workforce definition, recruitment, development, and retention at OrchordsAI. The NICE Framework provides a common, consistent vocabulary to describe cybersecurity work through Task, Knowledge, Skill, and Ability statements, organised into Work Roles and Categories. The card applies to all cybersecurity, information security, information technology risk, and adjacent privacy and resilience roles, and cross-references the earlier NIST SP 800-181 (2017) version where teams still rely on its role descriptions.

## 3. Terms and definitions

- Work role: a grouping of work for which one or more individuals are responsible within the cybersecurity function.
- Category: a broad grouping of work roles based on the dominant functions performed.
- Task (T): an activity performed to produce a specific cybersecurity outcome.
- Knowledge (K): a retrievable set of concepts, facts, or information applied to cybersecurity work.
- Skill (S): a practiced cognitive or psychomotor capability applied to cybersecurity work.
- Ability (A): a present competence to perform an observable behaviour or produce an observable outcome.
- TKSAs: collectively, the Tasks, Knowledge, Skills, and Abilities attached to a work role.
- Competency: an observable behaviour built from a combination of TKSAs in context.

## 4. NICE Framework overview and core concepts

The NICE Framework is a workforce-focused reference rather than a control framework. It separates the description of cybersecurity work from the personnel processes that staff, develop, and assess that work. NIST SP 800-181 Rev. 1 reorganised the prior 2017 structure around seven Categories and a refined set of Work Roles, while retaining and updating the TKSA building blocks. The Rev. 1 release emphasised modular use: organisations can adopt Categories, Work Roles, or individual TKSAs without committing to the entire taxonomy.

The core concepts align workforce activities to organisational outcomes. Categories group roles by dominant function. Work Roles group the TKSAs required to perform that role. TKSAs provide a measurable substrate for hiring, training, and assessment. The framework is intended to interoperate with learning program design (NIST SP 800-50 Rev. 1), role-based training (NIST SP 800-16 Rev. 1), and performance measurement (NIST SP 800-55 Rev. 1). It does not prescribe job titles or organisational structures, which remain the responsibility of each organisation.

For governance, the framework functions as a controlled vocabulary. All cybersecurity job descriptions, learning paths, performance goals, and capability assessments should reference NICE Work Roles and TKSAs by their canonical identifiers so that workforce analytics are comparable across teams and over time. Where business-as-usual teams still operate against the 2017 Work Roles, the migration path to Rev. 1 should be explicit, mapping old Work Roles to Rev. 1 roles and identifying the gaps that additional TKSAs fill.

## 5. Work role categories (Analyze, Operate, Defend, Govern, Design, Investigate, Collect)

NIST SP 800-181 Rev. 1 groups Work Roles into seven Categories. Analyze covers roles that review, evaluate, and interpret threat, vulnerability, and risk information, including threat analysts, vulnerability analysts, and exploitation analysts. Operate covers roles that administer and support the security technology stack, including systems administration, network operations, and database administration. Defend covers roles that identify, analyse, and counter threats, including cyber defense analysts, incident responders, and cyber defense infrastructure support.

Govern covers roles that establish and oversee the cybersecurity strategy, policy, and risk posture, including information systems security managers, cyber policy and strategy planners, and privacy officers. Design covers roles that engineer secure systems and architectures, including secure software assessors, security architects, and research and development specialists. Investigate covers roles that investigate cyber events and crimes, including digital forensics and cyber investigation roles. Collect covers roles that gather operational intelligence, including cyber operational planning and intelligence collection roles.

The Categories are not silos. Many work roles span multiple Categories, and the framework records overlap explicitly. Operationally, the Categories provide a planning lens for capability coverage analysis: governance-level posture (Govern), defensive capability (Defend), operational sustainability (Operate), engineering depth (Design), analytical depth (Analyze), investigative readiness (Investigate), and intelligence support (Collect). Capability gaps identified at this level drive hiring, contracting, or development priorities.

## 6. Task / knowledge / skill / ability (TKSAs) mapping

TKSAs are the atomic units of the framework. Each Work Role is composed of a defined set of T, K, S, and A statements. Tasks describe work activities; Knowledge statements describe what workers need to know; Skill statements describe practiced capabilities; Ability statements describe present competencies. Together they provide the substrate against which job descriptions, learning objectives, assessment questions, and performance criteria are written.

Mapping starts by selecting Work Roles that match organisational responsibilities. For each selected Work Role, the canonical TKSAs are recorded in the workforce capability register. Internal job descriptions reference these TKSAs explicitly, distinguishing "required" from "developing" elements. Learning paths are constructed so that each TKSA maps to one or more training modules with measurable completion and assessment criteria. Assessments sample TKSAs to confirm demonstrated competence rather than relying solely on course completion.

Coverage analysis compares the TKSAs held by current staff against the TKSAs demanded by current Work Roles. Surplus TKSAs indicate cross-training potential; deficit TKSAs indicate hiring, training, or contracting needs. The register is updated when NICE publishes TKSA changes, when business services expand into new Work Roles, or when teams adopt new technologies that introduce new TKSAs. The mapping is auditable, with each TKSA traceable to a source statement, an owner, and evidence of assessment.

## 7. Adoption and role alignment

Adoption proceeds by phases. The first phase publishes the canonical Work Role and TKSA register, harmonises internal job titles to the Work Role vocabulary, and trains people operations and hiring managers on the framework. The second phase integrates TKSAs into hiring assessments, onboarding plans, and performance reviews. The third phase extends the framework into career development, succession planning, and capability forecasting, with explicit link to strategic objectives such as new product lines, regulatory obligations, or threat landscape shifts.

Role alignment ensures that the Work Roles referenced in policy, control mapping, and incident response playbooks match the Work Roles staffed in the organisation. Mismatches between documented responsibilities and staffed competencies are governance findings and must be remediated through hiring, contracting, or scope adjustment. Where roles remain defined against the 2017 NICE Framework, a documented transition plan identifies the target Rev. 1 Work Roles, the TKSAs that have changed, and the workforce activities affected.

Capability reporting summarises coverage at the Category level and at the critical Work Role level, with trend indicators based on assessment results. Reporting cadence aligns with the management review of the ISMS so that workforce posture is treated as a control. Changes to the threat landscape, technology stack, or regulatory environment trigger a re-baselining of the selected Work Roles and the corresponding TKSA register. The framework is maintained as a living reference, with version control over every TKSA statement it adopts.
