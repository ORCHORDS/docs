# ITIL 4 Foundation (2019) — IT Service Management Governance

## 1. Scope

This card governs the adoption of ITIL 4 Foundation (2019), published by AXELOS and currently administered by PeopleCert, as the overarching framework for IT service management across the organization. It applies to every IT-managed service, including internal IT services, customer-facing platforms, and supporting infrastructure that together form the technology estate. The card establishes the vocabulary, structural model, and minimum set of practices that Service Management, Engineering, and Operations teams must apply to plan, deliver, and improve services.

## 2. Normative references

- ITIL 4 Foundation (2019), AXELOS / PeopleCert — the canonical IT service management framework.
- ITIL 4 Service Value System (SVS) and the four dimensions model (organizations and people; information and technology; partners and suppliers; value streams and processes).
- ITIL 4 Managing Professional and Strategic Leader streams — referenced for higher-level role expectations.
- ISO/IEC 20000-1:2018 — Information technology — Service management — Part 1: Service management system requirements.
- ISO/IEC 20000-2:2018 — Information technology — Service management — Part 2: Guidance on the application of service management systems.
- ISO/IEC 27001:2022 — Information security management systems, for alignment of information security within the SVS.

## 3. Terms and definitions

- **Service** — a means of enabling value co-creation by facilitating outcomes that customers want to achieve without owning specific costs and risks.
- **Service Value System (SVS)** — the ITIL model that describes how all the components and activities of the organization work together to facilitate value creation.
- **Service Value Chain (SVC)** — the set of interconnected activities an organization performs to deliver a valuable product or service.
- **Four Dimensions Model** — the four perspectives (organizations and people; information and technology; partners and suppliers; value streams and processes) that together holistically describe a service.
- **Guiding Principles** — seven universal recommendations that guide organizations in all circumstances.
- **Practice** — a set of organizational resources designed for performing work or accomplishing an objective (ITIL 4 defines 34 practices).
- **Service Level Agreement (SLA)** — a documented agreement between a service provider and a customer that identifies both the services required and the expected level of service.

## 4. Service value system

The Service Value System is the integrating model for all service-management activity across the organization.

- The organization's opportunity, demand, and value are captured as inputs to the SVS and converted through the Service Value Chain into outputs that realize value for stakeholders.
- Each of the six Service Value Chain activities (Plan, Improve, Engage, Design and Transition, Obtain and Build, Deliver and Support) has a documented owner and defined hand-offs.
- The ITIL practices are organized across the value-chain activities so that each practice has a clear home, supporting tools, and a continual-improvement feedback loop.
- Governance of the SVS is the responsibility of the IT Steering Committee, which reviews demand, value, performance, and risk on a quarterly cadence.
- The SVS integrates with ISO/IEC 20000-1 by mapping each SVS activity to the corresponding process and clause of the service management system.

## 5. Four dimensions model

The four dimensions model ensures that service decisions are evaluated holistically.

- **Organizations and people** — organizational structures, roles, culture, and workforce skills; reviewed for skill gaps and capability maturity at least annually.
- **Information and technology** — information assets, knowledge, platforms, and tooling; aligned with the data and security architecture of the organization.
- **Partners and suppliers** — external parties that contribute to service delivery; managed through the supplier management practice with documented contracts and SLAs.
- **Value streams and processes** — how work flows from demand to value; modelled using value-stream mapping and improved through the continual-improvement practice.
- A service that is designed without explicit consideration of all four dimensions is considered incomplete and must be returned for redesign.
- Security, risk, and privacy considerations are evaluated across all four dimensions and are integrated with ISO/IEC 27001 control selection.

## 6. Guiding principles

The seven ITIL 4 guiding principles are applied to every significant service-management decision.

- **Focus on value** — every activity must trace back to a customer, user, or stakeholder outcome.
- **Start where you are** — assess the current state with direct observation and data before proposing changes.
- **Progress iteratively with feedback** — deliver in small increments, gather empirical feedback, and adapt.
- **Collaborate and promote visibility** — share information openly across teams, suppliers, and customers.
- **Think and work holistically** — no service is improved in isolation; consequences across the SVS are evaluated.
- **Keep it simple and practical** — eliminate wasteful steps and use the minimum number of steps required to achieve the outcome.
- **Optimize and automate** — once a process is stabilized and understood, automate selectively to reduce toil and error.

## 7. Practices portfolio and incident/change alignment

The organization operates the ITIL 4 practices portfolio in scope of its service-management system and aligns incident and change practice with the wider SVS.

- The general management practices (architecture, continual improvement, information security, knowledge, measurement, organizational change, portfolio, project, risk, service financial management, workforce and talent) are operated with defined owners and maturity targets.
- The service management practices (availability, capacity, change, incident, incident resolution request fulfilment, monitoring, problem, release, service catalogue, service continuity, service desk, service level, service request, service validation and testing) each have documented purpose, activities, roles, and KPIs.
- The technical management practices (deployment, infrastructure, software development, platform) are aligned with engineering and DevSecOps operating models.
- The incident management practice classifies incidents by priority, restores service within SLA, and feeds the problem-management practice for root-cause investigation.
- The change management practice categorizes changes as standard, normal, or emergency; uses an advisory Change Enablement function; and maintains an audit trail of approvals.
- Performance of all practices is reported through the Continual Improvement register and reviewed monthly; ISO/IEC 20000-1 conformance is re-attested annually.
