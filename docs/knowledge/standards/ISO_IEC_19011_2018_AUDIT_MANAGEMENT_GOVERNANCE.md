# ISO/IEC 19011:2018 Audit Management Systems Guidance Governance

## Purpose

ISO/IEC 19011:2018 provides guidance on auditing management systems, including planning, conducting, reporting, and following up audits. Governance ensures that ORCHORDS audit programs follow a documented risk-based plan, that auditors meet competence requirements, and that audit results drive management system improvement.

## Current context and source status

ISO/IEC 19011:2018 (Edition 3) is the current edition and supersedes ISO 19011:2011. The 2018 edition expands the guidance to cover risk-based auditing, leadership, and the audit programme. Verify the current edition before relying on a specific clause.

## Governance workflow and controls

### 1. Establish the audit programme

- Define the objectives, scope, and criteria of the audit programme.
- Identify and assess risks to the programme and plan mitigations.
- Confirm top management commitment and resourcing for the programme.

### 2. Plan individual audits

- Define the audit objectives, scope, and criteria.
- Select the audit team based on competence and impartiality requirements.
- Prepare audit plans with documented evidence requests and timing.

### 3. Conduct audits

- Conduct the opening meeting, gather information, and verify against criteria.
- Document findings with evidence; classify as conformity, nonconformity, or opportunity for improvement.
- Conduct the closing meeting and confirm the findings with the auditee.

### 4. Report and follow up

- Issue the audit report with findings, conclusions, and recommendations.
- Verify corrective actions for nonconformities within agreed timelines.
- Review audit programme outcomes and feed them into management system improvement.

### 5. Auditor competence

- Define competence requirements for each audit role.
- Maintain records of auditor training, qualification, and ongoing professional development.
- Ensure impartiality through documented conflict-of-interest review.

## Validation and evidence

- Audit programme plan with risk assessment and top management approval.
- Audit plans with objectives, scope, criteria, and team composition.
- Audit reports with findings and conclusions.
- Corrective action records with verification evidence.
- Auditor competence records.

## Failure correction

Common defects include auditor competence gaps, missing risk-based prioritization, and unverified corrective actions. Corrective actions include competence gap analysis, programme risk review, and corrective action verification audit.
