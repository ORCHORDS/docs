# ISO/IEC 27006-1:2024 ISMS Certification Body Requirements Governance

## Purpose

ISO/IEC 27006-1:2024, "Information security, cybersecurity and privacy protection — Requirements for bodies providing audit and certification of information security management systems — Part 1: General," defines requirements for certification bodies performing ISMS audits and certifications against ISO/IEC 27001. The standard covers the certification body's structure, competence, audit process, certification decision, and surveillance activities. This article governs the application of ISO/IEC 27006-1 so a certification body auditing ISMSs operates per the standard's requirements.

## Scope

The standard applies to certification bodies performing ISMS audits and certifications. Within this knowledge base, the article covers the certification body's structure, the auditor competence, the audit process (initial audit, surveillance audit, recertification audit), the certification decision, and the obligations toward the accreditation body. It does not cover the ISMS requirements themselves; readers should consult ISO/IEC 27001 for that.

## Workflow

1. Establish the certification body per ISO/IEC 17065 and ISO/IEC 27006-1:
   - Structure, independence, and impartiality.
   - Management responsibility.
   - Resources (auditors, technical experts).
2. Demonstrate auditor competence:
   - Generic auditing competence (per ISO 19011).
   - ISMS-specific competence (per ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO/IEC 27006-1).
   - Sector-specific competence where required.
3. Perform the audit:
   - Application review.
   - Audit planning.
   - Stage 1 audit (documentation review and readiness).
   - Stage 2 audit (certification audit).
   - Reporting.
4. Make the certification decision:
   - Independent of the audit team.
   - Based on the audit findings and the corrective actions.
5. Issue the certificate and conduct surveillance audits (at least annually).
6. Recertify at the end of the certification cycle (typically three years).
7. Cooperate with the accreditation body in its oversight of the certification body.

## Controls and evidence

Certification body controls include the documented management system, the auditor competence records, the audit reports, the certification decisions, the surveillance records, and the accreditation body's records. Each certified client should be traceable through the certification body's records.

## Validation

Validation should confirm the certification body meets the standard's requirements, the audit process is consistent, the certification decisions are independent, and the surveillance operates. Accreditation by an accreditation body confirms the certification body's competence.

## Failure correction

Common failure modes: auditor competence is not maintained (correct: schedule continuing professional development); audit findings are not addressed (correct: require corrective action before certification); surveillance audits are missed (correct: schedule surveillance audits per the standard); certification decisions are made by the audit team (correct: ensure independence of the certification decision).

## Limitations

ISO/IEC 27006-1 is a standard for ISMS certification bodies; it does not certify the certification body outside the accreditation process. The standard does not replace ISO/IEC 17065 or ISO/IEC 17011; readers should understand how the standard fits with those. The standard does not address every sector; readers should consult sector-specific requirements where applicable.

## Scope note

This article summarizes project-neutral standards use of ISO/IEC 27006-1:2024. It does not assert any specific certification body's conformance or claim any certification outcome.
