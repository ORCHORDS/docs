# ISO/IEC 27042:2015 Digital Evidence Interpretation Governance

## Purpose

ISO/IEC 27042:2015, *Information technology — Security techniques — Guidelines for the analysis and interpretation of digital evidence*, defines the analytical and interpretative methods for digital evidence that complement ISO/IEC 27037 (digital evidence identification and collection) and ISO/IEC 27043 (incident investigation). Profiles that govern forensic analysis should reference ISO/IEC 27042 and bind it to ISO/IEC 27037, ISO/IEC 27043, NIST SP 800-86, and the applicable legal framework.

## Current context and source status

ISO/IEC 27042:2015 was published in June 2015 and remains the current edition as of September 2026. Profiles should call out the 2015 publication date and identify the companion standards. ISO/IEC 27037 was published in 2012 (with a 2018 update of the technical corrigendum) and ISO/IEC 27043 was published in 2015.

## Governance workflow and controls

1. Plan: identify the analytical objective, the evidence sources, the legal framework, the rules of engagement, and the relationship to the broader investigation.
2. Identification: identify the digital artefacts relevant to the investigation, using the ISO/IEC 27037 acquisition methods as input.
3. Acquisition: acquire the artefacts using the documented acquisition procedure with integrity and chain-of-custody controls.
4. Preservation: preserve the acquired artefacts in a form that supports later examination and re-examination.
5. Analysis: analyse the artefacts using validated methods, reproducible procedures, and documented findings.
6. Interpretation: interpret the findings in the context of the investigation, with the limitations and confidence levels documented.
7. Reporting: report the findings with the supporting evidence, the analytical methods, the limitations, and the recommendations.

## Validation and evidence

- Digital-evidence interpretation policy aligned with ISO/IEC 27042, ISO/IEC 27037, ISO/IEC 27043, and the applicable legal framework.
- Analytical procedure documentation with reproducibility notes.
- Examination and interpretation reports with the analytical methods, findings, limitations, and confidence levels.
- Tool validation records for the analytical tooling.
- Periodic competency assessment for the forensic practitioners.

Evidence that omits the reproducibility notes, the confidence levels, or the tool validation does not establish ISO/IEC 27042 conformance.

## Failure correction

Common defects include ad-hoc interpretation without documented confidence levels, unvalidated tooling, and missing reproducibility notes. Corrective actions include a documented confidence-level framework, a tool-validation cadence, and a reproducibility checklist.
