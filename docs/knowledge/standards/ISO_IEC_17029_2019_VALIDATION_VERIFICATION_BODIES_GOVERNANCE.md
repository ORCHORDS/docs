# ISO/IEC 17029:2019 Validation and Verification Bodies Governance

## Purpose

Govern the studio's engagement with bodies performing validation and verification under ISO/IEC 17029:2019 (conformity assessment — general principles and requirements for validation and verification bodies) so that claims the studio makes — greenhouse gas statements, environmental assertions, forecast data — are checked by competent, impartial bodies operating to a defined standard, and so the studio's side of the process (evidence, records, responsiveness) is managed deliberately.

## Scope

Applies to every validation or verification engagement the studio enters (as claimant) covering GHG statements under ISO 14064-3, environmental claims, sustainability assertions, and other declarative claims within 17029's scope. Covers body selection, impartiality requirements, the engagement process, and use of outcomes. Does not cover testing laboratories (17025), certification bodies (17021-1), or the claim substantiation work itself.

## Workflow

1. Select verification bodies accredited to ISO/IEC 17029 (via an ISO/IEC 17011 accreditation body) with accreditation scope covering the specific programme (e.g., GHG verification per 14064-3); unaccredited verification is purchased only with documented justification and accepted limitations.
2. Confirm impartiality per 17029's requirements: the body must not have provided consultancy to the studio on the claim under verification within the defined period; the check is documented before contracting.
3. Prepare the claim and evidence package per the applicable programme: the claim statement, quantification records, methodology documentation, and data trail; a claim submitted for verification without its evidence trail fails on the studio's side, not the body's.
4. Manage the engagement per the programme's steps — application, review, strategy/plan, evidence gathering, determination, and decision — tracking studio-side actions and deadlines.
5. Handle findings (misstatements, nonconformities, requests for clarification) through correction and resubmission within the engagement timeline; a material misstatement corrected late still delays the verification statement.
6. Receive and use the outcome deliberately: the validation/verification statement records the level of assurance and any qualifications; marketing and reporting use the statement only within its stated scope and qualifications.
7. Retain engagement records — submitted claims, evidence, correspondence, and the final statement — for the programme's required period and the studio's audit window.

## Controls and evidence

- Accreditation and scope verification record per verification body engaged.
- Impartiality check documentation before contracting.
- Claim and evidence package per engagement with data trail.
- Engagement action log with deadlines and studio-side completion dates.
- Final validation/verification statements with assurance level and qualifications.
- Retention configuration for engagement records.

## Validation

- Confirm each engaged body's accreditation covers the programme and scope of the current engagement.
- Sample one engagement and confirm the evidence package was complete at submission (no material late additions).
- Confirm every published use of a verification statement stays within the statement's scope and qualifications.

## Failure correction

- **Impartiality conflict discovered post-contract** → notify the body and the accreditation body, suspend the engagement, and re-engage a compliant body.
- **Material misstatement found by verifier** → correct the claim, re-run affected quantification, and root-cause the internal quality gap that produced the misstatement.
- **Statement used beyond its scope** → withdraw or correct the communication, and gate future claim communications on the statement's actual scope.

## Limitations

- Validation and verification examine claims against agreed criteria; they do not certify management systems or products.
- Accreditation status can change between engagements; re-verify per engagement, not annually in the abstract.
- Programme rules (e.g., GHG programmes layering on 14064-3) add requirements beyond 17029 itself.
