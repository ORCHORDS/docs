# NIST CSWP 23 (2024) — SBOM and Software Supply Chain Governance

## Scope

This card governs how ORCHORDS produces, attests, distributes, and
consumes Software Bills of Materials (SBOMs) and related Software Supply
Chain Levels for Secure Artifacts (SLSA) evidence. It binds the NIST
Cybersecurity White Paper 23 (October 2024), Executive Order 14028
(Improving the Nation's Cybersecurity, May 2021), and the SLSA v1.0
specification (August 2024) to a single reviewable artefact.

## Why CSWP 23 matters here

ORCHORDS software is shipped to customers, embedded in third-party
products, and consumed by federal agencies that require provenance
evidence under EO 14028. The combination of SBOMs, signed provenance
attestations, and SLSA-aligned build pipelines gives customers a
defensible answer to the question "what is in this binary?" without
forcing them to take the producer's word for it.

## SBOM baseline fields

ORCHORDS ships SBOMs that include at minimum:

- **Supplier** — the organisation that provided the component.
- **Component** — the package name, library, or firmware module.
- **Version** — the precise version string as published.
- **Unique identifier** — at least one of: package URL (purl), Common
  Platform Enumeration (CPE), SWID tag, CycloneDX bom-ref.
- **Dependency relationship** — direct, transitive, build-time, runtime.
- **Author of SBOM data** — the tool and operator that produced the SBOM.
- **Timestamp** — when the SBOM was generated.
- **Hash** — the SHA-256 of the SBOM file for tamper detection.

Two SBOM formats are accepted: SPDX (ISO/IEC 5962:2024) and CycloneDX
1.5+. ORCHORDS picks one per product line and documents the choice.

## Provenance and SLSA evidence

CSWP 23 aligns with SLSA v1.0 levels. ORCHORDS targets SLSA Build Level
3 for any software shipped to US federal customers and to any customer
contract that requires SLSA evidence:

- **Build Level 1** — provenance exists; provenance is signed but
  builds can run on untrusted runners.
- **Build Level 2** — builds run on a hosted, ephemeral, isolated
  platform; provenance is signed by a hardened signer.
- **Build Level 3** — builds run on a hardened, isolated platform with
  two-party review of the build configuration; provenance cannot be
  forged without collusion.

ORCHORDS implements SLSA Level 3 via ephemeral runners with no outbound
network except to a documented allow-list, signed and pinned build
dependencies, and an out-of-band provenance signer.

## Distribution and consumption

SBOMs and SLSA attestations are:

- Generated by the build pipeline, never by hand.
- Attached to the release as signed artifacts (sigstore / DSSE).
- Stored in a public transparency log entry with the digest of the
  artifact.
- Indexed for retrieval by digest, by component, and by release.
- Versioned together with the artifact so consumers can match a binary
  to its SBOM at any time in the future.

Consumers within ORCHORDS use SBOMs for vulnerability triage, license
review, and end-of-life planning. External consumers fetch SBOMs from the
documented transparency log endpoint.

## VEX (Vulnerability Exploitability eXchange)

CSWP 23 endorses the use of VEX statements (CISA VEX format, CycloneDX
VEX, SPDX VEX) to communicate whether a known vulnerability in a
component actually affects the produced software. ORCHORDS publishes a
VEX statement for every published CVE that affects a published release,
even when the answer is `not_affected`.

## Risk management alignment

- **NIST SP 800-161 Rev. 1 (Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management
  Practices).** CSWP 23 is the architectural companion to SP 800-161.
  Reviews must cite both.
- **NIST SP 800-218 Rev. 1 (SSDF v1.1).** Practices in the four SSDF
  groups (PO, PS, PW, RV) must be referenced when CSWP 23 controls are
  assessed.
- **ISO/IEC 27036.** External supplier relationships are tracked under
  ISO/IEC 27036-2 in parallel.

## Operational controls

- **Provenance verification.** Release pipelines verify the SLSA
  attestation before allowing a binary to publish.
- **SBOM freshness.** A SBOM is regenerated for every build; stale
  SBOMs are not accepted by downstream consumers.
- **In-toto attestations.** Source-to-binary attestations are stored
  with the SBOM and verified at deploy time.
- **Repository integrity.** Source repositories reject commits that
  bypass the attesting pipeline; commit signing is required for
  release tags.
- **Vulnerability correlation.** A daily job diffs the published SBOM
  against the CVE feed and produces a VEX draft for triage.

## Deprecations and superseded work

- **SPDX 1.x / CycloneDX 1.0–1.3** — superseded; ORCHORDS targets
  SPDX 2.3+ and CycloneDX 1.5+.
- **Webhook-style SBOM push only** — superseded by transparency log
  retrieval; push remains a convenience channel but is not the
  authoritative path.
- **Manual SBOM generation** — superseded by automated build-time
  generation.

## Reviewer checklist

- [ ] Every shipped release has a signed SBOM and SLSA attestation.
- [ ] SBOMs include supplier, component, version, unique identifier,
      dependency relationship, timestamp, and SHA-256.
- [ ] SBOMs are retrievable by digest from a transparency log.
- [ ] SLSA Build Level 3 evidence is captured for federal releases.
- [ ] VEX statements are issued for every relevant CVE.
- [ ] Source repositories enforce signed commits and attesting pipelines.

## Source of truth

CSWP 23 (October 2024) is the reference architecture. EO 14028
(May 2021) is the policy driver. SLSA v1.0 (August 2024) is the
provenance specification. NIST SP 800-161 Rev. 1 and SP 800-218 Rev. 1
are the operational companions.
