# IETF RFC 9419:2023 IETF Briefs Governance

## Purpose

IETF RFC 9419, "Considerations for Use of the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) in Internet Standards and Engineering," describes the structure, process, and artifacts of the IETF as a standards organization. The RFC explains the IETF's relationship to the Internet Society (ISOC), the Internet Architecture Board (IAB), and the Internet Engineering Steering Group (IESG); the working group process; the standards track; and the relationship between the IETF and other standards organizations. This article governs the application of RFC 9419 so engagement with the IETF is informed and effective.

## Scope

The RFC applies to anyone engaging with the IETF: working group participants, area directors, working group chairs, authors of internet drafts, and liaison representatives to the IETF. Within this knowledge base, the article covers the IETF structure, the standards process, the relationship to other standards bodies, and the artifacts of the process (RFCs, internet drafts, working group charters). It does not cover substantive technical work in any specific working group; readers should consult the relevant working group's charter and documents.

## Workflow

1. Identify the IETF engagement: the working group, the area, and the relationship to the IETF structure.
2. Apply RFC 9419's understanding of the IETF:
   - The IETF is a voluntary, consensus-based organization.
   - Standards track documents follow a defined process: internet draft, working group last call, IETF last call, IESG review, and publication as RFC.
   - The relationship to the IAB, IESG, ISOC, and the RFC Editor is defined in RFC 9280, RFC 9281, and related documents.
   - The relationship to other standards bodies is via liaison statements and joint working groups.
3. Engage with the IETF per the process:
   - Subscribe to the working group's mailing list.
   - Read the working group's charter, current internet drafts, and minutes.
   - Participate in working group meetings (in person or remotely).
   - Submit contributions through internet drafts.
   - Engage in the consensus process.
4. Document the engagement: the participation, the contributions, the consensus positions, and the liaison statements.

## Controls and evidence

Engagement evidence includes the mailing list subscriptions, the meeting attendance, the internet drafts, the comments, the consensus call responses, and the liaison statements. Each engagement should be traceable through the IETF's process.

## Validation

Validation should confirm the engagement follows the IETF process, the contributions are documented, the consensus process is respected, and the outcomes (RFCs, working group conclusions, liaison statements) are recorded. IETF records (the Datatracker, the archives) provide the validation evidence.

## Failure correction

Common failure modes: the engagement is informal and not documented (correct: document the engagement in the IETF's tools); contributions are submitted without working group review (correct: submit through the working group process); the consensus process is bypassed (correct: respect the consensus process and document objections); liaison statements are not coordinated (correct: coordinate liaison statements with the appropriate area director).

## Limitations

RFC 9419 is an overview of the IETF; it does not address every aspect of the IETF process. The RFC does not replace the working group charter, the IESG procedures, or the RFC Editor's requirements; readers should consult those documents. The RFC does not certify any IETF engagement.

## Scope note

This article summarizes project-neutral standards use of IETF RFC 9419. It does not assert any specific engagement's conformance or claim any certification outcome.
