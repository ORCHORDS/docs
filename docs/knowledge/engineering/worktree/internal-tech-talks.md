# internal-tech-talks

**Issue:** Technical knowledge from conference attendance and self-study never spreads beyond the individual
**Date:** 2026-08-11
**Status:** documented

## Symptom / Context
An engineer attends a major conference. They come back energized, full of ideas. Two weeks later, nothing has changed. Their colleagues didn't attend and don't know what was learned. The company paid thousands in travel but got one person's private education.

## Pattern / Solution
Build a structured pipeline from external learning to internal talks.

**Conference ROI requirement:**
Any engineer attending a conference on company budget must deliver an internal talk within 3 weeks of returning. This is a simple, non-negotiable expectation.

**Talk formats:**
1. **"What I learned at [Conference]"** — 20-min overview of 3 key talks/themes with takeaways
2. **Deep dive on one talk** — 30–40 min covering one talk in depth, with local applicability analysis
3. **Demo of a new technology** — 20–30 min hands-on introduction to a tool or framework

**Submission process:**
```
1. Submit talk title and abstract to #tech-talks channel (one paragraph)
2. Scheduled in next available slot (monthly cadence; slots fill fast → first-come)
3. Present live; record automatically
4. Upload recording + slides to knowledge base within 48 hours
```

**Internal talk quality bar:**
- Concrete takeaway: "What can we apply at work?"
- No sales pitches for vendor tooling
- Q&A built into the session (at least 10 min)
- Slides optional, but a written summary is required

**Speaker prep:**
- Dry run with one teammate 2 days before
- Feedback form distributed to attendees after

## Gotchas
- The 3-week deadline is critical — longer and the enthusiasm fades and the talk never happens
- Remote talks need extra production care: stable video, shared doc for questions, recorded from minute 1
- "I'll present at the all-hands instead" is not a substitute — all-hands are too short and wrong audience
