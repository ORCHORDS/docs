# ISO/IEC 42001:2023 AI Management System Governance

## Purpose

ISO/IEC 42001:2023, "Information technology — Artificial intelligence — Management system," specifies the requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an Artificial Intelligence Management System (AIMS) within the context of an organization. The standard uses the harmonized Annex SL structure shared with other ISO management system standards (ISO 9001, ISO/IEC 27001, ISO 37301), making it directly integrable with existing management systems. This article governs the application of ISO/IEC 42001 so the AIMS produces documented AI governance, risk treatment, and operational control.

## Scope

The standard applies to any organization that provides or uses AI-based products or services and seeks to govern AI responsibly. Within this knowledge base, the article covers the AIMS scope definition, the AI policy, the AI risk assessment and treatment process, the statement of applicability for AI controls, performance evaluation, internal audit, management review, and improvement. It does not cover sector-specific AI regulations; readers should overlay their sector requirements.

## Workflow

1. Define the AIMS scope: which parts of the organization are covered, which AI systems are in scope, and the boundaries and interfaces with other management systems.
2. Establish the AI policy at the appropriate level, supported by leadership, addressing the organization's commitment to responsible AI use, AI risk management, and continual improvement.
3. Identify AI risks using a documented AI risk assessment process. Reference ISO/IEC 23894 for AI risk management guidance and apply the AIMS controls to treat identified risks.
4. Develop the Statement of Applicability (SoA): for each control in Annex A, state whether it is applicable, why, and how it is implemented.
5. Implement the AIMS controls: roles and responsibilities, AI competence, awareness, communication, documented information, operational planning and control, and AI system life-cycle controls (data, models, third-party components, post-deployment monitoring).
6. Monitor, measure, analyze, and evaluate the AIMS: metrics, internal audits, nonconformity and corrective action, and continual improvement.
7. Conduct management reviews at planned intervals: review the AIMS performance, the AI risk status, the audit results, and the opportunities for improvement.

## Controls and evidence

AIMS evidence includes the AIMS scope, the AI policy, the AI risk assessment and treatment records, the Statement of Applicability, the documented roles and responsibilities, the life-cycle control records (data sheets, model cards, evaluation reports, deployment records), the internal audit results, the management review minutes, and the records of nonconformity and corrective action. The SoA is the central artifact demonstrating control applicability and implementation.

## Validation

Validation should confirm the AIMS scope is documented and current, the AI policy is communicated and supported, the AI risk assessment is performed using a documented method, the Statement of Applicability is consistent with actual practice, internal audits cover the AIMS scope, management reviews are conducted, and corrective actions are tracked to closure. Surveillance or certification audits (where pursued) verify these elements against the standard's requirements.

## Failure correction

Common failure modes: the AIMS is built without integration with existing management systems (corrective: align with ISO high-level structure and reuse compatible processes); AI risks are identified at design only and not reviewed operationally (corrective: schedule periodic AI risk reviews and trigger re-assessment on incidents); the SoA lists controls but does not show implementation (corrective: for each control, document the implementation and the evidence); management review becomes routine and uninformative (corrective: require specific performance metrics and decisions on improvement).

## Limitations

ISO/IEC 42001 specifies requirements for an AIMS; it does not prescribe AI technical controls. Annex A provides controls at a process level; the technical implementation of each control (e.g., how to test for bias) is governed by other standards. The standard does not guarantee AI systems are safe or ethical; it ensures the management system is in place to identify, treat, and monitor AI risks.

## Scope note

This article summarizes project-neutral engineering use of ISO/IEC 42001:2023. It does not assert any specific organization's AIMS certification or claim any AI system safety outcome.
