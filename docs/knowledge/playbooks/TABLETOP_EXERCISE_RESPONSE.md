# Tabletop Exercise Playbook

## Trigger

Use this playbook when resilience, security, business continuity, or crisis management training exercises must be planned, facilitated, evaluated, or remediated, including scenarios driven by incident trends, regulatory requirements, supplier changes, or recent real-world events.

## Scope

Apply the process to enterprise-wide, business-unit, and cross-functional exercises covering cybersecurity, privacy, supply chain, service continuity, crisis communications, and coordinated response across teams.

## Inputs

- exercise charter (scope, objectives, sponsor, attendees);
- scenario scripts and injects;
- participants' roles and any pre-read materials;
- the plans and playbooks being exercised;
- evaluation criteria and reporting templates.

## Steps

1. **Define objectives.** Identify what the exercise must demonstrate or test (decision-making, communications, coordination, controls, recovery).
2. **Select scenario.** Choose a scenario relevant to current risk profile and exercise goals; ensure injects escalate plausibly and exercise the relevant playbooks.
3. **Identify participants.** Engage executives, incident leads, communicators, technical owners, suppliers, and legal as required; ensure role coverage.
4. **Plan logistics.** Schedule the exercise; distribute pre-read materials and the scenario narrative; brief observers and note-takers on the evaluation criteria.
5. **Conduct the exercise.** Run the scenario through planned injects; record decisions, escalations, and actions; capture questions and gaps raised by participants in real time.
6. **Debrief in-room.** Capture immediate observations; identify what worked, what broke, what was slow, and what surprised participants.
7. **Evaluate.** Score decisions against the documented evaluation criteria; map findings to plan gaps, training gaps, tooling gaps, and process gaps.
8. **Document findings.** Produce an after-action report with findings, root causes, recommendations, and ownership; classify by severity and effort.
9. **Track remediation.** Assign owners and due dates; track corrective actions to completion; verify in subsequent exercises.
10. **Report.** Deliver the after-action report to leadership, governance bodies, and regulators as required; update plans and training.

## Escalation

Escalate to the Resilience Program Manager and Executive Sponsors when:
- the exercise reveals critical plan or capability gaps;
- the scenario prompts regulatory notification or external communication;
- a real-world incident overlaps with the exercise scenario;
- leadership wants to expand scope based on observed gaps.

## Evidence

- exercise charter, scenario scripts, and inject logs;
- participant attendance and role assignments;
- decisions, escalations, and timeline;
- after-action report and findings register;
- corrective action tracker entries.

## Completion Criteria

The exercise is considered complete when:
- participants have actively worked through the scenario and injects;
- after-action findings are documented with owners and due dates;
- corrective actions are tracked and reported;
- the exercise outcome informs plan or training updates.

## Exceptions

Document deviations from the planned exercise with the justification, scope, and risk acceptance. Critical findings remain open regardless of exercise schedule.
