# ISO/IEC 42005 AI Impact Assessment Version Guide

## Profile

ISO/IEC 42005 is the standard under development for AI impact assessment. It provides a process for organizations to identify, evaluate, and address the impacts of AI systems across the lifecycle, drawing on ISO/IEC 42001 (AIMS) Clause 6 risk planning, and coordinating with ISO/IEC 23894 (AI risk management) and the EU AI Act fundamental rights impact assessment (Article 27). It addresses AI-specific impact categories: fundamental rights, health and safety, diversity and inclusion, environment, society, and organizational.

The standard is intended to be referenced by AIMS implementations and by national regulators; it is designed to be jurisdiction-neutral so that it can serve as a baseline and be augmented by sector overlays or by regulator-specific templates (e.g., the EU Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment template for high-risk AI Act systems).

## Impact Assessment Process

| Step | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| 1 — Establish context | Identify AI system, intended use, deployment context, affected stakeholders, scope boundaries. |
| 2 — Identify impacts | Identify actual and potential impacts across the impact categories. |
| 3 — Evaluate impacts | Assess severity, likelihood, scope, and irreversibility; consider fundamental rights and beyond. |
| 4 — Treat impacts | Plan treatments (avoid, mitigate, transfer, accept) and assign owners and dates. |
| 5 — Document | Produce the AI impact assessment (AIIA) record and link to the AIMS risk register. |
| 6 — Monitor and review | Update the AIIA on material changes; review at planned intervals. |

## Impact Categories

| Category | Examples of impact considerations |
| --- | --- |
| Fundamental rights | Non-discrimination, freedom of expression, data protection, right to effective remedy, worker rights. |
| Health and safety | Physical harm, mental health, environmental harm, indirect risks (medical, mobility). |
| Diversity and inclusion | Exclusion of demographic groups, accessibility, cultural sensitivity. |
| Environment | Energy consumption, water, e-waste, climate impact. |
| Society | Democracy, civic participation, employment, education. |
| Organizational | Compliance, liability, brand, customer trust. |

## ORCHORDS Profile

| Field | ORCHORDS convention |
| --- | --- |
| Adoption | Cite this standard as the baseline for AI impact assessment, augmented by jurisdictional templates. |
| Documentation | Produce an AIIA for every AI system record; link from the AI system inventory. |
| Stakeholder engagement | Engage affected stakeholders or their representatives where disproportionate-impact risks exist. |
| Treatments | Document treatments in the AIMS risk register; align with ISO/IEC 42001 controls. |
| FRIA alignment | When the system is EU-regulated and high-risk, the AIIA MUST incorporate Article 27 FRIA content. |
| Reuse | Treat the AIIA as a living document, updated at lifecycle triggers (data change, model change, deployment change). |

## Implementation Notes

- The standard does not replace AIMS; it provides the impact-assessment methodology that AIMS Clause 6 and 8 expect.
- Stakeholder engagement is an assessment input, not a one-time consultation; design for ongoing feedback loops.
- Treat impacts as forward-looking and consider emergent properties, not just observed harms.
- The AIIA should be usable by non-technical reviewers; write for clarity over completeness.
