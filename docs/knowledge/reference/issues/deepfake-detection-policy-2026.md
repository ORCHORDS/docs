# Deepfake Detection and Platform Policy — 2026

**Date:** 2026-08-17
**Author:** the platform team
**Status:** published

## Symptom

A viral post shows a well-known person saying something they
never said. The video was generated by a consumer AI tool and
uploaded without provenance metadata. The content moderation
queue flags it only after 50,000 views because the classifier
was trained on 2024-era deepfakes and generation quality has
outpaced detection accuracy. The EU AI Act Article 50 requires
platforms to label AI-generated content or face fines, but the
anonymous upload flow has no mechanism to capture or display
provenance signals.

## Context

example project hosts user-generated video and images on a 21+
anonymous platform. Consumer-accessible generative AI tools
produce realistic synthetic media as of 2026. The regulatory
environment shifted: EU AI Act Article 50 requires synthetic
media labeling when content "could mislead a reasonable person";
the C2PA standard reached v2.1 in 2025; and the detection arms
race has accelerated as generation quality improved faster than
classifier accuracy.

## Detection method comparison

```
Detection approach    Strength            Weakness
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Pixel / frequency     Catches known       Fails on new
artifact detection    GAN artifacts       generators

Face inconsistency    Good for face-      Video compression
analysis              swaps               destroys signal

C2PA manifest         Fast, model-        Stripped on upload
check (metadata)      agnostic            / removed pre-upload

Steganographic        Survives JPEG       Model-specific;
watermark (SynthID)   re-encode ~70%      only detects its
                      quality             own generator

Foundation model      Strong on recent    Expensive; degrades
classifier (ensemble) content             on future models

Recommended 2026 stack:
  Priority 1: C2PA manifest check (fast, model-agnostic)
  Priority 2: Steganographic watermark check (SynthID etc.)
  Priority 3: Foundation model classifier (batch, async)
  Priority 4: Human escalation for high-engagement content
  Priority 5: User reporting surface (community detection)
```

## C2PA content provenance standard

```
C2PA v2.1 (2025) — Content Credentials:

  Cryptographically signed manifest bound to the asset.
  Records: creator tool, AI model used, edits, timestamps,
  optional identity.
  Stored in JFIF headers, XMP, or sidecar .c2pa file.

  At upload:
    → Extract manifest from file headers
    → Verify against C2PA trust list
    → Check: issuer trusted? manifest valid? AI label present?
    → Store: verification result + trust signal in content DB

  Absence of manifest:
    → Most content has no C2PA data (not yet ubiquitous)
    → No manifest ≠ synthetic; do not penalize absence
    → Presence of AI-generated label → Art. 50(4) obligation

  Re-encoding risk:
    Platform video compression may strip C2PA manifest.
    Either preserve the manifest through the transcode
    pipeline or document the provenance break in the DB.
```

## EU AI Act Article 50 obligations

```
Art. 50(2) — AI system providers:
  Must mark synthetic audio/video outputs in a machine-
  readable format.

Art. 50(4) — Online platform display obligation:
  Platforms subject to DSA must label AI-generated audio
  or video content "prominently" when:
    (a) Content was marked per Art. 50(2), AND
    (b) Content "could mislead a reasonable person"

  Exceptions:
    → Obvious parody or satire (context makes it clear)
    → Creative content not intended to mislead

  Enforcement:
    → National market surveillance authorities
    → Fines: up to EUR 15M or 3% global turnover (providers)
    → DSA-level fines for platforms breaching display duty

  "Technically feasible" clause:
    → Art. 50(4) applies "where technically feasible"
    → Document: detection attempted, confidence threshold,
      rate of undetectable content
    → Documentation is the audit defense when detection fails
```

## Anti-patterns

- **Blocking all AI-generated content** — Art. 50 requires
  disclosure, not prohibition. Labeled synthetic media is
  permitted. Blanket blocks are overinclusive.
- **Treating C2PA absence as a deepfake signal** — most
  content, including all pre-2024 content, has no manifest.
  Absence is not a positive detection signal.
- **Running detection only at upload** — new classifier
  generations will make previously undetected content
  detectable later. Build a retroactive scan pipeline for
  high-engagement content.
- **Single-model detection** — any single classifier becomes
  obsolete as generation quality improves. Ensemble approaches
  degrade significantly more slowly.

## Gotchas

- **C2PA manifests can be stripped** — any file transcoding
  (resize, re-encode) may remove C2PA data unless the tool
  explicitly preserves manifests. This is a pipeline risk.
- **Art. 50(4) applies only to marked content** — if the AI
  system provider did not embed a mark, the platform's display
  obligation does not trigger. Detection-based labeling is good
  practice but is a distinct action from the legal duty.
- **Parody exception is narrow** — ambiguous parody should err
  toward labeling to reduce Art. 50 liability exposure.
- **SynthID is generator-specific** — detects only Google /
  Runway outputs; other generators require other detectors or
  ensemble approaches.

## Verification

- C2PA manifest extracted and checked on every image/video
  upload.
- AI-generated label displayed when C2PA mark is present and
  Art. 50(4) threshold is met.
- Detection pipeline is ensemble, not single-model.
- Video re-encode pipeline preserves or logs provenance breaks.
- Documentation of detection attempts available for audit.
