# UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence

## Scope

Reference card for the UNESCO *Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence* (November 2021). The Recommendation is the first global standard on AI ethics, adopted by the 193 UNESCO member states. Profiles that govern AI ethics should reference the UNESCO Recommendation by adoption year and bind it to the OECD AI Principles, the EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 23894 (AI Risk Management), and NIST AI RMF.

## Plan

1. Reference the UNESCO Recommendation by adoption year whenever a profile governs AI ethics.
2. Apply the four core values: respect, protection and promotion of human rights, human dignity, and cultural diversity; environment and ecosystem flourishing; ensuring diversity and inclusiveness; building peaceful, just, and interconnected societies.
3. Apply the ten core principles: proportionality, fairness, transparency, explicability, safety, security, accountability, redress, sustainability, and the human oversight principle.
4. Apply the policy-action areas: ethical impact assessment, ethical governance, data governance, development and international cooperation, environment and ecosystems, gender equality, culture, education and research, communication and information, labour, and health and social well-being.
5. Conduct a Readiness Assessment methodology (RAM) per UNESCO guidance to evaluate the country's AI ethics readiness.
6. Bind to ISO/IEC 23894:2023 for the AI risk-management framework and to ISO/IEC 42001:2023 for the AIMS.
7. Document deviations with the approver, scope, expiration, compensating controls, and review schedule.

## Inputs

- UNESCO Recommendation text (November 2021).

- Internal AI ethics policy, impact-assessment procedure, and governance documentation.

## ORCHORDS Profile

ORCHORDS treats the UNESCO Recommendation as the canonical international reference for AI ethics. Profiles that reference AI ethics should cite the adoption year, identify the values and principles adopted, and bind to the OECD AI Principles, ISO/IEC 23894, and the EU AI Act where applicable.

A profile that references "AI ethics" without binding to a recognized international framework is non-conformant.

## Implementation Notes

- The UNESCO Recommendation is non-binding under international law but signals the policy direction of the 193 member states.
- The Readiness Assessment methodology (RAM) provides a structured approach to evaluating AI ethics readiness.
- The Recommendation applies to all AI actors (governments, companies, academic institutions, civil society).
- The Recommendation's policy-action areas are organized around values and principles; map each policy action to the organization's operations.
- AI ethics should be integrated with the broader governance, risk, and compliance (GRC) framework.
