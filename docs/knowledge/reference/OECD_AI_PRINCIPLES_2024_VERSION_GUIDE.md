# OECD AI Principles Version Guide

## Profile

The OECD AI Principles (originally adopted May 22, 2019; updated May 3, 2024 to add a Generative AI section) define values-based principles for responsible stewardship of trustworthy AI, plus recommendations to OECD members and adherents on national policies and international cooperation. The 2024 update added a section on generative AI and is the first intergovernmental standard on GAI. Adherents include OECD member states, G20 nations, and the EU; the G7 Hiroshima Process is aligned with the OECD framework.

## Core Principles

| # | Principle (2019, retained in 2024 update) |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | AI should benefit people and the planet by driving inclusive growth, sustainable development, and well-being. |
| 2 | AI systems should respect human rights, democratic values, fairness, and the rule of law. |
| 3 | AI systems should be transparent and explainable so that people understand and can challenge their outcomes. |
| 4 | AI systems must be robust, secure, and safe throughout their lifecycles; potential risks should be continuously assessed and managed. |
| 5 | Organizations and individuals developing, deploying, or operating AI systems should be held accountable for their proper functioning. |

## 2024 Generative AI Additions

| Theme | Substance |
| --- | --- |
| Transparency and disclosure | Disclose AI-generated content; develop labeling and provenance standards. |
| Quality and safety of training data | Address copyright, personal data, bias, and data quality in GAI training datasets. |
| Human oversight and review | Maintain human-in-the-loop review for material decisions; document review processes. |
| Robustness, security, and safety | Apply AI RMF-style controls to GAI; monitor for dual-use and CBRN-relevant capabilities. |
| Accountability and redress | Provide mechanisms for affected persons; document responsibility chains; remedy harms. |
| International cooperation | Cross-border data flows, joint research, shared evaluations. |

## Implementation Notes

- The 2024 update is a Section IV addition, not a replacement; 2019 principles remain authoritative.
- Compliance with OECD does not imply compliance with the EU AI Act (the latter is binding EU law); map overlapping and divergent requirements separately.
- The principles are values-based; pair them with the controls-based AIMS (ISO/IEC 42001) and the AI RMF Core functions.
- Adherents update periodically; treat this version guide as up to date as of May 2024 and confirm current version at use.
