# ISO/IEC 30134 Series Data Centre Key Metrics Governance

## Purpose

The ISO/IEC 30134 series defines key performance indicators (KPIs) for the operational sustainability of data centres. The series covers categories including energy effectiveness (PUE), renewable energy use, water usage effectiveness (WUE), carbon usage effectiveness (CUE), IT equipment utilization, and other KPIs. This article governs the application of ISO/IEC 30134 so an organization can measure and report data centre sustainability using the standard's KPIs.

## Scope

The series applies to any data centre operator (own or colocation). Within this knowledge base, the article covers the main KPIs in the series (PUE for energy, WUE for water, CUE for carbon, RER for renewable energy, ITEU for IT equipment utilization, ERF for energy reuse factor), the data sources for each KPI, the measurement methodology, and the documentation of the KPIs. It does not cover the specific KPIs from the EU Code of Conduct on Data Centres or other regulatory regimes; readers should overlay their sector requirements.

## Workflow

1. Identify the data centre facilities in scope and the boundaries for each KPI (the standard defines the boundary for each KPI explicitly).
3. Implement the measurement methodology for each selected KPI:
   - PUE: total data centre energy / IT equipment energy, measured at the facility boundary and at the IT equipment output.
   - WUE: annual water use (evaporation, consumption) / IT equipment energy.
   - CUE: total CO2 emissions (location or market-based) / IT equipment energy.
   - RER: renewable energy / total energy.
   - ITEU: IT equipment output (computation, storage, network capacity) / total IT equipment capacity.
   - ERF: energy reused outside the data centre / total data centre energy.
4. Calibrate and validate the measurement instruments. Document the measurement uncertainty.
5. Calculate the KPIs over the reporting period. Use the reporting boundary and methodology the standard defines.
6. Publish the KPIs in a sustainability report.

## Controls and evidence

KPI evidence includes the measurement records, the calculation methodology, the boundary definitions, the calibration records, and the published report. Each KPI should be traceable to its underlying measurements.

## Validation

Validation should confirm the boundary definitions match the standard's definitions, the measurement instruments are calibrated, the calculations follow the standard's methodology, and the published KPIs match the calculations. Periodic audits confirm the methodology remains consistent.

## Failure correction

Common failure modes: the PUE calculation includes non-IT energy that should be excluded (correct: apply the boundary definitions strictly); renewable energy claims are not backed by renewable energy certificates (correct: document the RECs and apply the standard's RER methodology); water use is not measured (correct: install metering where the standard requires); KPI values are reported without documentation (correct: document the calculation methodology and the data sources).

## Limitations

The ISO/IEC 30134 series defines KPIs; it does not certify sustainability performance. The KPIs are not directly comparable across data centres with different boundary choices; the boundary definitions must be published alongside the values. The standard does not address the substantive technology choices (cooling technology, power architecture) — those are governed by engineering and procurement.

## Scope note

This article summarizes project-neutral platform use of the ISO/IEC 30134 series. It does not assert any specific data centre's KPI conformance or claim any certification outcome.
