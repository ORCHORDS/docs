# Mailing List Alignment and Feedback Loops

A mailing list is an authentication machine that runs backward. It takes authenticated mail from authors, re-emits it from its own infrastructure, and by doing so strips the alignment DMARC requires: the connecting IP belongs to the list, the envelope often belongs to the list, and the From still belongs to the author. Meanwhile, every complaint generated by list mail flows into feedback loops - the ARF-format reports mailbox providers send to registered senders - and lands at whichever domain registered the loop. Alignment strategy and FBL registration strategy are therefore one design problem: who you pretend to be when re-emitting determines where complaint evidence arrives and whether you can act on it.

## Scope

This article covers the From-alignment decision space for mailing lists, the registration and operation of ARF feedback loops, and the closed-loop response process turning complaint reports into list hygiene. It applies to list operators and hosting platforms. It does not cover DKIM signature preservation techniques in full, ARC sealing, consent law, or mailbox-provider reputation thresholds, each of which is its own discipline.

## Workflow or implementation guidance

**Decide the identity strategy per list.** Three patterns dominate, and the choice sets everything downstream. Original-From preservation keeps the author's address visible, preserving DKIM where the list avoids modifying signed content, but guarantees DMARC misalignment for author domains publishing strict policies - receivers then apply their own indirect-mail handling, from junk-foldering to rejection. From-rewriting remaps the visible From to a list-owned address (often author-at-list-domain) with Reply-To pointing at the author, achieving alignment at the cost of author visibility and reply semantics. Munging variants sit between. Choose per list based on author-domain composition: traffic dominated by domains publishing p=reject almost forces rewriting; internal lists with permissive policies can preserve originals.

**Protect what the author signed.** Whatever the identity choice, minimize modifications to signed content: no footer injection into the body, no subject tagging, no header reordering beyond additions - intact DKIM is the strongest surviving evidence after alignment is lost. Where modification is unavoidable, restrict it to regions the signature's body length tag does not cover, or accept that the signature dies.

**Register feedback loops for the list's sending identity.** FBLs are per-domain registrations with each mailbox provider operating one. Register under the domain the list actually sends from - the envelope and header domain of re-emitted mail - with a dedicated role inbox, completing each provider's verification, which typically demonstrates domain control and accepts program terms. Multi-tenant platforms must map registered domains to the tenant operating each list, or complaints pool uselessly.

**Ingest and parse ARF reports.** Complaints arrive as multipart reports: a human-readable part, a machine-readable `message/feedback-report` part carrying feedback type, arrival date, and original headers, plus the original message. Parse to extract reporter provider, the original envelope recipient (usually encoded in a per-recipient address form), list identity from List-Id, and the feedback type - abuse, fraud, and other categories carry different response obligations.

**Close the loop.** Complaint events feed the hygiene engine: the implicated recipient is suppressed or unsubscribed per policy, repeat-complaint lists get throttled, and complaint-rate trends are compared against provider thresholds for bulk senders. The loop is closed only when suppression actually happens - an FBL inbox nobody acts on is worse than no FBL, because it creates the appearance of responsiveness.

**Report upstream.** For lists hosting third-party authors, forward abuse-class complaints to the author's domain when identifiable, since list behavior is spending the author's reputation.

## Controls

- Per-list identity policy record: chosen alignment strategy, the author-domain DMARC posture that justified it, and a review date.
- DKIM preservation checklist in the re-emission pipeline: additions only, no signed-content modification, verified per deployment.
- FBL registration register mapping each provider to registered domain, role inbox, and tenant owner, with re-verification dates.
- ARF parse pipeline tolerating provider variation in field completeness, flagging unparseable reports rather than discarding them.
- Suppression SLA: complaint-to-suppression time bounded and measured, with backlog alerts.
- Complaint-rate dashboards per list and provider against documented bulk-sender thresholds.
- Feedback-type routing: abuse, fraud, and other categories dispatched to different responders.
- Privacy constraints on FBL data, retained only as long as the hygiene action requires, per registration terms.

## Validation evidence

- An injected test complaint through a cooperative provider's FBL that parses, maps to the correct list and recipient, and produces suppression within the SLA.
- DKIM survival measurement: proportion of re-emitted list mail retaining a valid author signature, sampled across representative domains.
- Alignment audit: DMARC evaluation outcomes at a receiver for a sample of list mail, confirming the chosen strategy produces the intended result.
- FBL coverage matrix showing which major providers' loops are registered and the last verified report date from each.
- Complaint-rate trend reports per list with threshold breaches annotated and remediated.
- Suppression reconciliation: FBL events versus suppression-list mutations over a fixed window, matching one-to-one with documented exceptions.

## Failure modes and correction

Complaints arriving for unidentifiable lists usually mean the parser is not extracting List-Id, or the registration maps multiple tenants to one domain with no disambiguation - add per-list encodings in the envelope recipient. Mail from p=reject author domains bouncing under original-From preservation is the alignment debt coming due; rewrite From selectively for those domains or move to an ARC-sealing path and accept uneven receiver support. FBL registrations silently expiring - providers periodically re-verify - appear as gaps in the coverage matrix where reports used to arrive; calendar re-verification. An overflowing FBL inbox with no suppression changes means the closing step lost its owner; reassign and instrument the SLA. Complaint-rate spikes after list growth or member imports trace to consent quality, not authentication; halt sends, segment, re-verify. Fraud-class complaints mixed into bulk abuse handling indicate feedback-type routing was never wired; split it, since fraud implies spoofing rather than list fatigue. DKIM survival dropping after a template change - a new footer, a re-wrapped body - identifies the modification as signature-breaking; revert or move it outside signed regions.

## Limitations

FBL coverage is partial: many providers operate none, and several provide only aggregate or anonymized data, so complaint rates are a sample, not a census. Alignment outcomes are receiver-dependent - original-From survival under misalignment varies by provider policy, and rewriting degrades user experience without guaranteeing delivery. ARF field completeness varies, and recipient identification often depends on the list's own addressing conventions. Feedback loops report complaints, not deliverability: a list can be junk-foldered everywhere with a clean FBL. Provider complaint thresholds are documented for bulk senders generally and change over time. Multi-tenant platforms inherit a mapping problem - complaint evidence is only as actionable as the tenant attribution built alongside it.
