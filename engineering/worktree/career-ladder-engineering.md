# career-ladder-engineering

**Issue:** Engineers don't know what's expected for the next level, leading to surprise in performance reviews
**Date:** 2026-08-11
**Status:** documented

## Symptom / Context
An engineer says "I've been here two years, I think I deserve a promotion." The manager is caught off guard. There's no shared understanding of what the next level requires. Promotion decisions feel arbitrary.

## Pattern / Solution
A published engineering career ladder makes expectations explicit and gives engineers agency over their growth.

**Typical levels and themes:**
| Level | Title | Focus |
|-------|-------|-------|
| L1 | Junior Engineer | Learning; works on well-scoped tasks with guidance |
| L2 | Engineer | Independent; delivers features end-to-end |
| L3 | Senior Engineer | Multiplies others; owns cross-cutting concerns |
| L4 | Staff Engineer | Org-wide impact; sets technical direction |
| L5 | Principal Engineer | Company-wide scope; external influence |

**Dimensions to define per level:**
1. **Technical scope** — What size/complexity of problems can they solve?
2. **Independence** — How much guidance is needed?
3. **Impact** — Team / org / company?
4. **Leadership** — Do they mentor others? Lead projects? Set standards?
5. **Communication** — Who do they communicate with? At what level of abstraction?

**Ladder document structure per level:**
```markdown
## L3 — Senior Engineer

### Scope
Owns a service or significant subsystem. Identifies problems before being asked.

### Technical
- Designs systems with appropriate trade-offs
- Writes code that others learn from
- Drives architectural decisions within the team

### Collaboration
- Mentors L1/L2 engineers proactively
- Gives constructive code review beyond surface-level issues

### What does NOT make you an L3
- Tenure alone
- Being the most experienced person on the team
- Doing L3 work without recognition (document the impact)
```

## Gotchas
- Levels should describe demonstrated behaviors, not just skills or years of experience
- "Does the work of the next level before promotion" is the signal — not a reason to wait indefinitely
- Keep the ladder public internally; hidden ladders breed distrust
