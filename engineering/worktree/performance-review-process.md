# performance-review-process

**Issue:** Performance reviews are stressful, inconsistent, and disconnected from ongoing feedback
**Date:** 2026-08-11
**Status:** documented

## Symptom / Context
Reviews happen once a year. Engineers receive feedback they've never heard before. Ratings feel subjective. Calibration sessions are political. High performers leave because they felt undervalued; low performers are surprised by a PIP.

## Pattern / Solution
A well-designed performance review process is continuous, structured, and tied to the career ladder.

**Recommended cadence:**
- Monthly: informal feedback in 1-1s (no written record required)
- Mid-year: formal written check-in (not a rating — a growth conversation)
- Annual: formal review with compensation discussion

**Review inputs:**
1. **Self-review:** Engineer reflects on achievements, growth, and goals (template below)
2. **Peer feedback:** 2–3 peers selected by engineer + 1 added by manager; structured survey
3. **Manager assessment:** Manager writes narrative against career ladder dimensions
4. **Calibration:** Managers align ratings across teams to reduce bias

**Self-review template:**
```markdown
## Performance Review Self-Assessment — [Name] — [Period]

### Key accomplishments
List 3–5 with business impact (not just what you did, but the effect)

### Areas of growth since last review

### What I want to develop in the next period

### Feedback for my manager
What could your manager do more, less, or differently?

### Level assessment
Which ladder criteria do I meet? Where am I still developing?
```

**Calibration best practices:**
- Calibrate ratings before sharing them with engineers
- Use concrete examples, not impressions
- Flag potential bias (recency bias, halo effect, affinity bias)

## Gotchas
- No surprises: anything in a written review should have been said in a 1-1 first
- Avoid stack ranking — it destroys collaboration
- "Meets expectations" must be destigmatized; it means doing the job well, not failing
